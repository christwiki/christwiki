A large part of John VIII's register survives in a later copy, so that for this pope, unusually, there is a run of dated outgoing letters. Three matter for [[event:cyril-and-methodius]]: letter 200 to Svatopluk and letter 201 to [[person:methodius]] (both summer 879), which summon the archbishop to Rome and recall an earlier ban on mass in Slavonic; and letter 255, *Industriae tuae* (June 880), which declares him orthodox and allows the Slavonic liturgy. Letter 207 (879), to the emperors at [[place:constantinople]], argues that the Roman see may loose what a synod has bound, with [[person:photius]] in view. Beside the Latin of letters 207 to 209 the editor prints the Greek version read at the council of 879–880, which he heads *Versio Graeca adulterata*, a falsified version with Photius as its author; a letter on the creed that survives only in Greek (258a) he judges to be, as it seems, wholly invented. On the dispute see [[source:council-of-constantinople-879]].

These are the pope's own words, written at the time, and they are the firmest evidence on either question.
