The text is the first of two unrelated fragments that Henri Valois published in 1636 from a single ninth-century manuscript; they are still named after him. Its own title is *Origo Constantini Imperatoris*. The author is unknown. J. C. Rolfe, its Loeb translator, dates it to about 390 and judges that it rests on good sources.

It is useful as a check on Lactantius and Eusebius because it is a secular narrative with no interest in church affairs: it gives the marriage of [[person:licinius]] at [[place:milan]], the two wars between him and [[person:constantine]], his death at Thessalonica and the renaming of Byzantium. A few sentences on religion, among them the remark that Constantine was the first Christian emperor (6.33), are in the words of the fifth-century Christian historian Orosius, as the translator's notes point out; the comment that the death of Galerius was a punishment for the persecution (3.8) is of the same kind.
