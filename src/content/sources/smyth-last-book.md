Smyth writes that his purpose is to correct himself and not to condemn others, and that he has changed his mind before and is ready to do so again. Against Helwys he says that he never held it lawful for private persons to baptize where a true church and ministers exist from whom baptism can be had; what he held was that, knowing no church they could join with a good conscience, he and his people "might baptize our selves".

The editor's notes in the volume cited here say that the piece was printed, with a confession and an account of Smyth's death, by members of his congregation, and that it breaks off in the middle of a sentence. The date given above is approximate: the text answers Helwys's printed charges of 1611 and was published after Smyth died. It is cited in [[event:first-baptists]].
