The edict was issued in the name of [[person:charles-v]] after the hearing at [[place:worms]], with, as it says, the consent of the electors, princes and estates assembled there. After reciting the proceedings in [[person:martin-luther]]'s case, it declares that the emperor, in execution of the pope's bull, holds Luther to be a member cut off from the church of God, an obstinate schismatic and a manifest heretic. No one is to house, feed or help him; he is to be seized and handed over; his supporters may be attacked and their goods taken; his books are not to be bought, sold, read, copied or printed, and are to be burned. A final section places all printing on matters of faith under the approval of the bishops and a theological faculty.

Kidd prints extracts and dates the edict 26 May 1521. It remained the law of the empire on Luther's case, and the later diets of [[place:speyer]] turned on whether and how it was to be enforced.
