The bull has three parts. It first names the ten men, all masters of arts of the University of Paris, and describes what they had been doing: preaching, exhorting people in private, serving in hospitals, teaching children and the unlettered. It then recites the Formula of the Institute that they had submitted, and finally approves it, limiting the Society for the time being to sixty members.

The Formula is the founders' own text and the rule of the Jesuits as first approved. It speaks in the first person plural and sets out the purpose of the Society, the special vow of obedience to the pope concerning missions, the authority of the superior, poverty, and the colleges for students. A revised Formula was approved by Julius III in 1550 in the bull *Exposcit debitum*. The editors of the English translation give the date of the bull as 27 September 1540; Kidd's text omits the dating clause.
