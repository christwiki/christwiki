A calendar is a printed digest: for each document the editor gives the date, the writer and the recipient and then the contents in English, in full or in summary, with words of the Italian here and there in brackets. The entries are close to the originals but are not transcripts, and the English is the editor's.

The letters cited for the death of [[person:reginald-pole]] were written from London by Alvise Priuli, of the cardinal's household, who calls him "my master". In the letter to his brother dated 27 November 1558 (no. 1286) he writes as one who was in the room: Pole spoke "to his intimate friend, the Bishop of St. Asaph, and to me" on hearing that the queen was dead, and had left the carrying out of his will to him. He gives the hours: the queen died on the 17th, seven hours after midnight, and the cardinal at seven in the evening of the same day. He repeats the day in letters of December (nos. 1291 and 1292). He writes as a devoted friend of both.

The same pages hold a different report. The Venetian ambassador with King Philip, Michiel Surian, wrote from Brussels on 30 November, on what the king's confessor had told his secretary, that the queen died on the 17th and Pole "on the morrow" (no. 1289).
