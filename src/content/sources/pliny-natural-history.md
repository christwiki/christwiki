Pliny the Elder dedicated the *Natural History* to Titus in AD 77 and died two years later in the eruption of Vesuvius. Books 3 to 6 are a geography of the known world, compiled largely from earlier writers and official lists.

In his description of Judaea he says that the Essenes live on the west side of the Dead Sea, away from the shore, a people without women or money, with only palm trees for company, whose numbers are kept up by newcomers weary of life. "Below" them, he says, was the town of En-gedi, and then Masada. He writes as a geographer passing on a curiosity. He does not say where his information came from, and his remark that En-gedi now lies in ashes shows that the passage was written or revised after the Jewish war of AD 66–70.
