The convention has a preamble and seventeen articles. The government recognises the Catholic religion as that of the great majority of French citizens. The pope accepts a new map of dioceses, the resignation of the existing bishops and nomination of bishops by the First Consul, and undertakes not to disturb the buyers of alienated church lands; the government allows public worship, returns unsold churches and pays the clergy.

When the convention was promulgated in France on 8 April 1802 it was accompanied by the Organic Articles, a French law regulating the church in detail, which Anderson prints as no. 64B. See [[event:french-revolution-and-the-church]].
