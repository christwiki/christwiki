Wriothesley was Windsor Herald, an officer of arms in London. His editor shows that the chronicle down to the eleventh year of Henry VIII is copied from an older London chronicle and that from then on it is original, the events being set down as they happened, usually with the day of the month and the day of the week.

It is cited here for dates and public events: the trials and deaths of [[person:john-fisher]] and Thomas More, the fall of [[person:thomas-cromwell]], the death of [[person:henry-viii]], the coming of the new service books. He records the changes in religion with approval, calling the Acts of the Parliament of 1549 "godly", and his notes on those who died for refusing them should be read in that light. The footnotes in the printed edition are the Victorian editor's and are not cited.
