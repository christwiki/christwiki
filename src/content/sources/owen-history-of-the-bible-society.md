Owen was present at the meeting of 7 March 1804 and spoke at it. When the committee chose secretaries on 12 March he declined, and Josiah Pratt was appointed beside the Baptist Joseph Hughes; on 23 April Pratt resigned and Owen took his place (vol. 1, pp. 53–54, 60). For the two years before that meeting he was not himself a witness; he first heard of the plan, he says, from the circular that announced the meeting. He writes as the Society's advocate.

It is cited in [[event:bible-society]] for the sequence of events from Thomas Charles's visit to London in 1802 to the founding meeting. In the pages on Charles's visit (vol. 1, pp. 15–17) Owen does not mention the story, told in later accounts, of a Welsh girl who walked to Bala for a Bible; that story is not used in this wiki.
