Allen wrote this book against [[source:cecil-execution-of-justice]], which he calls a libel. Its first chapter holds that many were condemned "for mere matter of religion" under statutes of Elizabeth's own reign that turned cases of conscience into treason, and it names men executed under a statute of the thirteenth year of the reign and under one made in the latest Parliament. Its second chapter takes up [[person:edmund-campion]] and those tried with him, who were arraigned under the statute of the twenty-fifth year of Edward III for compassing the queen's death at Rome and Rheims, and argues that the charge was invented. The fourth chapter defends the answers that the priests gave when questioned about the bull of [[person:pius-v]].

Allen is as much a party to the quarrel as the tract he answers. Campion consulted him at Rheims on his way to England, as Allen's own [[source:allen-briefe-historie]] tells. The year 1584 is the one given on the title of the 1914 edition.
