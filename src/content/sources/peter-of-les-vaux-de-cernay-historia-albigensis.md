The author calls himself brother Peter, a monk of Vaux-de-Cernay, and dedicates the work to Pope [[person:innocent-iii]]. He says in the dedication that he has set down nothing that he did not see with his own eyes or hear from persons of great authority, and that the heretics of Toulouse and the other towns, with their defenders, are called Albigensians because that was the name other nations gave to the heretics of the region. The narrative ends with the death of [[person:simon-de-montfort]] before Toulouse.

Peter writes as a partisan. Simon is "our count" and the soldier of Christ; [[person:raymond-vi-of-toulouse]] is a deceiver; the dead of Béziers received what they deserved. His second chapter, on the heretics' sects, is a list of charges drawn up by an enemy, some of them doctrine and some scandal. It is cited in this wiki as what the crusade's own historian said the heretics believed, and for events at which he or his informants were present.
