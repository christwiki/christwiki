The prologue says that Marco Polo spent twenty-six years in various parts of the world and that in 1298, as a prisoner at Genoa, he had Rusticiano of Pisa, a fellow prisoner, put the whole into writing. It presents the book as a record of what he had seen and of what he had heard on good authority. Yule's introduction argues that the Old French text is the source of all the other versions.

Book 3 follows the sea route home from China by way of India. Chapter 18 describes the place "where lieth the body of St. Thomas the Apostle", a small town in the province of Maabar visited by Christian and Muslim pilgrims, and gives the story of the saint's death as the Christians who kept the church told it. Polo does not name the town. Yule's note identifies it with Mylapore, now part of [[place:chennai]]. See [[event:thomas-and-india]].
