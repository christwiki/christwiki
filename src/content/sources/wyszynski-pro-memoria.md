Wyszyński kept dated notes under the heading *Pro memoria*; the editors remark that in these years they have gaps (p. XIV). This volume prints those of his confinement from the manuscripts, with the letters and petitions he copied into them, and marks his deletions and the editors' additions. The editors say that the notes first appeared, in the main, as *Zapiski więzienne* ("prison notes"), prepared with the author and published in Paris in 1982, after his death (p. XIII).

The entry for 25 September 1953 tells how officials came to his house in Miodowa Street in Warsaw that night with a government decision dated the day before, and how he was driven away after midnight (pp. 3–7). The entries for 28 and 29 October 1955 copy the order of the Office for Religious Affairs, dated 27 October 1955, that allowed him to live in the convent of the Nazareth sisters at Komańcza without the right to leave the place, and that kept in force the ban on exercising his offices laid down in the government's resolution no. 700 of 24 September 1953 (pp. 144–149). The entry for 26 October 1956 records the visit of two envoys of Władysław Gomułka, who asked that he return to Warsaw (pp. 195–197).

The notes are the prisoner's own record, written at the time, and give his side only. The introduction and the footnotes are the editors'; the introduction says that he returned to Warsaw on 28 October 1956 (p. X), a day for which the volume prints no entry of his. See [[person:stefan-wyszynski]].
