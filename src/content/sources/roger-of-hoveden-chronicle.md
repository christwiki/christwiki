Roger of Hoveden (Howden, in Yorkshire) served in the royal administration; the Fordham editor describes him as a royal clerk who compiled his history in the early years of the thirteenth century. For the Becket dispute he gives a year-by-year narrative and transcribes many letters.

Two things should be kept in mind. He begins each year at Christmas, as the Fordham editor notes, so that the murder of 29 December 1170 stands under 1171. And he writes after [[person:thomas-becket]] had become a saint: the archbishop is "the blessed Thomas" throughout.
