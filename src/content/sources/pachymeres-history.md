Pachymeres was a deacon of the church of [[place:constantinople]] who held the offices of protekdikos and dikaiophylax, and he lived through what he describes. Six of his thirteen books cover the reign of [[person:michael-viii-palaiologos]] and seven that of his son Andronikos II; the narrative runs to 1308.

He belonged to the clergy on whom the union was pressed, and he records the emperor's arguments, the objections of the bishops and the punishments of those who refused in more detail than any Latin source. He is no friend of the union, but he is also critical of the zealots who took revenge after 1282. He is cited here from the Latin translation printed beneath the Greek in the Bonn edition.
