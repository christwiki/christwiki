Eusebius compiled his chronological tables early in the fourth century; the Greek is lost. Jerome translated them into Latin about 380 and added his own continuation for the years 326 to 378. The work places each event against the regnal year of the reigning emperor, from which the year of the Christian era can be read off.

Its value differs by period. For the first three centuries it passes on what Eusebius had gathered from older writers, such as the notice, credited to Julius Africanus, of a "holy man" Abgar who reigned at Edessa. For the middle of the fourth century Jerome is writing of his own lifetime. He puts the death of the monk [[person:antony-the-great]] in the nineteenth year of Constantius, AD 356. The English translation linked here misprints Antony's age in that entry as 150; the Latin has CV, 105, which agrees with [[source:athanasius-life-of-antony]].
