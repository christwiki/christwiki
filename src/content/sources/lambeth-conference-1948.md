The Lambeth Conference is the meeting of the bishops of the Anglican Communion, called by the Archbishop of Canterbury about once in ten years. Its resolutions express the mind of the bishops present. They have no force of law in any member church until that church adopts them.

The conference of 1948 answered two questions that had arisen since the bishops last met in 1930. Resolutions 113 to 115 reply to the Chinese church's question whether a deaconess might be ordained priest, raised by [[event:florence-li-tim-oi]]. Resolutions 52 to 54 deal with the new [[event:church-of-south-india]] and record that the bishops were divided about the standing of its ministers.
