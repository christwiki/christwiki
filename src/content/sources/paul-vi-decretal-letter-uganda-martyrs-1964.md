The decretal letter is the formal act of canonization, dated the day of the ceremony in St Peter's and printed in the official gazette of the Holy See the following year. It tells how the White Fathers came to Buganda in 1879, describes the persecution under [[person:mwanga-ii]], and says that the whole of it fell between 1885 and 1887, that the number of those killed was not exactly recorded, and that some Anglicans were killed together with the Catholics (p. 695). It divides the twenty-two into thirteen burned at [[place:namugongo]] on 3 June 1886 and nine killed in other places, gives a few lines on each, and ends the list with Jean-Marie Muzeyi, beheaded on 27 January 1887 (pp. 696–699). It then recounts the cause from the first inquiry at Rubaga in 1887 to the beatification of 1920 and the canonization.

It was written in Rome nearly eighty years after the events and rests on the inquiries made for the cause. It is cited in [[event:uganda-martyrs]] for the dates and the numbers as the Catholic Church's act of canonization states them.
