The bull is addressed to Ferdinand and Isabella and dated at St Peter's in [[place:rome]] on 4 May 1493, in the first year of [[person:alexander-vi]]. It recounts the voyage of [[person:christopher-columbus]] as the sovereigns' envoys had reported it, praises their purpose of bringing the newly found peoples to the faith, and then, "out of the fullness of our apostolic power", gives, grants and assigns the lands to them and their heirs, provided no Christian prince already possessed them. It orders the sovereigns to send learned and God-fearing men to instruct the inhabitants and forbids anyone else to go there without their licence.

The bull is addressed to the Spanish sovereigns alone. The peoples whose lands it assigns appear in it only as the sovereigns' envoys had described them: peaceful, unclothed, believing in one creator in heaven and apt to receive the faith. See [[event:christianity-reaches-the-americas]].
