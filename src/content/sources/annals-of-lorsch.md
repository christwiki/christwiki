These annals take their name from the monastery of Lorsch and are a different work from the court annals now called the [[source:royal-frankish-annals]]. They end with the year 803. Their entries for the years around 800 are full and are worded differently from those of the court annals; that they stop so soon afterwards suggests a writer close to the events.

Their importance for the coronation of 800 is that they give a reason and a procedure. The decision, they say, was taken by the pope, the bishops assembled in council and the rest of the Christian people, on the grounds that there was no emperor among the Greeks and that Charles already held Rome and the other old imperial seats; and Charles accepted because he would not refuse their request.
