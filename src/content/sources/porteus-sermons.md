Porteus, then bishop of Chester, preached the seventeenth sermon in February 1783 before the Incorporated Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in Foreign Parts. Its printed title is "The civilization, improvement, and conversion of the Negro Slaves in the British West-India islands recommended". He speaks of the Society's "trust-estates" in Barbados, left to it by General Codrington, and of "the slaves employed on their plantations", and he urges the Society to make them a model of Christian instruction.

The sermon is cited as the Society's own preacher describing, before the Society, the fact that it held enslaved people. [[person:thomas-clarkson]] counts Porteus, from this sermon onward, among the friends of abolition.
