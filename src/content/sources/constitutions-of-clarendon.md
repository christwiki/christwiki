The document calls itself a record of part of the "customs and liberties and dignities" of the king's predecessors, made in the tenth year of [[person:henry-ii-of-england]] in the presence of the bishops and barons, and it names [[person:thomas-becket]] first among the bishops who granted them. It is dated at Clarendon on the fourth day before the feast of the Purification, which falls on 2 February.

It is the royal side's formulation. It presents as old custom what the archbishop treated as an attack on the liberty of the church, and its statement that the bishops promised to observe the articles should be read beside the chroniclers' accounts of how that promise was obtained and withdrawn.
