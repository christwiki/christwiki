Alcuin wrote to kings, bishops, former pupils and friends in England and on the continent. To [[person:charlemagne]] he writes as a teacher to a patron, under the court nickname Flaccus and addressing the king as David.

Two letters are used in this wiki. In one, written from Tours at the end of 796 or early in 797, he describes what he teaches there, Scripture, the liberal arts, grammar and astronomy, and asks leave to send pupils to York for books he lacks (no. 78). In another, of the spring of 800, he tells the king's sister and daughter that he would have sent them more of his commentary on John had the king's command not kept him busy correcting the text of the Old and New Testament (no. 136).
