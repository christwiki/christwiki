summary: The history of the Plymouth colony by William Bradford, who sailed in 1620 and was chosen its governor in 1621. It runs from the origins of the congregation in England to 1647 and is cited here for the colony's first years.

Bradford wrote the first book, in ten chapters, on the congregation's beginnings in England, its years in Holland and the voyage; the second book goes on year by year from 1620; the introduction to this edition says that the annals extend to 1647. He was not writing a diary, and he says himself when he wrote. In a note headed "A late observation", which the editor of 1898 says stands on the reverse pages of the manuscript, he says that he began "these scribled writings" about the year 1630 and pieced them up "at times of leasure afterward"; the note ends with the date 1646 (pp. 9–11). The first book was thus begun about ten years after the landing. In the annals he remarks of the peace with Massasoit that it "hath now continued this 24 years" (p. 115) and twice names the year 1646 as the time of writing (pp. 123, 191), so that the early annals were written about twenty-five years after the events they tell. The list of the Mayflower's passengers printed as an appendix says that he has seen thirty years completed since the beginning and speaks of "this presente year, 1650" (pp. 534, 539). He copies in many letters and documents, among them the agreement signed at Cape Cod and letters from [[person:john-robinson]].

He was a participant and a leader, and he tells the story as the working of God's providence for his people. For the native people he is a witness from the other side: he records what the English did and understood, including the corn they took, the peace they made and the killings of 1623, together with Robinson's rebuke. The dates given above for the writing rest on these remarks of Bradford's own and are approximate. The manuscript was returned from London to Massachusetts in 1897, as the papers printed at the front of this edition relate.
