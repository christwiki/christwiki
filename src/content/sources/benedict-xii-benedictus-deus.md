summary: A constitution of 1336 in which Benedict XII defined that the souls of the blessed see the divine essence directly, face to face, before the general judgment. It states the Latin doctrine of the vision of God in the decade of the hesychast dispute.
---

The constitution defines that the souls of the just, once purified, are with Christ in heaven before they take up their bodies again and before the general judgment, and that they see the divine essence "face to face", with no creature standing between as the object of sight; the essence shows itself to them plainly and openly.

The text does not mention the Greek controversy, which began a few years later. It is cited in [[event:hesychast-controversy]] as a formal statement of what the Western church taught about the vision of God at the time when the Eastern councils were teaching that God is seen in his energies and not in his essence.
