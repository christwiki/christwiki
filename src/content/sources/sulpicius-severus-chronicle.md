Sulpicius Severus was a Christian writer of Aquitaine, the author also of a *Life* of Martin of Tours. His *Chronicle* retells the history of the Old Testament and carries the story on through the persecutions to his own time. He reckons his chronology down to the consulship of Stilicho (2.9, 2.27).

For the councils of 359 he writes about forty years after the events and from Gaul. He names an informant, his bishop Gavidius, for one detail (2.41), and his interest is in the Gallic and British bishops and in [[person:hilary-of-poitiers]], whom he credits with freeing Gaul from heresy (2.45). He is a firm partisan of the Nicene side and calls its opponents Arians throughout. His account of what happened at Ariminum after the envoys returned is fuller than that of Athanasius, whose main narrative was written before the council had ended.
