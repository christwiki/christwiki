The act extends the [[source:act-against-papal-bulls-1571]] to those who, it says, work "by other means than by bulls". Anyone who seeks to withdraw the queen's subjects from their natural obedience, or to withdraw them "for that intent" from the established religion to the Roman, is to be judged a traitor, and so is anyone who is willingly so reconciled.

It also sets money penalties. Saying mass costs two hundred marks and a year in prison; hearing it, one hundred marks and a year. Everyone over sixteen who stays away from church forfeits twenty pounds for every month of absence. The act was passed in the session of January to March 1581.
