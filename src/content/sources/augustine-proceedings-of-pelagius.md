When the record of the Palestinian synod reached Africa, Augustine went through it point by point to show that the fourteen bishops had cleared Pelagius only because he disowned the propositions charged against him, and that the propositions themselves stood condemned. In doing so he copies out the charges, Pelagius's replies and the synod's verdict.

The minutes do not survive elsewhere, so this treatise is the main source for the synod. Augustine quotes documents, but he quotes them to make a case, and his reading of Pelagius's answers as evasions is his own. The closing chapters (46–62) are his first-hand account of when he first heard of Pelagius, of Pelagius's brief stay in Africa and of the trial of Caelestius at Carthage.
