The author speaks of Methodius as "our teacher" and writes as one of the community he left behind. The Life is short and partisan. It tells of the mission from [[place:constantinople]], the approval of the Slavonic books at [[place:rome]], Methodius's trial and imprisonment by the Frankish bishops, his vindication, his translation of the Scriptures and his death on 6 April in the third indiction, AD 885. The Slavonic text gives the year from creation as 6393; the Latin translation linked here prints it, by a slip, as the 1393rd.

Chapter 8 gives the text of a letter of [[person:hadrian-ii]] permitting the liturgy in Slavonic. No Latin text of the letter survives; Ginzel, who printed the Latin version cited here, marked it as spurious, and its wording cannot be checked against a Roman copy. Chapter 6 credits to Pope Nicholas what [[source:life-of-constantine-the-philosopher]] credits to Hadrian.
