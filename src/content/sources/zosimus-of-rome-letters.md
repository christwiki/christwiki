Both letters are addressed to [[person:aurelius-of-carthage]] and all the bishops of Africa and are preserved in the collection of imperial and papal letters known as the *Collectio Avellana*. The text is damaged in places, and the editor marks several passages as corrupt.

The first, *Magnum pondus* (no. 45), reports the hearing of Caelestius in the basilica of St Clement at Rome. Its date line keeps the consuls of 417, Honorius for the eleventh time and Constantius, but the day has dropped out. The second, *Postquam a nobis* (no. 46), reports the public reading of a letter and statement of faith sent by [[person:pelagius]] and is dated the eleventh day before the Kalends of October, 21 September, with no year; the editor assigns it to 417. The collection heads the first letter as written "in defence of Caelestius against the African bishops"; that heading is the compiler's and not part of the letter.

These are the words of the bishop who judged the case, written while it was open. [[person:augustine]]'s account of the same hearing, in [[source:augustine-grace-of-christ-and-original-sin]], was written the next year, after Zosimus had condemned both men.
