Austen Henry Layard found the obelisk at Nimrud, ancient [[place:kalhu]], in 1846. It is just under two metres high and nearly undamaged. It was set up in a courtyard in the centre of the city about 825 BC, near the end of the reign of [[person:shalmaneser-iii]]. Its long inscription summarises his campaigns year by year down to his thirty-first year.

Five bands of relief run round all four sides. Each shows the tribute of one ruler or land and has a one-line caption. The second caption says that the king received the payment of *Iaua* of *Bit-Humri*, literally "Jehu son of Omri": silver, gold, a gold bowl, a gold tureen, gold vessels, gold pails, tin, staffs and spears. In the panel beneath it a figure bows to the ground before Shalmaneser. Assyrian scribes called Israel the house of Omri, after the founder of the dynasty that [[person:jehu]] had just destroyed; the phrase marks him as ruler of that land, not as Omri's descendant.

The caption gives no date. Other copies of the annals place the payment in the king's eighteenth year, 841 BC; see [[event:jehu-pays-tribute]]. The books of Kings do not mention the tribute.
