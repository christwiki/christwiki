The piece has no heading of its own in the manuscript. Wright's introduction says that it stands in the Vossianus manuscript in the middle of Julian's Letter to Themistius, that Petavius identified it and published it separately, and that it was probably written while Julian was at [[place:antioch]] on his way to Persia. Wright notes that the beginning and the conclusion are lost.

It is Julian's own statement of what he wanted the priests of the old religion to be. Near the end he says that the Galilaeans, as he calls the Christians, turned to works of charity when the priests neglected the poor. The same programme is set out in the letter to Arsacius among his [[source:julian-letters|Letters]], whose authenticity is disputed.
