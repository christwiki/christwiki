The "Little Beginning" opens in the first person plural: the Cistercians who first founded the church write so that those who come after may know how the monastery and its way of life began. It is a short narrative in eighteen chapters built around official documents quoted in full: letters of the papal legate Hugh of Lyon, of Pope Urban II and of local bishops, and a privilege of Pope Paschal II.

It is the Order's account of itself, written to show that the departure from Molesme was lawful and that the customs of [[place:citeaux]] were a return to the Rule of Benedict. It presents itself as the work of the founders. Constance H. Berman, *The Cistercian Evolution* (2000), argued that it and the Charter of Charity took the form in which they survive only in the 1160s and 1170s; the dating remains debated.
