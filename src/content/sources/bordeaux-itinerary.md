The author is unknown. The text is a road-book: it lists the halts and changes of horses from Bordeaux across northern Italy, the Balkans and Asia Minor to Palestine, and back by way of Rome and Milan. It dates itself by the consuls of the year: the journey from Chalcedon and back to Constantinople was made, it says, in the consulship of Dalmatius and Zenophilus, which is 333.

Only in Palestine does the list broaden into description. At Jerusalem the pilgrim notes the pools, the Temple mount, Sion, the hill of Golgotha, the tomb a stone's throw from it, and the basilica that had been built there by order of [[person:constantine]]. This is an eyewitness two years before the dedication recorded in [[event:church-of-the-holy-sepulchre]]. The pilgrim reports what was shown and what local guides said about each place, and does not mention [[person:helena]] or a relic of the cross.
