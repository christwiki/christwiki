The article is signed from Lima. It defines theology as critical reflection on the church's presence and action in the world, a reflection that comes after commitment and not before it (pp. 244–245). It argues that "liberation" names the hopes of poor and dependent peoples better than "development" does (p. 247), and distinguishes three levels of the word: the political liberation of oppressed peoples and classes, the liberation of humanity in the course of history, and liberation from sin (p. 248). It cites the Medellín documents repeatedly.

It is cited here as a primary statement of the author's case, in a form that can be read freely. His book *Teología de la liberación* (1971) is in copyright and no open edition was found, so this wiki does not cite it. See [[event:medellin-and-liberation-theology]].
