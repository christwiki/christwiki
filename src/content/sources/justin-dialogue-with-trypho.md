The *Dialogue* is the longest of Justin's surviving works. Its setting is the aftermath of a war: Trypho introduces himself as a Hebrew who has escaped from "the war lately carried on" (1), which is taken to be the Bar Kokhba revolt. The book was written later than the *First Apology*, to which it refers. Eusebius says the debate took place at Ephesus (*Church History* 4.18).

In chapters 2–8 Justin tells how he studied with a Stoic, a Peripatetic, a Pythagorean and a Platonist, and how an old man he met near the sea directed him to the prophets. The story is told by Justin himself, in a work composed to persuade, and its details cannot be checked. The rest of the book argues from the Greek translation of the Jewish Scriptures and is a main witness to how Christians and Jews disputed over those texts in the middle of the second century.
