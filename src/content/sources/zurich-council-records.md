Egli's collection prints or summarises, under running numbers, documents from the archives of [[place:zurich]]: the council's mandates, the minutes of hearings, and sentences. For the Reformation in the city they are the official record, made at the time by the authority that took the decisions. Where a decree is lost, Egli fills the gap with extracts from Zurich chroniclers such as Heinrich Bullinger and Gerold Edlibach, and says so.

They show what the council ordered and what witnesses told it. They are the record of one side in the conflict with the Anabaptists: the statements of the accused survive only as the court's clerks wrote them down. Many pieces are given in summary, partly in the editor's words, and are paraphrased here.
