[[person:athanasius-the-athonite]] wrote the typikon as superior of the Lavra, after the murder of his patron [[person:nikephoros-ii-phokas]] in 969. Sections 2–8 are a history of the foundation in the first person; sections 12 and 18 quote the emperor's chrysobull, which does not otherwise survive; the rest regulates the choice of the superior, the admission of monks, property and the place of solitaries beside the common life.

It is the founder's account and passes over what did not suit him. The translators note that it is silent about the attempt of the other Athonite monks to have him removed, which is the subject of [[source:tzimiskes-typikon]].
