The first tracts, dated September 1833, were leaflets of a few pages. [[person:john-henry-newman]] says that he began them on his own initiative and was their editor and chief author. Tract 1, his work, asks the clergy on what they will rest their authority if the state deserts the church, and answers: on their apostolical descent.

Tract 90, also by Newman and dated on the feast of the Conversion of St Paul (25 January) 1841, examines the Thirty-nine Articles one group at a time and argues that they can be signed by those who hold the faith of the early church. It was censured by the heads of the Oxford colleges, and the series ended with it. The tracts are the movement's own statement of what it taught. See [[event:oxford-movement]].
