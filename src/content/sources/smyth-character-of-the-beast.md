The book is cast as a written exchange with Richard Clifton, a minister among the English Separatists, whose arguments Smyth prints and answers in turn. Its title page states the two points at issue: that infants are not to be baptized, and that those converted from "Antichristian" churches are to be received into the true church by baptism. The preface explains why Smyth and his people had moved from Puritanism to Separatism and then to "true Christian baptism".

It is a primary source for what Smyth taught and for the fact that the group had baptized itself, which he defends as "that which is done by us" without describing the occasion. The account of who baptized whom comes from an opponent, [[person:john-robinson]], in [[source:robinson-of-religious-communion]]. It is cited in [[event:first-baptists]].
