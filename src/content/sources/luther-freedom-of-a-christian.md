[[person:martin-luther]] published the treatise with an open letter to Pope [[person:leo-x]]. The letter addresses Leo with respect, as a lamb and a Daniel among lions, and attacks the Roman court in the harshest terms. The treatise itself is not a polemic. It starts from two statements that Luther says are both Paul's: a Christian is a free lord of all, subject to none, and a Christian is a dutiful servant of all, subject to every one. The first he explains by faith, which alone justifies; the second by love, which works freely for the neighbour.

It is the calmest of the three books of 1520 and the plainest statement of what Luther meant by justification by faith at that date.
