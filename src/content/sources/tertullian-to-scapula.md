The letter is short, five chapters, and is addressed to a governor then in office in Africa. [[person:tertullian]] tells him that Christians do not fear him and wish him no harm, lists governors who came to a bad end after persecuting them, and points to recent portents at [[place:carthage]] and Utica (3).

Its second chapter is often quoted: it is "a fundamental human right, a privilege of nature, that every man should worship according to his own convictions", and "it is assuredly no part of religion to compel religion". The plea is made by a member of a persecuted minority to the official persecuting it. The date is fixed approximately by the darkening of the sun at Utica that the letter mentions, which is identified with a solar eclipse of the year 212.
