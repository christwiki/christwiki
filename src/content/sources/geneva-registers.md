Geneva was governed by a Little Council, a Council of Two Hundred and a General Council of the citizens, and the secretary of the Little Council kept a register of what was decided. The ministers of the city, the Company of Pastors, kept a register of their own.

These are official records written on the day, and they fix dates that the narratives leave vague. They are brief, and they give decisions more often than reasons. Kidd prints selected entries in their sixteenth-century French; they are paraphrased here.
