Lawrence of Březová reports that the Praguers asked for a public hearing before the king's army in which their masters could set out the four articles in Czech, German, Hungarian and Latin; when none was granted, the masters wrote the articles down with their proofs and sent them to the camp. The text he copies is issued in the name of the burgomasters, councillors, aldermen and whole community of [[place:prague]].

Each article is followed by its authorities, from the Gospels and Paul to Gregory, Augustine, Jerome and the canon law. The second, on the chalice, has the longest list. The closing paragraph protests that the city seeks only to please Christ and keep his law, that anyone who attacks it for these articles will be resisted as a tyrant, and that it is ready to be corrected from Scripture. The copy in Höfler's edition ends with the year 1421, though the chronicle places the sending in July 1420. See [[event:hussite-movement]].
