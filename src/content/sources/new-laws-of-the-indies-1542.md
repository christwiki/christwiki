The laws were drawn up after the debates at court that [[person:bartolome-de-las-casas]] describes at the end of his [[source:las-casas-short-account]]. Much of the text concerns the Council of the Indies and the royal courts. Among the clauses on the Indians are these: no Indian may be made a slave by war, rebellion, ransom or any other means; Indians held as slaves without proof of title are to be freed; officials and religious houses lose the Indians allotted to them; and no new allotment (*encomienda*) may be granted, the existing ones passing to the Crown on the holder's death.

It is the law as issued. It shows what the Crown commanded, and by forbidding certain things it shows that they were being done. It does not show how far the commands were obeyed.
