Jeremias installed Job at Moscow in January 1589. This charter is the confirmation given the next year by a council at Constantinople. In it Jeremias says that the tsar received him and asked for the patriarchal dignity for the archbishop of Moscow; that he installed Job and gave him a patriarchal charter; and that on his return the other patriarchs approved. The council therefore declares that Job is to be honoured and named with the patriarchs, that his place in the prayers is after the patriarch of Jerusalem, that he is to hold the see of Constantinople as his head, as the other patriarchs do, and that after him the council of Moscow may install its patriarchs.

According to the Russian record the charter was signed by Jeremias, Joachim of Antioch and Sophronius of Jerusalem and by forty-two metropolitans, nineteen archbishops and twenty bishops. No patriarch of Alexandria is among them: the letter that the bearer sent ahead to the tsar says that the council was called also because there was no longer a patriarch of Alexandria. The text used here is the translation made for the tsar's officials, so its wording is theirs.

The charter is dated May 7098, that is, 1590. It was brought to Russia by Dionysius, metropolitan of Tarnovo, whom the tsar received on 20 June, and the year of that reception is 1591, not 1590. Muravyov's digest prints "May 1590" for the arrival of Dionysius at Smolensk, but the same sentence puts it two years after the patriarch's departure, and the digest has Jeremias leave Moscow in May 1589. A charter dated at Constantinople in May 1590 can hardly have been at the Russian frontier in that same month; Dionysius also told Job, as the digest reports him, that once it was written he was sent to the voivode of the Voloshskaya land so that the bishops there could sign it. The digest then has the tsar order his dismissal in the middle of February and gives a letter of Boris Godunov to one of his companions under March 1592. Makary (Bulgakov), in his *History of the Russian Church* (1857–1883), likewise dates the arrival to May 1591 and the reception to 20 June; he also recounts a further council at Constantinople on 12 February 1593, with Meletius of Alexandria present, which again placed Moscow fifth. The act of 1593 has no record in this wiki.
