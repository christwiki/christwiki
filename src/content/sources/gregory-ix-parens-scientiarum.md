The bull opens with the words "Paris, the mother of the sciences", from which it is named. It was issued, as it says, to settle a dissension that had "greatly disturbed the university". It binds the chancellor of [[place:paris]] by oath in granting the licence to teach, forbids him to take money for it, and confirms the corporation's rights over lectures, dress, lodgings and discipline.

Munro, its translator, notes that it has been called the Magna Carta of the University of Paris, and that the students had dispersed and sworn not to return.
