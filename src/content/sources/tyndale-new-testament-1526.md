The book is a small octavo that names neither translator, printer nor place. The Stuttgart library, which identified its copy in the 1990s, attributes the printing to Peter Schöffer the younger at [[place:worms]] on the evidence of the woodcut border of the title page. That page, which survives only in this copy, reads in modern spelling: "The New Testament as it was written and caused to be written by them which heard it."

At the end [[person:william-tyndale]] added a short address to the reader, asking the learned to mend what he had done amiss and promising a revision. The library's page says that the British Library bought its copy, which lacks the title page, in 1994, and that a very defective third copy is at St Paul's Cathedral in London. The book is cited here as an object and for that address.
