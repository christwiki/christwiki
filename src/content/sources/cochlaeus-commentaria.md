Cochlaeus spent his life writing against [[person:martin-luther]]. In his history, printed near [[place:mainz]] in 1549, he relates that while he was living at Cologne he learned from printers over wine that two Englishmen were having three thousand copies of an English New Testament printed there, that he told Hermann Rinck, a patrician of the city, who had the senate forbid the printers to continue, and that the two fled up the Rhine to [[place:worms]] with the printed sheets.

The Cologne episode is told here by the man who caused the interruption. He wrote more than twenty years later, calls the translators apostates and their book Luther's Testament, and never saw them. Arber, who reprints the passage, notes these limits.
