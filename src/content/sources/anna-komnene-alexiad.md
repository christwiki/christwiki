Anna Komnene was a daughter of [[person:alexios-i-komnenos]]. In her preface she names herself the daughter of Alexios and Irene, "born and bred in the purple". A. C. Krey notes that she was barely fourteen when the crusading armies reached [[place:constantinople]] and began her history some forty years later; its hero is her father.

Her account shows how the expedition looked from the capital it passed through: as a mass movement of "Franks" whose leaders had to be watched, supplied and bound by oath. In book 10 she traces it to the preaching of Peter the Hermit. A. C. Krey, in 1921, judged her chronology uncertain and her facts often inexact, and valued the work as a statement of the Byzantine attitude to the Latins.
