Johannes Lepsius, a German Protestant pastor who had gone to Constantinople in the summer of 1915, says in his foreword that after the war he asked the German Foreign Office for access to its files on the Armenian question and was given it. The volume prints several hundred documents: reports from the ambassadors to the Chancellor in Berlin, telegrams from consuls at Aleppo, Erzurum, Trebizond, Adana and Mosul, and notes of conversations with Ottoman ministers and with the Armenian Patriarch.

Germany was the ally of the Ottoman Empire, and these are the working papers of its diplomats. The selection is the editor's, as he says in the foreword, where he takes sole responsibility for it, and the long introduction is his own account. The title names the years 1914–1918, but the first documents printed are of 1913, and the appendix ends with a report dated February 1919.

The texts as printed are not always the texts in the files. Wolfgang Gust went back to the originals in the Political Archive of the German Foreign Office and edited them anew: *Der Völkermord an den Armeniern 1915/16: Dokumente aus dem Politischen Archiv des deutschen Auswärtigen Amts* (2005), in English as *The Armenian Genocide: Evidence from the German Foreign Office Archives, 1915–1916* (2013). The review in *Choice* that the publisher of the English edition quotes says that in Lepsius's compilation some text was "deleted or manipulated by the German Foreign Office", that Gust and his wife restored some of the original text, and that they added many documents Lepsius does not have. A passage taken from Lepsius should therefore be compared with the original where Gust prints it; that has not been done for the passages this wiki cites.
