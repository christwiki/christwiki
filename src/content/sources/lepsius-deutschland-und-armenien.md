Johannes Lepsius, a German Protestant pastor who had gone to Constantinople in the summer of 1915, says in his foreword that after the war he asked the German Foreign Office for access to its files on the Armenian question and was given it. The volume prints several hundred documents: reports from the ambassadors to the Chancellor in Berlin, telegrams from consuls at Aleppo, Erzurum, Trebizond, Adana and Mosul, and notes of conversations with Ottoman ministers and with the Armenian Patriarch.

Germany was the ally of the Ottoman Empire, and these are the working papers of its diplomats. The selection is the editor's, and the long introduction is his own account.
