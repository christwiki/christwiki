The manifesto was drawn up by [[person:wu-yaozong]] and others after their meetings with the premier, Zhou Enlai, in May 1950, and was then circulated for signature in the churches. It is short. A section headed "The Task in General" commits Christians to support the government's Common Programme and to oppose imperialism, feudalism and bureaucratic capitalism under the government's leadership. Its "fundamental aims" are that the churches should recognise how imperialism had used Christianity, purge that influence, and watch against American imperialism in particular. The Communist Party's account adds that it set the churches the task of becoming self-governing, self-supporting and self-propagating in the shortest possible time.

It is the signers' own statement of their case. How freely the signatures that followed were given is disputed; the collection that prints this translation was compiled by former missionaries, and its notes say that some at first refused to sign. See [[event:three-self-movement]].
