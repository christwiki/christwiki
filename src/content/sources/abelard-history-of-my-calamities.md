The Historia calamitatum is an autobiography in the form of a letter. It is [[person:peter-abelard]]'s own account of his life and of the masters and students of northern France in the first decades of the twelfth century.

It is also a self-defence. Abelard presents his teachers and rivals as moved by envy and himself as the victim of his own fame, and what he says of William of Champeaux, Anselm of Laon and the council of Soissons is his side of each quarrel.
