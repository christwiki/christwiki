---

The patent is issued in the king's name at the Bohemian diet in Prague Castle. It calls the Protestant estates those who receive communion in both kinds, or Utraquists, and refers to the Bohemian Confession they had presented in 1575. It lets them practise their religion freely "in any place", gives them the lower consistory of Prague and the university, allows them to appoint Defenders, and permits the lords, knights and royal towns to build further churches. It is said to hold until a general religious settlement is reached in the Empire.

The same page prints the agreement made on the same occasion between the Catholic and Protestant estates. The document matters for [[event:thirty-years-war]] because the nobles who threw the king's regents from the window in 1618 said that they were defending the rights it gave them. The translation was made from the German text printed by Anton Gindely in 1858.
