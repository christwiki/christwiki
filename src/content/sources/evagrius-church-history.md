Evagrius was born at Epiphania in Syria about 536 and worked at Antioch as an advocate (*scholasticus*) and adviser to the patriarch Gregory. His history begins with the Nestorian controversy and ends in the twelfth year of the emperor Maurice. For Chalcedon he writes some 140 years after the event, but he had the acts of the council in front of him and gives a long summary of them, and he quotes official documents in full, among them the Henotikon of the emperor Zeno.

He accepts Chalcedon and writes from that side. He is nevertheless careful to report the other: he often cites Zachariah the Rhetor, an opponent of the council, and at one point he argues that the two formulas "in two natures" and "from two natures" imply each other. His accounts of events in Alexandria and Antioch should be read beside the anti-Chalcedonian sources.
