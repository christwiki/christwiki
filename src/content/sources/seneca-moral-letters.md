Lucius Annaeus Seneca was a senator, a Stoic philosopher and an adviser of the emperor Nero. He wrote the letters to Lucilius in his last years, and died in AD 65 when Nero ordered his death (Tacitus, *Annals* 15.60–64).

The letters matter for this history in one small point. [[person:gallio]], the proconsul of Achaia before whom Acts brings [[person:paul]], was Seneca's brother (Tacitus, *Annals* 15.73). In Letter 104 Seneca recalls that Gallio, when he began to run a fever in Achaia, at once took ship, saying that the sickness belonged to the place and not to his body. The remark comes from Gallio's own family. It places Gallio in Achaia, where Acts and the [[source:gallio-inscription]] make him proconsul, and it suggests that he left the province, at least for a time, on grounds of health.
