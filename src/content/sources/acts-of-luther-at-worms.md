The *Acta* narrate [[person:martin-luther]]'s journey to [[place:worms]], the hearings of 17 and 18 April 1521, and the negotiations that followed. They were put together on the Lutheran side and printed at the time. The speech of 18 April is told in the first person and, in the judgement of the editor of the imperial diet records, goes back to Luther himself.

Wrede's edition compares the manuscripts and early prints and records where they differ. Its apparatus is the evidence for the wording of the close of the speech: the edited text ends with a prayer for God's help, and the words "I cannot do otherwise, here I stand" are found only in two prints from a single [[place:wittenberg]] press. A report preserved among the papers of the papal nuncio Aleander is printed in the same volume.
