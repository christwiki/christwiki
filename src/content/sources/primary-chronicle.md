The chronicle was put together by monks at [[place:kyiv]]; one version carries a note by Sylvester, prior of St Michael's, dated 1116, and the oldest surviving copy, the Laurentian, was written in 1377. For the tenth century its compilers worked from materials of very uneven worth, and the account of [[person:vladimir-the-great]] combines at least two stories: a testing of the faiths that ends with the report of his envoys from [[place:constantinople]], and a baptism at [[place:chersonesus]] tied to his marriage. The chronicler himself says that others placed the baptism at Kyiv or at Vasil'ev.

Its years are counted from the creation of the world. They are not always reliable for this period and have to be checked against Byzantine and Arabic writers such as [[source:leo-the-deacon-history]] and [[source:yahya-of-antioch-history]].
