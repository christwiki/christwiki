summary: Medieval Lives of the popes who sat at Avignon, from Clement V to Clement VII, collected by Étienne Baluze in 1693. The First Life of Clement VII tells of his election at Fondi, his move to Avignon and his death, from the side of his own obedience.

Baluze gathered from manuscripts the Lives of the popes "who sat in Gaul from 1305 to 1394", often more than one for a pope, and printed them in 1693; Mollat's edition of 1914 re-collated them and notes the readings of each manuscript at the foot of the page.

The First Life of Clement VII (vol. 1, pp. 469–518) is printed without an author's name. It runs to the pope's death in September 1394 and so was finished after that. Its writer holds to the Avignon line: he calls [[person:urban-vi]] an intruder throughout and the election at Fondi canonical. He is cited here for places and days, and not for his judgment of the two elections. One manuscript of the Life gives the fifteenth of September for the pope's death where the others give the sixteenth; a separate account of the death printed in the same volume (p. 537), by a writer who says he saw the cardinals gather that evening, also has Wednesday the sixteenth. Used in [[event:western-schism]] and for [[person:clement-vii-of-avignon]].
