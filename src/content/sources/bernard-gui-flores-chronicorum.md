Muratori printed these Lives from a manuscript in the Ambrosian Library at Milan that contains Bernard Gui's *Flores chronicorum*, or catalogue of the Roman pontiffs. In his preface he says that Bernard was born about 1260, was a Dominican and from 1305 an inquisitor, became a bishop in 1323 and died in 1331; that the work is dedicated to Pope John XXII in a letter dated at Avignon in August 1320; and that its narrative was carried on to 1331. Those particulars are Muratori's.

For the last years of Boniface VIII Bernard is a contemporary, writing within about thirty years of the events. His Life of Boniface (pp. 670–672) is brief; it draws from the pope's fall the lesson that prelates should not lord it over clergy and people. It says that the pope was seized at Anagni on the vigil of the Nativity of Mary in September 1303 and died at Rome on the fifth day before the ides of October, the thirty-fifth day from his capture, and was buried in St Peter's on the day after (p. 672). Used for [[person:boniface-viii]] and in [[event:unam-sanctam]].
