The author, known as Cosmas Indicopleustes, "the sailor to India", had been a merchant before he became a monk. His translator, J. W. McCrindle, works out from the text that he was writing about the year 547. The purpose of the book is to defend a picture of the world drawn from Scripture against the round earth of the Greek philosophers, and most of it is argument of that kind.

In passing, Cosmas says what he saw and heard on his voyages. In the third book he lists the lands where the gospel has been preached and names a church with clergy on Taprobane, a church in Male "where the pepper grows", and a bishop at Calliana who is appointed from Persia. In the eleventh book he says that the church on Taprobane belongs to Persian Christians settled there. McCrindle identifies Taprobane with Sri Lanka, Male with the Malabar coast and Calliana with Kalyana near Bombay. These passages do not mention the apostle Thomas.
