The Latin text is headed as a second redaction, made after the amendments of members and experts by six theologians, A. Auer, R. Sigmond, P. Anciaux, M. Labourdette, J. Fuchs and P. de Locht, and is dated from Rome on 26 June. It has an introduction and two parts, on principles and on pastoral needs. The second chapter of the first part says that the morality of sexual acts between spouses takes its meaning from the ordering of their married life as a whole to responsible parenthood, and so does not depend on the direct fecundity of each particular act; it adds that a way of life selfishly opposed to having children is what the tradition condemned and will always condemn. The third chapter argues that to speak of regulating conception by "human and decent means" does not contradict the genuine sense of the tradition. The fourth says that the means are not left to arbitrary choice, and excludes abortion.

The document is a draft offered to the pope by a commission that had no authority to teach, and [[person:paul-vi]] did not adopt it: see [[source:paul-vi-humanae-vitae]] 6. It is cited here for what the commission's text says. For its publication in 1967 see [[source:christianity-today-catholic-dilemma-1967]] and [[event:humanae-vitae]].
