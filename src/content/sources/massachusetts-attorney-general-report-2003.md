Attorney General Thomas F. Reilly opened the investigation in January 2002 and published this report on 23 July 2003. His office obtained the archdiocese's files, issued subpoenas and used a grand jury. The report covers the years from 1940, with most attention to 1984–2002, when [[person:bernard-law]] was archbishop.

Its figures are counts of complaints and allegations found in the records, and the report says that the true number of victims is higher. It makes three findings: that there was no evidence of recent or continuing abuse, though it was too soon to say that abuse had stopped; that the evidence was not enough to charge the archdiocese or its senior managers under the state law then in force; and that the abuse was due to an institutional acceptance of abuse and a failure of leadership. It is the report of a prosecutor who brought no charges against the archdiocese, and it is written in strong terms.
