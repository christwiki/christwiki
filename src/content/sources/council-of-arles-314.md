The council's decisions survive in two forms. One is a letter to Silvester of Rome, headed by the names of thirty-three bishops, which reports that the accusers of [[person:caecilian]] were condemned and summarises a few rulings; it is preserved in the appendix to Optatus and in collections of Gallic canons. The other is a list of twenty-two canons with the names of those who subscribed, transmitted in early collections of church law. The manuscripts number canons 9 to 17 in two slightly different ways, so that the canon on rebaptism is 8 or 9 and the canon on those who handed over the Scriptures is 13 or 14.

These are the council's own words and the best evidence for what it decided. They do not include minutes of the hearing itself, which are lost. For the dispute that led to the council see [[event:donatist-schism]].
