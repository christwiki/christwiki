[[person:martin-luther]] provided each Testament and most of the books of his German Bible with a preface. Those written for the New Testament of 1522 are the most outspoken. The preface to the New Testament ranks John's Gospel, Paul's letters and 1 Peter above the rest, and the prefaces to Hebrews, James, Jude and Revelation explain why he sets these four apart from what he calls the chief books.

Luther revised the prefaces in later editions and dropped some of the sharpest judgements. The translation used here follows the final form of 1545 and prints in brackets the passages of 1522 that the final form lacks, so each citation says which edition it rests on. Its notes say that such passages stand only in the edition of 1522. For the close of the preface to the New Testament that is not exact: the Weimar edition's collation shows it in the separate New Testaments until 1537, as [[event:luther-bible]] sets out.
