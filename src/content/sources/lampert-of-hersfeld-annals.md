Lampert was a monk of the abbey of Hersfeld in Hesse. His annals grow from brief notes into a full narrative for the 1070s and end with the election of Rudolf as king in March 1077, where he says he will leave the rest to another writer. He was a contemporary of the events at [[place:canossa]], writing in a German monastery far from them.

He writes well and with a purpose: his sympathies are with the princes who opposed [[person:henry-iv-emperor]]. Oswald Holder-Egger, who edited the text in 1894, marks in his notes the places where Lampert's account of Canossa departs from the letter of [[person:gregory-vii]], which he judges Lampert had read, and treats several details as Lampert's own additions. The annals are cited here for what Lampert says, set beside Gregory's letter.
