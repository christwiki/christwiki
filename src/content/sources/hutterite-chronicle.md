Rudolf Wolkan's preface to the edition says that the chronicle was begun by Kaspar Braitmichel, whom he calls a head of the community, and that Braitmichel carried it to the year 1542; Hans Kräl and the community's scribe Hauprecht Zapff then continued his work. The surviving original, Wolkan says, is in Zapff's hand: in the initial I of a letter of Jakob Hutter included in the chronicle, Zapff marks himself as the scribe with the initials H. Z. and the year 1581. Zapff wrote as far as the year 1593, and four later hands carried the manuscript to its end, which the library record of the edition puts at 1665. Wolkan worked from a careful copy, not from the original, which could not be exposed to a sea voyage, and he modernised the spelling.

The chronicle opens with the history of the church and then tells how the brotherhood began in Switzerland: how [[person:conrad-grebel]], [[person:felix-manz]] and [[person:huldrych-zwingli]] discussed baptism, how the priest [[person:george-blaurock]] joined them, and how Blaurock asked Grebel to baptise him.

The year 1542 is where Braitmichel's part of the narrative stops; it is not a date of writing, and the preface gives none for him. The one date it gives for the writing is that of the surviving manuscript, which Zapff was writing in 1581, fifty-six years after the baptism of January 1525. The account was written far from [[place:zurich]], by a community that honoured these men as its founders, and it gives no day for the baptism. How the story reached the chronicler is not known. For the same weeks the [[source:zurich-council-records]] give the authorities' side.
