[[person:shalmaneser-iii]] had the record of his campaigns rewritten every few years and inscribed on monuments in his cities. Many versions survive. The two best known, [[source:kurkh-monolith]] and [[source:black-obelisk]], have their own entries; this entry covers the others cited in this wiki.

A statue of the king dedicated to the god Adad of Kurbail, found in the arsenal at [[place:kalhu]] and published in 1962, gives a full account of his eighteenth year. He crossed the Euphrates, defeated [[person:hazael]] of Damascus at Mount Saniru, shut him up in Damascus and cut down its orchards, marched to the coast, and there received the tribute of Tyre, Sidon and [[person:jehu]] of Israel (lines 21–30). A statue from Assur says that Hadad-ezer of Damascus died and that Hazael, "son of a nobody", took the throne (i 25–ii 6). That is the Assyrian way of saying he had no claim by birth, and it agrees with the account of his rise in [[bible:2 Kings 8:7-15]].

These are royal inscriptions written to glorify the king. Their figures for enemy dead grow from one version to the next, and they never record a defeat. For the order and dates of the campaigns they are contemporary and consistent with each other.
