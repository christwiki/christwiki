The council sat in eighteen sessions in the domed hall of the palace, with the emperor [[person:constantine-iv]] presiding. Its minutes survive in Greek and in an early Latin translation. They preserve the documents that were read out, chief among them the long letter of Pope [[person:agatho]] to the emperor, and the texts the council itself issued: the sentence of the thirteenth session against those who had taught one will in Christ, the definition of faith, an address to the emperor and a letter to the pope.

These are the council's own words and the first evidence for what it decided. Percival's version is a selection, with his summaries in square brackets and his own notes on the condemnation of Honorius; the notes are a later scholar's opinion and are not cited here.
