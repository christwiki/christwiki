The scroll is a parchment manuscript in Hebrew, found in Cave 11 at [[place:qumran]] and surviving only in fragments. The Israel Antiquities Authority, which holds it, assigns the copy to the Hasmonean period and classes it among the exegetical texts, as a collection of *pesher* commentaries on verses from the Hebrew Bible.

In the Authority's description the text focuses on [[person:melchizedek]] and portrays him as a heavenly saviour who will rescue the righteous at the final judgment; its writer seems to take the word *Elohim* in several biblical verses as referring to Melchizedek. The description notes the likeness to what the letter to the Hebrews says of Jesus. The scroll is evidence for Jewish interpretation of Melchizedek in the last centuries BC. It is not evidence for the meeting told in Genesis; see [[event:melchizedek-blesses-abram]].
