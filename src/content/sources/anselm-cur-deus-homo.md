The book is a dialogue. Boso, whom Anselm names as the one who pressed him hardest to write, puts the questions; Anselm answers. The preface says that it was begun in England "under great anguish of heart", that part of it was copied before it was finished, and that it was completed in exile. It also states the method: the argument is to proceed as if nothing were known of Christ and to show by reasoning alone that no one could be saved without him.

Its argument is usually called the satisfaction account of the atonement. It is cited here for what Anselm argues, in his own words.
