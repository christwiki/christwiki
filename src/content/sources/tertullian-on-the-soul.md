Tertullian wrote *De anima* at Carthage in the early third century. Most of it is argument, against the opinions of the philosophers, about what the soul is, where it comes from and where it goes at death.

It is cited in this history for one sentence. Arguing that only martyrs go straight to paradise, Tertullian asks how it is that "the most heroic martyr Perpetua", on the day of her passion, saw only her fellow martyrs there (55). He writes within a few years of her death and takes it for granted that his readers know the story told in the [[source:passion-of-perpetua-and-felicity]].
