[[person:william-booth]] published the book in 1890 and dedicated it to the memory of his wife, [[person:catherine-booth]]. The first part describes the homeless, the out-of-work, the criminal and their children; the second sets out a plan of relief and asks the public for the money to carry it out.

It is the founder's own statement of why the Salvation Army took up social work and how he meant it to proceed. Its figures are his estimates, drawn from the writers he names.
