Machyn was a London merchant-taylor whose trade, as the editor of the diary shows from its contents, was the furnishing of funerals. His book began as a list of the funerals he supplied and grew into a chronicle of what was seen in the streets of London: processions, sermons at Paul's Cross, proclamations, hangings and burnings. He writes in the spelling of the time and gives dates by the day of the month.

He is a witness from the time and place, with no argument to make about the burnings he records, and that is his value beside [[source:foxe-acts-and-monuments]]. Two cautions apply. He was not always present at what he reports, and his dates sometimes differ by a day or two from those in other records. And the manuscript was badly damaged in the fire of the Cotton library in 1731: where the edition prints words in square brackets, they are the editor's restorations from an earlier transcript or from the context, not surviving text.
