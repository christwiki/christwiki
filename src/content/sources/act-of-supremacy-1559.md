The act's full title is "An Act restoring to the Crown the ancient jurisdiction over the State ecclesiastical and spiritual, and abolishing all foreign power repugnant to the same". It repeals Mary I's [[source:second-act-of-repeal-1554]] and her [[source:heresy-acts-revival-1554]], revives a number of Henry VIII's statutes against Rome, and declares that no foreign prince or prelate shall exercise any spiritual jurisdiction in the realm.

Clergy, judges, officers of the crown and those taking degrees or holy orders are to swear that the queen is "the only supreme governor of this realm ... as well in all spiritual or ecclesiastical things or causes, as temporal". Those who refuse lose their offices. Anyone who defends the authority of a foreign prelate in speech or writing forfeits his goods for a first offence, falls under the penalties of praemunire for a second, and is guilty of high treason for a third. A proviso limits what the queen's commissioners may judge to be heresy to what Scripture, the first four general councils, or Parliament with the assent of the clergy in Convocation have so determined.

The act is paired with the [[source:act-of-uniformity-1559]], passed in the same session.
