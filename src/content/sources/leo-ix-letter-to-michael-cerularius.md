The letter answers Greek writings against unleavened bread and other Latin customs. It is addressed to [[person:michael-cerularius]] and to Leo, archbishop of Ohrid, and shows what Rome was prepared to say on the eve of the legation of 1054.

Its theme is that the Roman church is the head and mother of the churches and is judged by no one. It rehearses the heresies of past bishops of [[place:constantinople]], quotes the Donation of Constantine at length as a genuine grant (chapters 13–14), and demands that the Greeks stop calling the Latins "azymites" and closing their churches (chapter 20).
