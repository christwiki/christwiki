The bull is a short doctrinal statement, built from Scripture read allegorically: the one ark, the seamless tunic, the two swords of Luke 22. It names no king and no dispute. It was taken into the church's law among the *Extravagantes communes*, in book 1 under the title on majority and obedience.

The Latin text is dated at the Lateran on the fourteenth day before the kalends of December in the eighth year of Boniface, which is 18 November 1302. Henderson's translation prints this as "Nov. 14", a slip. Everything before the last sentence is argument and exposition; the last sentence alone uses the words of a formal definition, "we declare, announce and define". How much of the bull that sentence was meant to cover, and whether it concerns the pope's spiritual authority only, has been disputed ever since; see [[source:clement-v-meruit]], [[source:fifth-lateran-council-1516]] and [[source:manning-vatican-decrees]].
