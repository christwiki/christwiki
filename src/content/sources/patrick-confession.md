The *Confession* (Latin *Confessio*) is a defence and a thanksgiving in sixty-two short sections. Patrick wrote it in Ireland, in old age, to answer critics among the clergy of Britain who had questioned his fitness to be a bishop. It is addressed to fellow Christians, and it explains his mission by telling how he first came to Ireland as a slave.

It is not a biography. Patrick names his father, his grandfather and his home village, but no king, no pope, no Irish place except a wood "near the western sea", and no date. It does not mention Armagh or Tara; the stories that link him with those places are found in Lives written some two centuries later. The text survives in eight manuscripts, the oldest being the Book of Armagh, copied in Ireland about 807.
