Chauncy's title page announces a faithful account of "the things of a bad and dangerous tendency" in the religious appearance in the land. Part I takes them in turn, beginning with itinerant preaching, which he traces to George Whitefield, and going on to the swoonings, shriekings and convulsion-like tremblings that accompanied the preaching in many places. He does not deny that some were truly wrought upon by the Spirit of God; he holds that in the far greater number the terrors were a mechanical effect of frightening words and gestures. His footnotes cite and dispute Edwards's book on the revival.

It states the case against the awakening as a Boston minister put it at the time. See [[event:great-awakening]].
