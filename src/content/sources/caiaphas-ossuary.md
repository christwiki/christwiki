An ossuary is a stone chest in which the bones of the dead were gathered after the body had decayed. This one comes from a rock-cut family tomb found during building work in the south of Jerusalem in 1990. It is decorated, and it bears, inscribed twice, the name "Joseph son of Caiaphas" (*Yehosef bar Qafa*). Another ossuary from the same tomb carries the name *Qafa* alone. The Israel Museum showed the decorated one in 1992–1993 under the title "Ossuary of Caiaphas - The High Priest (18-36 CE)", and the page linked here is its notice of that exhibition, not a record of the object.

The identification rests on the name. Josephus calls the high priest of those years "Joseph, who was also called Caiaphas" (*Antiquities* 18.95). The inscription gives no title, however. The excavators proposed the identification and many scholars accept it; others have questioned it, pointing to the spelling of the name and the absence of a priestly title (see William Horbury, "The 'Caiaphas' Ossuaries and Joseph Caiaphas", *Palestine Exploration Quarterly* 126, 1994, pp. 32–48). The ossuary shows that a family of this name buried its dead at Jerusalem in the first century. It says nothing about the trial of Jesus.
