The declaration was made at the Vatican at the end of the two men's meetings. Its third paragraph confesses, in conformity with the first three ecumenical councils, that Jesus Christ is perfect God with respect to his divinity and perfect man with respect to his humanity, and that in him divinity and humanity are united in a real and perfect union without mingling, commixtion, confusion, alteration, division or separation. It does not use the phrases "one nature" or "two natures" and does not name the Council of Chalcedon.

The seventh paragraph says that theological differences have existed since the year 451, widened by non-theological factors, and that they cannot be ignored; the eighth sets up a joint commission to study them. The text speaks of a search for perfect communion and does not claim to have restored it. See [[event:christological-declarations]].
