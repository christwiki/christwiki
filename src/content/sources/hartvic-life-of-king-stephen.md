Hartvic says in his prologue that he wrote at the order of King Coloman, whose reign the editor gives as 1095 to 1114. He took an older Life, [[source:greater-legend-of-saint-stephen]], almost whole, wove in pieces of a second, and added matter of his own. The editor singles out as his chief addition the passage on the crown, of which the older Life says little.

In Hartvic's telling [[person:stephen-i-of-hungary]] sends Bishop Ascric to Rome; the pope, warned by a vision, gives him the crown he had prepared for the Polish duke, adds a cross to be carried before the king, and leaves the ordering of the Hungarian churches to Stephen. In the passage as printed Hartvic does not name the pope; the editor's preface identifies him as Sylvester II. Hartvic wrote a century after the event, and the one writer of Stephen's own time cited in this wiki, [[source:thietmar-chronicle|Thietmar]], credits the emperor and does not mention Rome.
