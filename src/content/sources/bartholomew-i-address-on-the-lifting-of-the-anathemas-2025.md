Bartholomew I spoke sixty years to the day after the declaration of Paul VI and Athenagoras I, to a congregation that included a representative of the apostolic nuncio in Turkey. In the second paragraph he calls the lifting of the anathemas a token of a new beginning that gave momentum to the "dialogue of love", and then states its limit: it points toward the restoration of full communion between the two churches and does not constitute it. He quotes the declaration's own words that the act is not sufficient to end the old and more recent differences.

It is cited in [[event:lifting-of-the-anathemas]] as the Ecumenical Patriarchate's own reading of the act, given on its own website. It is a statement made two generations later, by a successor of Athenagoras.
