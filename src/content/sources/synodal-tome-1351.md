The tome reviews the earlier councils, reports the sessions of 1351 at which [[person:gregory-palamas]], by then metropolitan of Thessalonica, answered his critics, and then works through six questions put by the emperor, with long chains of quotations from the Fathers. The translation notes that the original bore the signatures of the emperors John Kantakouzenos and John Palaiologos in red letters, followed by those of Patriarch Kallistos and the bishops, Palamas among them.

Like the tome of 1341 it is the judgment of the winning side, and it describes the opponents in the language of heresy-hunting. Its doctrinal conclusions are what the Orthodox Church later received; see [[event:hesychast-controversy]].
