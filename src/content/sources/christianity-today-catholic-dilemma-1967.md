The report says that in April 1967 a member of the pope's commission ignored an order of secrecy and gave its papers to the press: three papers of some 20,000 words, two of them giving the majority's opinion, which supported contraception as legitimate, and one a minority report that called the church's present teaching irreformable. It says that the documents were made public without official approval and that the commission had handed its papers to the pope the previous June. The note that follows says that the National Catholic Reporter printed the full texts when it learned that Le Monde was about to publish, and quotes the Vatican press officer, Fausto Vallainc, calling the release a very serious breach of secrecy.

The magazine is Protestant and stood outside the dispute it reports; it is not a source for the commission's proceedings, only for the fact and manner of the publication. The issue of the National Catholic Reporter that printed the texts could not be opened when this record was made. See [[source:papal-birth-control-commission-schema-1966]].
