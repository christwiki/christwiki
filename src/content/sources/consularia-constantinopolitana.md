The list names the consuls of each year and under some of them adds a line or two on what happened: emperors raised and dead, entries into Constantinople, victories, the arrival of relics. Many of these notes give the day in the Roman calendar. The work has no author's name. It is also known as the *Fasti Hydatiani*, because in the chief manuscript it follows the chronicle of the Spanish bishop Hydatius.

Its editor, Theodor Mommsen, divided it into three parts: a list from the city of Rome down to the founding of Constantinople, a part of Constantinopolitan origin from there to the death of [[person:theodosius-i]] in 395, and a continuation made in Spain that ends in 468. He judged that what survives is a shortened copy of the original, and he printed beside it the consular entries of the Greek [[source:chronicon-paschale]], which often run parallel. On his view the notes for the years from the founding of Constantinople to 395 go back to a record kept in the eastern capital.
