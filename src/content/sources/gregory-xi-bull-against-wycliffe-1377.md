The translation dates the bull at Santa Maria Maggiore in [[place:rome]] on 31 May in the sixth year of [[person:gregory-xi]], following the Latin copy in the [[source:fasciculi-zizaniorum]] (p. 244), whose editor notes that another copy reads "seventh". The companion bulls to the archbishop of Canterbury and the bishop of London, which the Victorian edition of [[source:foxe-acts-and-monuments|Foxe]] prints in Latin from the archbishop's register (vol. 3, notes to pp. 6–7), are dated 22 May (the eleventh day before the Kalends of June) in the seventh year, and the editor adds the year 1377. The year given here is that one; the day is left open. It describes [[person:john-wycliffe]] as rector of the church of [[place:lutterworth]] in the diocese of Lincoln and professor of the sacred Scriptures, compares his views to those of Marsilius of Padua, and orders the university to arrest him and hand him to the archbishop of Canterbury or the bishop of London.

The Sourcebook prints it on one page with the conclusions condemned at London in 1382 and with Wycliffe's letter to Urban VI. See [[event:wycliffe-bible]].
