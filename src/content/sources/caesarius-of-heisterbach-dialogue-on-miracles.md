Caesarius was a monk of Heisterbach, near Bonn, and wrote the Dialogue for the instruction of novices; it is dated to the years 1219 to 1223. He was not in the south of France. His chapter on the Albigensian heresy (5.21) rests on what reached the Cistercian order, whose abbot of Cîteaux, [[person:arnaud-amalric]], had led the crusade: he mentions a letter that a papal legate wrote "this year" to the Cistercian chapter.

He tells the story of Béziers as an example of God's vengeance on heretics, and gives the city more than a hundred thousand inhabitants. The abbot's answer is introduced with *fertur dixisse*, "he is said to have said". Caesarius does not offer it as something he witnessed, and he reports it without blame.
