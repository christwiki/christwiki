[[person:thomas-a-kempis]] wrote these lives for the younger members of his own community, as edifying reading. [[person:geert-groote]] died in 1384, when Thomas, by the age his own house chronicle gives him, was a small child; for Groote he depends on what he heard at [[place:deventer]] and on Groote's letters, some of which he quotes. For Florentius and the men of his house he writes from memory: he served at Florentius's table, sang in the choir beside him and lodged for a time in the house.

The lives are praise, not inquiry. They give few dates, pass over conflict quickly, and present the opponents of Groote as moved by envy. The translator notes that the latest year mentioned is 1439, so that Thomas wrote in old age, some fifty years after the events of his youth. See [[event:devotio-moderna]].
