Reims was the Roman Durocortorum, chief city of the Remi. Gregory of Tours names [[person:remigius]] as its bishop when he baptised [[person:clovis]], and describes the squares shaded with embroidered hangings and the baptistery prepared for the ceremony [[cite:baptism]]. Gregory does not say in so many words where the baptism was held; it is placed at Reims because the bishop who performed it was bishop of that city.
