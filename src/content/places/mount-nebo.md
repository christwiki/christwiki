Deuteronomy sets Mount Nebo in the land of Moab, opposite [[place:jericho]], and names its summit Pisgah [[bible:Deut 32:49]] [[bible:Deut 34:1]]. From it [[person:moses]] was shown the land. He died there and was buried in a valley in Moab, in a grave that no one knew [[bible:Deut 34:4-6]]. A later Jewish tradition, recorded in 2 Maccabees, says that Jeremiah hid the tabernacle and the ark in a cave on the mountain "where Moses went up" [[bible:2 Macc 2:4-8]].

The ridge now called Nebo rises above the north-eastern end of the Dead Sea, near Madaba in Jordan. The pilgrim Egeria climbed it in the 380s AD and found a small church on the summit, where the monks showed her the place in which, they said, Moses had been laid by angels [[cite:egeria-church]]. From the church door she saw the Jordan running into the Dead Sea, and Jericho beyond it [[cite:egeria-view]]. The church and the monastery that grew up around it were excavated by the Franciscans in the twentieth century (Sylvester J. Saller, *The Memorial of Moses on Mount Nebo*, 1941). The site matches the biblical description, but its identification rests on this tradition.
