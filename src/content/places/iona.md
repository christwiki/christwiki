Bede describes Iona as a small island, with land for about five families by English reckoning, separated from Britain by a narrow strait. He says that the Picts gave it to [[person:columba]] and that its monastery was for a long time the chief house of nearly all the monasteries of the northern Irish and of the Picts [[cite:bede]]. The Annals of Ulster record Columba's voyage to the island under 563 and say that it was granted to him by Conall son of Comgall [[cite:annals]]. Bishop Aidan, who began the conversion of Northumbria from Lindisfarne, was sent out from here [[cite:bede]].
