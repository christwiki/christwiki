In the bull of 1478 that let them appoint inquisitors, [[person:sixtus-iv]] told [[person:ferdinand-ii-of-aragon]] and [[person:isabella-i-of-castile]] that he hoped they would also bring the kingdom of Granada, where unbelievers lived, under their rule and convert its people [[cite:hope]]. The Instructions of 1484 direct that money penances laid on the reconciled be applied to the war against the Moors of Granada [[cite:war]].

Andrés Bernáldez tells how the city came to terms in December 1491, on condition that the Moors be left in their law and their property, and how on Monday 2 January 1492 its king came out and gave the keys to Ferdinand. A cross was then raised on the highest tower of the Alhambra [[cite:surrender]].

The edict that ordered the Jews to leave is dated in the city on 31 March 1492, and in it the king and queen count Granada among their kingdoms [[cite:edict]]. Bernáldez says that Isabella's body was brought to Granada and buried in the church of the Alhambra [[cite:burial]]. See [[event:spanish-inquisition]].
