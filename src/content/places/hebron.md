Hebron lies in the Judean hills, about thirty kilometres south of [[place:jerusalem]]. Genesis also calls it Kiriath Arba [[bible:Gen 23:2]]. Abram settled by the oaks of Mamre at Hebron and built an altar there [[bible:Gen 13:18]]. When [[person:sarah]] died he bought the field and cave of Machpelah from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place [[bible:Gen 23:17-20]]; Abraham, Isaac, Rebekah, Leah and Jacob were buried there after her [[bible:Gen 49:29-32]] [[bible:Gen 50:13]]. After the conquest the city was given to [[person:caleb]] [[bible:Josh 14:13-15]], and [[person:david]] reigned here for seven and a half years before he took Jerusalem [[bible:2 Sam 2:1-4]] [[bible:2 Sam 5:1-5]]. [[person:josephus]], in the first century AD, says that the tombs of Abraham's descendants, built of fine marble, were still shown in the town [[cite:josephus]]. A large walled enclosure stands today over the traditional site of the cave.
