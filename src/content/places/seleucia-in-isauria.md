Athanasius calls the city "Seleucia the Rugged" in Isauria, and says that about 160 bishops assembled there in September of the year in which the western bishops met at [[place:rimini|Ariminum]] [[cite:athanasius]]. Socrates explains that the eastern council had first been called to Nicomedia, which an earthquake ruined, then to Nicaea and to Tarsus, before it met at Seleucia in the presence of two imperial officials [[cite:socrates]]. The site is the modern town of Silifke, on the river Göksu (the ancient Calycadnus). See [[event:councils-of-rimini-and-seleucia]].
