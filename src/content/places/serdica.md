Serdica lay in the central Balkans, where the centre of Sofia now stands. An anonymous fourth-century life of Constantine says that [[person:galerius]] returned to Serdica, fell ill there with the disease that killed him, and died [[cite:galerius]]. The edict of toleration that he issued in his last weeks is therefore often called the Edict of Serdica. The text does not mention the edict, and neither Lactantius nor Eusebius, who preserve it, says where it was drawn up; its publication is recorded at [[place:nicomedia]]. The same source places [[person:constantine]] at Serdica a few years later, when he and [[person:licinius]] agreed on the appointment of their sons as Caesars [[cite:constantine]].
