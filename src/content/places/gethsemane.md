Mark and Matthew give the name Gethsemane to the place where Jesus prayed and was arrested [[bible:Mark 14:32; Matt 26:36]]. Luke sets the scene on the [[place:mount-of-olives]] without the name [[bible:Luke 22:39]], and John speaks of a garden across the Kidron valley where Jesus often met with his disciples [[bible:John 18:1-2]]. The Gospels give no closer description.

Eusebius, early in the fourth century, places Gethsemane at the Mount of Olives and says that the faithful still prayed there; Jerome's Latin version of about AD 390 adds that a church had been built over it [[cite:onomasticon]]. In the 380s the pilgrim Egeria found a church on the slope of the mount at the place where Jesus was said to have prayed, and below it a spot called Gethsemane, where the account of his arrest was read to the people on the night before Good Friday [[cite:egeria]]. The garden and church shown today at the foot of the mount, facing the east wall of [[place:jerusalem]], continue that tradition. None of these sources takes the tradition back beyond the fourth century.
