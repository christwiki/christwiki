The Life of Lalibela calls the place "the holy city of Roha" and urges every Ethiopian who has heard of its churches to go there [[cite:life-roha]]. Jules Perruchon, who edited the Life in 1892, notes that Roha, in the district of Lasta, had come to be known by the name of the king, [[person:gebre-meskel-lalibela]] [[cite:perruchon]]. Francisco Álvares, who visited in the 1520s, describes the town as lying on the slope of a mountain, a day and a half's descent below the summit, and found pilgrims coming to the king's tomb [[cite:alvares]].
