The Gospels call the place where Jesus was crucified Golgotha, "the place of a skull" [[bible:Mark 15:22; John 19:17]]. The English name Calvary comes from the Latin word for skull. John says that it was near the city and that there was a garden there with a new tomb, in which Jesus was buried [[bible:John 19:20, 41-42]]; the letter to the Hebrews says that he suffered "outside of the gate" [[bible:Heb 13:12]]. The New Testament gives no further direction.

The site shown today goes back at least to the fourth century. Eusebius, in his list of biblical places, says that Golgotha was pointed out in Jerusalem, north of Mount Zion [[cite:onomasticon]]. In his life of Constantine he reports that the emperor had a shrine of Venus pulled down and the earth beneath it dug away, that the cave of the tomb came to light, and that he ordered a church to be built there [[cite:eusebius]]. About AD 350 Cyril of Jerusalem, teaching candidates for baptism there, pointed to "Golgotha … near which we are now assembled" [[cite:cyril]]. The Church of the Holy Sepulchre, many times rebuilt, stands on the same spot inside the present Old City of [[place:jerusalem]]. The identification rests on the tradition of the Jerusalem church as Constantine's builders received it. It cannot be proved from the texts.
