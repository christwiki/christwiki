Bec lies in a valley of Normandy, between Rouen and Lisieux. Eadmer says that its first abbot, Herluin, built the monastery from the ground on his own inherited land, and that Lanfranc was its prior when [[person:anselm-of-canterbury]] became a monk there [[cite:eadmer]]. He places the writing of the Proslogion in the years when Anselm was himself prior [[cite:eadmer-proslogion]]. Anselm was "before Abbot of Bec" when he was made archbishop of [[place:canterbury]] in 1093 [[cite:asc]].
