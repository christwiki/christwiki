[[person:william-of-rubruck]] thought the city, leaving aside the khan's palace, smaller than the village of Saint-Denis. It had a quarter of the "Saracens", where the markets were, and a quarter of the Cathayans, who were craftsmen; palaces for the court secretaries; twelve temples of idols "of different nations", two mosques and, at the far end of the town, one church of Christians. A mud wall with four gates surrounded it [[cite:city]]. The khan Möngke had a walled palace beside the city wall [[cite:palace]].

His translator, W. W. Rockhill, notes that the site was fixed in 1889 by the Russian traveller N. Yadrintsev on the left bank of the Orkhon river [[cite:site]]. See [[event:franciscans-in-asia]].
