[[person:josephus|Josephus]] calls Antioch the metropolis of Syria and the third city of the Roman world, and says that it had a large Jewish community [[cite:third-city]] [[cite:jews]]. Acts says that believers scattered from [[place:jerusalem|Jerusalem]] preached there to non-Jews, [[person:barnabas]] and [[person:paul]] taught there, and "the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch" [[bible:Acts 11:19-26]]; see [[event:church-at-antioch]]. The church sent the two out on the [[event:first-missionary-journey|first missionary journey]] [[bible:Acts 13:1-3]]. The dispute that led to the [[event:council-of-jerusalem]] began there, and there Paul confronted [[person:peter|Peter]] [[bible:Acts 15:1-2]] [[bible:Gal 2:11-14]]. [[person:ignatius-of-antioch]] calls himself "the bishop of Syria" in his letter to the Romans [[cite:ignatius]]. It is to be distinguished from [[place:pisidian-antioch]] in Asia Minor.
