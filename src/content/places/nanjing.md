Lindesay Brine, writing in 1862, says that the Taiping stormed Nanjing on 19 March 1853 through a breach made by a mine [[cite:capture]], and that [[person:hong-xiuquan]], following the early Ming emperors, treated it as the capital of all China; his proclamations call it the "heavenly capital, and residence of the Heavenly King" [[cite:capital]]. The Qing commander Zeng Guofan reported that on 19 July 1864 his troops blew down part of the wall and took the city [[cite:fall]]. See [[event:taiping-rebellion]].
