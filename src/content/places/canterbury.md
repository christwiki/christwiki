Bede calls Canterbury the metropolis of King [[person:aethelberht-of-kent]]'s dominions. The king gave [[person:augustine-of-canterbury]] and his monks a dwelling there, and they first worshipped in a church of Saint Martin on the east side of the city, built in Roman times, where the Christian queen Bertha used to pray [[cite:arrival]]. Augustine then restored another old church inside the city and dedicated it to the Saviour as his cathedral, and founded outside the walls the monastery of Saints Peter and Paul, where he and the kings of Kent were buried [[cite:see]].
