[[person:geert-groote]] was the son of a magistrate of Deventer, left his house there to a community of poor women, and died and was buried in the town in 1384 [[cite:groote]]. A letter on the beginnings of Windesheim says that this house became the first of the congregations of women, and that the house named after Florentius was the first of the congregations of clerks [[cite:houses]]. [[person:thomas-a-kempis]] came to Deventer to study and describes the priests and clerks of Florentius's house, who held their goods in common and copied books [[cite:thomas]]. See [[event:devotio-moderna]].
