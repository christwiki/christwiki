Milan, the Roman Mediolanum, lies in the plain north of the river Po. [[person:constantine]] went there after his victory at Rome, and [[person:licinius]] joined him to marry Constantine's sister [[cite:lact-milan]]. The letter on freedom of worship that Licinius published a few months later begins by recalling their meeting in the city [[cite:lact-letter]], which is why it is known as the [[event:edict-of-milan]]. Two generations later [[person:ambrose]] was bishop of Milan when Augustine came to teach rhetoric there [[cite:confessions-arrival]], and Augustine was baptised in the city [[cite:confessions]].
