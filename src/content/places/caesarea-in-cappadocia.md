Caesarea, the modern Kayseri, was a city of Cappadocia; Jerome notes that it had formerly been called Mazaca [[cite:jerome]]. [[person:basil-of-caesarea]] was born into a family of the region and became its bishop. He defended to the governor the hospice he was building there for travellers and the sick [[cite:hospice]], and Gregory of Nazianzus, in his funeral oration, tells his hearers to go a little way out of the city and look at "the new city" that Basil had raised [[cite:new-city]]. It is to be distinguished from Caesarea in Palestine, the see of Eusebius. See [[event:cappadocian-fathers]].
