[[person:josephus]] describes how [[person:herod-the-great]] rebuilt the coastal town of Strato's Tower in white stone, gave it an artificial harbour with a great mole, a theatre, an amphitheatre and a temple with statues of Rome and of the emperor, and named it Caesarea. The work took twelve years [[cite:built]]. The Roman governors who ruled Judea from AD 6 resided there: Josephus shows [[person:pontius-pilate]] holding court there [[cite:pilate]], and Tacitus calls it the capital of Judea [[cite:capital]]; see [[event:judea-becomes-a-roman-province]].

In Acts the city is the home of the centurion [[person:cornelius]], whom [[person:peter]] has baptised with his household [[bible:Acts 10:1, 44-48]], and of [[person:philip-the-evangelist]] [[bible:Acts 21:8]]. Acts and Josephus both set the death of [[person:herod-agrippa-i]] there [[bible:Acts 12:19-23]] [[cite:agrippa]]. [[person:paul]] was brought to the governor at Caesarea and kept for two years in Herod's palace [[bible:Acts 23:33-35]] [[bible:Acts 24:27]]. It is called Maritima, "by the sea", to distinguish it from [[place:caesarea-philippi]].
