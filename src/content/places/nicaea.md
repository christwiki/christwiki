Nicaea stood at the eastern end of Lake Ascania in Bithynia, a short journey from the imperial residence at [[place:nicomedia]]. Eusebius remarks that its name, from the Greek word for victory, suited the occasion when [[person:constantine]] chose it for the council of 325 [[cite:eusebius]]. The bishops met in the largest hall of the palace there [[cite:palace]]. The town is modern İznik. See [[event:council-of-nicaea]] and, for the council of 787, [[event:second-council-of-nicaea]].
