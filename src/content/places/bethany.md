Bethany lay on the [[place:mount-of-olives]], "about fifteen stadia" from [[place:jerusalem]] [[bible:Mark 11:1; John 11:18]]. John names it as the village of [[person:martha]], [[person:mary-of-bethany]] and [[person:lazarus]], and sets the raising of Lazarus there [[bible:John 11:1, 38-44]]. During his last days in Jerusalem Jesus went out to Bethany in the evenings [[bible:Mark 11:11-12; Matt 21:17]], and it was there, in the house of Simon the leper, that a woman anointed him [[bible:Mark 14:3]]. Luke sets the ascension near it [[bible:Luke 24:50]]. It is not the "Bethany beyond the Jordan" where John baptised [[bible:John 1:28]].

Eusebius, early in the fourth century, describes Bethany as a village two miles from Jerusalem on the slope of the mount, where the place of Lazarus was still pointed out [[cite:onomasticon]]. The pilgrim Egeria, in the 380s, calls it the Lazarium and puts it at about the second milestone from the city [[cite:egeria]]. The Arabic name of the modern town, al-Eizariya, is usually derived from the name of Lazarus.
