Genesis says that the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat, in the plural [[bible:Gen 8:4]]. Elsewhere in the Old Testament Ararat is a country: the sons who murder [[person:sennacherib]] of Assyria escape to "the land of Ararat" [[bible:2 Kgs 19:37]], and Jeremiah lists Ararat among the kingdoms summoned against Babylon [[bible:Jer 51:27]]. It is the highland kingdom that Assyrian texts call Urartu, around Lake Van. [[person:josephus]], in the first century AD, puts the landing on a mountain in Armenia and quotes Berossus, who placed the remains of the vessel in Armenia, at the mountain of the Cordyaeans [[cite:josephus]]. The pin marks Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı, 5,137 metres), the peak that later tradition named as the landing place. Genesis itself does not single it out.
