The duc de Saint-Simon, who writes as an enemy of the Jesuits, says that learned men lived in retirement at the monastery of Port-Royal des Champs, taught the young and wrote, and that they were accused of Jansenism. There was a second house of the same name in [[place:paris]]. On the night of 28 to 29 October 1709, he says, troops surrounded Port-Royal des Champs and an officer carried the nuns off in coaches to other convents; later the dead were ordered to be exhumed and the house, the church and all the buildings were razed [[cite:saint-simon]]. [[person:blaise-pascal]], whose *Provincial Letters* defended its friends, wrote that he himself was "not of Port-Royal" [[cite:pascal]]. The coordinates are those of the site of Port-Royal des Champs. See [[event:jansenism]].
