Pi-Ramesses, "House of Ramesses", was the residence that Ramesses II (reigned about 1279–1213 BC) built in the eastern Nile Delta, beside the older city of Avaris. Excavation has fixed its site at the village of Qantir, near Faqus; James K. Hoffmeier calls the archaeological evidence "unequivocal" (*Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 50, 2007, pp. 232–233). The city was abandoned in the later twelfth century BC, after the branch of the Nile on which it stood moved away or silted up, and Tanis, about twenty kilometres off, became the capital of the Delta (Hoffmeier, p. 233; Bryant G. Wood, *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 48, 2005, p. 479).

Exodus says that the Israelites built the store cities Pithom and Raamses for Pharaoh [[bible:Exod 1:11]] and that they set out from Rameses [[bible:Exod 12:37]] [[bible:Num 33:3-5]]; Genesis calls the district where Jacob's family settled "the land of Rameses" [[bible:Gen 47:11]]. The identification of this place with Pi-Ramesses is generally accepted, and it is one of the main arguments for dating [[event:the-exodus]] to the thirteenth century BC. Those who date the exodus to about 1446 BC accept the location but hold that the name in Exodus is a later updating of an older one (Bryant G. Wood, *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 48, 2005, p. 479). The Psalms place the wonders of the exodus in "the field of Zoan", the Hebrew name of Tanis [[bible:Ps 78:12]] [[bible:Ps 78:43]].
