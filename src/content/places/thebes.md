Thebes stood on both banks of the Nile in Upper Egypt, where the town of Luxor is now. It was the city of the god Amun and a capital of Egypt under the New Kingdom. The temples of Karnak and Luxor are on the east bank; across the river, at the edge of the desert, the kings built their tombs and mortuary temples. In the first court of the temple of [[person:merneptah]] there, Flinders Petrie found the stele that names Israel [[cite:find]], and part of a second copy of its text survives at Karnak [[cite:karnak]]. See [[event:merneptah-stele]].

The Old Testament calls the city No, or No-amon, after its god. Nahum holds up its fall as a warning to Nineveh [[bible:Nah 3:7-10]], and Jeremiah and Ezekiel announce judgment on "Amon of No" and on the city [[bible:Jer 46:25]] [[bible:Ezek 30:14-16]].
