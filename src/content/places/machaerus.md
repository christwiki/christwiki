Machaerus stands on an isolated hill above the eastern shore of the Dead Sea. [[person:josephus]] describes it as a rocky height ringed by ravines, the one on the west running down to the Dead Sea. The Hasmonean king Alexander Jannaeus first fortified it, the Roman general Gabinius demolished it, and [[person:herod-the-great]] rebuilt it with walls, corner towers, cisterns and a palace, because it faced the Arabian frontier. The Romans besieged it after the fall of [[place:jerusalem]] [[cite:description]].

Under [[person:herod-antipas]] the fortress lay on the border with the Nabataean kingdom of Aretas [[cite:border]]. Josephus says that [[person:john-the-baptist]] was sent there in chains and put to death [[cite:john]]. The Gospels speak only of "the prison" and do not name the place [[bible:Mark 6:17, 27]]; see [[event:death-of-john-the-baptist]]. The site is the hill beside the village of Mukawir in Jordan, whose name preserves the ancient one and whose setting matches the description in Josephus.
