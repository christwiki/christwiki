Najran is an oasis in the south-west of modern Saudi Arabia, near the border with Yemen; the ancient town is identified with the ruin field of al-Ukhdud. The letter of Simeon of Beth Arsham calls it "Nagrin", says that it had a church and had had a bishop named Paul, and says that it held out against the Himyarite king until its chiefs came out under oath [[cite:letter]]. An inscription of one of the king's commanders records that he was posted against Najran with tribesmen and nomads during the same war [[cite:ja-1028]]. See [[event:martyrs-of-najran]].
