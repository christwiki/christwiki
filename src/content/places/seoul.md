Seoul was the capital of the Korean kingdom [[cite:return]]. A missionary writing from [[place:beijing]] in 1784 reckoned the journey by land between the two capitals at about three months [[cite:return]]. [[person:yi-seung-hun]] came back to Seoul from Beijing in the spring of 1784 with Christian books [[cite:return]], and the Chinese priest James Zhou Wen-mo, who entered Korea at the end of 1794, lived hidden in the city from 1795 [[cite:zhou]]. Yi Seung-hun and five others were beheaded outside its West Gate on 8 April 1801 [[cite:executions]]. See [[event:first-korean-baptism]].
