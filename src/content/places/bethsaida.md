John's Gospel calls Bethsaida the town of [[person:andrew]], [[person:peter]] and Philip [[bible:John 1:44]] [[bible:John 12:21]]. Mark sets there the healing of a blind man [[bible:Mark 8:22-26]], Luke places the feeding of the five thousand in its neighbourhood [[bible:Luke 9:10]], and [[person:jesus]] names it with Chorazin among the towns that did not repent [[bible:Matt 11:21]]. [[person:josephus]] says that the tetrarch Philip raised the village of Bethsaida, on the lake of Gennesaret, to the rank of a city and named it Julias [[cite:julias]]. He places Julias in lower Gaulanitis and says that the [[place:jordan-river|Jordan]] passes it just before entering the lake [[cite:gaulanitis]] [[cite:jordan]]. In AD 66–67 he fought a skirmish close to Julias and the Jordan, on marshy ground [[cite:life]].

These texts fix the town near the mouth of the Jordan on the north shore, but not the exact spot. Two sites are proposed. Et-Tell, a mound about two kilometres from the present shore, has been excavated since 1987 and is defended in Rami Arav and Richard A. Freund (editors), *Bethsaida: A City by the North Shore of the Sea of Galilee* (4 volumes, 1995–2009). El-Araj, on the shore itself, has been excavated since 2016; the case for it is made by R. Steven Notley, "Et-Tell Is Not Bethsaida", *Near Eastern Archaeology* 70 (2007), pp. 220–230. The marker follows the Pleiades gazetteer, which now places the name at el-Araj; et-Tell lies about two kilometres to the north.
