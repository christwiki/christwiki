Athens was an old centre of Greek learning. Acts remarks that its people spent their time telling or hearing something new [[bible:Acts 17:21]]. [[person:paul]] was brought there from Beroea, ahead of his companions. Acts says that he was provoked by the number of its idols, argued in the synagogue and in the marketplace, and was questioned by Epicurean and Stoic philosophers, who brought him to the Areopagus. His speech there starts from an altar he had seen inscribed "To an unknown god", quotes a Greek poet, and ends with the resurrection, at which some of his hearers mocked. A few believed, among them Dionysius, a member of the Areopagus, and a woman named Damaris [[bible:Acts 17:15-34]]. Paul mentions his stay in passing: he was "left behind at Athens alone" when he sent [[person:timothy|Timothy]] back to [[place:thessalonica|Thessalonica]] [[bible:1 Thess 3:1-2]].
