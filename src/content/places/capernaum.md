Matthew says that [[person:jesus]] left [[place:nazareth]] and lived at Capernaum "by the sea", and later calls it "his own city" [[bible:Matt 4:13]] [[bible:Matt 9:1]]. Mark sets there the first Sabbath of the ministry, in the synagogue and in the house of Simon [[person:peter]] and [[person:andrew]] [[bible:Mark 1:21-34]]; see [[event:galilean-ministry-begins]]. In Luke the elders of the town say that a centurion built their synagogue [[bible:Luke 7:1-5]], Matthew appears to place a tax office there [[bible:Matt 9:1, 9]], and John sets the discourse on the bread of life in its synagogue [[bible:John 6:59]]. Jesus is also reported to have said that the town would be brought low for failing to repent [[bible:Matt 11:23-24]].

[[person:josephus]], who commanded in Galilee in AD 66–67, tells of being carried injured to "a village named Cepharnome" after a skirmish near [[place:bethsaida|Julias]], by the [[place:jordan-river|Jordan]] [[cite:life]], and gives the name Capharnaum to a spring that watered the plain of Gennesaret [[cite:spring]]. The town is identified with the ruins at Tell Hum on the north-west shore, a few kilometres west of the point where the Jordan enters the lake.
