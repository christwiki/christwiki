Seleucia and Ctesiphon stood on opposite banks of the Tigris, about thirty kilometres below modern Baghdad, and together made up the capital of the Persian empire. The acts of the synod of 410 call the place "the great city", chief of all the cities of the East [[cite:capital]]. That synod named its bishop grand metropolitan and head of all the bishops of the Persian empire [[cite:canon21]] and refers to his church as the church of Kokhe [[cite:canon12]]. It also ruled that the bishops should gather here every two years, when the king was in residence [[cite:canon6]]. See [[event:synod-of-seleucia-ctesiphon]].
