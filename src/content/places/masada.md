Masada is an isolated rock in the Judaean desert overlooking the Dead Sea. [[person:josephus]] describes it as a high rock ringed by deep ravines, reached by two paths, one from the lake on the east, called the Serpent, and an easier one on the west. He says that a high priest named Jonathan first built a fortress there and that [[person:herod-the-great]] walled the summit, built a palace on it, cut cisterns and laid up food and arms [[cite:description]]. Pliny the Elder mentions it as a fortress on a rock not far from the Dead Sea [[cite:pliny]].

Josephus says that rebels seized Masada at the start of the Jewish revolt and that it was the one stronghold still holding out when the Romans besieged it; see [[event:fall-of-masada|the fall of Masada]]. The remains of Herod's palaces and of the Roman camps, fortifications and assault ramp are preserved, and the site was entered on the World Heritage List in 2001 [[cite:today]].
