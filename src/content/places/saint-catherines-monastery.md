The monastery stands below [[place:mount-sinai|Jebel Musa]] in the south of the Sinai peninsula. Procopius, writing in the reign of Justinian, says that monks lived on Mount Sina, that the emperor built them a church dedicated to the Mother of God, lower down and not on the summit, and that at the base of the mountain he built a strong fortress with a garrison [[cite:procopius]]. The Codex Sinaiticus takes its name from the monastery, where it was preserved until the middle of the nineteenth century [[cite:history]]. Parts of it remain there, among them leaves found on 26 May 1975 in a chamber beneath St George's Chapel on the north wall [[cite:history]]. See [[event:codex-sinaiticus]].
