William of Saint-Thierry describes Clairvaux as a place in the territory of Langres, not far from the river Aube, which had been a den of robbers and was once called the Valley of Wormwood. There, he says, [[person:bernard-of-clairvaux]] and his monks settled in poverty and made of it a house of prayer [[cite:vp-clairvaux]]. Geoffrey of Auxerre calls Bernard the first abbot of Clairvaux and the father of more than a hundred and sixty other monasteries [[cite:vp-death]].
