Exodus calls the mountain Sinai, "God's mountain" and, at the call of Moses, Horeb [[bible:Exod 19:18-20]] [[bible:Exod 3:1]]; Deuteronomy regularly calls it Horeb [[bible:Deut 5:2]]. [[person:moses]] is called there, Israel receives the law there, and Elijah later flees to it [[bible:1 Kgs 19:8]]. The Bible places it eleven days' journey from [[place:kadesh-barnea]] [[bible:Deut 1:2]], and Paul speaks of "Mount Sinai in Arabia" [[bible:Gal 4:25]], but no passage says exactly where it stood.

The identification with Jebel Musa, a peak in the south of the Sinai peninsula, is a Christian tradition. It is described in detail by the pilgrim Egeria in the 380s AD. She climbed the mountain that the local monks showed her as Sinai and found a small church on its summit [[cite:egeria-summit]]. At the head of the valley below she was shown the burning bush, in a garden beside another church [[cite:egeria-bush]]. Her editors identify the peak she climbed as Jebel Musa and the place of the bush as the site where [[place:saint-catherines-monastery]] now stands (introduction to the translation cited, pp. xviii–xix). No trace of an Israelite camp has been identified there or elsewhere, and other mountains in Sinai and in north-western Arabia have been proposed. The candidates are reviewed by James K. Hoffmeier, *Ancient Israel in Sinai* (2005). The coordinates are those of Jebel Musa.
