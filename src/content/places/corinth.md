Corinth stood beside the isthmus, with a harbour on each sea; Acts names the eastern one, Cenchreae [[bible:Acts 18:18]]. It was the seat of the proconsul of Achaia [[bible:Acts 18:12]]. [[person:paul]] came there from [[place:athens]], worked as a tentmaker with [[person:aquila]] and [[person:priscilla]], and stayed a year and a half. The ruler of the synagogue, Crispus, was among those who believed. When Paul was accused before the proconsul [[person:gallio]], the case was dismissed [[bible:Acts 18:1-17]]. See [[event:second-missionary-journey]].

Paul's two surviving letters to "the assembly of God which is at Corinth" deal with divisions, conduct and worship in the church and with his own strained relations with it; he speaks of coming to it a third time [[bible:1 Cor 1:2, 11]] [[bible:2 Cor 13:1]]. The letter to the Romans commends Phoebe, a servant of the church at Cenchreae [[bible:Rom 16:1]]. About AD 96 the church of [[place:rome|Rome]] wrote to the Corinthians about a new quarrel and reminded them of Paul's letter [[cite:clement]]; see [[event:first-clement]].
