Nazareth lies in the hills of lower Galilee, above the plain of Jezreel. Luke calls it "a city of Galilee" and places there the angel's message to [[person:mary]] [[bible:Luke 1:26-27]]. In Luke it is the home of Mary and Joseph before the birth of Jesus and the place to which they return [[bible:Luke 2:4, 39]]; in Matthew the family settles there after coming back from Egypt [[bible:Matt 2:22-23]]. [[person:jesus]] grew up in the town [[bible:Luke 2:51-52]], came from it to be baptised by John [[bible:Mark 1:9]], and was known by its name [[bible:Mark 1:24]] [[bible:John 1:45]]. John's Gospel preserves a slighting remark: "Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" [[bible:John 1:46]].

Luke tells of a visit to its synagogue that ended with the congregation trying to throw Jesus from the brow of the hill on which the town was built [[bible:Luke 4:16-30]]. Mark says that his own townspeople took offence at him and that he could do no mighty work there [[bible:Mark 6:1-6]]. The modern city stands on the ancient site.
