Beersheba lies in the northern Negev. The mound of the ancient town, Tel Be'er Sheva, is a few kilometres east of the modern city. Genesis explains the name by an oath sworn between [[person:abraham]] and Abimelech over a well [[bible:Gen 21:27-33]], and tells a similar story of [[person:isaac]] [[bible:Gen 26:26-33]]. Abraham returned here after [[event:binding-of-isaac]] [[bible:Gen 22:19]]. [[person:jacob]] set out from here for Haran [[bible:Gen 28:10]] and offered sacrifices here on his way down to Egypt [[bible:Gen 46:1-5]]. In later books the town marks the southern limit of the land, as in the phrase "from Dan even to Beersheba" [[bible:1 Sam 3:20]]. [[person:elijah]] fled this way from [[person:jezebel]] [[bible:1 Kgs 19:1-3]], and [[person:amos]] names it with [[place:bethel]] and [[place:gilgal]] among the sanctuaries that Israel is told not to seek [[bible:Amos 5:5]].
