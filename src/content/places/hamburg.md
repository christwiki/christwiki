Rimbert says that Louis the Pious set up an archbishop's see at Hamburg, in the furthest part of Saxony beyond the Elbe, so that bishops and priests could be sent from it to the Danes and Swedes [[cite:founded]]. Raiders from the sea took the town, and the church and monastery built under [[person:ansgar]] were burned with his books; the diocese, which had only four baptismal churches, was then united with Bremen [[cite:burned]].
