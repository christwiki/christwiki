Edessa, called Urhai in Syriac, lay in Upper Mesopotamia between the Euphrates and the Tigris, in the district the Romans called Osrhoene. It had its own line of kings, and Eusebius calls it "a royal city" [[cite:archives]]. The river Daisan ran through it and in November 201 flooded the palace of [[person:abgar-viii]] and the buildings along its banks; the record of that flood names a church of the Christians among the buildings destroyed [[cite:flood]]. A few years earlier the churches of Osrhoene had taken part in the correspondence about the date of Easter [[cite:osrhoene]]. Eusebius says that he drew the story of King Abgar's letter to Jesus from documents kept in the city's archives [[cite:archives]].
