Damascus lies in an oasis east of the Anti-Lebanon range. It is named in the story of Abraham [[bible:Gen 14:15]]. In the books of Kings it is the seat of the kings of Aram, Israel's neighbours and frequent enemies, and the home of the commander Naaman [[bible:1 Kings 15:18]] [[bible:2 Kings 5:1, 12]]; the prophets speak against it [[bible:Isa 17:1]]. By the first century it had synagogues, to which Saul carried letters from the high priest [[bible:Acts 9:2]]. Acts says that near the city he saw a light that blinded him, and that in it [[person:ananias-of-damascus]] laid hands on him and he was baptised and first preached [[bible:Acts 9:3-22]]. [[person:paul]] says that he returned there from Arabia, and that he escaped from it when the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city [[bible:Gal 1:17]] [[bible:2 Cor 11:32-33]].
