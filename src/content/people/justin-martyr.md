Justin calls himself the son of Priscus and grandson of Bacchius, from Flavia Neapolis in Palestine, the modern Nablus [[cite:apol-1]]. By his own account he tried one school of Greek philosophy after another before an old man turned him to the Hebrew prophets and to Christ, and he went on calling himself a philosopher afterwards [[cite:dialogue]].

At [[place:rome]] he taught in his lodging, wrote against [[person:marcion]] and other teachers, and addressed his *Apology* to [[person:antoninus-pius]] [[cite:acts]] [[cite:marcion]] [[cite:apol-1]]. He was tried with six companions before the prefect Rusticus and beheaded [[cite:acts]]. Irenaeus, who wrote about fifteen years later, already speaks of his martyrdom, and Eusebius places it under Marcus Aurelius [[cite:irenaeus]] [[cite:eusebius]]. See [[event:justin-martyr]].
