Zeno came from Isauria, where he took refuge when Basiliscus seized the throne [[cite:flight]]. He marched back on the capital, Evagrius says, after Basiliscus had ruled for two years, and annulled what the usurper had decreed in his circular letters against Chalcedon [[cite:return]]. Evagrius, who disapproved of him, describes his private life as shameless [[cite:character]].

When the see of Alexandria was disputed he expelled one claimant, restored Peter Mongus, and addressed to the Egyptian church the statement called the Henotikon [[cite:alexandria]]. It names Nicaea as the one creed, condemns Nestorius and Eutyches, and anathematises any other teaching "whether at Chalcedon or in any synod whatever" [[cite:henotikon]]. To the protests of Pope Felix he replied that he and all the churches received and revered the Council of Chalcedon [[cite:reply]]. He died in 491, in the consulship of Olybrius, in what Marcellinus counts, with the months of Basiliscus, as the sixth month of the seventeenth year of his reign [[cite:death]]; Evagrius says that he died of epilepsy and left no child [[cite:death-evagrius]].
