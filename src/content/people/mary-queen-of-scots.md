Most of what this wiki reports of Mary comes from [[person:john-knox]], who was her opponent. In 1560 she was in France, married to its king, and the acts of the Scottish Parliament against the pope and the mass were sent to the two of them there; no ratification was returned [[cite:france]].

She landed in Scotland as a widow on 19 August 1561. Mass was said in her chapel on the first Sunday, to the anger of the Protestant lords, and the next day a proclamation in her name forbade any alteration of the state of religion that she had found in the realm at her arrival [[cite:arrival]]. Knox reports that she charged him to his face with stirring her subjects against her mother and herself [[cite:interview]]. In July 1567 she resigned the crown to her son, [[person:james-vi-and-i]], and the Parliament held in his name that December, with the earl of Moray as regent, ratified the acts of 1560 [[cite:demission]] [[cite:ratified]].
