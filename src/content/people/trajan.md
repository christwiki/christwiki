Trajan was a Spaniard, governor of Germany when the aged emperor Nerva adopted him as his son and successor; Cassius Dio remarks that no one from outside Italy had ruled Rome before him [[cite:adoption]]. He reigned for nineteen and a half years and died at Selinus in Cilicia on his way back from a war against Parthia [[cite:death]].

His ruling on Christians survives in his answer to [[person:pliny-the-younger]]: they are not to be sought out, those accused and convicted are to be punished, those who deny the charge and pray to the gods are to be pardoned, and unsigned accusations are to be ignored [[cite:rescript]]. Eusebius, two centuries later, places in his reign the deaths of Symeon, bishop of Jerusalem, and of [[person:ignatius-of-antioch]] [[cite:symeon]] [[cite:ignatius]].
