The English chronicler Knighton records under 1378 that some of the cardinals left [[person:urban-vi]], went to [[place:avignon]] and raised Robert of Geneva to the papacy under the name Clement, and that a great schism followed, with kings and kingdoms on either side [[cite:knighton]]. A Life of Clement written by one of his adherents puts the election not at Avignon but at Fondi, in the palace of its count, on 20 September 1378 [[cite:fondi]], and says that he took ship in May 1379, landed at Marseille on 10 June and went on to Avignon [[cite:to-avignon]]. [[person:catherine-of-siena]], who held to Urban, called him an antipope [[cite:catherine]].

The same Life says that he died at Avignon on 16 September 1394, after a short illness, and was buried in the principal church there [[cite:death]]. At the time of the Council of Constance the claimant of this line was Peter de Luna, called Benedict XIII, whom the council deposed in 1417 [[cite:three]] [[cite:benedict]]. See [[event:western-schism]].
