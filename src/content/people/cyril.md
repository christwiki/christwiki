His Life says that the emperor Michael III chose him to answer the request of [[person:rastislav]] for a teacher, that he composed letters for the Slavonic language before he set out, and that in Moravia he translated the services of the church and defended worship in Slavonic against Latin clergy [[cite:mission]]. A decade after his death Pope John VIII, permitting the Slavonic liturgy, praised the Slavonic letters as the invention of Constantine the Philosopher [[cite:letters]].

At [[place:rome]] he was received by Pope [[person:hadrian-ii]], fell ill, took the monastic habit and the name Cyril, and died fifty days later, on 14 February 869, aged forty-two [[cite:rome]]. See [[event:cyril-and-methodius]].
