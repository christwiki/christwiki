Everything known of Monica comes from the *Confessions* of her son [[person:augustine]]. She was given in marriage to a man who was not a Christian, and near the end of his life she won him to the faith [[cite:husband]]. During Augustine's years as a Manichaean she begged a bishop to argue with him; he refused, and sent her away with the words that "it is not possible that the son of these tears should perish" [[cite:tears]].

She followed her son by sea to [[place:milan]] [[cite:milan]] and was told at once of his conversion [[cite:garden]]. At Ostia, waiting for a ship home, the two talked at a window about the life of the saints, a conversation Augustine describes as an ascent of the mind beyond all created things [[cite:ostia]]. A few days later she fell ill with a fever and died there, in the fifty-sixth year of her age and the thirty-third of his [[cite:death]]. See [[event:conversion-of-augustine]]. Her dates are reckoned from his: he was born in 354.
