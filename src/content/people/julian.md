Julian was the son of a half-brother of [[person:constantine]]. Socrates says that he was eight when Constantine died, that he was educated at [[place:constantinople]] and [[place:nicomedia]], where he was made a reader in the church, and that the philosopher Maximus of Ephesus drew him to the old religion [[cite:socrates]]. Ammianus, who served under him, says that he kept this secret until he had sole power and then ordered the temples reopened and the sacrifices restored [[cite:secret]]. His own rescript on teachers tells Christians who will not honour the gods of Homer to go and expound Matthew and Luke in their churches [[cite:rescript]].

He was wounded in battle against the Persians and died on 26 June 363, in the thirty-first year of his age by Socrates's reckoning [[cite:death]]; Ammianus, who was on the campaign, says the thirty-second [[cite:ammianus-death]]. See [[event:julian-the-apostate]].
