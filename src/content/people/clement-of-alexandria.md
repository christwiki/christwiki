Clement says in his own *Stromata* that he sought out teachers in Greece, Italy and the East and found rest with the last of them, in Egypt; Eusebius identifies this teacher as Pantaenus [[cite:pantaenus]]. Eusebius reports that Clement succeeded Pantaenus in charge of catechetical instruction at [[place:alexandria]] and that [[person:origen]] as a boy was among his pupils [[cite:school]]. He lists Clement's works: the *Stromata* in eight books, the *Exhortation to the Greeks*, the *Instructor*, the homily *What Rich Man is Saved?* and others, and notes how often he quotes Greek authors [[cite:works]].

He appears again in letters of Alexander, who became bishop of Jerusalem, as Eusebius quotes them. He sent a letter to Antioch by the hand of "Clement, the blessed presbyter" and, writing afterwards to Origen, speaks of Clement as his master and as one who had gone before [[cite:alexander]]. The passages cited here do not give the years of his birth or death.
