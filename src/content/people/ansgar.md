Ansgar was trained at Corbie and sent to teach at its daughter house of Corvey in Saxony. From there he was chosen to go home with the baptized Danish king Harald, and afterwards to answer a Swedish request for priests [[cite:early]]. Louis the Pious made him archbishop of a new see at [[place:hamburg]], and Pope Gregory IV named him legate for the northern peoples; after Hamburg was burned by raiders he was given the see of Bremen as well [[cite:see]]. See [[event:ansgar]].

Rimbert says that his last illness began in the sixty-fourth year of his life, the thirty-fourth of his episcopate, and that he died on the day after the feast of the Purification [[cite:death]]. Rimbert names no year; 865 is the year given by the translator of the Life, and the year of birth is reckoned from it.
