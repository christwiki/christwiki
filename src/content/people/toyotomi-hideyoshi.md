Hideyoshi's two edicts of 1587 give his own reasons for acting against Christianity: Japan is the country of the gods; lords must not force their people to convert; the missionaries have had shrines and temples destroyed and must leave, though the foreign ships may still trade [[cite:edicts]].

The Spanish official Antonio de Morga, who calls him Taicosama and "sovereign of Japon", describes him ten years later tolerating a Franciscan house in his capital, seizing the cargo of the wrecked galleon *San Felipe*, and being told that Spain's friars prepared the way for its soldiers [[cite:morga-wreck]]. Morga, no friend of his, puts the order to crucify the friars down to greed and anger [[cite:morga-order]]. The sentence that Morga prints gives a different ground: the condemned had stayed to preach a religion that he had forbidden [[cite:morga-sentence]]. See [[event:japan-martyrs]].
