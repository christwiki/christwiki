By his own account Equiano was born in 1745 in a district he calls Eboe, in the kingdom of Benin [[cite:birth]], was kidnapped as a child and was carried across the Atlantic in a slave ship to Barbados [[cite:crossing]]. He was baptized at St Margaret's, Westminster, in February 1759 under the name Gustavus Vassa [[cite:baptism]]. He prints the deed by which his owner in Montserrat set him free on 10 July 1766 for seventy pounds of the island's money [[cite:freedom]], and he describes a conversion on 6 October 1774, when he came to believe that salvation was the gift of God and not the reward of his own good deeds [[cite:conversion]].

In March 1788 he presented a petition to the queen on behalf of his "African brethren" [[cite:queen]], and a year later he dedicated his *Interesting Narrative* to Parliament [[cite:dedication]]. See [[event:abolition-of-the-slave-trade]].

The African birth rests on his own word: Vincent Carretta, *Equiano, the African: Biography of a Self-Made Man* (2005), found that the register of his baptism and a ship's muster roll of 1773 give his birthplace as Carolina, and argues that the African chapters may be a reconstruction.
