In the first Gospel [[person:jesus]] sees "a man called Matthew" sitting at the tax office, apparently at [[place:capernaum]], and says, "Follow me"; the list of the Twelve in the same Gospel calls him "Matthew the tax collector" [[bible:Matt 9:1, 9; 10:3]]. Mark and Luke tell the same story of a tax collector named Levi, son of Alphaeus, who then gives a feast at which Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners [[bible:Mark 2:13-17]] [[bible:Luke 5:27-32]]. Matthew appears in all four lists of the Twelve [[bible:Mark 3:18]] [[bible:Luke 6:15]] [[bible:Acts 1:13]]; see [[event:calling-of-the-twelve]]. Nothing more is told of him in the New Testament.

Papias, a Christian writer of the early second century whose words survive in Eusebius, wrote that "Matthew wrote the oracles in the Hebrew language, and every one interpreted them as he was able" [[cite:papias]]. [[person:irenaeus]], about AD 180, says that Matthew issued a written Gospel among the Hebrews in their own language [[cite:irenaeus]]. The Gospel itself names no author. How these statements relate to the Greek Gospel we have is discussed under [[event:writing-of-the-gospels]].
