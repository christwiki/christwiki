The papal sentence against him names him "Henry the king, son of Henry the emperor" [[cite:deposition]]. In his letter of 1076 to [[person:gregory-vii]] he set out the royal position: he held his kingdom from God and not from the pope, and an anointed king could be judged by God alone unless he strayed from the faith [[cite:henry]].

Gregory declared him deposed and excommunicate in 1076 [[cite:deposition]], absolved him at [[place:canossa]] in January 1077 [[cite:canossa]] and excommunicated him again in 1080 [[cite:second-ban]]. Bernold of Constance, who writes as his enemy, records that in 1084 Henry entered the Lateran palace in [[place:rome]] and was crowned at Easter by Guibert of Ravenna, whom excommunicated bishops had ordained pope [[cite:bernold]]. Ekkehard of Aura records his death under the year 1106 and says that his own party called him the fourth emperor of his name, while those loyal to the popes called him a heresiarch [[cite:ekkehard-1106]].
