The tract that [[person:bartolome-de-las-casas]] printed in 1552 calls Sepúlveda a doctor and the emperor's chronicler, and says that he contended that the conquests of the Indies were lawful [[cite:dispute]]. Its introduction, written by his opponent, reports that he had composed a dialogue in elegant Latin with two chief conclusions, that the wars the Spaniards had made on the Indians were just and that the Indians were bound to submit to the rule of the Spaniards as the less understanding to the more prudent, and that the Council of the Indies had refused him leave to print it [[cite:argument]].
