When [[person:susenyos]] swore obedience to the pope in 1626, the Jesuit historian Tellez says, the nobles also took an oath to "Prince Faciladas" as heir to the crown [[cite:heir]]. The Ethiopian chronicle tells that in his father's twenty-fifth year he came to him and said that the land was troubled by the belief of the Franks, which was not in the books of their fathers, and asked him to pray as the faith of Alexandria taught [[cite:plea]]. His father's proclamation restoring that faith named him to reign, and he succeeded when Susenyos died [[cite:succession]]. The chronicle gives his throne name as Alam Sagad and says that in his first year a new patriarch arrived and the Frank Afonsu, [[person:afonso-mendes]], returned to his country [[cite:reign]].

The Jesuit writers judge him as an enemy. Lobo says that he had embraced the Catholic religion "rather through complaisance than conviction" and showed his hatred of the missionaries while his father still lived [[cite:lobo]]. Tellez calls him a persecutor of Catholics "like another Dioclesian" [[cite:persecution]] and tells how, on his orders, the patriarch and the Jesuits were handed to the Turks on the coast in 1634 [[cite:expulsion]].
