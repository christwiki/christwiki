John writes that he left Tabriz in Persia in 1291, stayed thirteen months in India, where his Dominican companion died, and went on to Cathay, where he gave the great khan a letter from the pope and invited him to become a Catholic. The khan, he says, "had grown too old in idolatry" but was kind to the Christians. For eleven years he worked alone. He built a church in [[place:beijing|Khanbaliq]], baptised by his own estimate some six thousand people, and trained a choir of boys [[cite:first]].

In his second letter he styles himself legate and nuncio of the Apostolic See and describes a second church close to the khan's palace [[cite:second]]. Andrew of Perugia, one of the bishops sent to him, says that John was consecrated archbishop after they arrived and that he later made a church at Zayton a cathedral [[cite:andrew]].
