[[person:josephus]] calls him in one place a man of Gamala in Gaulanitis and elsewhere "the Galilean". When [[person:quirinius]] registered property in Judea, Judas and a Pharisee named Zadok told the people that the assessment was a step into slavery and that they should have no ruler but God [[cite:antiquities]] [[cite:war]]; see [[event:judea-becomes-a-roman-province]]. Josephus, who is hostile to him, describes his followers as a fourth school beside the Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, agreeing with the Pharisees in everything except their refusal to call any man lord [[cite:antiquities]].

These passages do not tell how Judas died. In Acts the Pharisee Gamaliel says that he perished and that his followers were scattered [[bible:Acts 5:37]]. His family remained prominent among the rebels: two sons were crucified under the governor Tiberius Alexander [[cite:sons]], Menahem, another son, armed his followers from Herod's armoury at Masada and became a leader of the revolt in [[place:jerusalem]] [[cite:menahem]], and the commander of Masada at its fall was a descendant [[cite:masada]].
