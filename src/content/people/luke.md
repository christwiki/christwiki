Letters under [[person:paul]]'s name mention Luke three times: as "the beloved physician" who sends greetings, in a list of fellow workers, and as the only companion still with Paul [[bible:Col 4:14]] [[bible:Phlm 24]] [[bible:2 Tim 4:11]].

The third Gospel and Acts are both addressed to a Theophilus, and the second refers back to the first [[bible:Luke 1:3]] [[bible:Acts 1:1-2]]. Neither gives its author's name. The preface to the Gospel says that the writer worked from what had been handed down by those who were eyewitnesses from the beginning [[bible:Luke 1:1-4]]. Several stretches of Acts are told as "we", among them the voyage to Rome [[bible:Acts 16:10-17]] [[bible:Acts 20:5-15]] [[bible:Acts 27:1-28:16]].

Irenaeus, about AD 180, says that Luke, the companion of Paul, set down in a book the gospel that Paul preached, and he argues from the "we" passages that Luke was with Paul on his journeys [[cite:irenaeus-gospel]] [[cite:irenaeus-companion]]. Eusebius, in the early fourth century, adds that Luke's family was from Antioch [[cite:eusebius]]. The attribution rests on this tradition; see [[event:writing-of-the-gospels]].
