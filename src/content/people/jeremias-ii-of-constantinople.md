Jeremias wrote to the tsar from Smolensk in June 1588. In the letter, as the Russian record gives it, he says that he had been imprisoned by the unbeliever, had lately been released, and had come to seek help for his church [[cite:arrival]]. In Moscow he declined the tsar's proposal that he stay as patriarch at Vladimir, and on 26 January 1589 he installed [[person:job-of-moscow]] as patriarch of [[place:moscow]] [[cite:talks]].

The Russian charter of May 1589 gives him a speech calling the tsar's realm the Third Rome [[cite:charter-rome]]. The charter issued by his own council at [[place:constantinople]] a year later tells the story from his side: the tsar asked, he saw the greatness of the realm and its Orthodox ruler and consented, and the other patriarchs approved; it ranks Moscow after Jerusalem and under Constantinople as head [[cite:synod]].
