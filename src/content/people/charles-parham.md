The Los Angeles paper tells Parham's story as its readers knew it. He opened a Bible school at Topeka, Kansas, to train missionaries, with the Bible as its only textbook. Around New Year 1901 the students studied what the Bible gives as the evidence of the baptism with the Holy Ghost; a student, Agnes Ozman, spoke in tongues when hands were laid on her, and on 3 January twelve more did so [[cite:af2-p1]].

In September 1906 the paper called him "God's leader in the Apostolic Faith Movement" and reported a letter in which he said that he expected to come to [[place:los-angeles]] [[cite:af1-p1]]. In December it said that he was not the leader of the work at Azusa Street [[cite:af4-p1]]. See [[event:azusa-street-revival]].
