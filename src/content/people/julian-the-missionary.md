Julian is known here only from John of Ephesus, who admired him. He was a presbyter among the clergy around Theodosius, the patriarch of Alexandria whom the emperor had driven from his see, and he proposed to the empress [[person:theodora]] a mission to the people beyond the Thebaid. John tells how she contrived that he should reach the king of the Nobadae before the envoys of [[person:justinian-i]], and how the king accepted baptism from him and refused the emperor's faith. Julian stayed two years. He told afterwards how he sat in caves full of water from nine in the morning until four in the afternoon to escape the heat. He handed the converts to Theodore, bishop of Philae, and returned to Constantinople [[cite:mission]]. He had died shortly before Theodosius did [[cite:death]]. See [[event:conversion-of-nubia]].
