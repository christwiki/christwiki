The tome of 1341 says that Barlaam came from Calabria and trusted in his knowledge of secular philosophy, that he sought out hesychast monks as if to learn from them, then accused them in writing of claiming to share in God's essence, attacked their prayer and breathing exercises, and denounced [[person:gregory-palamas]] by name to the patriarch. The council found his charges blasphemous and ordered him to repent or be excommunicated [[cite:tome1341]].

In the sources cited here his teaching is reported only by his opponents. Palamas quotes him as holding that only the divine essence is uncreated and that everything else is of a created nature [[cite:triads]]. The council of 1351 says that he did not repent in his lifetime and anathematises him with Akindynos [[cite:tome1351]]. See [[event:hesychast-controversy]].
