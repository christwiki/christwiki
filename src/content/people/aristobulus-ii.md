Josephus, the main narrative source, calls Aristobulus active and bold, where his elder brother [[person:hyrcanus-ii]] preferred a quiet life [[cite:character]]. After their mother's death he defeated Hyrcanus near Jericho and became king and high priest [[cite:brothers]]. He reigned three years and three months [[cite:reign]].

When [[person:pompey]] marched on [[place:jerusalem]], Aristobulus offered to surrender the city, could not make his soldiers comply, and was arrested. After the temple mount fell he was taken to Rome with his children [[cite:arrest]]. Julius Caesar released him at the start of the civil war and meant to send him to Syria with two legions, but Pompey's supporters poisoned him first [[cite:death]]. That was in 49 BC. His son Antigonus was the last Hasmonean king; see [[event:herod-becomes-king]].
