Theophanes reckons Leo's reign from 25 March 717 to 18 June 741, twenty-four years, and says that he was succeeded by his son Constantine [[cite:reign]]. In the same writer's account Leo began to speak against the icons about 725, treated a volcanic eruption in 726 as a sign in his favour, and had the image of Christ taken down from the Chalke gate [[cite:first]]. In the dialogue Theophanes gives him with the patriarch Germanus, Leo says that his baptismal name was Konon [[cite:germanus]].

The Roman *Liber Pontificalis* reports that his officials tried to impose a tax on Italy and to remove Pope [[person:gregory-ii]], and that he then sent orders forbidding images [[cite:lp]]. The sources cited here preserve no statement of Leo's own reasons; in them he is known only through writers who condemned him. See [[event:iconoclasm-begins]].
