Coligny, called simply "the Admiral" in the sources, stood high with [[person:charles-ix-of-france]] in the summer of 1572. Marguerite de Valois says that the king esteemed him for his bravery and that he had won influence by proposing a campaign in Flanders; she also reports what was held against him: that he had caused the old duke of Guise to be killed, and that he had deprived the queen mother of a trusted servant by assassination [[cite:marguerite-4]].

On 22 August 1572 he was shot from a window in [[place:paris]] as he returned from court, losing a finger and taking two wounds in the arm [[cite:csp-538]]. At the start of the massacre armed men broke into his lodging. De Thou, drawing on the report of one of them, says that Coligny sent his companions away, told the man who came at him to respect his white hairs, and was run through; his body was thrown from the window, mutilated and hung on the public gibbet [[cite:de-thou]]. In October the Parlement of Paris declared him guilty of treason and of a conspiracy against the king and ordered his arms broken and his house razed [[cite:csp-622]].
