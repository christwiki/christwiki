The act by which the Russian Orthodox Church declared him a saint gives the outline of his life; its nineteenth-century dates are those of the Julian calendar then used in Russia. He was born John Popov-Veniaminov on 26 August 1797 in a village of Irkutsk province [[cite:birth]]. As a married priest he reached Unalaska on 29 July 1824, learned the local dialects, devised an alphabet for Aleut and translated the Gospel of Matthew [[cite:unalaska]]. After his wife's death he took monastic vows on 29 November 1840 with the name Innocent, and on 15 December was consecrated bishop of Kamchatka, the Kurile and Aleutian Islands, with his see at Sitka [[cite:bishop]]. He was appointed metropolitan of Moscow on 5 January 1868 [[cite:moscow]] and died on 31 March 1879 [[cite:death]]. He was numbered among the saints on 6 October 1977, at the request of the Orthodox Church in America [[cite:saint]].

His *Indication of the Way into the Kingdom of Heaven* teaches that the one way is the way Christ himself walked [[cite:way]]. In a letter of 1867 he tells how he invoked [[person:herman-of-alaska]] at sea off Spruce Island in 1842 [[cite:herman]]. See [[event:russian-mission-to-alaska]].
