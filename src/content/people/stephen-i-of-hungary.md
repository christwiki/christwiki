The older Life says that he was the son of the prince Géza, that Bishop Adalbert baptized him, and that he was given the name Stephen [[cite:baptism]]. Thietmar of Merseburg, writing in his lifetime, calls him Waic [[cite:thietmar]]. As ruler he defeated the pagans who rose against him, brought in clergy and monks, and set up ten bishoprics with [[place:esztergom]] at their head [[cite:rule]].

The Annals of Pressburg date his coronation to 1000 and his death to 1038 [[cite:annals]]. Thietmar credits the crown to the favour of the emperor Otto III [[cite:thietmar]]; the older Life speaks of letters of apostolic blessing [[cite:crowned]]; Hartvic, a century later, tells of a crown sent by the pope [[cite:hartvic]]. The older Life adds that he married Gisela, sister of the emperor Henry, placed his kingdom under the protection of the Virgin Mary and began a great church in her honour at Alba [[cite:crowned]]. The editor of the Lives notes that his body was solemnly raised in 1083, and that the Lives were written for the cult that followed [[cite:raised]]. See [[event:stephen-of-hungary]].
