Joshua son of Nun, of the tribe of Ephraim, was first called Hoshea; Moses gave him the name Joshua [[bible:Num 13:8]] [[bible:Num 13:16]]. He commanded the fighting men against Amalek soon after the exodus [[bible:Exod 17:8-14]] and served as Moses' attendant at Sinai [[bible:Exod 24:13]] [[bible:Exod 33:11]]. Of the twelve scouts sent into Canaan, only he and [[person:caleb]] urged the people to go in, and only they of that generation lived to enter the land [[bible:Num 14:6-9]] [[bible:Num 14:30]]. [[person:moses]] laid hands on him and named him as his successor [[bible:Num 27:18-23]] [[bible:Deut 34:9]].

The book of Joshua tells how he led the crossing of the Jordan and the capture of [[place:jericho]] [[bible:Josh 3:14-17]] [[bible:Josh 6:20]], gave the land to the tribes [[bible:Josh 11:23]] and gathered them at [[place:shechem]] to renew the covenant [[bible:Josh 24:1-28]]. He died aged 110 and was buried at Timnath-serah in the hill country of Ephraim [[bible:Josh 24:29-30]]. The Bible does not say how long he led Israel; Josephus, writing in the first century AD, gives twenty-five years [[cite:josephus]]. The letter to the Hebrews argues that the rest into which Joshua led Israel was not the final one [[bible:Heb 4:8]].
