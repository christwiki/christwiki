Pole came to England as legate of Pope Julius III, sent, in the words of Mary's Parliament, "to call us home again into the right way" [[cite:repeal]]. At the end of November 1554 the Lords and Commons presented their petition to be reconciled, and Pole pronounced the absolution of the realm from heresy and schism; John Foxe, no friend of his, prints the words [[cite:absolution]].

He was consecrated archbishop of Canterbury at Greenwich on 22 March 1556, the day after [[person:thomas-cranmer]] was burned [[cite:consecration]], and held the see through the rest of the burnings described in [[event:marian-persecutions]]. He died on 17 November 1558, the same day as [[person:mary-i-of-england]]: Alvise Priuli, of his household, who was with him, wrote ten days later that the queen died seven hours after midnight and the cardinal at seven that evening [[cite:priuli]]. Other reports put his death later: the diarist Henry Machyn enters it, at Lambeth, under 19 November [[cite:death]], and Foxe says he died the day after the queen [[cite:foxe-death]].
