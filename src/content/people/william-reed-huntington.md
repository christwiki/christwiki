Huntington was rector of All Saints, Worcester, when he published *The Church-Idea: An Essay towards Unity* in 1870 [[cite:title]]. He wrote that he did not favour imposing the whole Anglican system on America, but believed the Anglican principle to be America's best hope [[cite:principle]]. That principle, he said, lies "foursquare": the Holy Scriptures as the Word of God, the primitive creeds as the rule of faith, the two sacraments ordained by Christ himself, and the episcopate as the keystone of governmental unity [[cite:four]]. He called these four points "the Quadrilateral" of pure Anglicanism [[cite:four]].

Sixteen years later the bishops of the Episcopal Church named four things as essential to the restoration of unity: the Scriptures, the Nicene Creed, the two sacraments and the historic episcopate [[cite:chicago]]. See [[event:lambeth-quadrilateral]].
