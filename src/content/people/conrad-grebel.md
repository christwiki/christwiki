Grebel's name stands first under the letter that he and his friends sent from [[place:zurich]] to [[person:thomas-muntzer]] in September 1524. It rejects infant baptism and the use of the sword in defence of the gospel, and in it Grebel says that he means to write against infant baptism himself [[cite:letter]]. On 21 January 1525 the council ordered him and [[person:felix-manz]] to stop disputing and to accept its decision [[cite:mandates]].

The chronicle of the Hutterian Brethren calls him a man of noble family, learned in Latin, Greek and Hebrew, and says that when [[person:george-blaurock]] asked him for baptism he gave it, there being no ordained minister to do so [[cite:chronicle]]. See [[event:anabaptists]].
