Bede calls Columba a priest and abbot, "marked as a monk by habit and manner of life", who had founded the monastery of Durrow in Ireland before he crossed to Britain to preach to the northern Picts [[cite:bede]]. The Annals of Ulster enter his voyage to Iona under 563, in his forty-second year, which puts his birth about 521 [[cite:annals]]. Adomnán says that he sailed about two years after the battle of Cúl Dreimne [[cite:sailing]].

He remained abbot of [[place:iona]] until his death. Adomnán gives him thirty-four years in Britain and describes his last day in detail: he was copying a psalter, stopped at the foot of a page, and died in the church at midnight as Sunday began [[cite:death]]. The annals give the day as 9 June [[cite:annals]]; the year usually given is 597 ([[event:columba-on-iona]]). Bede adds that he was never a bishop, and that for this reason the abbots of Iona, who were priests, had authority even over bishops [[cite:bede]].
