Clement dated his letters of 1348 and 1349 from [[place:avignon]], in the seventh and eighth years of his pontificate [[cite:jews]] [[cite:flagellants]]. A Life quoted by the annalist Raynaldus says that during the plague he appointed physicians to visit the poor of the city and bought a field for a cemetery [[cite:life]]. A Paris chronicler records that he gave the dying, in many towns, absolution from guilt and penalty through their confessors [[cite:absolution]], and the English chronicler Knighton that a count of the dead at Avignon was made before him and that he wrote to the kings urging peace, since these things had come upon men for their sins [[cite:knighton]].

Twice in 1348 he forbade violence against the Jews, the second time arguing that they could not have caused a plague from which they themselves were dying [[cite:jews]]. In October 1349 he ordered the bishops to put down the flagellant companies [[cite:flagellants]]. His bull of 1343 announcing the jubilee sets out the doctrine of the treasury of merits [[cite:jubilee]]. See [[event:black-death]].
