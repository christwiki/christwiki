Philip, landgrave of Hesse, is named among the princes who appealed against the majority at [[place:speyer]] in April 1529 [[cite:princes]]; see [[event:protestation-at-speyer]]. That autumn he summoned the reformers to [[place:marburg]], attended the debate from beginning to end, and afterwards sent for the participants one by one to seek a compromise [[cite:osiander]]. [[person:martin-luther]] wrote that the landgrave was making every effort to unite them, or at least to have them regard each other as brothers [[cite:wife]]. [[person:huldrych-zwingli]] claimed that Philip had come over to his side, though he did not show it in public [[cite:zwingli]]. See [[event:marburg-colloquy]].

His name stands among the signatures of the confession presented at [[place:augsburg]] in 1530 [[cite:augsburg]].
