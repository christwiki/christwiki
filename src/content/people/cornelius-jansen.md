The title page of his book names him Cornelius Jansen, bishop of Ypres, and gives Louvain and the year 1640 as the place and date of printing [[cite:title]]. His will, printed in the same volume, is dated 6 May 1638 and is said in its heading to have been dictated scarcely half an hour before he died. In it he leaves his writings on Augustine to be published as faithfully as possible and declares himself an obedient son of the Roman See and of the church in which he had always lived [[cite:will]].

In 1656 Pope Alexander VII defined that five propositions condemned three years earlier had been taken from his *Augustinus* and condemned in the sense he intended [[cite:ad-sanctam]]. [[person:blaise-pascal]] answered for his defenders that they condemned the propositions but could not admit that Jansen had taught them [[cite:pascal]]. See [[event:jansenism]].
