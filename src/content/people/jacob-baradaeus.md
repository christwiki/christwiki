The account of Jacob given here comes from John of Ephesus, who was consecrated by him and writes as his admirer. John says that he came from Tella, was trained in the monastery of Fsiltha, and lived for about fifteen years as a recluse at [[place:constantinople]] under the protection of the empress [[person:theodora]]; the nickname Burd'ana, his editor notes, comes from the ragged cloak he wore [[cite:life-49]]. In the sixteenth year of Justinian he was made bishop for [[place:edessa]], at Theodora's order and at the request of Harith, king of the Saracens. With other bishops he then ordained, by John's count, twenty-seven bishops and two patriarchs of Antioch [[cite:life-50]], and John credits him with the ordination of a hundred thousand clergy between the Persian frontier and Alexandria [[cite:life-49]].

In his *Ecclesiastical History* John shows another side: an old man of great zeal and simplicity who was managed by those around him, and whose break with the patriarch Paul split his church [[cite:character]]. He died at the monastery of Cassian on the border of Egypt, on his way to Alexandria [[cite:death]]; a note in one manuscript of a later *Life*, which John's editor prints as spurious, puts his death in the year 889 of the Greeks, which is 577–578, and the heading of that *Life* in another manuscript says that the "Jacobites" were named after him [[cite:note]].
