Wesley says that he preached by the authority conveyed to him when the bishop who became archbishop of Canterbury laid hands on him [[cite:nash]]. He sailed for Georgia in October 1735 [[cite:voyage]], and his Journal for 24 May 1738 records the evening in Aldersgate Street in [[place:london]] when he "felt I did trust in Christ, Christ alone, for salvation" [[cite:aldersgate]]. On 2 April 1739 he began to preach in the open air near [[place:bristol]] [[cite:bristol]], and he defended preaching outside any parish of his own with the words "I look upon all the world as my parish" [[cite:parish]].

The rules of the United Societies, which he wrote in the first person and signed with his brother [[person:charles-wesley]] on 1 May 1743, describe the weekly meetings he began in London at the end of 1739 and the classes into which each society was divided [[cite:rules]].
