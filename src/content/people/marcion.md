Every report about Marcion comes from an opponent. Justin, who wrote at [[place:rome]] while Marcion was still alive, calls him "a man of Pontus" who taught his disciples to believe in a god greater than the creator [[cite:justin]]. Irenaeus says that he flourished when Anicetus was bishop of Rome [[cite:anicetus]] and describes his Gospel as a cut-down Luke [[cite:irenaeus]]. Tertullian, two generations later, calls him a shipmaster, says that he gave money to the Roman church and had it returned when he was expelled, and adds that he sought to be reconciled before he died [[cite:prescription]]. Tertullian also refers to a letter of Marcion's own, now lost, as proof that he had first shared the faith of the church [[cite:letter]].

Churches that named him or his followers as their founders outlived him; Tertullian, early in the third century, speaks of them as still existing [[cite:churches]]. See [[event:marcion]].
