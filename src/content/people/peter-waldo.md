The two twelfth-century writers cited here give him one name only: the Chronicle of Laon calls him Valdesius [[cite:laon-1173-latin]] and Walter Map calls him Valdes, a citizen of [[place:lyon]] [[cite:map]]. The name Peter, by which he is usually known in English, does not occur in either passage. The Waldensian Church calls him Valdo [[cite:church]].

The Chronicle of Laon tells how he gave up a fortune made by usury, provided for his wife and daughters, fed the poor and took to begging [[cite:laon-1173]], and how the pope embraced him at the Lateran council, approved his poverty and forbade him to preach except at the request of priests [[cite:laon-1178]].
