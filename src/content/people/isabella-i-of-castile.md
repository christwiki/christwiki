Andrés Bernáldez, a priest of her own time, calls her the daughter of King Juan II of Castile and records her marriage at Valladolid in 1469 to [[person:ferdinand-ii-of-aragon]], when she was princess of Castile and León and he king of Sicily and prince of Aragon [[cite:lineage]].

The bull of 1 November 1478 answers a petition made in the names of both and is addressed to them as king and queen of Castile and León [[cite:bull]]. The commission of 27 September 1480 that named two Dominicans as inquisitors runs in both names and is signed "I the King, I the Queen" [[cite:commission]]. When [[person:sixtus-iv]] wrote in January 1482 of the complaints from [[place:seville]], he wrote to both [[cite:brief]]. The Seville Instructions of 1484 call [[person:tomas-de-torquemada]] the confessor of the king and queen and inquisitor general in their name [[cite:instructions]]. The edict of 31 March 1492, which ordered the Jews to leave, carries both signatures again [[cite:edict]].

Bernáldez writes that she died at Medina del Campo in November 1504 and was carried to [[place:granada]] for burial. Among her merits he counts that through her the heresy of the judaizers, as he calls it, was burned and destroyed [[cite:death]]. See [[event:spanish-inquisition]].
