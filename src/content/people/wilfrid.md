Wilfrid entered the monastery of Lindisfarne at fourteen, then travelled to Rome and spent time at Lyons, where he received the Roman tonsure [[cite:life]] [[cite:synod]]. King Alchfrid gave him the monastery of Ripon, from which monks of the Irish observance had withdrawn. At the [[event:synod-of-whitby]] he spoke for the Roman reckoning of Easter, and shortly afterwards he was chosen bishop [[cite:synod]] [[cite:stephen]].

Bede tells how he was twice put out of his bishopric by Northumbrian kings and twice went to Rome to plead his case before the pope [[cite:life]]. He says that Wilfrid was a bishop for forty-five years and died in the district of Oundle in the year that King Coenred of Mercia left for Rome, which Bede dates to 709 [[cite:recap]]; he was buried in his church of St Peter at Ripon [[cite:life]]. His follower Stephen wrote his Life.
