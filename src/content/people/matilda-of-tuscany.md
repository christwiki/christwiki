Lampert of Hersfeld introduces her as the daughter of Margrave Boniface and Countess Beatrice and the widow of a duke of Lorraine. A great part of Italy obeyed her, he says, and after her husband's death she was almost always at the side of [[person:gregory-vii]] and served him as a father or lord. He adds that the king's supporters spread a slander about the two, and that no sensible person believed it [[cite:lampert]].

By Lampert's account it was at her urging that Gregory withdrew to [[place:canossa]], and it was she whom [[person:henry-iv-emperor]] sent to the pope, with others, to plead for him [[cite:lampert]]. Gregory names her among those from whom he took guarantees for the king [[cite:canossa]]. Bernold of Constance, writing of the year 1095, names "the lady Matilda" first among the faithful of St Peter in Lombardy [[cite:bernold]]. Fulcher of Chartres credits her with helping [[person:urban-ii]] to gain control of Rome [[cite:fulcher]].
