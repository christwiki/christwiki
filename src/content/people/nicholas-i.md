Nicholas held that the Roman see had the right to judge the dispute over the see of [[place:constantinople]]. A synod under him stripped [[person:photius]] of priestly office, punished the legates who had sided with him and upheld [[person:ignatius-of-constantinople]] [[cite:sentence]]. In 866 he answered the questions of the newly baptized Bulgarian ruler and told him that Constantinople was a patriarchate by imperial favour and not by apostolic foundation [[cite:bulgars]]. A letter of 23 October 867 lists the charges the Greeks were now bringing against Latin practice and teaching [[cite:charges]].

The council of 869–870 praised him and likened his sentence on Photius to Peter's judgement on Ananias [[cite:council]]. See [[event:photian-schism]].
