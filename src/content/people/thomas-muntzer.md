In July 1524 Müntzer preached before the dukes of Saxony at the castle of Allstedt and printed the sermon. It told them that the sword was theirs to root out the godless and would be taken from them if they did not use it [[cite:sermon]]. That September [[person:conrad-grebel]] and his friends at [[place:zurich]] wrote to him at Allstedt as to a brother and told him that the gospel and its adherents are not to be protected with the sword [[cite:grebel]].

[[person:martin-luther]] blamed the rising of 1525 above all on the man he called the archdevil who ruled at Mühlhausen [[cite:against]]. Müntzer was taken with the rebel army at [[place:frankenhausen]], and a confession taken from him was printed with the date 16 May 1525; the library that holds the pamphlet describes it as obtained under torture [[cite:confession]]. Luther asked for news of his capture a week later; the editors of Luther's letters give 27 May as the day of his execution at Mühlhausen [[cite:ruhel]]. See [[event:peasants-war]].
