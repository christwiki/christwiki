Jerome's Chronicle records that Ambrose was made bishop of [[place:milan]] after the death of the previous bishop, Auxentius [[cite:chronicle]]. The letter of the eastern bishops in 382 names him next after Damasus among the bishops of the West [[cite:letter382]]. [[person:augustine]] went to hear him preach, was received by him "like a father", and was baptised by him [[cite:augustine]] [[cite:baptism]]; he also tells how the people of Milan kept watch in the church with their bishop when the empress Justina, who favoured the Arians, moved against him [[cite:justina]]. See [[event:conversion-of-augustine]].

After the massacre at Thessalonica Ambrose wrote privately to [[person:theodosius-i]], urging him to repent and declining to offer the eucharist in his presence until he did [[cite:letter]] (see [[event:ambrose-and-theodosius]]). Jerome, writing in 392–393, notes that Ambrose was still alive and still writing, and for that reason withholds his judgment of him [[cite:catalogue]].
