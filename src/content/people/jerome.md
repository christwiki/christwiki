Jerome names himself as the son of Eusebius, from the town of Stridon on the border of Dalmatia and Pannonia [[cite:catalogue]]. He came to [[place:rome]] with two eastern bishops, helped its bishop [[person:damasus-i]] with his correspondence, and at his request revised the Latin Gospels against the Greek [[cite:arrival]] [[cite:secretary]] [[cite:gospels]]. After Damasus died he left for the East [[cite:departure]] and settled at [[place:bethlehem]] with [[person:paula]], where he translated the Old Testament from the Hebrew [[cite:progress]]. See [[event:jerome-and-the-vulgate]].

He held that the books absent from the Hebrew canon are read in church but are not canonical [[cite:helmeted]] [[cite:solomon]], a view that differed from that of the African councils of his day (see [[event:councils-of-hippo-and-carthage]]). His letters record how the news of [[event:sack-of-rome|the fall of Rome]] reached Bethlehem [[cite:rome]]. Prosper's chronicle dates his death to 30 September 420 [[cite:death]].
