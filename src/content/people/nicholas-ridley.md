John Foxe, who admired him, says that Ridley was born in Northumberland, studied at Cambridge and became head of Pembroke Hall there, served [[person:henry-viii]] as chaplain and was made bishop of Rochester, and was moved to the see of London in the days of [[person:edward-vi]] [[cite:life]].

Under [[person:mary-i-of-england]] he was burned as a heretic at [[place:oxford]] on 16 October 1555 together with [[person:hugh-latimer]]. At the stake, Foxe reports, he was told he might live if he recanted and refused, and he asked Lord Williams to plead with the queen for the poor tenants to whom he had granted leases as bishop. His fire was badly built and he died slowly [[cite:death]]. See [[event:marian-persecutions]].
