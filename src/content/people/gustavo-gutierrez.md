Gustavo Gutiérrez was born in Lima on 8 June 1928 and ordained priest in 1959. He taught at the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru and later at the University of Notre Dame, was closely involved in the conference of Latin American bishops of 1968, and founded the Instituto Bartolomé de las Casas in Lima in 1974. He joined the Dominican order in 2001 and died in Lima on 22 October 2024. The archive that holds his papers describes him as widely recognised as the father of liberation theology [[cite:note]].

In an article of 1970 he defined theology as critical reflection on the church's presence and action in the world, coming after commitment and not before it [[cite:gut-244]]. He preferred the word "liberation" to "development" and gave it three levels of meaning: the political liberation of oppressed peoples and classes, the liberation of humanity in the course of history, and liberation from sin [[cite:gut-247]]. His book on the subject appeared in 1971 [[cite:note]]. See [[event:medellin-and-liberation-theology]].
