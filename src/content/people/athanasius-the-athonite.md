Athanasius tells his own story in the rule he wrote for his monastery. He had hoped to live alone, he says, and thought only of his own salvation; the general [[person:nikephoros-ii-phokas]], his friend from Kyminas, pressed him to build a house where the general might end his days as a monk, and when Nikephoros became emperor instead Athanasius went to the capital to reproach him [[cite:story]].

Some years later the leader of the Athonite monks complained of him to the emperor John Tzimiskes. The inquiry cleared both sides, and Athanasius signed the resulting charter as superior of the Great Lavra [[cite:inquiry]]. See [[event:mount-athos]].
