Eadmer, who knew him, says that Anselm became a monk at [[place:bec]] in his twenty-seventh year, was made prior three years later when Lanfranc left for Caen, and was elected abbot by the whole community when the founder, Herluin, died [[cite:eadmer-bec]] [[cite:eadmer-abbot]]. At Bec he wrote the Monologion and the Proslogion [[cite:preface]] [[cite:eadmer-proslogion]].

In 1093 King William II, lying gravely ill, gave him the archbishopric of [[place:canterbury]] [[cite:asc-1093]]. He went abroad in 1097 [[cite:asc-1097]], finished Cur Deus Homo in exile [[cite:cdh-preface]], returned in 1100 at the invitation of Henry I [[cite:asc-1100]], left again for Rome in 1103 [[cite:asc-1103]], and died in 1109 [[cite:asc-1109]].
