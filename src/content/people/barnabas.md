---
name: Barnabas
altNames: [Joseph Barnabas]
role: Missionary; companion of Paul
summary: A Levite from Cyprus and an early member of the Jerusalem church. He introduced Paul to the apostles, taught in the church at Antioch with him, and shared his first missionary journey before the two parted over John Mark.
---

His name was Joseph, or Joses; the apostles called him Barnabas, which Acts translates "Son of Encouragement". He was a Levite from Cyprus who sold a field and gave the money to the apostles [[bible:Acts 4:36-37]]. He vouched for the newly converted Saul in [[place:jerusalem|Jerusalem]], was sent by the Jerusalem church to [[place:antioch]], and fetched Saul from Tarsus to work with him there [[bible:Acts 9:27]] [[bible:Acts 11:22-26]]. The two carried famine relief to Judea and were then sent out together on the [[event:first-missionary-journey|first missionary journey]], where Acts calls them both apostles [[bible:Acts 11:30]] [[bible:Acts 14:14]].

Acts has Barnabas go up with [[person:paul]] to the [[event:council-of-jerusalem]] [[bible:Acts 15:2]]. Paul, telling of a visit to Jerusalem with Barnabas, names him with himself as receiving "the right hand of fellowship" for the mission to the Gentiles [[bible:Gal 2:1, 9]]. Paul also records that at Antioch Barnabas followed [[person:peter|Peter]] in withdrawing from meals with Gentiles [[bible:Gal 2:13]]. Acts says that the two separated over whether to take [[person:john-mark]] on a second journey, and that Barnabas sailed with Mark to Cyprus [[bible:Acts 15:36-39]]; the letter to the Colossians calls a Mark the cousin of Barnabas [[bible:Col 4:10]]. Paul also names him as one who, like himself, worked for his own living [[bible:1 Cor 9:6]].
