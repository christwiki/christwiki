Socrates says that Gratian, once in possession of the empire, condemned the policy of his uncle Valens towards the Nicene Christians, recalled the bishops whom Valens had exiled and allowed every Christian group except the Eunomians, Photinians and Manichaeans to meet freely. Then, with the barbarians pressing on the frontiers, he proclaimed [[person:theodosius-i]] emperor at Sirmium in January 379 and divided the war with him [[cite:accession]]. His name stands first in the heading of the edict issued at Thessalonica in February 380 [[cite:edict]] (see [[event:edict-of-thessalonica]]).

In 383 Maximus rebelled in Britain and attacked him. Gratian was caught near Lyon by one of Maximus's generals and killed, in the twenty-fourth year of his age and the fifteenth of his reign [[cite:death]].
