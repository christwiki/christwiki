Agathangelos, the fifth-century source for his reign, says that Tiridates was the son of King Chosroes, was taken to the Roman empire as a child when his father was murdered and the Persians overran Armenia, served in the Roman army, and returned with a great army to recover his kingdom [[cite:restored]]. He ordered his subjects to denounce Christians and had the nun Hripsime and her companions put to death at [[place:vagharshapat]] [[cite:persecutor]]. The story goes on that he lost his reason and took on the likeness of a beast until [[person:gregory-the-illuminator]] was fetched from the pit and prayed for him [[cite:madness]]. He then joined Gregory in destroying the pagan shrines [[cite:shrines]] and was baptised with his court in the Euphrates [[cite:baptism]].

Eusebius, a contemporary, does not name him. He says only that the Armenians were Christians and allies of Rome when the emperor Maximinus made war on them, about 312 [[cite:eusebius]].
