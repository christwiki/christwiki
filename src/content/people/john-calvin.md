Calvin tells his own story in the preface to his commentary on the Psalms. His father first meant him for theology and then set him to study law; God, he says, "by a sudden conversion" brought his mind to a teachable frame; he left France and was living in retirement at [[place:basel]] when he published the *Institutes* [[cite:memoir]], with an address to King Francis I in defence of those persecuted in France [[cite:address]].

Passing through [[place:geneva]], he was kept there by [[person:guillaume-farel]] [[cite:memoir]]. The city's council banished both men in April 1538 [[cite:banished]] and received Calvin back in September 1541 [[cite:return]]; see [[event:calvin-in-geneva]]. In 1553 the ministers had Michael Servetus imprisoned for heresy, and the council condemned him to be burned [[cite:servetus]]. In the same preface Calvin writes of years of conflict in Geneva with those he calls libertines, and of attacks on his teaching on providence and predestination [[cite:memoir]].
