What is told of him here comes from the history that his fellow Jesuit Balthazar Tellez compiled, in part from Páez's own manuscript. Tellez calls him a Castilian and says that he was chosen at Goa, with one other father, to go to Ethiopia [[cite:origin]]. The two were taken on the way, and Tellez speaks of Páez's "7 Years Captivity" in Arabia before he tried again [[cite:captivity]]. He landed at Massawa in April 1603 and reached Fremona on 15 May [[cite:arrival]].

Tellez says that in 1621 Páez built a church of squared stone, which [[person:susenyos]] travelled two days to see and entered barefoot; that in 1622 the emperor publicly professed "the Latin Faith"; and that Páez, "who had converted, and receiv'd him into the Church", died soon afterwards at [[place:gorgora]], after nineteen years in the mission, leaving a written account of Ethiopian affairs [[cite:death]].
