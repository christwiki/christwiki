King was born in Atlanta, Georgia, on 15 January 1929 [[cite:facts]]. He became pastor of the Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama, in 1954, was elected president of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference in 1957, and from 1960 served with his father as co-pastor of the Ebenezer Baptist Church in Atlanta [[cite:life]].

In April 1963 he was jailed in [[place:birmingham-alabama]] after leading the Good Friday march [[cite:good-friday]]. From the jail he wrote the letter in which he explains why he had come to the city [[cite:letter-1]] and speaks, as a minister who loves the church, of his disappointment with the white churches of the South [[cite:letter-7]]; see [[event:letter-from-birmingham-jail]]. He received the Nobel Peace Prize for 1964, awarded for his non-violent struggle for civil rights [[cite:facts]]. He was assassinated in Memphis, Tennessee, on 4 April 1968 [[cite:death]].
