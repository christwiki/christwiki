The Lateran Treaty of 11 February 1929 was made in his name: by it the Holy See declared the Roman question settled and Italy recognised the State of Vatican City under the pope's sovereignty [[cite:treaty]]. Speaking that day, on the eve of the seventh anniversary of his coronation, he said that he had wanted the concordat joined to the treaty from the first [[cite:address-11]], and two days later that he believed he had given God back to Italy and Italy to God [[cite:address-13]]. He remarked in the same address that the task had perhaps needed a pope who was a mountaineer and a librarian [[cite:address-13]].

In *Mit brennender Sorge* (14 March 1937) he wrote that he had consented to the concordat with the German Reich in 1933 despite grave misgivings, protested that it was being violated, and condemned the raising of race, people or state to an idol [[cite:mbs-3]]. Five days later, in *Divini Redemptoris*, he condemned atheistic communism [[cite:divini]]. See [[event:lateran-treaty]] and [[event:mit-brennender-sorge]].
