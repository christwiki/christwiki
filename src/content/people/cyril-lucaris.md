The Synod of Jerusalem calls him "Cyril Lucar of Crete" and says that he became pope of Alexandria and was then translated to the throne of Constantinople [[cite:claims]] [[cite:career]]. The confession that bears his name is dated at Constantinople in January 1631 [[cite:text]].

Who wrote that confession was disputed. The synod of 1672 says that Cyril denied the chapters on oath many times, that no undisputed work of his contains their teaching, and that a synod at Constantinople nevertheless anathematised him by name because he never wrote against them [[cite:claims]] [[cite:career]] [[cite:earlier]]. The same synod judges him harshly on other grounds: it says that he gained the throne of Constantinople four times, three of them unlawfully and with the help of the Dutch ambassador, and that he was put to death, and it refuses to count him a saint [[cite:death]].
