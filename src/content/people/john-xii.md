A chronicler close to Otto's court calls him John, son of Alberic, and says that he promised never to desert the emperor, a promise with a very different outcome [[cite:coronation]]. Liudprand of Cremona tells how he appealed to Otto against Berengar, anointed him emperor and then sought the help of Berengar's son [[cite:appeal]].

Almost everything else known of him comes from his accusers. Liudprand reports the charges made at the Roman synod, from simony and perjury to hunting and drinking the devil's health, and the sentence of deposition [[cite:synod]]. John returned to Rome when the emperor left and died soon after; Liudprand says that the devil struck him down [[cite:death]]. See [[event:otto-the-great]].
