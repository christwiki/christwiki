William Roper names "Secretary Cromwell" among the commissioners at Lambeth before whom [[person:thomas-more]] was called to take the oath [[cite:lambeth]]. An Act of 1539 on the seating of the House of Lords says that the king, as supreme head of the Church of England, had made Thomas Lord Cromwell, lord privy seal, his vicegerent for the administration of justice in all ecclesiastical causes and for the reform of errors, heresies and abuses, and it places him above the archbishop of Canterbury [[cite:vicegerent]]. See [[event:act-of-supremacy]].

A London chronicler records that Cromwell, by then earl of Essex, was beheaded on Tower Hill on 28 July 1540 [[cite:death]].
