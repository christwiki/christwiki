Hosius was bishop of Cordoba. A letter of [[person:constantine]] to [[person:caecilian]] of Carthage about a grant of money, written at the start of the Donatist dispute, refers to a schedule sent by one Hosius, whom the translator's note identifies with the bishop of Cordoba [[cite:africa]]. Constantine sent him to [[place:alexandria]] with the letter urging [[person:alexander-of-alexandria]] and [[person:arius]] to make peace [[cite:envoy]]. At the [[event:council-of-nicaea]] Socrates names him first among those present [[cite:nicaea]], and Eusebius, without naming him, mentions a bishop of wide fame who came from Spain [[cite:eusebius]].

Under Constantius II he was exiled and then brought to a council at Sirmium, where, Socrates says, the old man was beaten until he signed its statement of faith [[cite:sirmium]].
