Acts speaks of "John who was called Mark". His mother Mary had a house in [[place:jerusalem]] where the believers met, and to which [[person:peter]] went on his escape from prison [[bible:Acts 12:12]]. He went with [[person:barnabas]] and [[person:paul]] to Cyprus as their assistant, but left them at Perga and returned to Jerusalem [[bible:Acts 13:5, 13]]. Paul refused to take him again, and the quarrel that followed parted Paul and Barnabas; Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus [[bible:Acts 15:37-39]]. See [[event:first-missionary-journey]].

Letters in Paul's name mention a Mark who is the cousin of Barnabas, a fellow worker, and "useful" to him [[bible:Col 4:10]] [[bible:Phlm 24]] [[bible:2 Tim 4:11]], and 1 Peter sends greetings from "Mark, my son" [[bible:1 Pet 5:13]]. Papias, a bishop of the early second century quoted by [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]], reports a tradition that Mark became Peter's interpreter and wrote down what he remembered of the Lord's words and deeds [[cite:papias]]. The New Testament does not say that all these references are to one man, though they have generally been read so. On the Gospel see [[event:writing-of-the-gospels]].
