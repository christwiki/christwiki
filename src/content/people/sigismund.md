Sigismund styles himself king of the Romans and king of Hungary in the safe-conduct he issued for [[person:jan-hus]] at Speyer on 18 October 1414, which took Hus under his own and the empire's protection [[cite:safe-conduct]]. The Council of Constance calls him its defender and protector and looked to him to see its decisions carried out [[cite:protector]].

Hus came to think that the king had abandoned him and wrote that his word was no more to be trusted than his safe-conduct [[cite:letter63]]. According to Peter of Mladoňovice, Sigismund presided, wearing his crown, at the session of 6 July 1415; Hus reminded the assembly that he had come with the safe-conduct of the king there present, and when the sentence had been passed it was Sigismund who told Duke Ludwig to take him [[cite:burning]].
