Einhard calls him "Albin of Britain, surnamed Alcuin", a deacon of Saxon, that is English, descent, the greatest scholar of his day, and the king's teacher in rhetoric, dialectic and astronomy [[cite:einhard]]. At the synod of [[place:frankfurt]] in 794 [[person:charlemagne]] asked the bishops to receive him into their fellowship and prayers as a man learned in the teaching of the church [[cite:frankfurt]].

His own letters show him at Tours, in "the house of St Martin", teaching Scripture, the liberal arts, grammar and the stars, and asking to send pupils to York for the books he had used there under his master [[cite:tours]]. In 800 he wrote that the king had set him to correct the text of the Old and New Testament [[cite:bible]]. See [[event:carolingian-renewal]].
