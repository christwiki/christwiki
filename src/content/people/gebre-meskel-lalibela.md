The Synaxarium of the Ethiopian church calls him Lalibala, surnamed Gabra Masqal, king of Ethiopia. It tells that his elder brother, the king, had him beaten out of jealousy, that an angel kept him from harm, that he inherited the kingdom, gave much to the poor, built ten churches as an angel showed him in a dream, and left the throne to his brother's son [[cite:synaxarium]]. His Life adds the vision of the churches in heaven and the building of their likeness on earth with the help of angels [[cite:life-vision]] [[cite:life-building]].

Jules Perruchon, who edited the Life, reports a near-contemporary notice in the Arabic History of the Patriarchs of Alexandria, where a metropolitan ordained in 1210 finds reigning in Ethiopia a king whose name Ignazio Guidi read as Lalibala [[cite:perruchon-reign]]. In the 1520s Francisco Álvares was told at [[place:lalibela]] that the name means "miracle", that bees had covered the king at his birth without harming him, and that he was a saint whose tomb drew pilgrims [[cite:alvares]].
