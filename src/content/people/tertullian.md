The main ancient account of Tertullian's life is Jerome's notice, written about 393. It says that he came from [[place:carthage]], that his father was a centurion, that he was a presbyter, that he flourished under Severus and Caracalla and lived to a great age, and that in middle life, provoked by the Roman clergy, he "lapsed to the doctrine of Montanus". Jerome adds a story he heard from an old man: Cyprian, bishop of Carthage a generation later, read Tertullian every day and would ask for him with the words "Give me the master" [[cite:jerome]].

Tertullian's own writings confirm two points. He was writing the first book of *Against Marcion* in the fifteenth year of Severus, AD 207–208 [[cite:marcion]], and in his later works he sides openly with the New Prophecy, saying that he "withdrew" from its opponents [[cite:paraclete]]. Neither his birth nor his death can be dated. See [[event:tertullian]] and [[event:montanism]].
