Manz signed the letter of September 1524 with [[person:conrad-grebel]] [[cite:letter]]. A witness told the council of [[place:zurich]] in February 1525 that Manz had baptised two men in a house at Zollikon [[cite:zollikon]].

The sentence of 5 January 1527 calls him a chief beginner of rebaptism. It says that he had been shown from Scripture that infant baptism is right, had been released on oath, and had then baptised a woman; that he taught that no Christian may be a magistrate or punish with the sword; and that he and his followers had separated from the Christian community and set up a sect. He was to be bound, taken out in a boat and thrown into the water, and his property went to the city [[cite:sentence]]. The *Martyrs Mirror* preserves an exhortation attributed to him and places his death in 1526 [[cite:mirror]]. See [[event:anabaptists]].
