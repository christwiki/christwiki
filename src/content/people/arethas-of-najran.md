The letter of Simeon of Beth Arsham calls him Harith son of Khanab, a venerable old man and the chief of the leading men of [[place:najran]]. As Simeon heard it from a messenger sent to the town, Harith told the king that he had warned his companions not to trust the king's oath, that he had lived long, had children and grandchildren and had won renown in war, and that he would not deny Christ to become a Jew. He asked his companions whether any of them feared the sword, left part of his estate to the church that would be rebuilt, made the sign over them and was the first to be beheaded [[cite:letter]].

The *Book of the Himyarites* gives the day as Sunday, 25 November, and lists him first among the "freeborn men" of Najran who died, with the names that those who buried them could remember [[cite:book]]. The Greek account of the persecution bears his name, the *Martyrdom of Arethas* [[cite:greek]]. For the year, 523 or 518, see [[event:martyrs-of-najran]] [[cite:dates]].
