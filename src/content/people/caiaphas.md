Josephus says that the Roman governor Valerius Gratus, after appointing and removing several high priests within a few years, gave the office to Joseph Caiaphas, and that Gratus was then succeeded by [[person:pontius-pilate]] [[cite:appointed]]. Caiaphas held the office until Vitellius, the governor of Syria, deposed "Joseph, who was also called Caiaphas" about the time Pilate was recalled [[cite:removed]]. The years usually given are about AD 18 to 36. Neither passage connects him with Jesus.

In the New Testament he is the son-in-law of Annas, a former high priest who is still named beside him [[bible:John 18:13; Luke 3:2; Acts 4:6]]. John reports his advice to the council that it was better for one man to die for the people than for the whole nation to perish [[bible:John 11:49-53]]. Matthew names him as the high priest in whose house the plot against Jesus was laid and before whom Jesus was questioned [[bible:Matt 26:3-4, 57]]; see [[event:trial-of-jesus]]. He appears once more in Acts, when Peter and John are examined by the council [[bible:Acts 4:5-7]].

A decorated ossuary inscribed "Joseph son of Caiaphas", found in a family tomb in Jerusalem, may be his. Many scholars accept the identification and some question it; the inscription gives no title [[cite:ossuary]].
