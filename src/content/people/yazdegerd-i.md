The acts of the synod of 410, dated to the eleventh year of his reign, praise Yazdegerd for ending the persecution of Christians: he ordered the churches destroyed under earlier kings to be rebuilt and those imprisoned for their faith to be released [[cite:opening]]. On hearing the letter of the Roman bishops he ordered his provincial governors to send the bishops to the capital [[cite:summons]], and his officials told the synod that its decisions had his backing [[cite:edict]]. Chabot, following Nöldeke, counts the years of his reign from August 399 [[cite:opening]]. See [[event:synod-of-seleucia-ctesiphon]].

The Greek historians give two pictures of him. Socrates says that [[person:marutha-of-maypherkat]] won his respect, that he allowed churches to be built, and that he never harmed the Christians of his realm; persecution began, on this account, under his son [[cite:marutha]] [[cite:successor]]. Theodoret reports that after a bishop named Abdas destroyed a fire temple and refused to rebuild it, Yazdegerd had him executed and ordered the churches destroyed [[cite:abdas]].
