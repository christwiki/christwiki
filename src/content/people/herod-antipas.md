Antipas received Galilee and Perea, with the title of tetrarch, when [[person:augustus]] divided the kingdom of his father [[person:herod-the-great]] [[cite:tetrarch]]. He fortified Sepphoris and built a new city on the [[place:sea-of-galilee]], which he named Tiberias after the emperor [[person:tiberius]] [[cite:cities]]. Luke names him as tetrarch of Galilee when John the Baptist began to preach [[bible:Luke 3:1]].

He agreed to divorce his wife, a Nabataean princess, in order to marry [[person:herodias]], the wife of one of his half-brothers; the princess fled to her father, King Aretas, who later destroyed his army [[cite:marriage]]. He imprisoned [[person:john-the-baptist]] and had him executed [[bible:Mark 6:14-29]] [[cite:john]]; see [[event:death-of-john-the-baptist]]. In Luke, [[person:jesus]] calls him "that fox", and [[person:pontius-pilate|Pilate]] sends Jesus to him during the trial because Jesus was a Galilean [[bible:Luke 13:31-32]] [[bible:Luke 23:6-12]].

[[person:josephus]] relates that Herodias urged him to ask the emperor Gaius for the title of king. Her brother Agrippa accused him of storing arms for seventy thousand men, and Gaius deposed him and banished him to Gaul, where Herodias chose to follow him [[cite:exile]]. In his earlier work Josephus gives the place of exile as Spain [[cite:exile-war]].
