Eusebius was bishop of Caesarea in Palestine. His *Church History* ([[source:eusebius-church-history]]) is a principal source for the first three centuries of Christianity, and his *Life of Constantine* ([[source:eusebius-life-of-constantine]]) for the emperor's dealings with the church.

Before the [[event:council-of-nicaea]] [[person:arius]] counted him among the bishops on his side [[cite:arius]]. Eusebius attended the council and describes its opening [[cite:council]]. Afterwards he wrote to his own church to explain why he had signed a creed containing the word *homoousios*, after questioning what it meant [[cite:letter]]; [[person:athanasius]] later cited the letter against those who rejected the creed [[cite:athanasius]]. He died a few years after Constantine and was succeeded by his pupil Acacius [[cite:death]].
