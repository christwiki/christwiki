The Reverend Fred L. Shuttlesworth was president of the Alabama Christian Movement for Human Rights [[cite:acmhr]], the [[place:birmingham-alabama]] affiliate of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, which asked [[person:martin-luther-king-jr]] and his staff to come to the city [[cite:letter-1]]. King's letter from the jail recalls that Shuttlesworth and the movement's leaders had agreed, after talks the previous September, to suspend demonstrations when the city's merchants promised to take down their racial signs, and that the promise was broken [[cite:letter-2]].

In April 1963 he sent a member of the movement to city hall to ask about a permit to demonstrate, and then asked for one himself by telegram. Commissioner Eugene Connor refused the first request and answered the second by insisting that no picketing begin [[cite:permit]]. On Good Friday, 12 April, he led the march with King and Ralph Abernathy and was jailed with them [[cite:good-friday]]. For that march he was sentenced to 90 days at hard labour for parading without a permit, a conviction the Supreme Court of the United States reversed in 1969 [[cite:conviction]].
