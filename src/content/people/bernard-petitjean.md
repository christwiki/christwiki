Francisque Marnas, the historian of the mission, says that Petitjean was born at Blanzy in the French department of Saône-et-Loire on 14 June 1829 [[cite:birth]]. On 17 March 1865 a group of villagers from Urakami came to the church he served at [[place:nagasaki]], and a woman among them told him that their hearts were the same as his; Marnas tells the scene from Petitjean's letters of 18 and 22 March [[cite:discovery]]. The journal that Petitjean kept in the days after records his questions to them and his care not to expose them to the officers who watched the church [[cite:journal]].

In 1873 he signs himself bishop of Myriophite and vicar apostolic of Japan. His circular of 6 May that year reports that the edicts against Christianity had been taken down and the exiles of Urakami released, and adds that this was not yet religious liberty [[cite:circular]].
