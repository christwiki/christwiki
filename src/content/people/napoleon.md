As general commanding the French army in Italy, Bonaparte concluded the treaty of Tolentino with the envoys of [[person:pius-vi]] on 19 February 1797 [[cite:tolentino]]. The constitution of December 1799 named him First Consul [[cite:first-consul]]. The Concordat of 1801 with [[person:pius-vii]] allowed Catholic worship to be public again in France [[cite:concordat-worship]] and gave the First Consul the naming of bishops, who were to swear fidelity to the government [[cite:concordat-bishops]]. The Organic Articles of 1802 added that no papal document, legate or council might act in France without the government's permission [[cite:organic]].

A senatus-consultum of 18 May 1804 declared him Emperor of the French [[cite:emperor]]. By a decree given at his camp at Vienna on 17 May 1809 he united the Papal States to the French Empire, giving as his reason that the union of spiritual and temporal power had been a source of discord [[cite:annexation]].
