Philostorgius says that the ancestors of Ulfilas were Christians from Cappadocia taken captive by the Goths in the third century, that he was ordained bishop while on an embassy to the emperor, and that he gave Gothic a script and translated the Scriptures [[cite:philostorgius]]. His pupil Auxentius gives the frame of his life: bishop at thirty, seven years among the Goths beyond the Danube, thirty-three more on Roman soil after a persecution drove him out, and death at Constantinople at seventy. Auxentius also sets out his teaching, in which the Son is God but subordinate to the Father and the Spirit subordinate to the Son [[cite:auxentius]].

Socrates records that he assented to the creed of Constantinople of 360 [[cite:socrates-creed]] and counts him among the Arians [[cite:socrates]]. The Gothic Gospels of the Codex Argenteus are the longest surviving text of the translation that goes under his name [[cite:codex]]. See [[event:ulfilas]].
