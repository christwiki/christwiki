Pepin ruled the Franks as mayor of the palace with his brother Carloman, and with him supported the reforming synods of [[person:boniface]] [[cite:boniface]]. The Royal Frankish Annals say that envoys asked Pope Zacharias whether it was good that kings in Francia should have the name without the power, that the pope answered it was better for the one who held power to be called king, and that Pepin was then chosen king and anointed, by the hand of Boniface according to the annals, the last Merovingian being sent to a monastery [[cite:king]]. The annals enter this under 750; G. W. Robinson's note to Willibald's Life puts it in November 751 [[cite:boniface]].

Pope [[person:stephen-ii]] anointed him again in 754, with his sons Charles and Carloman [[cite:anointing]]. After this Pepin made war on the Lombards and gave the cities of the exarchate of [[place:ravenna]] to St Peter ([[event:donation-of-pepin]]) [[cite:lp]]. He died at Saint-Denis on 24 September 768 [[cite:death]].
