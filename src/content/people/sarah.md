Sarai is the wife of [[person:abraham|Abram]] and is childless when the family leaves Ur [[bible:Gen 11:29-31]]. After ten years in Canaan she gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram, and Ishmael is born [[bible:Gen 16:1-4]] [[bible:Gen 16:15]]. When God renames her Sarah and promises her a son, Abraham laughs; she laughs in turn when she overhears the promise at Mamre [[bible:Gen 17:15-17]] [[bible:Gen 18:10-12]]. She bears [[person:isaac]] [[bible:Gen 21:1-7]] and has Hagar and Ishmael sent away [[bible:Gen 21:9-14]]. She dies at 127 at [[place:hebron]], where Abraham buys the cave of Machpelah for her tomb [[bible:Gen 23:1-2]] [[bible:Gen 23:19-20]].

The book of [[person:isaiah|Isaiah]] tells Israel to look "to Abraham your father, and to Sarah who bore you" [[bible:Isa 51:2]]. Hebrews counts her among those who lived by faith [[bible:Heb 11:11]], and [[person:paul]] makes her and Hagar figures of two covenants [[bible:Gal 4:22-31]]. Her dates follow from Abraham's: she is ten years younger than he is [[bible:Gen 17:17]].
