Crowther wrote in 1837 that early in 1821 his town, in what he calls the Eyo country, was taken by slave raiders [[cite:capture]]. He was captured with his mother and sisters, parted from them, and sold from one owner to another until Portuguese traders bought him at a town on the coast that he calls E'-ko [[cite:capture]]. Two British warships seized the slave ship on the river Lagos on 7 April 1822 and landed him at Sierra Leone on 17 June [[cite:rescue]]. There, under the care of the Church Missionary Society, he learned to read and was baptized on 11 December 1825 [[cite:rescue]].

The history of the Church Missionary Society records that the bishop of London ordained him in 1843 and that in August 1846 he entered [[place:abeokuta]] with the first mission to the Yoruba country [[cite:ordained]]. By 1864, it says, he had written a Yoruba grammar and dictionary and translated many books of the Bible into Yoruba, and on 29 June of that year he was consecrated bishop in Canterbury Cathedral [[cite:bishop]]. In his charge of 1866 he dates the Niger Mission from 1857 and lists its stations along the river [[cite:charge]]. The same history gives his death at Lagos on 31 December 1891 [[cite:death]].
