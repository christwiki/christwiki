Tischendorf writes that he sailed for Egypt in April 1844 hoping to find biblical manuscripts from the early centuries of Christianity, and that at [[place:saint-catherines-monastery]] he found leaves of a very old Greek Old Testament, which he deposited at Leipzig and named the Codex Frederick Augustus after the king of Saxony, his patron [[cite:first]]. He returned in 1853, and again in 1859 with funds from the Russian emperor, having just brought out the seventh edition of his New Testament [[cite:third]].

After the third visit he carried the manuscript to St Petersburg and published it in facsimile in four volumes, which he presented to the emperor in 1862 [[cite:edition]]. How he came by it is told differently by him and by the monastery; the history agreed by the institutions that now hold the manuscript sets out what the documents show [[cite:history]].
