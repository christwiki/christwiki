The Primary Chronicle presents Vladimir as a pagan ruler who weighed the faiths of his neighbours, sent envoys to see their worship and chose that of the Greeks [[cite:choice]]. It places his baptism at [[place:chersonesus]], after he had taken the city and demanded the emperors' sister in marriage, while admitting that others placed it elsewhere [[cite:baptism]]. Yahya of Antioch, writing nearer the time, says simply that the king of the Rus' married the sister of [[person:basil-ii]] on condition of being baptized with his people, and sent troops to help the emperor against a rebel [[cite:yahya]].

At [[place:kyiv]], the chronicle says, he threw down the idols, had the people baptized in the Dnieper and built a church of the Mother of God with craftsmen from Greece [[cite:kyiv]]. It records his death on 15 July in the year 6523, AD 1015 [[cite:death]]. See [[event:baptism-of-rus]].
