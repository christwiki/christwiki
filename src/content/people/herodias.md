Herodias was a daughter of Aristobulus, one of the sons of [[person:herod-the-great]], and a sister of the later king [[person:herod-agrippa-i]]. [[person:josephus]] says that she was first married to a son of Herod the Great, also named Herod, and had by him a daughter, Salome. She then left him to marry his half-brother [[person:herod-antipas]], which Josephus calls a breach of the ancestral laws [[cite:family]]. Mark names her first husband Philip [[bible:Mark 6:17]]; the "Philip" that stands in brackets in the linked translation of Josephus was added by the translator.

In Mark and Matthew, [[person:john-the-baptist]] tells Antipas that the marriage is unlawful. Herodias holds a grudge against him, and when her daughter's dancing wins a promise from the tetrarch she tells the girl to ask for John's head [[bible:Mark 6:17-28]] [[bible:Matt 14:3-11]]; see [[event:death-of-john-the-baptist]]. Josephus's account of John's death does not mention her [[cite:john]]. He relates that she later pressed Antipas to seek the title of king from the emperor Gaius, and that when Antipas was banished instead she refused the emperor's offer to spare her and went into exile with her husband [[cite:exile]].
