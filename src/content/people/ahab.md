Ahab succeeded his father Omri and reigned twenty-two years in [[place:samaria]]. He married [[person:jezebel]] of Sidon and built a temple of Baal in his capital, for which Kings judges him worse than all the kings before him [[bible:1 Kings 16:29-33]]. The prophet [[person:elijah]] opposed him through a long drought [[bible:1 Kings 17:1; 18:17-18]]; see [[event:elijah-on-carmel]]. When Jezebel had Naboth killed so that Ahab could take his vineyard, Elijah announced the end of his house; Ahab humbled himself and the sentence was put off to his son's time [[bible:1 Kings 21:1-29]].

Kings also shows him as a soldier. He twice defeated Ben-hadad of Aram and then made a treaty with him [[bible:1 Kings 20:1-34]], and he died of an arrow wound fighting Aram at Ramoth-gilead [[bible:1 Kings 22:29-37]]. The stela of [[person:shalmaneser-iii]] from Kurkh lists "Ahab the Israelite" with 2,000 chariots and 10,000 soldiers among the allies who met the Assyrians at [[place:qarqar]] in 853 BC [[cite:kurkh]], a battle Kings does not mention; see [[event:battle-of-qarqar]]. That inscription fixes the one firm date in his reign. The year of his death is reckoned from it and from the reigns of his two sons [[bible:1 Kings 22:51; 2 Kings 3:1]].
