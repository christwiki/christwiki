Thietmar of Merseburg gives a short portrait of him. Gerbert came from the western lands and was schooled from boyhood in the liberal arts. He was raised to the see of Reims, unjustly as Thietmar thinks, and was later driven from his country and came to the emperor Otto. He excelled his contemporaries in learning and knew how to read the courses of the stars; at Magdeburg he made a clock, setting it by sighting through a tube the star that guides sailors. After the death of Pope Gregory he succeeded by the emperor's favour, took the name Sylvester and sat until the time of King Henry. Thietmar's editor dates the pontificate from 999 to 1003 [[cite:thietmar]].

Hartvic's Life of King Stephen tells how the pope, warned in a vision, gave the Hungarian envoy a crown that had been made for the duke of the Poles [[cite:hartvic]]. The passage does not name the pope; the editor identifies him as Sylvester II [[cite:editor]]. Thietmar, the contemporary, says that [[person:stephen-i-of-hungary]] received crown and blessing by the favour and urging of the emperor, and does not mention the pope [[cite:emperor]].
