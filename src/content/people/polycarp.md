Ignatius, on his way to Rome, addressed a letter to "Polycarp, Bishop of the Church of the Smyrnaeans" [[cite:ignatius]], and Polycarp afterwards sent copies of Ignatius's letters to Philippi with a letter of his own [[cite:philippians]]. Irenaeus, who grew up in Asia, remembered him sitting and teaching and telling of his dealings "with John and with the others who had seen the Lord" [[cite:florinus]], and says that apostles had appointed him bishop of [[place:smyrna]] [[cite:irenaeus]].

He travelled to [[place:rome]] in the time of bishop Anicetus and discussed the keeping of Easter with him. Neither persuaded the other, and they shared the Eucharist and parted in peace [[cite:anicetus]]. Irenaeus adds that in Rome he won back followers of Valentinus and [[person:marcion]] [[cite:irenaeus]]. At his trial he said that he had served Christ for eighty-six years [[cite:martyrdom]], which, if he died in 155, puts his birth or his baptism about AD 70. See [[event:martyrdom-of-polycarp]].
