Bede describes Aethelberht as a powerful king whose authority reached to the Humber. He already knew of Christianity through his wife Bertha, a Frankish princess who had come to Kent with her own bishop. When [[person:augustine-of-canterbury]] landed he met the missionaries in the open air, told them that he could not at once abandon the beliefs of his people, and gave them food, a house in [[place:canterbury]] and freedom to preach [[cite:meeting]]. He was later baptised, and Bede says that he forced no one to follow him, having learned that the service of Christ must be voluntary [[cite:baptism]]. [[person:gregory-i]] wrote to him comparing him to Constantine [[cite:letter]].

Bede records his death on 24 February 616, twenty-one years after he received the faith, and adds that he issued written laws in English, the first of which protected the property of the church [[cite:death]].
