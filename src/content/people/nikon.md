Nikon was metropolitan of Novgorod before he became patriarch, and [[person:avvakum]], who knew him, says the tsar chose him on the advice of his chaplain [[cite:rise]]. The Russian church dates his patriarchate to 1652–1666 [[cite:act1971]]. Paul of Aleppo, who watched him at work, describes a synod at which Nikon had the order of the liturgy translated from Greek and declared that, though a Russian, his faith and religion were Greek [[cite:synod]].

The council that confirmed his corrected books in 1667 refers to him as the former patriarch [[cite:decree]]. See [[event:nikon-and-the-old-believers]].
