The Primary Chronicle tells her baptism as a story: the emperor Constantine wished to marry her, she asked him to stand sponsor at her baptism, and then pointed out that a godfather may not marry his goddaughter. She was named Helena and went home to a son and people who were still pagan [[cite:baptism]]. A German chronicler of her own time confirms the essentials. Under 959 he records envoys of Helena, queen of the Rus', "baptized at Constantinople" in the reign of an emperor he calls Romanus, asking King [[person:otto-i]] for a bishop and priests [[cite:helena]].

The chronicle puts her death in 969 and says that a priest buried her, at her own wish, without the pagan funeral feast [[cite:death]]. When [[person:vladimir-the-great]]'s counsellors recommended the Greek faith, the chronicle has them argue that Olga, the wisest of all, would not have chosen it had it been bad [[cite:envoys]].
