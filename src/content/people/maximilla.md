Maximilla and Priscilla were the two women who began to prophesy in the same way as [[person:montanus]] and were counted with him as the prophets of the movement [[cite:named]]. Apollonius places her at [[place:pepuza]], where a bishop named Zoticus tried to refute the spirit that spoke in her and was prevented by her supporters [[cite:pepuza]]. Her answer to such attempts is quoted by an unnamed writer: "I am driven away from the sheep like a wolf. I am not a wolf. I am word and spirit and power" [[cite:wolf]].

She foretold wars and upheaval [[cite:years]], and Epiphanius, whose Greek is paraphrased here, quotes her as saying that after her there would be no more prophets, only the end [[cite:end]]. The unnamed writer, composing his work more than thirteen years after her death, points out that those years had been peaceful [[cite:years]]. See [[event:montanism]].
