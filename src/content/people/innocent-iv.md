Two letters of Innocent to "the king and people of the Tartars" are dated at [[place:lyon]] on 5 and 13 March in the second year of his pontificate, 1245. In the first he writes as the successor of the vicar to whom Christ gave the keys of the kingdom of heaven, responsible for the salvation of all peoples, and sends Franciscans to teach the faith [[cite:dei-patris]]. In the second he urges the Mongols to stop their attacks, warns them of the anger of God, and asks what they intend [[cite:cum-non-solum]]. The reply of the khan Güyük told him to come in person with the kings and submit [[cite:guyuk]].

His bull *Ad extirpanda* ordered the city governments of northern Italy to hand arrested heretics to the bishop or the inquisitors and to force them, short of loss of limb or danger of death, to confess and to name others [[cite:extirpanda]].
