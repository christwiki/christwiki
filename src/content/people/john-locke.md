In the *Letter Concerning Toleration* Locke calls mutual toleration among Christians "the chief characteristic mark of the true Church" [[cite:mark]] and argues that the care of souls is not committed to the civil magistrate, whose power consists only in outward force [[cite:magistrate]]. He denies toleration to those who deny the being of God and to a church whose members owe service to another prince [[cite:limits]]. See [[event:toleration-act]].
