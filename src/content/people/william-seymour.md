Seymour told his own story in the first issue of the mission's paper: he came to [[place:los-angeles]] from Houston, Texas, to take charge of a mission on Santa Fe Street, was locked out of it, argued against holiness preachers who held that the baptism with the Holy Ghost was simply sanctification, and went on to the cottage prayer meeting at 214 Bonnie Brae Street where the revival began [[cite:af1-p1]]. Frank Bartleman, who met him shortly before 26 March 1906, describes him as a "colored man, very plain, spiritual, and humble", blind in one eye [[cite:bartleman-43]].

The same issue prints an article signed by him, "The Precious Atonement", which teaches forgiveness, sanctification, bodily healing and the baptism with the Holy Ghost as gifts of Christ's atonement [[cite:af1-p2]]. In December 1906 the paper called him "simply a humble pastor of the flock" over which the Holy Ghost had made him overseer [[cite:af4-p1]]. See [[event:azusa-street-revival]].
