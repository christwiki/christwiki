The Journal of the House of Lords for 24 May 1689 records that the king sat on the throne in his robes, sent for the Commons and gave his assent to the Act exempting Protestant dissenters from the penalties of certain laws; the clerk pronounced the assent in the names of the king and the queen [[cite:assent]]. The declaration of fidelity that the Act prescribes for those who would not swear is a promise to be true and faithful "to King William and Queene Mary" [[cite:fidelity]].
