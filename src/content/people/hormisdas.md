The *Liber Pontificalis* calls Hormisdas a Campanian from the town of Frisino (Frosinone), bishop for nine years and seventeen days from the consulship of Senator, which is 514 [[cite:life]]. It says that he twice sent bishops to the emperor Anastasius with a written profession by which the Greeks might return to communion, that the emperor refused it and sent the second embassy away under guard, and that he wrote to the pope that he meant to give commands and not to take them [[cite:embassies]].

When Justin succeeded Anastasius and asked for legates, Hormisdas sent Germanus, bishop of Capua, with others, carrying the same profession. The Roman account says that the clergy of [[place:constantinople]] at first refused to agree unless they were told why [[person:acacius-of-constantinople|Acacius]] had been condemned, that the deacon Dioscorus explained it before the emperor, and that Justin then ordered every bishop in his realm to sign [[cite:reunion]]. The copy signed by John of Constantinople on 28 March 519 survives [[cite:formula]]. Hormisdas was buried at St Peter's on 6 August in the consulship of Maximus, which is 523 [[cite:burial]].
