The Jesuit account says that Xu was born at [[place:shanghai|Shanghai]], took first place in the provincial examination at Beijing in 1597, met [[person:matteo-ricci]] at Nanjing in 1600, and in 1603 came to the Jesuit house there, read the catechism in manuscript overnight and was baptized as Paul after eight days of instruction [[cite:baptism]]. In 1604 he took the highest degree and was chosen for the Hanlin academy; he later persuaded his father, who was over seventy, to be baptized [[cite:doctor]].

According to Ricci it was Xu who insisted on translating Euclid and who wrote the Chinese text down as Ricci explained it [[cite:ricci-preface]]. In his own preface Xu says that Ricci's learning has a greater part, the cultivation of the self and the service of Heaven, and a lesser, the study of things, and that he is publishing the lesser first because it is easier to believe [[cite:preface]]. When the Board of Rites accused the Jesuits in 1616, Xu told the emperor that he was himself one of the scholars who believed their books; he argued that their teaching could strengthen that of the Confucian scholars, and he offered to share their punishment if an examination of their books went against them [[cite:memorial]].
