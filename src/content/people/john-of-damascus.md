Theophanes names him among the defenders of images in the time of Leo III: "John Chrysorrhoas", the golden-flowing, son of Mansur, a priest and monk at [[place:damascus]] in Syria, an excellent teacher, who together with the bishops of the East placed the emperor under anathema [[cite:theophanes]].

His treatises argue that the God who became visible in the flesh may be depicted, that matter is honoured because salvation came through it, and that the honour paid to images differs from the worship owed to God alone [[cite:matter]]. He denies that emperors may legislate for the church [[cite:kings]]. The council of Hieria in 754 anathematized him as "Mansur", with "Saracen opinions" [[cite:hieria]], and Theophanes records that the sentence was proclaimed in the forum of Constantinople [[cite:th-hieria]].
