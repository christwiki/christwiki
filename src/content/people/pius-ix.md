Giovanni Maria Mastai Ferretti was born at Senigallia on 13 May 1792, became archbishop of Spoleto and then bishop of Imola, and was elected pope on 16 June 1846 [[cite:life]]. On 8 December 1854 he defined that Mary was preserved from all stain of original sin from the first instant of her conception [[cite:ineffabilis]]. Ten years later he issued a list of eighty condemned propositions, the last of which is that the Roman pontiff ought to reconcile himself with progress, liberalism and modern civilization [[cite:syllabus]].

He presided in person at the session of 18 July 1870 at which the First Vatican Council defined papal infallibility, and confirmed the decree [[cite:session]]. See [[event:first-vatican-council]]. Rome fell on 20 September 1870, and with it the temporal power; the Holy See's account says that he thereafter considered himself a prisoner of the Vatican [[cite:life]]. He died on 7 February 1878 [[cite:life]].
