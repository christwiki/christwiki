What is told here of Antony comes from the *Life* that Athanasius wrote soon after his death. It says that he was an Egyptian of a well-to-do Christian family, that he could not bear schooling, and that at about eighteen or twenty, after his parents died, he heard the Gospel read in church and gave his land and goods away [[cite:call]]. He lived for nearly twenty years shut in a deserted fort across the river, by tradition at [[place:pispir]], and came out to find himself the teacher of others [[cite:fort]]. During the persecution under Maximinus he went to [[place:alexandria]] to serve the confessors [[cite:alexandria]]. He then withdrew to the "inner mountain", three days' journey into the desert, which tradition identifies with [[place:mount-colzim]] [[cite:mountain]].

Athanasius says that he died there at the age of a hundred and five [[cite:death]]. Jerome's Chronicle puts his death in 356 [[cite:jerome]], which gives a birth about 251.
