The Portuguese chronicler Rui de Pina calls him the king of Congo, or Manicongo; modern histories use the Kongo name Nzinga a Nkuwu. Pina says that he sent an embassy to Portugal asking for priests, builders and farmers [[cite:embassy]], and describes him receiving the Portuguese in 1491 seated on a dais, bare to the waist, with a tall cap of palm cloth and a horse's tail trimmed with silver at his shoulder [[cite:court]]. He was baptised on 3 May 1491 with six of his nobles and took the name of the Portuguese king, João [[cite:baptism]].

Pina's story ends with the king a zealous convert. A proclamation issued by his son [[person:afonso-i-of-kongo]] in 1512 says that he afterwards turned away from the faith, banished Afonso for keeping to it, and died just before Afonso came back from exile [[cite:afonso-1512]].
