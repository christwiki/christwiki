William opens his report by calling himself "the meanest in the order of Minor Friars" and addresses it to King Louis of France, who had asked him to write down what he saw among the Tartars. He entered the Black Sea on 7 May 1253 [[cite:opening]]. He says he had preached publicly that he was not an envoy of the king or of anyone, but was going to the unbelievers as the rule of his order directed [[cite:envoy]].

He reached the camp of the khan Möngke on 27 December and had his first audience in January [[cite:arrival]]. At the end of May 1254 he took part in a debate ordered by the khan and was then questioned by Möngke himself [[cite:debate]]. His companion, too ill for the journey, stayed behind, and William left with the khan's letter for King Louis, having baptised six people [[cite:leave]]. See [[event:franciscans-in-asia]].
