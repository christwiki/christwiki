Ignatius told his own story to a fellow Jesuit between 1553 and 1555 [[cite:dictation]]. Until his twenty-sixth year, he said, he was given to the vanities of the world and to soldiering; wounded in the defence of Pamplona, he read a life of Christ and lives of the saints during his recovery and began to love spiritual things [[cite:pamplona]]. At Paris he won [[person:peter-faber]] and [[person:francis-xavier]] by giving them the Exercises, and with his companions resolved to go to Jerusalem or, failing that, to put themselves at the pope's disposal [[cite:paris]].

His *Spiritual Exercises* open with the principle that man is created to praise, reverence and serve God and by this means to save his soul [[cite:exercises]]. The bull of Paul III that approved the [[event:society-of-jesus]] names him first of the ten companions and describes them as priests and masters of arts of the University of Paris [[cite:bull]].
