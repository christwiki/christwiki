The General Assembly of the Church of Scotland named "Master Samuel Rutherfoord" in August 1643 as one of five ministers commissioned to go to England and treat with the Assembly of Divines at Westminster on a common form of church government, confession of faith, catechism and directory of worship [[cite:commission]]. The Assembly's minutes record him speaking in its debates: on 22 October 1645, in the debate on whether Christ intended to redeem the elect only, he argued against Edmund Calamy that the universal offer of the gospel does not require that Christ died in some sense for all [[cite:debate]].

His *Lex, Rex* was printed in London in October 1644; its title page presents it as a defence of the Scots' "defensive wars" and of the just prerogative of king and people, and the edition used here styles him "sometime Professor of Divinity in the University of St. Andrews" [[cite:lex-rex]].
