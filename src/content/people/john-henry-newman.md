Newman says that he had no formed religious convictions until he was fifteen, in the autumn of 1816, and that in 1828 he became vicar of St Mary's at [[place:oxford]] [[cite:youth]]. He returned from a journey to the Mediterranean in July 1833, a few days before [[person:john-keble]]'s assize sermon, and he dated the movement from that day [[cite:start]]. He began the Tracts for the Times on his own initiative and was their editor and main author [[cite:tracts]]. The last of them, Tract 90, argued that the Thirty-nine Articles could be signed by those who aimed at being "catholic in heart and doctrine" [[cite:tract90]]. See [[event:oxford-movement]].

On 8 October 1845 he wrote from Littlemore that he was expecting the Passionist priest Dominic that night and meant to ask him for admission into "the One Fold of Christ" [[cite:received]]. On 12 May 1879 he received in Rome the notice that Pope [[person:leo-xiii]] had made him a cardinal [[cite:cardinal]]. Pope Francis canonized him on 13 October 2019 [[cite:saint]].
