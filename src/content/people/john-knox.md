What is told here comes from Knox's own History, in which he writes of himself in the third person. He was a preacher at Berwick, Newcastle and London in the reign of [[person:edward-vi]]. After the king's death he went to [[place:geneva]], was sent at the bidding of [[person:john-calvin]] to serve the English congregation at Frankfurt, was forced out of that city after a quarrel over ceremonies, and returned to Geneva [[cite:early]].

He arrived in Scotland on 2 May 1559 [[cite:return]]. After the Parliament of 1560 he was one of six ministers charged with writing the Book of Discipline, who, he says, had already drawn up the doctrine, that is, the Confession [[cite:discipline]]. When [[person:mary-queen-of-scots]] returned in 1561 and had mass said in her chapel, he preached against it, and the queen had a long conversation with him, which he reports [[cite:queen]].
