[[person:william-bradford]] names him as pastor of one of the two Separatist churches formed where Nottinghamshire, Lincolnshire and Yorkshire meet, and says that its members went over to the Low Countries [[cite:bradford]]. Smyth describes himself as having passed from Puritanism to Separatism and from there to "true Christian baptism", the baptism of those who confess their faith and their sins [[cite:epistle]]. [[person:john-robinson]] reports that he baptized himself and then the rest of his congregation [[cite:robinson]], and Smyth defended the lawfulness of doing so where no true church exists [[cite:self-baptism]]. See [[event:first-baptists]].

In his last writing he says that he has often been charged with inconstancy and is ready to change again for the better; he there holds that baptism ought to be received from an existing true church where there is one, which set him against [[person:thomas-helwys]] [[cite:last-book]].
