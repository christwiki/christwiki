[[person:john-wesley]] records that Whitefield was preaching in the fields at [[place:bristol]] in the spring of 1739 and pressed him to come [[cite:bristol]]. Whitefield's own journal follows him through New England in the autumn of 1740, where he reckoned his last congregation on Boston Common at nearly thirty thousand [[cite:farewell]]. Charles Chauncy of [[place:boston]] wrote that itinerant preaching in those parts began with him, and questioned his warrant for it [[cite:itinerant]].

In March 1741, Wesley writes, Whitefield told him that the two of them preached two different gospels and that he would preach against him publicly [[cite:dispute]]. See [[event:great-awakening]] and [[event:wesley-and-methodism]].
