The Chronicle of Edessa calls him Abgar the king, son of Maano, and sets him in the reign of Severus; modern lists of the kings of Edessa number him either VIII or IX. The chronicle tells how he watched the river rise from a tower by torchlight, ordered the sluices opened too late to save the western wall, and afterwards forbade building beside the river, posted watchmen and remitted the city's taxes for five years; four years later he built a new palace [[cite:flood]].

A dialogue from the school of [[person:bardaisan]], who lived in his city, says that when King Abgar "became a believer" he punished those who castrated themselves in honour of the goddess Tharatha by cutting off a hand [[cite:laws]]. Jerome's Chronicle, on the authority of Julius Africanus, records "a holy man" Abgar reigning at [[place:edessa]] [[cite:africanus]]. Neither text says which king of that name it means, and the flood record itself, though it mentions the Christians' church, says nothing of the king's religion.
