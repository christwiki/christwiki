Ezana is known from his own inscriptions at [[place:aksum]]. One, in Greek and Ge'ez, gives his titles as king of the Aksumites, the Himyarites and other peoples and calls him son of the unconquered god Ares; it mentions his brothers Saiazana and Adephas [[cite:pagan]]. Another, in Greek, records a war against the Noba [[cite:noba]] and opens by invoking the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit [[cite:christian]]. The Greek text of the first carries no date.

A datable notice of him comes from the Roman side. About 356 the emperor Constantius II wrote to "Aezanes and Sazanes", rulers of Aksum, requiring them to send their bishop [[person:frumentius]] to Egypt [[cite:letter]]. See [[event:ezana-of-aksum]].
