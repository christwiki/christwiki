Jerome says that Cyprian was a well-known teacher of rhetoric before the presbyter Caecilius persuaded him to become a Christian [[cite:jerome]]. His deacon Pontius adds that he was chosen bishop of [[place:carthage]] by popular demand while still a recent convert [[cite:pontius]]. When the persecution of [[person:decius]] began and the crowd called for him, he withdrew from the city and directed his church by letter, a decision he had to defend to the clergy of Rome [[cite:withdrawal]].

After his return he dealt with the Christians who had sacrificed, opposed the rival bishop [[person:novatian]], and held, against [[person:stephen-i]] of Rome, that baptism given by heretics was no baptism; see [[event:decian-persecution]] and [[event:cyprian-on-unity]]. Under the emperor Valerian he was exiled to Curubis on 30 August 257, brought back, tried before the proconsul and beheaded on 14 September 258 [[cite:acts]].
