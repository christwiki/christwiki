Evagrius, writing about 140 years later and drawing on the historian Priscus, says that Marcian was a Thracian and a soldier's son, and that he was chosen emperor at the suggestion of [[person:pulcheria]], whom he married, though she remained a virgin [[cite:rise]]. He called the bishops to [[place:chalcedon]], came to the council in person after the definition of faith had been approved, and afterwards sent troops to Alexandria, where the people had risen against the bishop the council appointed [[cite:council]]. The minutes record the bishops' acclamations of him and his grant of the rank of metropolis to the city [[cite:session-6]]. He died after a reign of seven years [[cite:death]].
