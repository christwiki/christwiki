Theophanes, writing about 810, calls Maximus an abbot revered for his monastic life. He says that Maximus disputed in Africa with Pyrrhus, the former patriarch of [[place:constantinople]], and brought him for a time to renounce the doctrine of one will; that he then went to [[place:rome]] and moved Pope Martin to gather a synod of bishops, which condemned that doctrine and proclaimed two wills and two operations in Christ; and that the emperor Constans had both men brought to the capital, tortured and exiled [[cite:maximus]]. Elsewhere he adds that Constans had the tongue of Maximus cut out and his hand cut off, and he calls him "most wise" and a confessor [[cite:constans]].

The [[event:third-council-of-constantinople]] met a generation later. The parts of its acts cited in this wiki do not name him, but its definition of two natural wills in Christ, the human following the divine [[cite:definition]], is the doctrine for which Theophanes says he suffered.
