Catherine Mumford was born on 17 January 1829 [[cite:dates]] and married [[person:william-booth]] on 16 June 1855 [[cite:married]]. His biographer, an officer who knew them both, says that she first prayed aloud in church at Gateshead, defended in the press an American woman preacher whom a pastor in a neighbouring city had opposed, and then began to speak in the services herself, with her husband's support [[cite:gateshead]]. In *Female Ministry* she argued that the public ministry of women is not merely unforbidden but "absolutely enjoined by both precept and example in the word of God" [[cite:pamphlet]].

When her husband came home from the tent in Whitechapel and proposed to stay among the poor of East London, she recalled, in words the same biographer quotes, answering that they had trusted God once for their support and could do so again [[cite:tent]]. She died on 4 October 1890 [[cite:death]]. See [[event:salvation-army]].
