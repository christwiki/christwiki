The title page of his *Dialogue* calls More a knight, one of the king's council and chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster, and, in the revised edition dated 1530, Chancellor of England. It says that the book treats of images, relics, prayer to saints and pilgrimage, and of the sect of Luther and [[person:william-tyndale]] [[cite:title]].

In the third book More argues that Tyndale's New Testament was rightly burned. It put "seniors" for priests, "congregation" for church and "love" for charity, and More held that these changes were made on purpose to serve Luther's teaching [[cite:ch8]]. He did not object to an English Bible as such: the church's law, he says, allowed translations approved by the bishops, and a good one ought to be made [[cite:ch16]].
