The two sides describe him in opposite terms. The legates' bull calls him patriarch in name only, a man who took the monastic habit out of fear, and charges him with closing the Latin churches in the city and persecuting those who used unleavened bread [[cite:bull]]; their report says he falsified the bull in translation and raised a riot against the emperor [[cite:account]]. In the edict of his own synod he is the guardian of the orthodox faith against men who had come from the West with forged letters to corrupt it [[cite:edict]].

His letter to the patriarch of Antioch shows that he believed the popes had long since been dropped from the liturgy of his church [[cite:letter]]. Peter's reply asked him to be patient with the Latins and not to make the tear worse [[cite:peter]]. See [[event:east-west-schism]].
