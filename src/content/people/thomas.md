Thomas is named in every list of the twelve [[bible:Mark 3:18; Matt 10:3; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13]]. Only John tells anything of him, and calls him also Didymus, the Greek for "twin". When Jesus set out for Bethany and danger, Thomas said to the others, "Let's go also, that we may die with him" [[bible:John 11:16]]. At the last supper he said that they did not know where Jesus was going, and was answered, "I am the way, the truth, and the life" [[bible:John 14:5-6]].

John says that he was absent when the risen Jesus first came to the disciples, and that he said he would not believe unless he put his finger into the marks of the nails. Eight days later Jesus offered him his hands and his side, and Thomas answered, "My Lord and my God!" [[bible:John 20:24-29]]; see [[event:appearances-and-great-commission]]. He is among the seven who meet Jesus by the lake [[bible:John 21:2]].

The New Testament does not say where he went afterwards. Eusebius, in the early fourth century, cites Origen for a tradition that Parthia was allotted to Thomas as his field of work [[cite:eusebius]]. Gregory of Nazianzus, about AD 380, links him with India [[cite:gregory]]. Both write some three centuries after his time.
