The two churches left opposite pictures of Photius. In the letters of Pope [[person:nicholas-i]] he is a layman from the imperial service, tonsured and consecrated in haste, who seized the see of a living patriarch [[cite:rome]]; the council of 869–870 declared that he had never been a bishop [[cite:council]]. In his own encyclical he is the defender of the faith of the councils against Latin clergy who were teaching the newly baptized Bulgarians to add "and from the Son" to the creed [[cite:encyclical]].

He was removed in favour of [[person:ignatius-of-constantinople]] and later became patriarch again; in 879 Pope John VIII argued that the Roman see could release him from the earlier sentence [[cite:john]]. The Synodikon of Orthodoxy commemorates Ignatius and Photius together among the holy and orthodox patriarchs [[cite:synodikon]]. See [[event:photian-schism]].
