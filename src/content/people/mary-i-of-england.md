Mary was proclaimed queen in [[place:london]] on 19 July 1553 [[cite:accession]]. The statute that restored the pope's authority in England addresses her as the daughter of [[person:henry-viii]] and speaks of her marriage to "the king our sovereign lord your husband", Philip; it records that Parliament asked through the two of them to be absolved of schism by the legate [[person:reginald-pole]] [[cite:repeal]].

The same Parliament revived the medieval statutes against heresy [[cite:revival]]. In May 1555 a letter in the names of the king and queen told the bishop of London to proceed against obstinate heretics according to the laws [[cite:letter]]. The burnings that followed are described in [[event:marian-persecutions]]. Mary died on 17 November 1558 and her sister [[person:elizabeth-i]] was proclaimed the same day [[cite:death]]. Twelve years later a papal bull remembered her as the lawful queen who had restored the Catholic faith [[cite:regnans]].
