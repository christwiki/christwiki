His biographer George Railton, an officer under him, gives his birth at Nottingham on 10 April 1829 [[cite:birth]]. He became a minister of the Methodist New Connexion, and left it when its Conference refused to release him for work as a travelling evangelist [[cite:left]]. He was asked to take services in a tent in Whitechapel, and his biographer quotes him as telling his wife [[person:catherine-booth]] that night that he had found his destiny among the poor of East London [[cite:tent]]. The mission he began there in July 1865 took the name Salvation Army at the end of 1878, and he took the title of General [[cite:army]].

In the preface to *In Darkest England* (1890) he writes that the sight of the poor stocking-weavers of his native town had moved him as a child, and that he had come to think the usual remedies of Christian philanthropy "lamentably inadequate" [[cite:darkest]]. He died on 20 August 1912 [[cite:death]]. See [[event:salvation-army]].
