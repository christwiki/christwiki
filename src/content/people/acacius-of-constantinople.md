When the usurper Basiliscus issued a circular against the Council of Chalcedon, Acacius roused the monks and people of [[place:constantinople]] against him, and Basiliscus withdrew it [[cite:basiliscus]]. Under the restored emperor [[person:zeno]] he advised on the Henotikon [[cite:henotikon]]. The Syriac chronicle of Zachariah, which opposed Chalcedon, says that he gave up his earlier support of the council and attached himself to the principles of the Henotikon [[cite:zachariah]]. Evagrius shows him still concerned for the council: when he heard that Peter Mongus of Alexandria had anathematised Chalcedon he sent to inquire, and Peter wrote to assure him that he had not [[cite:peter]].

Pope Felix judged otherwise. His sentence of 28 July 484 charges Acacius with supporting Peter, whom he had himself reported to Rome as condemned, and with corrupting the Roman envoys, and declares him removed from the priesthood and from communion [[cite:sentence]]. Acacius did not accept it [[cite:refusal]]. Evagrius records his death and the succession of Fravitas to his see [[cite:death]]. The profession by which communion with Rome was restored in 519 anathematises him by name [[cite:formula]]. See [[event:henotikon]].
