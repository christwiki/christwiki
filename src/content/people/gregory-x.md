The introduction printed with the council's decrees gives his election as 1 September 1271, after a vacancy of almost three years, and his death as 10 January 1276 [[cite:dates]]. His conclave law begins by recalling how much the Roman church loses in a long vacancy, and orders the cardinals to be enclosed and their food reduced until they elect [[cite:conclave]].

The record of the council shows him presiding at [[place:lyon]]: preaching at the first session on the Holy Land, the Greeks and reform [[cite:opening]], and intoning the Te Deum in tears when the envoys of [[person:michael-viii-palaiologos]] had sworn to the Roman faith [[cite:union]]. In his closing address he told the bishops that prelates were bringing the whole world to ruin, and that if they did not correct themselves he would deal with them severely [[cite:closing]].
