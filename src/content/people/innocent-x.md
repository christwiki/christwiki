His brief against the peace is dated 26 November 1648, in the fifth year of his pontificate; in it he says that his nuncio had already protested publicly against the treaties, lists the concessions made to the Protestants, and declares all articles prejudicial to the Catholic religion, the Apostolic See and the churches null and void for ever [[cite:zelo]]. The treaty of Osnabrück had provided that no protest by anyone, clerical or lay, inside or outside the Empire, should have any force against it [[cite:protest-clause]].
