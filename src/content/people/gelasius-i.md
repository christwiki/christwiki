Gelasius is remembered for his letter of 494 to the emperor Anastasius. It distinguishes the "sacred authority of the priests" from the "royal power", gives the priests the heavier responsibility because they answer to God for kings too, and tells the emperor that in matters of religion he is to obey and not to command [[cite:letter]]. The setting is described under [[event:fall-of-the-western-empire]].

The *Liber Pontificalis*, compiled a generation or so after his death, says that he was an African by origin, held the see for four years and eight months, expelled Manicheans found in Rome and burned their books, cared for the clergy and the poor, and saved the city from famine [[cite:lp]]. Eugippius mentions that the body of Severinus of Noricum was laid to rest near Naples with his authorisation [[cite:severinus]].
