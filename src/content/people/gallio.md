Acts says that when Gallio was proconsul of Achaia the Jews of [[place:corinth]] brought [[person:paul]] before his tribunal, accusing him of persuading people to worship God contrary to the law. Gallio ruled that the matter was a dispute about their own law and not a crime, and dismissed it before Paul could speak [[bible:Acts 18:12-17]].

A letter of the emperor [[person:claudius]] cut in stone at Delphi, of which fragments survive, mentions a Gallio whose name the editors complete as Junius Gallio, "my friend and proconsul"; parts of the words for "friend" and "proconsul" are restored. The editors date the letter to AD 52 [[cite:inscription]]. That date puts his year of office at 51–52, or possibly 52–53; see [[event:second-missionary-journey]] for the reckoning. The philosopher Seneca mentions that Gallio began to run a fever in Achaia and took ship at once [[cite:seneca]]. Tacitus, who calls Seneca his brother, shows him in the senate in AD 65, after Seneca's death, pleading for his own life [[cite:tacitus]].
