Fox writes that he was born in July 1624 at Drayton-in-the-Clay in Leicestershire, where his father was a weaver, and that he was put to work with a shoemaker who also dealt in wool and sold cattle [[cite:youth]]. He left home in 1643 [[cite:youth]]. In 1650 he was imprisoned at Derby as a blasphemer, and there, he says, a justice first called his followers Quakers [[cite:derby]].

The close of the Journal, added by its editors, tells how he preached at the Gracechurch Street meeting in London on a Sunday, felt the cold strike to his heart as he came out, and died two days later [[cite:death]]. The title page gives the day as the thirteenth of the eleventh month, 1690, which is 13 January 1691 by modern reckoning [[cite:title]]. See [[event:george-fox-and-the-quakers]].
