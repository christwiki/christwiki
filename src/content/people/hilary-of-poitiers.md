Jerome records that Hilary, bishop of Poitiers in Aquitania, was banished to Phrygia by a synod at Béziers, that he wrote twelve books against the Arians and a book *On Councils* for the bishops of Gaul, and that he died at Poitiers in the reign of Valentinian and Valens [[cite:jerome]]. Hilary himself blames his exile on Saturninus, who, he says, had misled the emperor [[cite:exile]]. He wrote *On Councils* when he heard that synods were to meet, so that the western bishops should know what their eastern colleagues had decided [[cite:purpose]]. In it he says that although he had long been baptised and had been a bishop for some time, he had never heard the Nicene creed until he was going into exile [[cite:nicene]].

Sulpicius Severus says that in the fourth year of his exile Hilary was brought with the eastern bishops to Seleucia and received into communion there [[cite:seleucia]]. He followed the delegates to [[place:constantinople]], petitioned [[person:constantius-ii]] three times in writing for a hearing against his opponents, and was sent back to Gaul as a troubler of the East. There, in councils of the Gallic bishops, he had the decisions of Ariminum condemned; he died in the sixth year after his return [[cite:return]]. See [[event:councils-of-rimini-and-seleucia]].
