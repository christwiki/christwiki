The safe-conduct that [[person:sigismund]] issued for him in October 1414 calls Hus a master of arts and bachelor of theology from the kingdom of Bohemia, travelling to the general council [[cite:safe-conduct]]. At [[place:constance]] he was imprisoned, examined and pressed to recant. He refused, writing on 1 July 1415 that he could not abjure articles he had never taught, and that he would gladly abjure before the whole world any falsehood or error he had spoken [[cite:declaration]].

The council's sentence treats him as a disciple of [[person:john-wycliffe]], condemns thirty articles from his books, and hands him to the secular power [[cite:sentence]]. He was burned the same day; his companion Peter of Mladoňovice wrote the account of his death [[cite:burning]]. In 1999 [[person:john-paul-ii]] expressed deep regret for it [[cite:jp2]].
