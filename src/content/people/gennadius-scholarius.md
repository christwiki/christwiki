Doukas lists George Scholarios, the general judge (in Greek *katholikos krites*), among the laymen who represented the Greek side at Florence [[cite:florence]]. By 1452 he was a monk named Gennadius in the monastery of the Pantokrator at [[place:constantinople]]. When the union was about to be celebrated in Hagia Sophia he shut himself in his cell and posted on the door a written warning against it, and the opponents of the union took him as their teacher [[cite:manifesto]]. Doukas, a supporter of the union, says that he wrote daily against the unionists and against the works of [[person:thomas-aquinas]] [[cite:writings]].

Kritoboulos relates that [[person:mehmed-ii]], who had heard of his learning, had him sought out after the conquest, found him at Adrianople, admired his conversation and appointed him patriarch [[cite:patriarch]]. See [[event:fall-of-constantinople]].
