The Spanish official Antonio de Morga, whose book was printed in 1609, calls him Yeyasudono, lord of "Quanto", provinces in the north. He relates that [[person:toyotomi-hideyoshi]], dying, left the government to him and four others until his young son should be of age; that Ieyasu defeated the others in battle and remained "the supreme ruler of Japon"; and that he then took the name Daifusama [[cite:morga]].

The proclamation of 27 January 1614 speaks in the ruler's voice: "We have been blessed by the commission of Heaven to be lord in Japan". It sets the way of the gods and the law of Buddha against the "evil law" of the Christians, charges them with seeking to change the government and take possession of the land, and commands that they be swept out of Japan [[cite:edict]]. Its translator, Ernest Satow, prints it as the proclamation of Ieyasu [[cite:edict]]. See [[event:japan-martyrs]].
