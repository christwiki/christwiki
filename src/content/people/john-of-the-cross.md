[[person:teresa-of-avila]] tells how she met him at Medina del Campo: a young Carmelite, still a student at Salamanca, who was thinking of leaving for the Carthusians. She persuaded him to wait and join the house of friars she hoped to found [[cite:meeting]]. He and an older friar, Antonio, were the first to enter it, and the first mass there was said in Advent 1568 [[cite:first-house]].

In December 1577 Teresa wrote to the king that friars of the unreformed observance had carried off the two confessors of the reform serving a convent at [[place:avila]], and that she feared for the life of one of them [[cite:seized]]. A letter of the following August names him and says that he had been held for nine months in a cell hardly large enough for him, and had escaped [[cite:prison]].

His poem of the dark night, with his own prose commentary, describes the soul's way to union with God through a purgation of the senses and then of the spirit [[cite:dark-night]]. See [[event:teresa-of-avila]].
