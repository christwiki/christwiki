Sophronius was patriarch of [[place:jerusalem]] when the Arab armies entered Palestine. In his Christmas sermon of 634 he told his congregation that the Saracens held [[place:bethlehem]] and that fear of them kept the people inside the walls [[cite:sermon]].

Theophanes, writing about 810, says that Sophronius obtained a pledge of safety when the city surrendered to [[person:umar]], and that he died soon afterwards. The same passage remembers him as an opponent of the emperor Heraclius and the patriarchs Sergius and Pyrrhus over the teaching that Christ had a single will [[cite:theophanes]], the question later settled at the [[event:third-council-of-constantinople]].
