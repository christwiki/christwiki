---
name: Joseph of Arimathea
role: Member of the council who buried Jesus
summary: A man of Arimathea, a member of the council according to Mark and Luke, who in all four Gospels asks Pilate for the body of Jesus and lays it in a tomb on the evening of the crucifixion.
---

Mark calls Joseph a prominent member of the council who was himself looking for the kingdom of God. On the evening of the crucifixion he went to Pilate, asked for the body of Jesus, wrapped it in a linen cloth and laid it in a tomb cut out of rock [[bible:Mark 15:42-46]]. Luke adds that he was a good and righteous man who had not agreed to the council's decision [[bible:Luke 23:50-53]]. Matthew calls him a rich man and a disciple of Jesus, and says that the tomb was his own, newly cut [[bible:Matt 27:57-60]]. In John he is a disciple "secretly", and Nicodemus helps him with the burial [[bible:John 19:38-42]]. See [[event:resurrection]].

Luke calls Arimathea "a city of the Jews" [[bible:Luke 23:51]]. Joseph is not mentioned again in the New Testament.
