Stefan Wyszyński was born at Zuzela in Poland on 3 August 1901 and ordained priest on 3 August 1924. Pius XII named him bishop of Lublin in 1946 and transferred him in November 1948 to Gniezno and Warsaw, the primatial see of Poland. In May 1953 he addressed to the state authorities, in the name of the bishops, the message known by its words "Non possumus" [[cite:profile]].

His own notes tell how officials came to his house in [[place:warsaw]] late on 25 September 1953 with a government decision dated the day before, and drove him away after midnight [[cite:arrest]]. On 29 October 1955 he was moved to the convent of the Nazareth sisters at Komańcza, under an order that forbade him to leave the place or to exercise his offices [[cite:komancza]]; he wrote to his father that the authorities had freed him from police isolation in prison [[cite:father]]. On 26 October 1956 two envoys of Władysław Gomułka, the new party leader, came to Komańcza to say that he should return to Warsaw as soon as possible [[cite:return]]. The order of 1955 dates the government's resolution against him to 24 September 1953 [[cite:komancza]]; the Dicastery's profile gives that day for his arrest, says that he was shut up in isolation, and dates his release to 28 October 1956 [[cite:profile]]. He died in Warsaw on 28 May 1981 and was beatified on 12 September 2021 [[cite:profile]].

He was at the head of the Polish bishops who came to [[place:rome]] for the inauguration of [[person:john-paul-ii]] in October 1978 [[cite:inaug-7]]. The new pope wrote, in a letter dated the next day, that he would not be on Peter's chair but for the primate's faith, which had not retreated before prison and suffering [[cite:letter-5]].
