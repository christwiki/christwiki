Gregory was the son of the bishop of Nazianzus in Cappadocia and a fellow student of [[person:basil-of-caesarea]] at Athens [[cite:socrates]]. He went to [[place:constantinople]] to minister to the Nicene believers there, holding his assemblies, Socrates says, in a small oratory later rebuilt as the church of the Anastasia. He was made bishop of the city, met with objections to his appointment, and resigned; Socrates tells this before his account of the council of 381 [[cite:resignation]].

In the fifth of his Theological Orations he says outright that the Holy Spirit is God and of one substance with the Father [[cite:spirit]], and in his funeral oration for Basil he explains that his friend had left it to him to say so openly [[cite:basil]]. Jerome, who calls Gregory his teacher in the Scriptures, says that he retired to the country to live as a monk and died about three years before Jerome wrote in 392–393 [[cite:jerome]].
