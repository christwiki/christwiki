Clement dates the bull *Magnus Dominus* to the fourth year of his pontificate. In it he describes how the bishops Hypatius and Cyril came to [[place:rome]] from the metropolitan of Kiev, made their profession of faith and obedience before him and the cardinals, and were received; he permits the Ruthenians their eastern rites, so long as these do not conflict with the Catholic faith or exclude communion with Rome [[cite:bull]].

His later letters follow the union's fortunes. In January 1597 he rejoiced that the synod of [[place:brest]] had ratified it and regretted that some Ruthenian bishops had not [[cite:brief-1597]]. In June 1597 he annulled the excommunication pronounced from Constantinople against those who had joined Rome [[cite:const-1597]]. In January 1605 he urged [[person:konstanty-ostrogski]] to come over himself [[cite:ostrogski]]. See [[event:union-of-brest]].
