The account of Novatian's career given here comes from his opponents. Eusebius, who calls him Novatus, says that he held there was no hope of salvation for those who had fallen in the persecution, whatever penance they did, and that his party called themselves the Cathari, "the pure". A synod of sixty bishops at Rome excluded him. Eusebius then quotes a letter of his rival Cornelius, which says that Novatian had been baptised on a sickbed, had been ordained presbyter over the objections of the clergy, and had three country bishops lay hands on him to make him bishop [[cite:cornelius]].

His envoys came to [[place:carthage]] to announce his consecration and were refused communion [[cite:envoys]]. [[person:cyprian]] would not discuss what he taught: whoever is outside the church, he wrote, is no Christian, however learned or eloquent [[cite:verdict]].
