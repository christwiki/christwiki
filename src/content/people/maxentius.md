Maxentius was the son of the retired emperor Maximian and the son-in-law of [[person:galerius]]. The remnant of the praetorian guard at [[place:rome]] clothed him in the purple when Galerius tried to tax the city [[cite:acclaimed]]. He survived two attempts to remove him, by the Caesar Severus and by Galerius in person [[cite:origo]].

Eusebius, who has nothing good to say of him, says that at the start of his reign he ordered the persecution of Christians to stop, and puts this down to a pretence of piety meant to please the Roman people; the rest of his portrait is of a tyrant who preyed on senators and their wives [[cite:tolerance]]. That portrait comes from the side that defeated him. He died on 28 October 312, driven into the Tiber in the rout at the Milvian Bridge [[cite:death]] [[cite:calendar]]. The English translation of the anonymous life of [[person:constantine]] gives him a reign of six years; the number is missing in the manuscript and is supplied by the editor [[cite:origo]]. Lactantius sets the battle on the anniversary of his accession [[cite:death]].
