The Venetian Barbaro calls him Mahomet Bey, son of Murat, and says that he came in person to the walls for the final assault on 29 May 1453 [[cite:assault]]. Kritoboulos, the Greek who wrote his history, has him enter the captured city, grieve at its ruin, and repent of the plunder he had allowed [[cite:sack]]. Doukas, who calls him a tyrant and forerunner of Antichrist, reports that he went to Hagia Sophia, stopped a soldier from breaking up its floor with the words that the buildings of the city were his, and had the Muslim prayer said there [[cite:mosque]].

Kritoboulos says that after the conquest Mehmed laid the foundations of a palace in the city and installed [[person:gennadius-scholarius]] as patriarch, with authority over the church no less than it had held under the emperors [[cite:patriarch]]. See [[event:fall-of-constantinople]].
