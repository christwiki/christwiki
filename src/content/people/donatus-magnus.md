The party that set up Majorinus as bishop of [[place:carthage]] against [[person:caecilian]] came to be called the party of Donatus [[cite:augustine]]. Optatus names Donatus as the leader of the accusers at the hearing in Rome in 313, where the bishops condemned him for rebaptising and for laying hands in penance on bishops who had lapsed [[cite:opt-rome]], and says that he afterwards returned to Carthage [[cite:opt-return]].

Optatus, who calls him "Donatus of Carthage", reports that when the emperor Constans sent officials with alms for the African churches, Donatus turned them away with the words "What has the Emperor to do with the Church?" [[cite:opt-emperor]]. Jerome, writing in 392–393, says that he persuaded almost all Africa, and Numidia above all, and left many writings, including one on the Holy Spirit [[cite:jerome]]. Every one of these reports comes from a writer of the other communion. See [[event:donatist-schism]].
