Felix was a freedman whom Claudius favoured and placed over Judaea [[cite:suetonius]]. Josephus calls him the brother of Pallas and puts his appointment late in Claudius's reign [[cite:appointed]]; Tacitus has him already governing Samaria while Cumanus held Galilee [[cite:annals]]. He persuaded Drusilla, a sister of [[person:herod-agrippa-ii]], to leave her husband and marry him [[cite:drusilla]].

Josephus describes his years in office as a time of growing disorder: Felix crucified bandits in great numbers, sent soldiers against prophets who led crowds into the wilderness, and scattered the following of an Egyptian on the Mount of Olives [[cite:rule]]. Josephus also says that he arranged the murder of the high priest Jonathan [[cite:jonathan]]. Tacitus judged that he used a king's power in the spirit of a slave [[cite:histories]].

In Acts he receives [[person:paul]] as a prisoner at [[place:caesarea-maritima]], hears him more than once, hopes for a bribe and leaves him in custody when [[person:festus]] replaces him [[bible:Acts 23:33-24:27]]. After his recall the Jews of Caesarea accused him at Rome, and Josephus says that only the influence of Pallas saved him [[cite:recall]].
