Karol Józef Wojtyła was born at Wadowice in Poland on 18 May 1920 and ordained priest in Kraków on 1 November 1946. He became auxiliary bishop of Kraków in 1958 and its archbishop in 1964, took part in the Second Vatican Council, and was made a cardinal in 1967. He was elected pope on 16 October 1978 and died in the Vatican on 2 April 2005 [[cite:life]].

At the Mass that began his ministry he asked his hearers not to be afraid and to open the frontiers of states and political systems to Christ [[cite:inaug-5]]. His encyclical of March 1979 treated religious freedom as a human right [[cite:rh-17]], and in [[place:warsaw]] in June 1979 he preached that Christ cannot be kept out of human history [[cite:warsaw-3]]; see [[event:john-paul-ii-in-poland]]. He approved the instruction of 1984 on liberation theology [[cite:ln-end]] (see [[event:medellin-and-liberation-theology]]), signed a common declaration on Christ with the patriarch of the Assyrian Church of the East in 1994 [[cite:d1994]] (see [[event:christological-declarations]]), and promulgated the Catechism of the Catholic Church [[cite:life]] (see [[event:catechism-of-the-catholic-church]]). He was canonised on 27 April 2014 [[cite:life]].
