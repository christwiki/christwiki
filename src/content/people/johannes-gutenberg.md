Gutenberg appears in the notarial deed of 6 November 1455 as the partner and debtor of Johann Fust in an enterprise it calls "the work of the books", for which Fust had advanced 1,550 gulden; the court's ruling went against him [[cite:helmasperger]]. He is not named as printer in the Bible or in any of the books attributed to him, as the Göttingen library points out [[cite:goettingen]]. The one fifteenth-century text that links his name to a printed Bible is the Cologne Chronicle of 1499 [[cite:princeton]].

The Library of Congress says that he is generally credited with inventing the making of uniform, interchangeable metal types and with solving the problems of materials and method that printing raised [[cite:loc]]. See [[event:gutenberg-bible]].
