The Life of Constantine says that Hadrian came out of [[place:rome]] with the citizens to meet the brothers, who brought relics of St Clement, that he laid the Slavonic books in the church of St Mary and had the liturgy sung over them, and that he ordered Constantine buried with the honours due to a pope [[cite:welcome]]. The Life of Methodius gives, in Slavonic, a letter in his name allowing the Slavonic liturgy; it is not preserved in Latin and has been judged spurious by some editors [[cite:letter]]. His successor John VIII confirms that it was Hadrian who ordained [[person:methodius]] and sent him to the Slavs [[cite:ordained]].

His legates led the council of 869–870, which upheld the sentences of [[person:nicholas-i]] and his own against [[person:photius]] [[cite:council]]. See [[event:cyril-and-methodius]] and [[event:photian-schism]].
