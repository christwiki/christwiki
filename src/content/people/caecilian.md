Caecilian was archdeacon of [[place:carthage]] under Bishop Mensurius and, according to Optatus, had made an enemy of an influential woman named Lucilla by rebuking her for kissing a martyr's bone before communion. After the persecution he was elected bishop and consecrated by Felix of Aptunga. His opponents, with bishops from Numidia, charged that Felix had handed over the Scriptures and consecrated Majorinus against him [[cite:opt-election]].

[[person:constantine]] treated Caecilian as the lawful bishop from the start and wrote to him about a grant of money for his clergy [[cite:eus-letter]]. The bishops who heard the case at Rome in 313 found nothing proved against him [[cite:opt-rome]], and Constantine then ordered him to Arles for a second hearing [[cite:summons]], where the council again rejected his accusers [[cite:arles]]. Augustine reports that the emperor finally heard both parties himself at Milan and declared Caecilian innocent [[cite:augustine]]. See [[event:donatist-schism]].
