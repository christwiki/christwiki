Innocent appears in several of the turning points of the early thirteenth century. The Fourth Crusade attacked the Christian city of Zara against a prohibition given in his name, and he afterwards absolved the barons [[cite:zara]]; when it had sacked [[place:constantinople]], he wrote to his legate that the crusaders' swords were dripping with Christian blood [[cite:letter]]. Thomas of Celano, who calls him learned and zealous for justice, tells how he blessed [[person:francis-of-assisi]] and his first brothers and sent them to preach repentance [[cite:francis]]. Jordan of Saxony says that he told [[person:dominic]] to go home and choose an approved rule [[cite:dominic]].

Roger of Wendover records that in November 1215, in the eighteenth year of his pontificate, he presided over the general council at the Lateran [[cite:council]], and that he died on 16 July after a pontificate of eighteen years, five months and four days [[cite:death]]; that reckoning puts his death in 1216 and his accession early in 1198. See [[event:sack-of-constantinople]], [[event:francis-of-assisi]], [[event:fourth-lateran-council]] and [[event:dominicans]].
