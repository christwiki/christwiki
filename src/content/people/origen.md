What is told here of his life comes from the sixth book of Eusebius, who collected his letters and wrote a defence of him [[cite:letters]]. Origen was not quite seventeen when his father Leonides was beheaded in the persecution under Severus, and in his eighteenth year he took charge of the instruction of converts at [[place:alexandria]] [[cite:youth]]. He learned Hebrew and set the Hebrew Scriptures and the Greek translations side by side in the Hexapla [[cite:hexapla]].

He was ordained a presbyter by the bishops of Palestine, to the anger of his own bishop Demetrius, and moved to [[place:caesarea-maritima]], where pupils came to him from other countries [[cite:caesarea]]. Under [[person:decius]] he was imprisoned and tortured [[cite:torture]]. Eusebius says that he died in the reign of Gallus, aged sixty-nine [[cite:death]].
