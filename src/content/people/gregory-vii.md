Gregory said of himself that he had taken holy orders unwillingly, had gone beyond the mountains with his master Gregory, had come back to [[place:rome]] with Pope Leo, and had been set on the papal throne against his will [[cite:second-ban]]. [[person:henry-iv-emperor]], breaking with him in 1076, addressed him by his own name, Hildebrand, and charged him with having gained the office by money and force [[cite:henry]].

His register holds the list of papal prerogatives known as the Dictatus papae [[cite:dictatus]], the decree of 1078 against investiture by laymen [[cite:decree-1078]] and his own account of the absolution of Henry at [[place:canossa]] [[cite:canossa]]. Bernold of Constance, an admirer, records that he died at Salerno on 25 May 1085, after twelve years and one month as pope, and was buried there in the church of St Matthew [[cite:bernold]].
