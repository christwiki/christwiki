Aurelius presided over the council held at [[place:carthage]] on 28 August 397, which confirmed the decisions taken at Hippo four years earlier, among them the list of the canonical Scriptures; his signature stands first, before that of [[person:augustine]] [[cite:breviarium]]. See [[event:councils-of-hippo-and-carthage]].

He questioned Caelestius, the disciple of [[person:pelagius]], at his trial in Carthage [[cite:caelestius]], and presided over the council of 1 May 418 that issued canons against Pelagian teaching [[cite:canons418]] and over the council of May 419 that gathered the canons of the African church into one collection [[cite:code]]. Augustine addressed to him his examination of the synod in Palestine that had acquitted Pelagius [[cite:proceedings]]. See [[event:pelagian-controversy]].
