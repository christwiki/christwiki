Stephen succeeded Lucius as bishop of [[place:rome]]. Eusebius says that he regarded the rebaptism of heretics as an innovation against the tradition handed down from the beginning, that he was "very indignant" with [[person:cyprian]], and, quoting Dionysius of Alexandria, that he wrote that he would not hold communion with the bishops of Cilicia, Cappadocia and Galatia over the same question. Eusebius gives him two years in office [[cite:eusebius]]; the Roman list of bishops gives him four [[cite:catalogue]].

His ruling survives in one sentence quoted by Cyprian: nothing is to be introduced beyond what has been handed down, namely that hands be laid on such converts in penance [[cite:ruling]]. Firmilian of Caesarea in Cappadocia, writing to Cyprian, complains that Stephen boasted of his see and of holding the succession from Peter, and that he refused to receive the African bishops' envoys [[cite:firmilian]]. Both witnesses are his opponents.
