Gregory XI's bull of 1377 describes Wycliffe as rector of [[place:lutterworth]] and professor of the sacred Scriptures at [[place:oxford]], and accuses him of teaching the errors of Marsilius of Padua [[cite:bull]]. The conclusions condemned at London in 1382 show what his opponents took his teaching to be: that the bread remains bread after the consecration, that a pope or priest in mortal sin has no power, and that the church should not hold property [[cite:conclusions]]. Knighton, a chronicler hostile to him, says that he translated the Gospel from Latin into English [[cite:knighton]].

John Foxe, in the sixteenth century, gives his death at Lutterworth on St Silvester's day, 31 December, under the year 1384 [[cite:death]]. The Council of Constance declared him a heretic in 1415 and ordered his bones removed from consecrated ground [[cite:constance]]. See [[event:wycliffe-bible]].
