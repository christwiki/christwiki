Macrina is known from the Life written by her brother [[person:gregory-of-nyssa]]. He says that when she was about twelve her father chose a young man for her, who died before the marriage, and that she then resolved to stay with her mother and not to marry [[cite:youth]]. When [[person:basil-of-caesarea]] returned from his studies she drew him to what Gregory calls the philosophic life, and she persuaded her mother to give up her household state and live on equal terms with her maids [[cite:basil]].

Gregory visited her after a synod at Antioch, some nine months after Basil's death, and found her dying [[cite:death]]. He presents her as the teacher of the family. See [[event:cappadocian-fathers]].
