John and his brother [[person:james-son-of-zebedee|James]] were fishermen whom [[person:jesus]] called from their father's boat [[bible:Mark 1:19-20]]. With [[person:peter]] they form, in Mark, the inner group present at the transfiguration and in [[place:gethsemane]] [[bible:Mark 9:2; 14:33]]. In Acts John is Peter's companion in the temple and on a visit to Samaria [[bible:Acts 3:1-4]] [[bible:Acts 8:14-17]]; the council in [[place:jerusalem]] regards the two as "unlearned and ignorant men" [[bible:Acts 4:13]]. Paul met him in Jerusalem and counts him with James and Cephas among the "pillars" [[bible:Gal 2:9]].

The fourth Gospel attributes its testimony to an unnamed "disciple whom Jesus loved" [[bible:John 21:20-24]]. [[person:irenaeus]], about AD 180, identifies that disciple as John, says that he published the Gospel while living at [[place:ephesus]], and that he remained there until the time of [[person:trajan]] [[cite:gospel]] [[cite:ephesus]]. Eusebius quotes the earlier writer Papias, who names John twice, once beside Peter, James and Matthew and once as "the presbyter John"; Eusebius concludes that there were two men of that name, notes two tombs of John at Ephesus and suggests that the second John wrote the Revelation [[cite:papias]]. Whether the apostle wrote the Gospel, the three letters and the Revelation that bear the name John is debated; see [[event:writing-of-the-gospels]] and [[person:john-of-patmos]].
