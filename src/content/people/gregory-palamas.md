Palamas was a priest-monk when [[person:barlaam-of-calabria]] denounced him and the hesychast monks to the patriarch [[cite:tome1341]]. The monks of [[place:mount-athos]] call him their brother and endorse his treatise in defence of those who practise stillness [[cite:hagioretic]]. In that work, the Triads, he argues that God's essence cannot be shared but that his uncreated energies can, and that God is wholly present in them [[cite:triads]].

By 1351 he was metropolitan of [[place:thessalonica]]. He spoke for himself at the council of that year, signed its tome, and was declared in it a defender of the church and of the faith [[cite:tome1351]]. The encyclical of the Orthodox council held on Crete in 2016 names the councils held in his time among those of universal authority [[cite:crete]]. See [[event:hesychast-controversy]].
