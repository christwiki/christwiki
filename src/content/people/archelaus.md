Archelaus was the son whom [[person:herod-the-great]] named in his last will as heir to the throne [[cite:will]]. Before he could sail to Rome to have the will confirmed, a protest in the temple at Passover ended with his troops killing about three thousand people [[cite:passover]]. [[person:augustus]] gave him half of his father's kingdom, with the lesser title of ethnarch and the promise of the crown if he governed well [[cite:ethnarch]]; see [[event:death-of-herod]]. Matthew reports that Joseph, returning from Egypt, was afraid to settle in Judea when he heard that Archelaus reigned there [[bible:Matt 2:22]].

[[person:josephus]] says that he deposed high priests, rebuilt the palace at [[place:jericho]], and married his brother's widow against Jewish law. In the tenth year of his rule the leading men of Judea and Samaria accused him of cruelty before the emperor, who banished him to Vienne in Gaul [[cite:rule]]. Cassius Dio dates the sentence to AD 6 [[cite:dio]]. His territory then passed under a Roman governor; see [[event:judea-becomes-a-roman-province]].
