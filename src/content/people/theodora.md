During the Nika riot of 532, when the court was debating whether to escape by ship, Procopius has Theodora speak against flight and declare that she would not live to be seen without the purple; her words, he says, gave the others courage to resist [[cite:speech]]. Procopius gives her words "to the following effect", not as a verbatim record.

Evagrius, a supporter of Chalcedon, writes that [[person:justinian-i]] upheld the council while Theodora took the part of those who confessed one nature in Christ, took the greatest care of them, and persuaded the emperor to send for Severus. He is unsure whether the two truly differed or had agreed to divide their favour [[cite:evagrius]]. She died of an illness after twenty-one years and three months as empress [[cite:death]].
