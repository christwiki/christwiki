Shalmaneser III, son of Ashurnasirpal II, ruled Assyria from [[place:kalhu]] and spent most of his reign trying to bring the kingdoms west of the Euphrates under tribute. The Bible does not name him, but his inscriptions name two kings of Israel.

His stela from Kurkh lists [[person:ahab]] among the twelve kings who fought him at [[place:qarqar]] in his sixth year [[cite:kurkh]]; see [[event:battle-of-qarqar]]. In his eighteenth year he defeated [[person:hazael]] of Damascus and received tribute from [[person:jehu]] of Israel [[cite:annals]], a scene carved on the Black Obelisk he set up at Kalhu [[cite:caption]]; see [[event:jehu-pays-tribute]]. Because his years can be fixed from the Assyrian eponym lists, these two notices give the earliest fixed dates in the history of the Hebrew kings.
