John Foxe tells that Tyndale said he would make the boy who drives the plough know more of Scripture than a learned opponent, that he sought and failed to find a place with the bishop of [[place:london]], and that he left for Germany and afterwards lived mostly at [[place:antwerp]] [[cite:foxe]]. His New Testament was printed at [[place:worms]] in 1526. In its closing address he says that he had no earlier English version to follow and asks the learned to amend what is amiss [[cite:reader]]. Two years later he argued that Scripture ought to be in the mother tongue and that Greek agrees better with English than with Latin [[cite:obedience]].

The Catholic writer Johannes Cochlaeus called him an apostate and his book Luther's New Testament [[cite:cochlaeus]]. Foxe relates that he was betrayed, held at [[place:vilvoorde]] for a year and a half, condemned under the emperor's decree, and strangled and burned there in 1536 [[cite:death]].
