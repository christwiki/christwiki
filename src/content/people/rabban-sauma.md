The History says that Sauma was the long-awaited son of a Christian nobleman of Khanbaliq, that he was ordained and betrothed, and that at twenty he gave up the world. He received the monastic tonsure from the metropolitan, shut himself in a cell for seven years and then lived in a cave in the mountains a day's journey from the city, where Mark joined him [[cite:origin]]. In Rome he described himself as a deacon in the cell of the catholicos, director of the disciples and visitor-general [[cite:office]]. The papal letter calls him "Bersauma, bishop in the parts of the East" [[cite:letter]].

When the Mongol king Arghun asked for an envoy to the kings of the West, the catholicos chose him because he knew the language [[cite:mission]]. In Rome he received communion from [[person:nicholas-iv]] on Palm Sunday [[cite:pope]]. The History says that he fell ill at a royal feast and dates his death to 10 January in the year 1605 of the Greeks, AD 1294; he was buried, it says, among the patriarchs [[cite:death]].
