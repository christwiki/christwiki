Eudoxia was the wife of the emperor Arcadius, who ruled the East from [[place:constantinople]] after the death of [[person:theodosius-i]] [[cite:arcadius]]. Socrates says that she took a sermon of [[person:john-chrysostom]] against the vices of women as aimed at herself, complained to the emperor, and so brought about the synod that deposed him [[cite:oak]]. Sozomen tells the same story but adds that he could not establish whether the sermon was meant for her [[cite:sozomen]]. Among the charges the synod sent to the emperor was that John had called the empress Jezebel [[cite:jezebel]].

When John afterwards denounced the games held around her silver statue near the church, she again moved against him, and he was exiled for the second time in June 404 [[cite:statue]]. She died that autumn, four days after a hailstorm on 30 September which John's supporters took as a sign of divine anger [[cite:death]]. Theodoret relates that her son, the emperor [[person:theodosius-ii]], later prayed over John's coffin for the pardon of his parents [[cite:relics]]. See [[event:john-chrysostom]].
