Licinius came from New Dacia and was of humble birth, according to an anonymous Latin life of Constantine [[cite:origo-origin]]. Galerius made him emperor in the place of Severus [[cite:lact-made]]. He married Constantia, the sister of [[person:constantine]], at [[place:milan]] [[cite:origo-origin]], and after defeating Maximinus Daia he had the letter granting freedom of worship posted at [[place:nicomedia]] in June 313 [[cite:lact-letter]].

Eusebius, who lived under his rule, reports that Licinius later dismissed Christians from his household and army, restricted the churches and had bishops in Pontus put to death [[cite:eusebius]]. Constantine defeated him by land and sea. His life was spared at Constantia's request, and he was then killed at Thessalonica on Constantine's orders [[cite:origo-end]].
