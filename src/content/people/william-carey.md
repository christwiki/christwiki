The first record of the Baptist Missionary Society traces its origin to Carey, whose mind, it says, had been set on the conversion of the heathen since before he came to live at Moulton in 1786; it notes his thirst for geography and his aptitude for languages [[cite:moulton]]. He wrote the *Enquiry* there and published it in 1792, proposing a society to send missionaries [[cite:enquiry]]. That May he preached at Nottingham on expecting and attempting great things [[cite:sermon]], and in October he was named to the committee of the society formed at [[place:kettering]] [[cite:committee]].

He embarked for India with his family on 13 June 1793 [[cite:embarked]]. The agreement he and his colleagues made at [[place:serampore]] in 1805 commits them to translate the Scriptures into the languages of India and to raise up Indian preachers and churches [[cite:form]].
