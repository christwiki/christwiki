Paul describes himself as an Israelite of the tribe of Benjamin, circumcised on the eighth day, a Pharisee, and a persecutor of the church [[bible:Phil 3:5-6]] [[bible:Gal 1:13-14]]. Acts adds that he was born at Tarsus in Cilicia, was a Roman citizen by birth, studied in [[place:jerusalem|Jerusalem]] under Gamaliel and worked as a tentmaker; it calls him Saul until his first missionary journey [[bible:Acts 22:3, 25-28]] [[bible:Acts 18:3]] [[bible:Acts 13:9]]. Near [[place:damascus]] he became a follower of Jesus, an event he describes as God revealing his Son to him [[bible:Gal 1:15-16]]; see [[event:conversion-of-paul]].

From [[place:antioch]] he travelled through Cyprus, Asia Minor, Macedonia and Greece, founding churches and writing to them; see [[event:first-missionary-journey]], [[event:second-missionary-journey]], [[event:third-missionary-journey]] and [[event:earliest-christian-letters]]. He called himself an apostle to the Gentiles [[bible:Rom 11:13]]. Acts ends with him at [[place:rome|Rome]], where he had been sent as a prisoner, teaching for two years in his own rented house [[bible:Acts 28:30-31]]. The letter of the Roman church known as 1 Clement, about AD 96, says that he reached "the extreme limit of the west" and died a martyr [[cite:clement]], and [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]], writing in the early fourth century, reports that he was beheaded at Rome under [[person:nero|Nero]] [[cite:eusebius]]. The dates of his death given here are the traditional range; see [[event:martyrdom-of-peter-and-paul]].
