John VIII was pope from 872 to 882 [[cite:dates]]. The letters cited here come from the register of his correspondence ([[source:john-viii-letters]]).

In August 879 he wrote to the emperor [[person:basil-i]] that he received [[person:photius]] as patriarch of [[place:constantinople]], on condition that Photius ask for mercy before a synod, that no layman be made patriarch in future and that Photius claim no authority in Bulgaria [[cite:terms]]. The Roman see, he argued, has the power to loose what bishops have bound, as it had done for Athanasius and John Chrysostom [[cite:keys]]. After [[event:council-of-879|the council of 879–880]] he wrote to Photius that he accepted what had been done for his restoration and rejected anything his legates might have done against their instructions [[cite:reply]].

Bulgaria runs through these letters. In 878 he urged its ruler [[person:boris-i]] to turn from the Greeks and return to Saint Peter [[cite:boris]], and ordered the Greek clergy there to leave [[cite:greeks]]. In 880 he thanked the emperors for allowing Saint Peter to have the Bulgarian diocese [[cite:thanks]]. His letters about Methodius and the Slavonic liturgy are treated under [[event:cyril-and-methodius]].
