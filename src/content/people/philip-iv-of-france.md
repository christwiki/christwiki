Giovanni Villani, a Florentine contemporary, tells how the king arrested the bishop of Pamiers, kept the revenues of vacant sees, refused to let the French bishops go to the pope's court and had [[person:boniface-viii]] accused of heresy before an assembly at Paris, and how his envoy William of Nogaret organized the seizure of the pope at Anagni in 1303 [[cite:quarrel]]. Villani also reports, as something done in secret, a bargain by which Philip secured the election of the archbishop of Bordeaux as [[person:clement-v]] in return for promised favours [[cite:clement]]. Clement declared in 1306 that the bull [[event:unam-sanctam]] did the king and kingdom no prejudice [[cite:meruit]].

Villani dates Philip's death to November 1314, after a fall from his horse while hunting, when he had reigned twenty-nine years [[cite:death]].
