Hadrian was born on 24 January in the year when Vespasian was consul for the seventh time, AD 76, and was brought up as a ward of [[person:trajan]], whom he succeeded [[cite:birth]]. He ruled for almost twenty-one years and died at sixty-two [[cite:death]]. Cassius Dio says that he founded Aelia Capitolina on the site of [[place:jerusalem]], that this brought on a Jewish revolt, and that his generals crushed it [[cite:aelia]]. See [[event:bar-kokhba-revolt]].

[[person:justin-martyr]] appends to his *Apology* a letter of Hadrian to a provincial governor, whom Eusebius names as Minucius Fundanus, proconsul of Asia. It rules that Christians are to be punished only if an accuser proves in court that they have broken the law, and that false accusers are themselves to be punished [[cite:rescript]] [[cite:rescript-eusebius]]. Eusebius also says that Quadratus and Aristides addressed defences of the faith to him [[cite:apologists]].
