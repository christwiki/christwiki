The Catholic bishops of Uganda record that Lwanga was born about 1861 in Buddu, that he was head of the king's pages, and that on Thursday 3 June 1886 he was separated from the other prisoners at Namugongo and burned in a slow fire [[cite:life]]. John Paul II, preaching at [[place:namugongo]] in 1993, called it the place of "the great fire which consumed Saint Charles Lwanga and his companions" [[cite:shrine]]. Pope Francis said there in 2015 that Lwanga and Joseph Mukasa, having been taught the faith by others, wanted to pass it on, at a time when their own lives and those of the younger boys in their care were in danger [[cite:pages]].

Benedict XV beatified Lwanga and twenty-one others on 6 June 1920, and Paul VI canonized them on 18 October 1964 [[cite:canonized]]. See [[event:uganda-martyrs]].
