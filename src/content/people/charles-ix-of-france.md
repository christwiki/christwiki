Charles was the son of [[person:catherine-de-medici]] and had come to the throne as a minor [[cite:marguerite-4]]. When [[person:gaspard-de-coligny]] was shot on 22 August 1572, an English correspondent in [[place:paris]] wrote that the king took it "very grievously", offered the admiral his own guards and had the gates of the city kept [[cite:csp-538]].

His sister Marguerite says that he esteemed Coligny and other Huguenot captains, wished to punish the duke of Guise for the attack, and gave his consent to the massacre only when persuaded that his own life and the safety of the kingdom depended on it [[cite:marguerite-4]] [[cite:marguerite-5]]. De Thou reports that the killers were told they acted by the king's command [[cite:de-thou]]. An English report of September says that when the governor of Rouen asked whether the Protestants there were to be killed too, the king swore that it was his mind, though he would give no written order, and that he rode to Montfaucon to see the admiral's body on the gibbet [[cite:csp-584]].
