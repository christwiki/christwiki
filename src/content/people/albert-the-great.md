William of Tocco says that when [[person:thomas-aquinas]] was sent to Cologne, the order's school there flourished under brother Albert, a master in theology who was held to be without equal in every branch of learning. Albert heard the young man answer in a disputation, is reported to have foretold his fame, and later wrote to the master of the order to have him made bachelor at [[place:paris]] [[cite:cologne]].

Bartholomew of Capua testified in 1319 to what he had heard from a Dominican who was with Albert in his old age. Albert wept when he learned of Thomas's death. When word came that Thomas's writings were under attack at Paris, he insisted, against the advice of his brethren, on travelling there, spoke in the Dominican school in their defence, and went back to Cologne [[cite:defence]].
