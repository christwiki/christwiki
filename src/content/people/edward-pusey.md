Pusey was born at Pusey House in Berkshire on 22 August 1800 [[cite:birth]]. [[person:john-henry-newman]] says that Pusey's tract on fasting appeared in the series with the date 21 December 1833, that he was fully part of the movement from 1835 and 1836, when he published his tract on baptism and started the Library of the Fathers, and that as a professor and canon of Christ Church he gave the movement "a position and a name" [[cite:joined]].

On 14 May 1843 he preached before the university on the Eucharist [[cite:suspended]]. The vice-chancellor, with six doctors of divinity, found that he had taught things contrary to the doctrine of the Church of England and on 2 June suspended him from preaching within the university for two years [[cite:suspended]]. His *Life* says that the claims of the catholic church, which led Newman to Rome, kept Pusey steadfast in the Anglican communion [[cite:stayed]]. He died on 16 September 1882 [[cite:death]].
