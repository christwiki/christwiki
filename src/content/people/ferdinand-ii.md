The Edict of Restitution is issued in his name as elected Roman emperor, at Vienna on 6 March 1629, in what it calls the tenth year of his reign over the Empire and the twelfth over Bohemia [[cite:restitution]]. In it he sets out the Catholic reading of the Religious Peace of 1555 and sends commissioners to recover the bishoprics, monasteries and church property lost since the Treaty of Passau [[cite:restitution]].

The treaty of Osnabrück names him as the emperor against whom King [[person:gustavus-adolphus]] of Sweden went to war, and says that the war went on under his successor Ferdinand III after both men had died [[cite:preamble]]. The same treaty fixed 1 January 1624 as the date for the possession of church property and forbade anyone to invoke the edict of 1629 against the peace [[cite:set-aside]].
