Ignatius opens each letter as "Ignatius, who is also called Theophorus" and speaks of himself as "the bishop of Syria" [[cite:rom-greeting]] [[cite:rom-2]]. Eusebius, two centuries later, lists him as the second bishop of [[place:antioch]] after Evodius [[cite:eusebius-22]]. He calls himself the last of the Christians of Syria and "one born out of due time", using Paul's words about himself [[cite:rom-9]] [[bible:1 Cor 15:8-9]].

His letters all come from one journey: see [[event:martyrdom-of-ignatius]]. Eusebius reports as tradition that he was killed by wild beasts at [[place:rome]] [[cite:eusebius-36]] and places him in the reign of [[person:trajan]] [[cite:eusebius-22]]; the date of his death shown here rests on that later tradition.
