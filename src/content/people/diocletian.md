Diocletian shared the empire with three colleagues, an arrangement that Lactantius blames for multiplying armies and taxes [[cite:four]]. For most of his reign the churches were left in peace: Eusebius says that Christians governed provinces and served in the palace, and that large new churches were built in every city [[cite:peace]].

Lactantius, who lived at his court city of [[place:nicomedia]], presents him as a cautious ruler pushed into persecution by his junior colleague [[person:galerius]] and by an oracle of Apollo, and says that he wished it to be carried through without bloodshed [[cite:council]]. The first edict was issued in the nineteenth year of his reign [[cite:edict]]. After celebrating his twentieth year at Rome he fell gravely ill, and at a ceremony outside Nicomedia he laid down the purple and took back his old name of Diocles [[cite:abdication]]. Lactantius says that he lived to see his portraits taken down and died worn out by grief, unable to eat or rest [[cite:end]].
