Schleiermacher's own account of his life begins with his birth at Breslau on 21 November 1768, the son of a Reformed army chaplain [[cite:birth]]. The editors of his letters record that in 1796 he was appointed chaplain to the Charité in [[place:berlin]], that he went to Stolpe as court preacher in 1802, and that in 1804 he became professor and university preacher at Halle [[cite:posts]]. He finished the *Speeches on Religion* on 15 April 1799 [[cite:finished]]. They define true religion as "sense and taste for the Infinite" [[cite:second]], and his later dogmatics defines piety as the consciousness of absolute dependence [[cite:faith]].
