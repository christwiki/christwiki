Leo's Tome, written in June 449 to Flavian of Constantinople, sets out that Christ is one person in whom the divine and the human nature each keep their own properties [[cite:tome]]. He did not attend [[event:council-of-chalcedon]] but sent legates, in whom, he wrote, the bishops should consider him to be presiding [[cite:legates]]. When the Tome was read there the bishops cried out that Peter had spoken through Leo [[cite:acclamation]]. He then declared the canon that raised the see of Constantinople void "by the blessed Apostle Peter's authority" [[cite:canon-28]].

The *Liber Pontificalis*, compiled in the next century, says that he was a Tuscan, that he held the see for twenty-one years, and that he went out to meet Attila, king of the Huns, and so delivered Italy from danger [[cite:lp]].
