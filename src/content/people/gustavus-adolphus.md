The treaty of Osnabrück names him, as king of the Swedes, Goths and Vandals, as the first opponent of the emperor [[person:ferdinand-ii]] in the war that it ends, and records that the war continued under his successor Queen Christina after his death [[cite:preamble]].

A broadsheet printed by his own side after the battle of Lützen, fought on 6 November 1632 by the old calendar, tells how he rode in too far among the enemy with his cavalry, was mortally wounded and was found afterwards with five shots in his body [[cite:lutzen]]. It puts into his mouth the words that, if he fell, he would die for the honour of God, for God's word and for German liberty [[cite:lutzen]]. See [[event:thirty-years-war]].
