[[person:josephus]] says that Flavius Silva succeeded Bassus as governor of Judaea and gathered his forces against [[place:masada]] [[cite:command]]. He walled the rock round, built a ramp against its western side, broke the wall with a ram and burned the defenders' second wall [[cite:siege]]. After the fortress fell he left a garrison in it and went back to Caesarea [[cite:after]]; see [[event:fall-of-masada|the fall of Masada]].

Two copies of an inscription from Urbs Salvia in Italy give the career of a Silva Nonius Bassus who is taken to be the same man. The surviving text calls him consul and pontifex, has the words "of the province of Judaea", and says that he was enrolled among the patricians, commanded the legion XXI Rapax and was patron of the colony. His first names and the title of his post in Judaea are restored by the editors [[cite:inscription]].
