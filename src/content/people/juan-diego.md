The *Nican Mopohua*, printed in 1649, calls Juan Diego a poor commoner who lived at Cuauhtitlan and belonged to the church at Tlatelolco, and tells how the Virgin Mary appeared to him on the hill of [[place:tepeyac]] early in December 1531 [[cite:account]]; the story is told under [[event:our-lady-of-guadalupe]].

What else is said of him comes from the tradition the Catholic Church accepts. The biography issued by the Holy See gives his native name as Cuauhtlatoatzin, the years 1474 and 1548 for his birth and death, and says that he lived his last years as a hermit beside the chapel at Tepeyac; it records that [[person:john-paul-ii]] beatified him on 6 May 1990 [[cite:life]]. The same pope declared him a saint on 31 July 2002 and called him the first indigenous saint of the American continent [[cite:canonization]]. Historians who doubt that the apparition tradition goes back to the 1530s are cited in the dating note of the event.
