Prosper calls Pelagius a Briton [[cite:briton]]. The synod in Palestine, in the verdict that [[person:augustine]] quotes, calls him a monk [[cite:acquittal]], and Augustine says that he first heard of him as a man much praised at Rome [[cite:africa]]. His letter to Demetrias, the fullest of his surviving writings, teaches that human nature was created good, that the power to choose between good and evil is God's gift, and that a just God has commanded nothing impossible [[cite:demetrias]]. In a work quoted by Augustine he distinguished the ability to do good, which comes from God, from the will and the deed, which are our own [[cite:faculties]].

He passed through Africa and went on to Palestine, where a synod of fourteen bishops accepted his answers and declared him in communion with the church [[cite:africa]] [[cite:acquittal]]. The African bishops judged his teaching otherwise, and in 418 it was condemned at Carthage and, Prosper says, throughout the world [[cite:canons]] [[cite:prosper418]].
