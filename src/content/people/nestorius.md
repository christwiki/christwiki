Nestorius, a native of Germanicia, was brought from Antioch for his reputation as a preacher and ordained bishop of [[place:constantinople]] on 10 April 428 [[cite:appointment]]. When one of his presbyters preached against calling Mary *Theotokos*, "God-bearer", he took the same side in his own sermons [[cite:theotokos]]. In his reply to [[person:cyril-of-alexandria]] he argued that Scripture ascribes birth and suffering to Christ's humanity and not to the godhead, that the name Christ covers both natures in one person, and that Mary is therefore more exactly called mother of Christ [[cite:letter]].

The council that met under Cyril at [[place:ephesus]] in 431 deposed him, and he was banished to the Oasis, where Socrates says he was still living when he wrote [[cite:synod]]. Contemporaries judged him differently. Prosper, in the West, wrote that he preached Christ as a mere man [[cite:prosper]]. Socrates, who had read his sermons, denied this and thought that he had simply taken fright at a word [[cite:theotokos]]. See [[event:council-of-ephesus]].
