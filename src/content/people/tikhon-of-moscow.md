Tikhon announced his accession in a letter of 18 December 1917: the Council sitting in [[place:moscow]] had resolved to give the Russian church the head it had lacked for more than two centuries, had named candidates, and the lot had fallen on him [[cite:accession]]. He took his seat in the Dormition Cathedral of the Kremlin on 21 November 1917, saying that the patriarchate was being restored in terrible days and that he was unworthy of it [[cite:address]]. Both dates are in the Julian calendar.

His message of 19 January 1918 denounced the killing of innocent people and the seizure of churches and monasteries, pronounced anathema on those responsible if they still called themselves Christians, and summoned the faithful to defend the church by faith and by suffering [[cite:message]]. In February 1922 he permitted the giving of church valuables for famine relief but condemned the forced removal of consecrated vessels [[cite:message-1922]]. Lenin, in a secret letter of that March, held him responsible for the resistance and advised that he himself be left untouched and that his communications be watched [[cite:lenin]].
