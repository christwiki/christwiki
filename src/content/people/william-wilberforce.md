Wilberforce was born at Hull on 24 August 1759 [[cite:birth]]. His sons, who wrote his life from his papers, quote him as saying that in his first years in Parliament his own distinction was his "darling object", and they print the entry in his journal for 28 October 1787: "God Almighty has set before me two great objects, the suppression of the slave trade and the reformation of manners" [[cite:journal]].

In 1787, at a dinner which [[person:thomas-clarkson]] describes, he said that he would bring the question before Parliament when he was better prepared for it, if no one more fit could be found [[cite:pledge]], and on 12 May 1789 he brought it before the House of Commons [[cite:motion]]. See [[event:abolition-of-the-slave-trade]]. In 1797, as member for the county of York, he published *A Practical View*, which sets what he calls real Christianity against the religion of the higher and middle classes [[cite:view]]. He died on 29 July 1833 [[cite:death]].
