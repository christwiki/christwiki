The History of Rabban Sauma says that the pope chosen while the envoy waited at Genoa was Nicholas, the bishop who had spoken with him when he first came to Rome. It describes him blessing palms, preaching, washing the feet of his household on the Thursday before Easter and giving [[person:rabban-sauma]] communion with his own hand [[cite:history]].

His letters to the Mongol king Arghun are dated on 2 April in his first year, 1288. They set out the faith, call himself the vicar of Christ and successor of Peter, and urge the king to be baptised without delay [[cite:to-arghun]]. His letter to [[person:yahballaha-iii]], five days later, commends the Friars Minor working in the East and encloses a profession of faith [[cite:to-yahballaha]]. See [[event:rabban-sauma]].
