Elizabeth was proclaimed queen in [[place:london]] on 17 November 1558, hours after the death of her sister [[person:mary-i-of-england]] [[cite:accession]]. The statutes of her first Parliament ended the pope's jurisdiction in England, required an oath that she was "the only supreme governor" of the realm in spiritual as in temporal things [[cite:oath]], and restored the English Prayer Book [[cite:uniformity]]. The Articles of Religion issued under her explain that this government does not include the ministry of the word or of the sacraments [[cite:articles]].

In 1570 Pope Pius V called her "the pretended queen" of England, declared her a heretic and released her subjects from their allegiance [[cite:regnans]]. The laws that followed treated Catholic priests in England as traitors [[cite:act-1585]]. See [[event:elizabethan-settlement]].
