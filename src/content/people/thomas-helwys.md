[[person:john-robinson]] names Helwys as the first person [[person:john-smyth]] baptized after baptizing himself [[cite:robinson]]. When Smyth came to doubt what they had done, Helwys held to it and accused him of sinning against the Holy Spirit [[cite:last-book]]. The confession printed by his party in 1611 defines the church as believers joined by baptism on their own confession of faith and denies baptism to infants [[cite:declaration]].

In 1612 he called on those who had fled abroad to come and lay down their lives in their own country [[cite:flight]], and Robinson, writing in 1614, speaks of him and his people as being in London [[cite:robinson]]. His book of 1612 says that the king has no authority over anyone's religion and that it is not for the earthly power to punish heretics, Turks or Jews [[cite:conscience]].
