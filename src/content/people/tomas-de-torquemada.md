He appears in a letter of [[person:sixtus-iv]] of 11 February 1482 as "Thomas de Turrecremata", bachelor of theology, one of eight friars of the Order of Preachers whom the pope made inquisitors in Castile and León. The pope says that the men had been recommended to him by the envoy of the king and queen, a Dominican [[cite:named]].

On 29 November 1484 inquisitors and lawyers met at [[place:seville]], called together by order of the king and queen and by him. The record of the meeting calls him prior of the monastery of Santa Cruz at Segovia, confessor of [[person:ferdinand-ii-of-aragon]] and [[person:isabella-i-of-castile]], and inquisitor general in their name [[cite:preamble]]. The collection of the Inquisition's rules printed in 1667 calls him on its title page the first inquisitor general of the kingdoms and lordships of Spain [[cite:title]].

The articles agreed at that meeting set a term of grace of thirty or forty days, in which those who confessed fully would escape death, perpetual prison and confiscation [[cite:grace]]. They also laid on the reconciled public abjuration and lifelong exclusion from public office, and took the property of those who came forward late [[cite:penalties]]. See [[event:spanish-inquisition]].
