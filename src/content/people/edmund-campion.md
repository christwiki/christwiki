William Allen's account says that Campion was born in [[place:london]], studied at St John's College in [[place:oxford]] and was ordained deacon in the English church. Regretting that step, he went to the seminary at Douai and then to [[place:rome]], entered the Society of Jesus, spent eight years in Bohemia and was ordained priest at Prague. Allen says that he lived about forty-two years, which is the basis of the birth year given here [[cite:life]].

He landed at Dover in June 1580 [[cite:arrival]]. In a paper addressed to the Privy Council he said that he had come to preach and to minister the sacraments, not to meddle with the state, and asked to dispute with the Protestant divines [[cite:challenge]]. He was arrested on 17 July 1581 [[cite:arrest]], convicted in November of plotting against the queen [[cite:trial]], and executed at Tyburn on 1 December 1581 [[cite:death]]. The government's tract *The Execution of Justice* says that he "suffered for his Treasons" [[cite:cecil]]; Allen says that he died "for the Catholike Faith and religion" [[cite:death]]. Pope Paul VI canonised him in 1970 [[cite:paul-vi]]. See [[event:regnans-in-excelsis]].
