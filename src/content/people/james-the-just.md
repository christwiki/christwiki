Mark and Matthew name James first among the brothers of Jesus [[bible:Mark 6:3]] [[bible:Matt 13:55]]. [[person:paul|Paul]] lists him among those to whom the risen Christ appeared, met him on his first visit to [[place:jerusalem]] after his conversion, calls him "the Lord's brother", and names him with Cephas and [[person:john-the-apostle|John]] as those "reputed to be pillars" [[bible:1 Cor 15:7]] [[bible:Gal 1:19]] [[bible:Gal 2:9]]. In Acts he is the leading figure of the Jerusalem church from the time of [[person:peter]]'s escape from prison: he gives the judgement at the [[event:council-of-jerusalem]] and receives Paul on his last visit [[bible:Acts 12:17]] [[bible:Acts 15:13-21]] [[bible:Acts 21:18]]. The New Testament letter of James opens in the name of "James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ", without saying which James is meant [[bible:Jas 1:1]].

[[person:josephus|Josephus]] says that the high priest Ananus had "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James" condemned and stoned, in the interval between two Roman governors [[cite:josephus]]; on the year, AD 62, see [[event:death-of-james-the-just]]. Hegesippus, a Christian writer of the second century quoted by [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]], says that everyone called him "the Just" [[cite:hegesippus]]. Early Christians explained the word "brother" in three ways: Helvidius, as [[person:jerome|Jerome]] reports him, took the brothers of Jesus to be younger children of Mary and Joseph, and Jerome argued that they were cousins [[cite:jerome]]; a second-century narrative makes Joseph an old man who already had children [[cite:protoevangelium]].

He is not [[person:james-son-of-zebedee]], one of the Twelve, whom [[person:herod-agrippa-i|Herod Agrippa]] put to death [[bible:Acts 12:2]].
