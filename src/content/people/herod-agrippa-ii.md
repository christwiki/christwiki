Agrippa was in his seventeenth year and living at the court of Claudius in Rome when his father, [[person:herod-agrippa-i]], died. Claudius judged him too young to succeed and sent a Roman governor to Judaea instead [[cite:youth]]. He was later given the former tetrarchy of Philip with neighbouring districts, and Nero added Tiberias and other towns [[cite:kingdom]] [[cite:nero]]. Judaea itself stayed under Roman governors, but Josephus says that Claudius had entrusted Agrippa with the care of the temple, and he appointed and removed its high priests [[cite:temple]].

Acts calls him King Agrippa. He came to [[place:caesarea-maritima]] with Bernice, who was his sister [[cite:youth]], to greet the new governor [[person:festus]], heard the defence of [[person:paul]], and said that the prisoner could have been freed if he had not appealed to Caesar [[bible:Acts 25:13-26:32]]. Paul addressed him as a man expert in Jewish customs and controversies [[bible:Acts 26:2-3]].

When revolt threatened in AD 66 he urged the people of Jerusalem to submit and was driven from the city [[cite:revolt]]. Tacitus lists him among the allied princes who accompanied the army of [[person:titus]] against Jerusalem [[cite:titus]]. [[person:josephus]] later sent him his history of the war and quotes two of the king's letters approving it [[cite:letters]].
