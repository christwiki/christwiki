Yahya of Antioch says that Basil's treasury was empty and the rebel's army at the gates of the capital when he turned to the king of the Rus', until then an enemy. The marriage alliance and the baptism were its terms, and the Rus' troops helped to defeat the rebel at Chrysopolis and Abydos [[cite:yahya]]. Psellus, writing later, remembers the picked foreign troops who arrived in time [[cite:psellus]].

The Primary Chronicle names Basil and his brother and co-emperor Constantine as the emperors who would not give their sister to a pagan and who sent her to [[person:vladimir-the-great]] with priests once he agreed to be baptized [[cite:chronicle]]. See [[event:baptism-of-rus]].
