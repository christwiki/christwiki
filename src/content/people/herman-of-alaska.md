Herman was one of the monks of Valaam who reached [[place:kodiak]] in 1794 [[cite:mission]]. His superior wrote in 1795 that he had him working in the bakery [[cite:bakery]], and Herman's own letter of that year to Abbot Nazarius describes the baptisms on Kodiak and his joy at the zeal of the priest-monks [[cite:letter]]. By 1818 he lived on Spruce Island, where, according to the Russian official Yanovsky, he grew his own food and Aleuts came to him for advice and with their grievances [[cite:spruce]].

The Valaam history says that he died on 13 December 1837, by the Julian calendar, in the eighty-first year of his life [[cite:death]]; the year of his birth is reckoned from that. Archbishop [[person:innocent-of-alaska]] wrote in 1867 that he had once asked Herman's help in a contrary wind off Spruce Island and had later prayed at his grave [[cite:innocent-letter]].
