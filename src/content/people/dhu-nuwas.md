The name by which this king is usually known comes from Arabic tradition [[cite:name]]. The inscription of one of his commanders calls him Yusuf As'ar Yath'ar, king of all the tribes, and says that he burned the church and killed the Abyssinians at Zafar, made war on [[place:najran]] and guarded the coast against an Abyssinian landing; it ends by invoking the Lord of the Jews [[cite:ja-1028]]. The Syriac *Book of the Himyarites* calls him Masruq, and the Greek *Martyrdom of Arethas* Dunaas [[cite:masruq]] [[cite:names]].

In the letter of Simeon of Beth Arsham the king himself, writing to a Saracen ruler, is made to say that he seized the throne when the king set up by the Ethiopians died, and that he resolved to kill all Christians who would not become Jews [[cite:letter]]. That is a Christian bishop's report of a letter he heard read. Procopius says that the king of the Ethiopians defeated the Himyarites and killed their king [[cite:procopius]], and a Himyarite inscription speaks of the Abyssinians killing the king of Himyar without naming him [[cite:cih-621]]. See [[event:martyrs-of-najran]].
