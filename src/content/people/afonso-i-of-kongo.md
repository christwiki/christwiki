According to Rui de Pina, in the weeks before the queen of Kongo was baptised in June 1491 the prince, the king's son, was away in his own lands and the king was waiting for him; Pina does not give the prince's name [[cite:queen]]. The proclamation that Afonso issued in 1512 as "king of Manicongo" tells his own story: he embraced Christian teaching as a youth, was exiled by his father for it, and on his father's death came to the capital with thirty-six men and defeated the brother who had taken the kingdom. The enemy, it says, saw a white cross in the air and the apostle James with armed horsemen, and the arms that the king of Portugal sent him recalled the vision [[cite:afonso-1512]].

His letter of 6 July 1526 to John III of Portugal, written "from our city of Congo", asks for priests, schoolmasters, and wine and flour for the mass, and protests that merchants are seizing free people, nobles and his own relatives among them, and that the country is being depopulated [[cite:afonso-1526]].
