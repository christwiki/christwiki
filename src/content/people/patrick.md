Patrick says that he was the son of a deacon, Calpornius, from a place in Britain called Bannavem Taburniae, and that he was captured at about sixteen and taken to Ireland [[cite:home]]. After six years as a herdsman he escaped by ship [[cite:escape]]. He returned as a bishop [[cite:bishop]] and writes of baptising thousands, ordaining clergy and travelling to places where no one had yet come to baptise [[cite:work]].

He gives no dates. The Irish annals, compiled much later, place his arrival in 432 and enter his death under both 461 and 493 [[cite:annals]]. The stories of his contest with the druids at Tara and his founding of [[place:armagh]] are told in the Life by Muirchú, written some two hundred years after his time [[cite:muirchu]].
