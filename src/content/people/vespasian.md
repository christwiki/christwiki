Vespasian was born in the Sabine country in AD 9 [[cite:birth]] and made his name commanding a legion in the conquest of Britain under Claudius [[cite:britain]]. When the governor of Syria was defeated by the Jewish rebels, [[person:nero]] chose him to restore Roman control [[cite:command]]. In two summers, Tacitus says, he recovered the whole country except [[place:jerusalem]] [[cite:tacitus]].

After Nero's death and a year of civil war the legions of Egypt and Judaea swore allegiance to him in July 69 [[cite:accession]]. He left the war to his son [[person:titus]] [[cite:tacitus]]. After the triumph he built a Temple of Peace and placed in it the golden vessels taken from the Jerusalem temple [[cite:peace]], and he ordered Jews everywhere to pay to the Capitol the two drachmas that they had paid each year to their own temple [[cite:tax]]. He died in June 79 [[cite:death]].

Eusebius, in the early fourth century, did not count him among the persecutors: he says that Vespasian undertook nothing against the church [[cite:eusebius]]. See [[event:destruction-of-the-second-temple]].
