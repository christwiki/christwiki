His Life says that he governed a Slavic district for the emperor before becoming a monk on Mount Olympus, and that he went with his brother first to the Khazars and then to Moravia [[cite:early]]. After his brother's death he was sent back by Pope [[person:hadrian-ii]], made bishop for Pannonia at the request of the prince Kocel, tried by the Frankish king's bishops and held for two and a half years until the pope intervened [[cite:trial]].

Pope John VIII summoned him to Rome in 879 on reports that he taught otherwise than he had professed and sang mass in Slavonic [[cite:summons]], and the next year declared him orthodox and confirmed him as archbishop [[cite:cleared]]. He translated the books of Scripture that he and his brother had not already done, and died on 6 April 885; his funeral was held in Latin, Greek and Slavonic [[cite:death]].
