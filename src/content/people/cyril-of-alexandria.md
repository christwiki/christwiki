Cyril was the nephew of Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, and succeeded him in October 412 after a contested election. Socrates remarks that from his time the bishops of Alexandria reached beyond their priestly office into the government of the city [[cite:election]].

When [[person:nestorius]] rejected the title *Theotokos* for Mary, Cyril wrote to him that the Word of God had personally united flesh to himself, that there is therefore one Christ and one Son, and that the fathers were right to call the Virgin Mother of God [[cite:letter]]. He opened the council at [[place:ephesus]] in 431 before the bishops of Syria arrived, and it deposed Nestorius [[cite:synod]] [[cite:decree]]. In 433 he made peace with John of Antioch on a statement that speaks of a union of two natures in the one Christ [[cite:reunion]]. The Council of Chalcedon named him, with Celestine of Rome, as leader of the council of Ephesus and received his synodical letters to Nestorius and to the eastern bishops [[cite:chalcedon]].
