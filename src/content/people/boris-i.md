A Byzantine history compiled in the tenth century calls him Bogoris and says that he was baptized by a bishop sent from [[place:constantinople]] and named Michael after the emperor [[cite:baptism]]. [[person:photius]] wrote to him as to a spiritual son and compared what he had done to the deeds of Constantine the Great [[cite:letter]]. The Annals of St Bertin, a Frankish chronicle of his own time, tell how his nobles rose against him after the baptism and how he put fifty-two of them to death [[cite:revolt]].

He then sent an embassy to Rome. The answer of Pope [[person:nicholas-i]] repeats his report that the rebel leaders were killed with their children, and takes up his question whether the Bulgars might have a patriarch [[cite:questions]]. In 878 Pope [[person:john-viii]] wrote to him as Michael, king of the Bulgars, urging him not to follow the Greeks and to return to Saint Peter [[cite:return]]. See [[event:conversion-of-bulgaria]].
