Macarius was bishop of [[place:jerusalem]] when the dispute over [[person:arius]] began; Arius names him among the bishops who opposed him [[cite:arius]]. He attended the [[event:council-of-nicaea]] [[cite:nicaea]], whose seventh canon confirmed the honour traditionally paid to the bishop of Aelia, the Roman name of his city [[cite:canon7]].

After the tomb of Christ had been uncovered, [[person:constantine]] wrote to him to say that the church built there should surpass every other, and asked him to report what materials and workmen were needed [[cite:letter]]. Eusebius, who preserves the letter, says no more about him. Rufinus, about seventy-five years later, makes him the bishop who identified the cross of Christ for [[person:helena]], among three that were found, by a healing [[cite:rufinus]]. See [[event:church-of-the-holy-sepulchre]].
