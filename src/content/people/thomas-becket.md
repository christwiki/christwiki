Thomas was chancellor to [[person:henry-ii-of-england]] when the king gave him the see of [[place:canterbury]] in 1162, and within a year he was resisting the king's wish to try clergy in the lay courts [[cite:hov-1162]]. He would not seal the customs written down at Clarendon [[cite:hov-1164]], fled in disguise by Sandwich to Flanders and France [[cite:hov-1165]], and came back only at the end of 1170.

Edward Grim, who stood beside him, records his answer to the knights who came for him in the cathedral: he was no traitor to the king but a priest, and ready to die so that the church might have liberty and peace [[cite:grim]]. Roger of Hoveden gives the day of his death as the fifth day of Christmas and says that miracles followed at his tomb and in many countries [[cite:hov-1171]].
