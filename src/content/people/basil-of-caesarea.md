Basil studied rhetoric at Athens with [[person:gregory-of-nazianzus]] [[cite:socrates]]. His brother says that their sister [[person:macrina]] turned him from a public career to the ascetic life [[cite:macrina]]; Basil himself says that he read the Gospel, sold his goods and went to see the monks of Egypt, Palestine, Syria and Mesopotamia [[cite:conversion]]. He founded monasteries in the region of Pontus and resisted the emperor Valens over the creed [[cite:socrates]].

He was bishop of [[place:caesarea-in-cappadocia]] for eight years [[cite:death]]; the translator of the Life places his death at the beginning of 379. In his letters he set out the distinction between the one essence and the three hypostases [[cite:hypostasis]], and in *On the Holy Spirit* he defended giving the Spirit the same glory as the Father and the Son [[cite:spirit]]. Jerome lists his works against Eunomius, on the Spirit, on the six days of creation and on the ascetic life, and says that he died under the emperor Gratian [[cite:jerome]].
