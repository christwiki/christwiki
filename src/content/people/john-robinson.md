[[person:william-bradford]] names him among the members of the congregation in the north of England and as its pastor after the move to [[place:leiden]] [[cite:pastor]]. When part of the church sailed for America in 1620 the majority, who stayed, asked him to remain with them [[cite:stayed]]. In 1614 he had answered [[person:thomas-helwys]] in print, defending infant baptism and the exiles' flight from persecution, and recording how [[person:john-smyth]] had baptized himself [[cite:communion]]; see [[event:first-baptists]].

After the Plymouth colonists killed several Indians in 1623 he wrote to them that it would have been a happy thing if they had converted some before they killed any, and that it was more glorious in men's eyes than pleasing to God to be a terror to poor barbarous people [[cite:rebuke]]. A letter from Leiden reports that he fell sick on 22 February 1625 and died on 1 March [[cite:death]]. See [[event:pilgrims-at-plymouth]].
