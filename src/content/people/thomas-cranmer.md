In a letter of June 1533 Cranmer describes how, as archbishop, he held a court at Dunstable, gave sentence that the pope could not license the marriage of [[person:henry-viii]] and Katherine of Aragon, and afterwards set the crown on Anne Boleyn's head at Westminster [[cite:dunstable]]. William Roper names him first among the commissioners before whom [[person:thomas-more]] was called at Lambeth to take the oath [[cite:lambeth]]. See [[event:act-of-supremacy]].

The Act of Uniformity of 1549 says that the king appointed the archbishop of Canterbury, with other bishops and learned men, to draw up one order of common prayer for the realm [[cite:uniformity]]; see [[event:book-of-common-prayer]]. Under [[person:mary-i-of-england|Mary I]] he was deprived of his orders and, as a London chronicler records, burned at [[place:oxford]] on 21 March 1556 [[cite:death]]; see [[event:marian-persecutions]].
