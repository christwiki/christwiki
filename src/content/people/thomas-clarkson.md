Clarkson tells his own story in his *History*: in 1785, as a senior bachelor at Cambridge, he won the first prize for a Latin essay on the question set by the vice-chancellor, whether it is right to make slaves of others against their will [[cite:essay]]. Riding back to London afterwards he stopped near Wades Mill in Hertfordshire with the thought that, if what he had written was true, someone should see these calamities to their end [[cite:essay]]. He says that he was then twenty-four [[cite:essay]], that he had advanced as far as deacon's orders, and that he gave up his prospects in the church for the cause [[cite:resolve]].

He was one of the members of the committee formed in London on 22 May 1787, and he tells how, at a dinner arranged for the purpose, [[person:william-wilberforce]] agreed to raise the matter in Parliament [[cite:committee]]. See [[event:abolition-of-the-slave-trade]].
