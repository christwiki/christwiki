The instruction issued in his name gives Albert's titles: archbishop of Magdeburg and of [[place:mainz]], primate and arch-chancellor of the empire in Germany, prince-elector, administrator of Halberstadt and margrave of Brandenburg. It names him as the pope's commissary for the indulgence granted for the building of St Peter's [[cite:instructio]].

[[person:martin-luther]] wrote to him on 31 October 1517, asked him to withdraw the instruction and enclosed his theses [[cite:albert]]. In 1545 Luther claimed that Albert had received half the money from the indulgence and the pope the other half, which he said he had not known at the time, and he laid the blame for the whole dispute on Albert [[cite:preface]]. See [[event:ninety-five-theses]].
