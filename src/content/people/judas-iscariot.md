Judas stands last in each Gospel's list of the Twelve, always with the note that he betrayed [[person:jesus]] [[bible:Mark 3:19]] [[bible:Matt 10:4]] [[bible:Luke 6:16]]. John's Gospel calls him the son of Simon Iscariot and says that he kept the group's money box and stole from it [[bible:John 6:71]] [[bible:John 12:4-6]].

Shortly before the Passover he went to the chief priests and offered to hand Jesus over, and they promised him money [[bible:Mark 14:10-11]]; Matthew gives the sum as thirty pieces of silver [[bible:Matt 26:14-16]]. Luke and John say that Satan entered into him [[bible:Luke 22:3]] [[bible:John 13:27]]. He left the supper, brought an armed party to the place where Jesus was praying, and pointed him out with a kiss [[bible:John 13:30]] [[bible:Mark 14:43-46]]; see [[event:arrest-in-gethsemane]].

Two accounts of his death survive. In Matthew he is seized with remorse when Jesus is condemned, throws the silver down in the temple, and hangs himself; the priests use the money to buy a potter's field as a burial ground [[bible:Matt 27:3-10]]. In Acts, [[person:peter]] says that Judas himself bought a field with his reward, fell headlong and burst open [[bible:Acts 1:16-19]]. Both connect his end with a place called the Field of Blood. Matthias was chosen by lot to take his place [[bible:Acts 1:21-26]].
