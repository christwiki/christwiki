Rufinus tells of a king of the Iberians who was caught in sudden darkness while hunting, vowed to worship Christ if he were delivered, and afterwards preached the new faith to his own people and sent to the emperor [[person:constantine]] for priests. He does not give the king's name [[cite:rufinus]].

The Georgian texts call him Mirian, or Mirean, and his queen Nana. *The Conversion of Kartli* says that he was converted "by a miracle" in the seventh year after [[person:nino]] came to [[place:mtskheta]], built a church in the royal garden, and was baptised with his household when Bishop Ioane arrived from Constantine [[cite:kartli]]. The *Life of Nino* sets the hunt on Mount Tkhoti [[cite:life-hunt]] and says that the king was baptised under the hand of Nino, and the queen and their children by the priests and deacons [[cite:life-baptism]]. The chronicle says that after her burial he built the Upper Church of stone and that he died four years later [[cite:kartli-death]]. The texts cited here call him simply King Mirian, without a numeral. See [[event:conversion-of-georgia]].
