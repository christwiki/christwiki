Helena was of humble birth. She was the first wife of Constantius, who put her away to marry the daughter of the emperor Maximian, and she was the mother of [[person:constantine]] [[cite:origin]]. Eusebius says that her son brought her to the Christian faith, gave her the title Augusta, put her portrait on the gold coinage and let her draw on the imperial treasury [[cite:death]]. Late in life she toured the eastern provinces, gave freely to the poor and to churches, and founded two churches in Palestine, at the cave of the nativity in Bethlehem and on the Mount of Olives [[cite:journey]]. She died at about eighty with her son beside her [[cite:death]].

In his account of her journey Eusebius does not connect her with the tomb of Christ or with the cross. That story is told by Ambrose in 395, who also repeats a report that she had been an innkeeper [[cite:ambrose]], and soon afterwards by Rufinus [[cite:rufinus]].
