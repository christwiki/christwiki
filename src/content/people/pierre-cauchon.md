The record of the trial is written in his name. He sat as ordinary judge in [[place:rouen]], outside his own diocese, on territory granted him for the purpose by the chapter of Rouen, on the ground that [[person:joan-of-arc]] had been taken in the diocese of Beauvais; the deputy inquisitor at first declined to sit as judge, while agreeing that the trial should go on [[cite:judge]]. Both sentences, of 24 May and of 30 May, were given in the bishop's name and the inquisitor's deputy's [[cite:sentence]].

At the second trial the friar Martin Ladvenu testified that Joan, seeing the bishop on the day of her death, told him that he was the cause of it, since he had promised to put her in the hands of the church and had left her with her enemies [[cite:ladvenu]]. The sentence of 1456 names "the late Lord Pierre Cauchon, then Bishop of Beauvais" as one of the three who had conducted the process that it annulled [[cite:nullity]]. See [[event:joan-of-arc]].
