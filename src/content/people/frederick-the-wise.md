[[person:martin-luther]] recalled that in 1518 Duke Frederick of Saxony, prince-elector, took up his cause with the pope's legate, asked that he be heard in Germany instead of [[place:rome]], and paid for his journey to [[place:augsburg]]. Luther credited the spread of his teaching to Frederick's protection [[cite:preface]]. From the diet of [[place:worms]] Frederick wrote to his brother [[person:john-of-saxony]] that he would gladly help Martin as far as he was in the right, and that anyone who showed him goodwill was called a heretic [[cite:brother]].

Luther afterwards told Frederick that he had stayed in hiding for a year in obedience to him [[cite:obey]]; see [[event:diet-of-worms]]. Reporting his death in May 1525, Luther wrote that the elector had received the sacrament in both kinds, had known little of the peasants' rising, and had urged his brother to try every peaceful means before it came to a battle [[cite:death]].
