Pané introduces himself as "a poor hermit of the Order of St Jerome" writing at the admiral's command about the beliefs and idolatry of the Indians [[cite:opening]]. He says that when the other friars returned to Castile he stayed, first at the fort of Magdalena in the province of Macorix, whose language he learned, and then for almost two years with the cacique Guarionex, taking with him a convert who knew both languages [[cite:mission]].

He records the first baptism on the island, that of Guaticaua, renamed John Matthew, on St Matthew's day 1496, and the deaths of John Matthew and his brother at the hands of Guarionex's people, whom he regards as martyrs [[cite:mission]] [[cite:baptism]]. He ends by urging that chiefs who keep their people from Christian teaching be punished [[cite:baptism]]. His treatise survives only at second hand; see [[source:pane-antiquities-of-the-indians]] and [[event:christianity-reaches-the-americas]].
