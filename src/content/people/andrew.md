Andrew and his brother Simon [[person:peter]] were fishermen on the [[place:sea-of-galilee]], and [[person:jesus]] called them together [[bible:Mark 1:16-18]]. John's Gospel tells it differently: Andrew was a disciple of [[person:john-the-baptist]], followed Jesus after hearing the Baptist speak of him, and then brought Simon, saying, "We have found the Messiah" [[bible:John 1:35-42]]. The same Gospel says that the brothers came from [[place:bethsaida]] [[bible:John 1:44]]. He is named among the first four in every list of the Twelve [[bible:Mark 3:18]] [[bible:Acts 1:13]]; see [[event:calling-of-the-twelve]].

He appears again pointing out the boy with five loaves and two fish [[bible:John 6:8-9]], bringing some Greeks who wished to see Jesus [[bible:John 12:20-22]], and with Peter, James and John questioning Jesus privately on the [[place:mount-of-olives]] [[bible:Mark 13:3]]. The New Testament says nothing of his later life. Eusebius, in the early fourth century, passes on from [[person:origen]] a tradition that Scythia fell to him as his field of work [[cite:eusebius]].
