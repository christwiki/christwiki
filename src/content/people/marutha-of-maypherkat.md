The Synodicon calls Marutha bishop of Maypherkat and says that he came down on an embassy to the Persians in the time of King [[person:yazdegerd-i]] [[cite:title]]. Socrates, who calls him a bishop of Mesopotamia, says that the Roman emperor sent him to the Persian court more than once, that he cured the king of an illness and exposed the tricks of the magi, and that the king allowed him to build churches [[cite:socrates]]. Socrates also mentions him at Chalcedon among the bishops assembling for the synod that deposed [[person:john-chrysostom]] [[cite:oak]].

The acts of 410 describe him as the mediator of peace between East and West. He carried a letter from the bishops of Syria and Mesopotamia, showed it to [[person:isaac-of-seleucia]], and with him had it read to the king [[cite:letter]]. At the synod Isaac called on him to produce the book of the canons of Nicaea, and it was Marutha who proposed that all should sign them [[cite:session]]. The synod also adopted the order of worship that he and Isaac had shown the bishops [[cite:liturgy]]. See [[event:synod-of-seleucia-ctesiphon]].
