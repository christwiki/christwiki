In the letter announcing his first voyage Columbus told the Spanish court that the islanders had no idolatry and would easily be made Christians, and he ended by calling on Christendom to give thanks for the conversion of so many peoples; he also proposed to ship slaves [[cite:letter]]. The bull Inter caetera names him as the man the sovereigns had sent and repeats what their envoys reported of his discoveries [[cite:bull]].

On the second voyage he chose the site of the settlement on Hispaniola where a church was built and mass said in January 1494 [[cite:mass]]. The friar [[person:ramon-pane]] says that it was at the admiral's command that he went to live with the cacique Guarionex and wrote his account of the islanders' beliefs [[cite:pane]].
