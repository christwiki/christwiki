Rufinus, who had the story from Frumentius's companion Aedesius, says that the two were boys travelling with a philosopher of Tyre when their ship was attacked at a port on the way home from "India". They were taken to the king, and Frumentius was put in charge of the royal accounts and records. After the king's death he helped the queen to govern, and used his position to gather the Christians among the foreign merchants and give them places to worship [[cite:rufinus]]. When the heir came of age Frumentius went to [[place:alexandria]], where [[person:athanasius]] ordained him bishop and sent him back [[cite:ordination]].

Rufinus does not say which kingdom this was. Athanasius does: he calls Frumentius "Bishop of Auxumis" and quotes a letter in which the emperor Constantius II, about 356, demands that the rulers of [[place:aksum]] send him to Egypt to be examined [[cite:athanasius]]. See [[event:ezana-of-aksum]].
