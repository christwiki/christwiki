[[person:athanasius-the-athonite]] remembers Nikephoros as a soldier who lived like a monk, fasting, keeping vigil and sleeping on the ground, who founded monasteries and wished to retire to one. He describes how the general summoned him to Crete during the campaign there, sent gold for the building on [[place:mount-athos]], and, once emperor, swore that he still meant to come. Athanasius writes that Nikephoros would have kept his promise had he not died a martyr's death [[cite:patron]]. The emperor's charter for the Lavra survives only in the passages Athanasius quotes [[cite:chrysobull]]. See [[event:mount-athos]].
