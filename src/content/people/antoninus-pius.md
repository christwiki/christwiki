Antoninus was born on 19 September in the year of Domitian's twelfth consulship, AD 86 [[cite:birth]]. He succeeded Hadrian in 138 and died after twenty-two full years of rule, when Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus followed him [[cite:death]]. [[person:hadrian]], near death and without a son, adopted him on condition that he in turn adopt the young Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus [[cite:adoption]] [[cite:adoption-ha]].

[[person:justin-martyr]] addressed his *First Apology* to him and to those two adopted sons [[cite:justin]]. Melito, bishop of Sardis, writing to Marcus Aurelius, says that Antoninus wrote to Larissa, Thessalonica, Athens and "all the Greeks" forbidding new measures against Christians [[cite:melito]]. Eusebius quotes as his a letter to the assembly of Asia which orders that accusers of Christians be punished, though the copy he gives is headed with the name of Marcus Aurelius; another copy, under Antoninus's own name, is attached to Justin's *Apology* [[cite:rescript]] [[cite:justin-appendix]]. A. C. McGiffert, the translator of Eusebius used here, calls it "undoubtedly spurious" in his note [[cite:rescript-note]]. Polycarp is commonly held to have died in his reign: see [[event:martyrdom-of-polycarp]].
