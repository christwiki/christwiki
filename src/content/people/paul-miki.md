What is told here comes from the report of the Jesuit Luís Fróis, written in the months after the crucifixion. Fróis lists Miki among the three members of the Society of Jesus who were crucified with the six Franciscans and seventeen other Japanese at [[place:nagasaki]], and calls him "our brother" [[cite:frois]].

Seeing himself, Fróis writes, in the most honoured pulpit he had ever had, Miki told the bystanders that he was a Japanese and of the Society of Jesus, that he was dying for having preached the gospel, and that he thanked God for it. He declared that there was no way to salvation but the one the Christians hold, and, since that way taught him to forgive his enemies, he forgave the ruler and all who were responsible for his death and asked them to receive baptism. He then turned to encourage his companions. The executioners killed each of the crucified with one or two thrusts of a lance [[cite:frois]]. See [[event:japan-martyrs]].
