Claudius was born at Lugdunum, the modern Lyon, on 1 August in the consulship of Iullus Antonius and Fabius Africanus, which is 10 BC [[cite:birth]]. The soldiers of the guard hailed him as emperor in his fiftieth year, on the day the emperor Gaius was murdered in AD 41, and he accepted the next day [[cite:accession]]. He died on 13 October AD 54, in the sixty-fourth year of his age and the fourteenth of his reign [[cite:death]], and was succeeded by [[person:nero]] [[cite:nero]].

He appears in this history at four points. On his accession he gave Judea and Samaria to [[person:herod-agrippa-i]] [[cite:agrippa]]. The famine that Acts mentions fell in his reign [[bible:Acts 11:28]]. He ordered Jews to leave [[place:rome]], an order that brought [[person:aquila]] and [[person:priscilla]] to [[place:corinth|Corinth]] [[cite:expulsion]] [[bible:Acts 18:2]]; see [[event:claudius-expels-the-jews]]. And a letter of his, cut in stone at Delphi, mentions [[person:gallio]] in words partly restored as "my friend and proconsul" and so helps to date [[person:paul|Paul]]'s stay at Corinth [[cite:gallio]].
