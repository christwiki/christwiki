The conclave at [[place:rome]] chose Bartholomew, archbishop of Bari, in April 1378. The cardinals wrote in May that they had elected him freely and unanimously and that he had taken the name Urban VI [[cite:letter-of-may]]. In August they declared that they had acted in fear of the Roman crowd and that he was no pope but an apostate [[cite:cardinals]]. [[person:catherine-of-siena]] defended his election as lawful [[cite:catherine]].

The line of popes that claimed succession from him ended when Gregory XII resigned at the Council of Constance in 1415 [[cite:gregory]]. See [[event:western-schism]].
