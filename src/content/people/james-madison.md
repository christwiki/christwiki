Madison drafted the *Memorial and Remonstrance* of 1785, a petition against a bill to provide for teachers of the Christian religion [[cite:intro]]. It holds that religion must be left to the conviction and conscience of every man [[cite:memorial]] and that the assembly has no authority to pass such a bill [[cite:memorial-15]]. He sat through the session of the Virginia assembly that ended on 21 January 1786 and reported to [[person:thomas-jefferson]] the next day that the bill for religious freedom had become law with its enacting clauses unaltered, adding that he hoped it had put an end in Virginia to "the ambitious hope of making laws for the human mind" [[cite:passage]].
