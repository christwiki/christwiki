Abelard says that he came from the town of Le Pallet, east of Nantes, the eldest son of a soldier who loved letters, and gave up his inheritance for the study of logic, travelling wherever that art was taught [[cite:hc-1]]. At [[place:paris]] he studied under William of Champeaux, defeated him in disputation by his own account, and opened a school of his own on the Mont Sainte-Geneviève [[cite:hc-2]].

He tells of his love for Heloise, their secret marriage and his castration by her uncle's kinsmen, after which he became a monk at Saint-Denis and she a nun at Argenteuil [[cite:hc-6]]. A council at Soissons condemned his tract on the Trinity and made him burn it [[cite:hc-9]]. The prologue of his *Sic et Non* sets out rules for reconciling authorities that disagree and commends persistent questioning as the first key to wisdom [[cite:sic]].
