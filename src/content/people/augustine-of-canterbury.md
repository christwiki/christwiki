Gregory calls Augustine "a monk of my monastery" and says that he was made a bishop, with Gregory's leave, on his way to the English [[cite:gregory]]; Bede says that he was consecrated at Arles [[cite:questions]]. Bede tells how the party tried to turn back, how Gregory made Augustine their abbot and sent them on, and how they landed in Kent with Frankish interpreters, nearly forty in number [[cite:sending]].

Once established he sent Gregory a list of practical questions about bishops, liturgy, marriage and ritual purity, and Bede copies the pope's answers [[cite:questions]]. He restored a church at [[place:canterbury]] that was said to have been built by Roman Christians, dedicated it to the Saviour and made it his seat, and began a monastery of Saints Peter and Paul outside the walls [[cite:see]]. In 604 he consecrated bishops for London and Rochester [[cite:death]]. Bede does not give the year of his death; his epitaph dates it to 26 May in the reign of King [[person:aethelberht-of-kent]] [[cite:death]].
