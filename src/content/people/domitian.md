Domitian was born in October of AD 51, the younger son of [[person:vespasian]] [[cite:birth]], and succeeded his brother [[person:titus]] in 81. The arch that commemorates Titus's triumph over Judaea was built in his reign [[cite:arch]]. Suetonius says that he exacted the tax on the Jews with great harshness and that he had himself styled "Our Master and our God" [[cite:tax]] [[cite:lord]].

Near the end of his reign he executed his cousin, the consul Flavius Clemens [[cite:clemens]]. Cassius Dio, as preserved in a later abridgement, gives the charge as "atheism", brought also against others who had drifted into Jewish ways, and says that Clemens's wife Flavia Domitilla was banished [[cite:dio]]. Whether these were Christians is not said by either writer.

Christian memory made him a persecutor. Tertullian, about AD 197, says that Domitian tried what Nero had done but soon stopped and recalled those he had banished [[cite:tertullian]]. Eusebius, in the early fourth century, calls him the second emperor to persecute the church [[cite:second]]. He reports that a Flavia Domitilla, whom he calls a niece of Clemens, was exiled for her witness to Christ, and gives it as something said that John was banished to [[place:patmos]] in this reign [[cite:exiles]]. He also preserves a story from Hegesippus that Domitian questioned the grandsons of Jude, a brother of Jesus, and dismissed them as harmless [[cite:hegesippus]]. Irenaeus says that the Revelation was seen towards the end of his reign [[cite:irenaeus]].

He was murdered in September 96 [[cite:death]]. His successor Nerva recalled the exiles [[cite:nerva]].
