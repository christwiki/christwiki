A legation from the emperor of [[place:constantinople]] came to the synod of Piacenza in 1095 and asked the pope and all Christians for help against the pagans, who held the country up to the walls of the city [[cite:bernold-1095]].

Anna Komnene, who names herself his daughter [[cite:alexiad-preface]], tells the sequel from his side. He dreaded the arrival of the Frankish armies, sent officers to receive them courteously, supply them and watch them [[cite:alexiad-5]], and required their leaders to swear that they would hand over to him any towns they took that had belonged to the empire [[cite:alexiad-9]]. The Latin chronicler Bernold tells it otherwise: the "king of Constantinople", he writes under 1098, withdrew his help from the crusaders and closed the road to pilgrims [[cite:bernold-1098]].
