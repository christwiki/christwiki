Giovanni Villani, a Florentine contemporary, says that the cardinals at Perugia, after more than nine months of deadlock, elected the archbishop of Bordeaux in 1305, and repeats a story of a secret bargain between him and [[person:philip-iv-of-france]] [[cite:election]]. Villani calls him Raymond de Got. From Lyon, in February 1306, Clement declared that the bull [[event:unam-sanctam]] was to bring no prejudice on the king or kingdom of France [[cite:meruit]].

Villani places his court at [[place:avignon]] by 1308 and again in 1310 [[cite:court]]. He dates Clement's death to 20 April 1314, at Roquemaure on the Rhône, on the way to Bordeaux, and gives him a bad character: greedy for money and a seller of benefices [[cite:death]]. See [[event:avignon-papacy]].
