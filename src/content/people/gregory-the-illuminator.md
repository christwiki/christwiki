What is told here of Gregory comes from the *History* that goes under the name of Agathangelos, written well over a century after his time, in the abridged English version linked. In that account he is the son of Anak, the Parthian noble who murdered the Armenian king Chosroes. Carried as an infant to Roman territory, he was brought up a Christian at Caesarea in Cappadocia and entered the service of the dead king's son [[person:tiridates-iii]] without revealing who he was [[cite:origins]]. When he refused to honour the goddess Anahit he was tortured and, once his parentage was known, thrown into a pit, where he remained for thirteen years [[cite:pit]].

He was brought out when the king fell into madness, and his prayers restored him [[cite:release]]. Tiridates then sent him to Caesarea, where Bishop Leontius and his fellow bishops consecrated him [[cite:consecration]]. He baptised the royal camp in the Euphrates, and at the king's request made his son Aristaces a bishop to carry on his work [[cite:sons]]. The abridgement gives no dates.
