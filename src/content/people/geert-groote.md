[[person:thomas-a-kempis]], who wrote his Life, calls him Gerard "the Great" and tells of a rich citizen of [[place:deventer]], master of arts at Paris at eighteen and holder of a canonry at Aix, who was turned from a worldly life by a Carthusian prior [[cite:early]]. The chronicle of Thomas's monastery names his parents, Werner Groote, a magistrate of the city, and Heylwige, and says that he resigned his benefices, left his house to a community of poor women and took deacon's orders in order to preach [[cite:chronicle]].

He preached in the towns of the diocese of Utrecht and in Holland until he was forbidden by an edict, which he obeyed [[cite:forbidden]] [[cite:communities]]. He set those who wished to live together to earn their bread by their own work, and he wanted a monastery of canons regular for some of them [[cite:communities]]. He died at Deventer on the feast of Saint Bernard, 20 August, in 1384, in his forty-fourth year, and was buried in the church of Saint Mary there [[cite:death]] [[cite:chronicle]].
