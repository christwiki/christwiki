Lawrence of Březová introduces him among the four captains chosen by the Taborites in the spring of 1420 as John Žižka the one-eyed, formerly of the king's household and a foremost zealot for the law of Christ [[cite:captain]]. When [[person:sigismund]] meant to fortify the hill of Vítkov and so cut off the food supply of [[place:prague]], Žižka built two wooden bulwarks there with a ditch and a wall of earth and stone. On 14 July 1420 the king's troops stormed it; Žižka himself was nearly struck down, Lawrence says, before the attackers were driven off. The next day he had the works enlarged, and some came to call the hill by his name [[cite:vitkov]].

A Prague chronicle records his battles against the Praguers and the lords in 1423 and 1424, his peace with Prague in September 1424, and his death at the siege of the castle of Přibyslav on the Wednesday before Saint Gall, 11 October 1424. He was buried at Hradec Králové, and his field army was afterwards called the Orphans [[cite:death]]. See [[event:hussite-movement]].
