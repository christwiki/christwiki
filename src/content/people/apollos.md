---
name: Apollos
role: Jewish Christian teacher from Alexandria
summary: An eloquent Jew from Alexandria who taught at Ephesus and Corinth in the 50s. Paul names him as a fellow worker, and one of the parties at Corinth used his name.
---

Acts introduces Apollos as a Jew born in Alexandria, eloquent and strong in the Scriptures, who came to [[place:ephesus]] teaching about Jesus although he knew only the baptism of John. [[person:priscilla]] and [[person:aquila]] heard him in the synagogue and explained the way to him more accurately. He then crossed to Achaia with a letter of introduction and argued in public that Jesus was the Christ [[bible:Acts 18:24-28]]. He was at [[place:corinth]] when [[person:paul]] arrived in Ephesus [[bible:Acts 19:1]].

At Corinth some believers began to say "I follow Apollos" against others who claimed Paul or Cephas [[bible:1 Cor 1:12]]. Paul answered that the two of them were servants and fellow workers in one task: "I planted. Apollos watered. But God gave the increase" [[bible:1 Cor 3:5-9]]. When he wrote, Apollos was with him or near him and had declined, for the present, to return to Corinth [[bible:1 Cor 16:12]]. The letter to Titus asks that he be helped on a journey [[bible:Titus 3:13]]. The New Testament does not mention him again.
