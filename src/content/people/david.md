David was the youngest son of Jesse of [[place:bethlehem]], anointed by [[person:samuel]] while [[person:saul]] was still king [[bible:1 Sam 16:11-13]]. He won fame by killing [[person:goliath]], fled when Saul sought his life, and for a time lived under the protection of the Philistine king of Gath [[bible:1 Sam 17:49-51]] [[bible:1 Sam 19:1-2]] [[bible:1 Sam 27:1-7]]. After Saul's death he was made king of Judah at [[place:hebron]] and then of all Israel. He began to reign at thirty and reigned forty years, thirty-three of them in [[place:jerusalem]], which he captured and to which he brought the ark [[bible:2 Sam 5:3-7]] [[bible:2 Sam 6:12-15]].

Through [[person:nathan]] he received the promise that his house and his throne would stand for ever [[bible:2 Sam 7:12-16]]. The same prophet rebuked him for taking Bathsheba and causing the death of her husband Uriah [[bible:2 Sam 11:2-5, 14-17]] [[bible:2 Sam 12:7-10]]. His last words call him "the sweet psalmist of Israel" [[bible:2 Sam 23:1]]. He had [[person:solomon]] anointed as his successor, and was buried in Jerusalem [[bible:1 Kings 1:32-35]] [[bible:1 Kings 2:10-12]]. In the Gospels "son of David" is a title of the Messiah, and Matthew's first line gives it to Jesus [[bible:Matt 1:1]] [[bible:Mark 12:35-37]]. Outside the Bible the earliest widely accepted mention of his name is the phrase "House of David" on a stele of the ninth century BC ([[event:tel-dan-stele]]). His dates are reckoned from the Bible's regnal figures; see [[event:david-becomes-king]].
