Gregory of Tours, a contemporary, says that Gregory belonged to one of the leading senatorial families of Rome, founded seven monasteries from his own wealth, and was a deacon when the people chose him as bishop during the plague of 590 [[cite:election]]. Bede adds that he had been the pope's representative at Constantinople, that he governed for thirteen and a half years and was buried at Saint Peter's on 12 March, and he lists his writings [[cite:bede]].

On taking office he declared that he received the four councils as he did the four Gospels, and venerated the fifth [[cite:councils]]. He refused the title "Universal Pope" [[cite:universal]]. His *Dialogues* are the main early source for [[person:benedict-of-nursia]] [[cite:benedict]]. His government of the church and the mission to Kent are told under [[event:gregory-the-great]].
