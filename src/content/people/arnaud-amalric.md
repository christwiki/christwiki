William of Tudela says that it was the abbot of Cîteaux who brought the great host before [[place:beziers]] on the feast of the Magdalene in 1209 [[cite:song]]. The report on what followed went to the pope in the name of "brother Arnaud, called abbot of Cîteaux", and his fellow legate Milo. It says that the city was stormed by camp servants without orders, that nearly twenty thousand people were put to the sword without regard to rank, sex or age, and that divine vengeance had raged wonderfully against the place [[cite:report]].

Caesarius of Heisterbach, a monk of the same order writing between about 1219 and 1223, names "Arnold, abbot of Cîteaux, afterwards archbishop of Narbonne" as the preacher and head of the crusade. In his telling the soldiers ask the abbot how to tell the good from the bad, and the abbot, fearing that heretics would feign to be Catholics, "is said to have said" (*fertur dixisse*): Kill them, for the Lord knows who are his (*Caedite eos. Novit enim Dominus qui sunt eius*) [[cite:caesarius]]. The legates' own letter has no such exchange. See [[event:albigensian-crusade]].
