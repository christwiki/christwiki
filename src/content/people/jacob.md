Jacob is the younger twin of [[person:esau]], born holding his brother's heel [[bible:Gen 25:24-26]]. He gains Esau's birthright and his father's blessing [[bible:Gen 25:29-34]] [[bible:Gen 27:27-29]], flees to [[place:haran]], and on the way receives the promise of Abraham at [[place:bethel]] [[bible:Gen 28:10-15]]. By Leah, Rachel and their servants Zilpah and Bilhah he has twelve sons [[bible:Gen 35:22-26]]. On his way home he is renamed Israel ([[event:jacob-becomes-israel]]) [[bible:Gen 32:28]]. In old age he goes down to Egypt, where his son [[person:joseph]] is governor, and is 130 when he stands before Pharaoh ([[event:joseph-and-israel-in-egypt]]) [[bible:Gen 46:5-7]] [[bible:Gen 47:9]]. Before he dies at 147 he blesses his sons and says that the sceptre will not depart from Judah [[bible:Gen 47:28]] [[bible:Gen 49:8-10]]. He is buried in the cave of Machpelah at [[place:hebron]] [[bible:Gen 50:13]].

The prophets use both his names for the whole people [[bible:Isa 41:8]], and Matthew and Luke trace the descent of Jesus through him [[bible:Matt 1:2]] [[bible:Luke 3:34]]. His dates are worked out from the Bible's own figures; see the dating note at [[event:call-of-abraham]].
