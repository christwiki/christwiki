Ekkehard of Aura, recording under 1106 the death of the emperor whom his own party called Henry the fourth, says that the dead man's supporters then submitted to the king and that he was later laid to rest at [[place:speyer]] in the presence of his son [[cite:ekkehard-1106]]. That son came to [[place:rome]] in February 1111 to be crowned emperor. Ekkehard of Aura reports that he accepted the pope's offer to exchange investiture for the return of the crown's lands, on condition that the church and the princes agreed, and that when the princes rose in protest the pope was held under guard until the coronation should take place [[cite:ekkehard-1111]]. In April Paschal II granted him investiture with staff and ring and addressed him as emperor [[cite:paschal-apr]].

In 1122 at [[place:worms]] he renounced that investiture, speaking in his charter of the losses of the Roman church "in the time of my father" and in his own [[cite:emperor]]. Ekkehard says that he was then received back into communion and that everyone went home rejoicing [[cite:ekkehard-1122]].
