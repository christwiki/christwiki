Agrippa was a grandson of [[person:herod-the-great|Herod the Great]] [[cite:claudius]]. He was brought up at [[place:rome|Rome]] in the circle of the imperial family, where he ran through his money [[cite:youth]]. When Gaius became emperor in AD 37 he was made king of the former tetrarchy of Philip, and he was later given the territory of [[person:herod-antipas|Herod Antipas]] as well [[cite:gaius]] [[cite:antipas]]. In AD 41 [[person:claudius|Claudius]] added Judea and Samaria, so that he ruled all that his grandfather had held [[cite:claudius]]. [[person:josephus|Josephus]] praises his generosity and says that he lived by choice in [[place:jerusalem]] and observed the law scrupulously [[cite:piety]].

Acts, which calls him "King Herod", says that he put [[person:james-son-of-zebedee]] to death and imprisoned [[person:peter]] [[bible:Acts 12:1-4]]. He died at [[place:caesarea-maritima]] after being hailed as a god on a public occasion, in the fifty-fourth year of his age and the seventh of his reign [[bible:Acts 12:20-23]] [[cite:death]]. From those figures his birth falls in 11 or 10 BC. See [[event:death-of-herod-agrippa]]. He was the father of [[person:herod-agrippa-ii]], the "King Agrippa" before whom [[person:paul|Paul]] later spoke [[cite:son]] [[bible:Acts 25:13]].
