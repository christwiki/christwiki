[[person:philip-melanchthon]] calls Tetzel a friar of the Dominican order whose preaching of indulgences moved [[person:martin-luther]] to publish his theses [[cite:melanchthon]]. In the sample sermons he wrote for parish priests, the dead cry out to their children for the small alms that would free them, and the letter of indulgence is compared to a safe-conduct for the soul [[cite:sermons]]. Luther said in 1517 that he had not himself heard the preachers but only what the people took from them [[cite:albert]].

Looking back in 1545, Luther called Tetzel the originator of the affair. He added that a papal envoy had rebuked Tetzel so harshly that he wasted away, and that he himself had written him a letter of comfort before he died [[cite:preface]]. See [[event:ninety-five-theses]].
