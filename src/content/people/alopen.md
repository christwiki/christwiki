Alopen is known by the Chinese form of his name, Aluoben. The Xi'an Stele of 781 says that he came from Da Qin to [[place:changan|Chang'an]] in 635 with sacred books, was received by the emperor [[person:taizong]], and saw his books translated in the imperial library [[cite:arrival]]. The edict of 638 preserved in the Tang court's records calls him a Persian monk and orders a monastery built for his teaching [[cite:huiyao]].

The stele adds that the next emperor, Gaozong, confirmed the honours paid to him and gave him a title as guardian of the teaching for the realm [[cite:later]]. Neither text records his birth, his death or his life before China.
