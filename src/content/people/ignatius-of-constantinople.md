Ignatius is known here mainly through others. Pope [[person:nicholas-i]] treated him as the lawful patriarch, wrongly driven out while still alive and afterwards deposed by a council of [[person:photius]]'s supporters, and threatened Photius with anathema if he hindered Ignatius from governing his church [[cite:rome]]. The council of 869–870 records that the emperor recalled him to his see and confirmed him in it [[cite:council]]. The Synodikon of Orthodoxy names him and Photius together [[cite:synodikon]]. See [[event:photian-schism]].
