Lazarus appears only in the Gospel of John. He lived at [[place:bethany]] with his sisters [[person:martha]] and [[person:mary-of-bethany]], and John says that Jesus loved the three of them [[bible:John 11:1, 5]]. John tells that he fell ill and died, and that Jesus, arriving when he had been four days in the tomb, called him out of it [[bible:John 11:17, 38-44]]; see [[event:raising-of-lazarus]]. Six days before the Passover he sat at table with Jesus at Bethany. Many came to see him, and the chief priests planned to put him to death too, because people believed in Jesus on his account [[bible:John 12:1-2, 9-11]]. The New Testament says nothing more of him.

He is a different figure from the beggar Lazarus in one of Jesus's parables [[bible:Luke 16:20]]. By the 380s the pilgrim Egeria knew Bethany as "the Lazarium", where the Christians of Jerusalem gathered on the day before Palm Sunday [[cite:egeria]].
