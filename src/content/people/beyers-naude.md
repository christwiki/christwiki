South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission reports that the Cottesloe statement of 1960 led to the marginalisation of some of the Dutch Reformed Church's own representatives, Beyers Naudé among them, and that he went on to establish the Christian Institute, at first to press for change in that church [[cite:cottesloe]]. It names him among those members of faith communities who opposed apartheid as individuals, often despite the institutions to which they belonged [[cite:opponents]].

The commission records that the Institute was declared an "affected" organisation in 1975, which cut it off from funds from abroad, and was banned two years later together with its executive leadership, and that the churches which had supported it in words gave it little material help [[cite:banned]].
