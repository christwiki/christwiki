Montanus is known only through writers who opposed him. One, an unnamed author quoted by Eusebius, calls him a recent convert who began to prophesy in a trance at Ardabau, on the border of Mysia and Phrygia, when Gratus was proconsul of Asia [[cite:origin]]. Another opponent, Apollonius, says that he regulated fasts, organised the collection of money, and gave the name Jerusalem to [[place:pepuza]] and Tymion [[cite:apollonius]]. One saying attributed to him by Epiphanius, paraphrased here from the Greek, likens a man to a lyre and the one who speaks through him to the plectrum [[cite:lyre]].

The unnamed writer passes on a rumour that he hanged himself, and says that he cannot vouch for it [[cite:death]]. See [[event:montanism]].
