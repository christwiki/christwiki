At her trial she said that at home she was called Jeannette and in France Jeanne, that her father was Jacques d'Arc and her mother Ysabelle, that she was born at Domrémy and was about nineteen years old. She had learned her prayers from her mother and could spin and sew. From the age of thirteen, she said, she heard a voice from God, which in time told her to go into France; she went to the captain of Vaucouleurs, who gave her an escort, and rode to the king in men's clothes [[cite:herself]]. Her letter summoning the English to leave France was read to her in court; it is dated on Tuesday in Holy Week, 22 March, in the year the record writes as 1428 and modern reckoning as 1429 [[cite:letter]].

The court of [[person:pierre-cauchon]] declared her a relapsed heretic on 30 May 1431 and gave her up to the secular power [[cite:sentence]]. In 1456 judges commissioned by [[person:callixtus-iii]] pronounced that trial and sentence null [[cite:nullity]]. Benedict XV declared her a saint in 1920 [[cite:canonized]].
