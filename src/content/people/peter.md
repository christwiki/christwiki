Simon was a fisherman, the brother of [[person:andrew]]. John's Gospel says that they came from [[place:bethsaida]] [[bible:John 1:44]]; Mark places Simon's house, and his wife's mother, at [[place:capernaum]] [[bible:Mark 1:21, 29-31]]. [[person:jesus]] called him from his nets [[bible:Mark 1:16-18]] and gave him the name Cephas, in Greek Peter, which means rock [[bible:John 1:42]] [[bible:Mark 3:16]]. He stands first in every list of the Twelve, and with James and John he is present at moments from which the others are absent [[bible:Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33]]. He speaks for the disciples in confessing Jesus as the Christ [[bible:Mark 8:29]]; see [[event:peters-confession]]. The Gospels tell that he three times denied knowing Jesus on the night of the arrest [[bible:Mark 14:66-72]]. Paul names him first among those to whom the risen Jesus appeared, and Luke reports an appearance to him [[bible:1 Cor 15:5]] [[bible:Luke 24:34]].

In Acts he leads the first community in [[place:jerusalem]], preaches at Pentecost, and baptises the Gentile household of [[person:cornelius]] [[bible:Acts 2:14]] [[bible:Acts 10:44-48]]. Paul counts him with James and John as one of the "pillars", describes him as entrusted with the mission to the Jews, and also tells of opposing him to his face at [[place:antioch]] [[bible:Gal 2:7-14]]. The Gospel of John hints at a violent death [[bible:John 21:18-19]]. A letter from Rome of about AD 96 says that he bore his witness and went to glory, without saying where [[cite:clement]]. Eusebius, writing in the early fourth century, reports that he was crucified at Rome under [[person:nero]], and quotes writers of the late second and early third centuries in support [[cite:eusebius]]; see [[event:martyrdom-of-peter-and-paul]].
