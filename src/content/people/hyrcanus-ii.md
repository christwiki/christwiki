Hyrcanus was the elder son of the Hasmonean king Alexander Jannaeus [[cite:parents]]. Josephus, the main source for his life, sums it up at his death. He was made high priest when his mother Alexandra began her nine-year reign. On her death he was king for three months, until his brother [[person:aristobulus-ii]] defeated him [[cite:career]]. He agreed to retire, but the Idumean Antipater, father of [[person:herod-the-great]], persuaded him to fight for his position with Arab help [[cite:brothers]]. [[person:pompey]] restored him as high priest and head of the nation, without a crown [[cite:restored]], and a decree of Julius Caesar quoted by Josephus confirms him as high priest and ethnarch of the Jews [[cite:ethnarch]].

In 40 BC the Parthians invaded and set up his nephew Antigonus, who cut off his ears so that he could never again be high priest [[cite:ears]]. He later returned from Parthian captivity to Herod's court [[cite:career]]. After the battle of Actium, which is dated to 31 BC, Herod had him put to death when he was over eighty, on a charge of treason that Josephus reports in two versions, Herod's own and that of other historians who held that the charge was invented [[cite:death]]. Josephus describes him as mild and unfit to rule, a man who let others govern for him [[cite:career]]. See [[event:pompey-takes-jerusalem]].
