John calls himself an envoy of the pope, "the lord and father of Christians", and says that he explained the pope's message to the first Mongol chiefs he met and presented the pope's letters to their commander [[cite:message]]. The letter of [[person:innocent-iv]] that protests at the Mongol invasions names its bearer only as "brother J." [[cite:pope]]. Riding with Mongol guides, he reached the camp of Güyük on 22 July and watched his enthronement on 24 August [[cite:court]].

In November the khan's secretaries dictated the reply, which he and his companions put into Latin, and on the feast of Saint Brice they were dismissed [[cite:letter]]. They travelled through the winter and came back to Kiev in June [[cite:return]]. See [[event:franciscans-in-asia]].
