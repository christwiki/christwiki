William Miller writes that he lived at Hampton, New York, from the age of four, moved to Poultney in Vermont at twenty-two, and there took up the opinions of the Deists [[cite:early]]. After service as an army captain he settled at [[place:low-hampton]] [[cite:low-hampton]]. His study of the Bible led him to reckon that the 2300 days of Daniel 8 would end about 1843 [[cite:reckoning]]. He weighed objections to his conclusion from 1818 to 1823 [[cite:objections]] and says that he struggled on for nine years longer and "was then fifty years old" [[cite:fifty]], which puts his birth about 1782. In 1834 his Baptist brethren granted him a licence to preach [[cite:licence]].

He gave two periods for the return of Christ, the year ending 21 March 1844 and then 22 October 1844, and was disappointed in both [[cite:disappointed]]. In 1845 he wrote that to contend that he and his friends had not been mistaken was dishonest [[cite:mistaken]]. See [[event:great-disappointment]].
