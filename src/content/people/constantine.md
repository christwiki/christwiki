Constantine was the son of the emperor Constantius and of Helena, and was brought up at Naissus. He was kept as a hostage at the court of [[person:diocletian]] and [[person:galerius]], rejoined his father in Gaul, and was hailed as Caesar by the troops when Constantius died at York [[cite:origins]]. In 312 he marched on Rome and defeated [[person:maxentius]] at the Milvian Bridge, after a dream in which, Lactantius says, he was told to mark his soldiers' shields with the sign of Christ [[cite:bridge]]. The next year he and [[person:licinius]] agreed at [[place:milan]] that Christians and all others should be free to follow their own religion [[cite:milan]].

He defeated Licinius and became sole emperor, and renamed Byzantium Constantinople [[cite:licinius]]. He called the bishops together at [[place:nicaea]] [[cite:nicaea]]. Eusebius reports that he was baptised only in his last illness, in the suburbs of [[place:nicomedia]], and died at Pentecost [[cite:baptism]]. The historian Socrates, a century later, gives the day, 22 May 337, and a reign of thirty-one years [[cite:death]].
