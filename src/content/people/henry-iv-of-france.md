The Edict of Nantes opens in his name as king of France and Navarre and is dated in the ninth year of his reign [[cite:title]]. In its preamble he says that he had first to end the civil war by force and could only then turn to the grievances of his Catholic and Reformed subjects [[cite:title]]. The edict restored Catholic worship across the kingdom and allowed the Reformed to live anywhere in it without being forced to act against their conscience [[cite:conscience]]. See [[event:edict-of-nantes]].

His grandson [[person:louis-xiv]], revoking the edict in 1685, called him "Henry the Great" and said that his plans for the reunion of the Reformed with the Catholic church had been cut short by his sudden death [[cite:revocation]].
