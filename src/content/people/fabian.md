Eusebius, writing some seventy years later, tells the story that Fabian was a stranger from the country whom no one had thought of as bishop until a dove settled on his head in the assembly [[cite:dove]]. The list of Roman bishops compiled in the fourth century gives him fourteen years in office, from the consulship of 236 to that of 250, and says that he divided the districts of the city among the deacons and had much building done in the cemeteries [[cite:catalogue]].

He died under [[person:decius]] [[cite:martyr]]. The Roman calendar of martyrs commemorates him on 20 January in the cemetery of Callistus; the list of bishops gives the 21st [[cite:burial]] [[cite:catalogue]]. [[person:cyprian]], on receiving the Roman clergy's report, wrote to congratulate them on their bishop's "glorious end" [[cite:cyprian]]. The same list dates his successor Cornelius from the consuls of 251 [[cite:catalogue]].
