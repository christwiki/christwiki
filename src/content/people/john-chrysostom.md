John was born at [[place:antioch]], studied rhetoric under Libanius, and gave up a career in law for the ascetic life and the service of the church [[cite:youth]]. As a presbyter there he preached to the city through the weeks of fear that followed the riot of 387 [[cite:statues]]. In 398 he was brought to [[place:constantinople]] and ordained its bishop [[cite:ordination]], where, his supporter Palladius says, he disciplined the clergy, spent the bishop's income on hospitals and preached against the rich [[cite:reforms]].

A synod led by Theophilus of Alexandria deposed him at the Oak, near Chalcedon [[cite:oak]]. He was recalled, condemned again, and exiled in June 404 [[cite:exile]]; his own account of these events survives in a letter to Innocent of Rome [[cite:innocent]]. He died at Comana on 14 September 407, while being moved to a more distant place of exile [[cite:death]] [[cite:last]]. His body was brought back to Constantinople in 438 [[cite:relics]].
