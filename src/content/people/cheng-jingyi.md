The conference record spells his name Cheng Ching-yi and lists him under the London Missionary Society [[cite:minutes]]. In the debate on co-operation and unity on 21 June 1910 he said that he spoke for the Chinese church and from the Chinese standpoint: federation among the missions had already borne fruit, denominational differences had never interested the Chinese mind, and Chinese Christians hoped soon to see a united church. He asked that the Continuation Committee consult Chinese pastors and leaders about it [[cite:speech]]. The conference named him the member for China on that committee [[cite:members]].
