In Luke the angel Gabriel comes to Mary at [[place:nazareth]], a virgin betrothed to [[person:joseph-of-nazareth|Joseph]], and tells her that she will bear a son by the power of the Holy Spirit; she answers, "let it be done to me according to your word" [[bible:Luke 1:26-38]]. Luke gives her the song that begins "My soul magnifies the Lord" [[bible:Luke 1:46-55]] and tells of the birth at [[place:bethlehem]] [[bible:Luke 2:1-7]]. Matthew tells of the conception and birth from Joseph's side [[bible:Matt 1:18-25]]; see [[event:birth-of-jesus]]. During the ministry of [[person:jesus]] she appears at the wedding at Cana [[bible:John 2:1-11]], and once comes with his brothers to look for him [[bible:Mark 3:31-35]]. John places her at the cross, where Jesus entrusts her to the disciple whom he loved [[bible:John 19:25-27]]. She is last seen praying with the apostles in [[place:jerusalem]] after the ascension [[bible:Acts 1:14]]. The New Testament does not tell of her death.

The Gospels name four brothers of Jesus and mention sisters [[bible:Mark 6:3]]. Early Christians explained them in three ways, and Christians still differ. Helvidius, as [[person:jerome]] reports him, took them to be children born later to Mary and Joseph; Jerome himself argued that they were cousins [[cite:jerome]]. A second-century narrative makes Joseph an old man who already had children [[cite:protoevangelium]]. The Catholic Church teaches that Mary remained a virgin throughout her life and that the brothers were close relations [[cite:catechism]]; the Orthodox Confession of Dositheus (1672) likewise calls her "ever-virgin" [[cite:dositheus]]. Among the Reformers, [[person:john-calvin]], in his commentary of 1555, recalled the dispute between Helvidius and Jerome and held that Matthew's words allow no well-grounded inference about what took place after the birth of Jesus [[cite:calvin]]. On the title Theotokos, "God-bearer", see [[event:council-of-ephesus]].
