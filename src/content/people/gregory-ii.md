The *Liber Pontificalis* says that Gregory was a Roman, the son of Marcellus, brought up from boyhood in the papal household, and that he held the see for more than fifteen years. It records that through Bishop [[person:boniface]] he preached the word of salvation in Germany [[cite:lp-life]], and that he was buried at St Peter's on 11 February in the fourteenth indiction, which is 731 [[cite:lp-end]]. His letter of 15 May 719 gave Boniface his commission, and in 722 he consecrated him bishop and received his oath [[cite:commission]] [[cite:oath]].

The same Life, written at Rome close to the events, says that the emperor [[person:leo-iii-the-isaurian|Leo III]] tried to impose a tax on the Italian provinces, which Gregory resisted, and then sent orders that no image of any saint, martyr or angel should be kept, offering the pope his favour if he complied and deposition if he did not. Gregory rejected the order and wrote everywhere to warn Christians against it. When the armies of Italy wanted to elect an emperor of their own, he restrained them [[cite:lp-images]].
