His bull *Regnans in excelsis* is dated at Rome on 25 February 1570, in the fifth year of his pontificate. It calls [[person:elizabeth-i]] "the pretended queen of England", lists what the pope holds against her, declares her a heretic and her adherents excommunicated, and absolves her subjects from their oaths to her [[cite:regnans]]. The English historian William Camden, whose account was printed in 1615, says that the sentence was given "without any admonition or citation" beforehand [[cite:camden]].

A paper printed in the English government's tract *The Execution of Justice* records that on 14 April 1580 two Jesuits about to leave for England asked the reigning pope to explain the bull, and were granted the answer that as things stood it did not bind Catholics [[cite:faculties]].
