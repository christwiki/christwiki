Roger of Hoveden records that in 1162 Pope Alexander came into France, driven from his see by the emperor Frederick, and that the kings of France and England received him as the true pope; the next year he held a council at Tours and excommunicated his rival [[cite:hov-1162]]. In 1170, at the complaint of [[person:thomas-becket]], he punished the bishops who had crowned the king's son [[cite:hov-1170]], and in 1172 his legates took the oath of [[person:henry-ii-of-england]] at Avranches [[cite:hov-1172]].

In March 1179 he held a general council at the Lateran, whose last canon condemns the Cathars and their protectors [[cite:lateran]]. Walter Map saw followers of [[person:peter-waldo]] petition him there for leave to preach [[cite:map]], and the Chronicle of Laon says that he embraced Waldo, approved his vow of poverty and restricted his preaching [[cite:laon-1178]].
