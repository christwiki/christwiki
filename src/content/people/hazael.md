In Kings, Hazael is an officer of Ben-hadad of Damascus. [[person:elisha]] tells him, weeping, that he will be king and will do great harm to Israel; the next day the king is dead and Hazael reigns [[bible:2 Kings 8:7-15]]. An inscription of [[person:shalmaneser-iii]] puts it more bluntly: Hadad-ezer died and Hazael, "son of a nobody", took the throne [[cite:nobody]]. The Assyrian annals still name Hadad-ezer as king of Damascus in 848 BC [[cite:year11]] and name Hazael in 841 BC, when Shalmaneser defeated him and shut him up in Damascus without claiming to have taken the city [[cite:year18]].

Hazael fought Joram of Israel at Ramoth-gilead [[bible:2 Kings 8:28-29]], took Israel's land east of the Jordan in the time of [[person:jehu]] [[bible:2 Kings 10:32-33]], and later marched as far as Gath and was bought off from attacking [[place:jerusalem]] with the temple treasure [[bible:2 Kings 12:17-18]]. Kings says he oppressed Israel throughout the reign of Jehu's son and was succeeded by his own son Ben-hadad [[bible:2 Kings 13:22-24]]; Amos remembers his house in an oracle against [[place:damascus]] [[bible:Amos 1:3-4]]. The victory stele found at [[place:dan]], whose author's name is lost, is usually assigned to him [[cite:teldan]]; see [[event:tel-dan-stele]].
