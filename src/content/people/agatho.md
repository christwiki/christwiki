The *Liber Pontificalis* says that Agatho was a Sicilian, that he held the see for two years and six months, and that he was buried at St Peter's on 10 January. It records that he received the emperor's request for delegates, which had been addressed to his predecessor, and sent three bishops with priests, a deacon and monks to [[place:constantinople]] [[cite:lp]].

The letter he gave them teaches two natural wills and two natural operations in Christ and says that the Roman church has never strayed from the apostolic tradition [[cite:letter]]. The [[event:third-council-of-constantinople]] adopted it, and its bishops told the emperor that Peter had spoken through Agatho [[cite:address]]. Bede records that Agatho also sent John, the chief cantor of St Peter's, to England with Benedict Biscop, both to teach the Roman chant and to report on the faith of the English church [[cite:bede]].
