Dallet says that Yi Seung-hun was born in 1756 into a family of officials and took the doctor's degree in 1780 [[cite:birth]]. He went to [[place:beijing]] with his father's embassy at the end of 1783 and was baptized there before it left, taking the name Peter; the missionary who reported it described him as twenty-seven years old and a good scholar [[cite:ventavon]]. Back in [[place:seoul]] he baptized [[person:yi-byeok]] and Kwon Il-sin [[cite:baptisms]]. The Catholic bishops of Korea call these three the founding fathers of their church [[cite:founders]].

Dallet records that in 1785, under pressure from his family, he burned his books and put out a writing to excuse himself for having been a Christian [[cite:fall]], that he later returned and was among those who drafted the letter of 1789 to the bishop of Beijing [[cite:letter]], and that he was beheaded outside the West Gate of the capital on 8 April 1801, aged forty-five [[cite:death]]. See [[event:first-korean-baptism]].
