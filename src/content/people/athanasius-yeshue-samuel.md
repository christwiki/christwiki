Samuel was head of the Syrian Orthodox Monastery of St Mark in [[place:jerusalem]] when a Bethlehem dealer sold him four of the scrolls brought in by the Bedouin. In 1948 John C. Trever of the American Schools of Oriental Research photographed three of them; the fourth could not yet be opened. Samuel took the scrolls to the United States, where he kept them at a Syrian church in New Jersey, and in June 1954 advertised them in the Wall Street Journal. They were bought for the State of Israel by Yigael Yadin, son of [[person:eleazar-sukenik]] [[cite:discovery]]. See [[event:dead-sea-scrolls-discovered]].
