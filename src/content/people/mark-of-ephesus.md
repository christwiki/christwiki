Doukas calls Mark the chief spokesman of the Greek bishops at Florence, and in Greek learning and the decrees of the holy councils a rule and an unswerving standard. He says that Mark alone held out when the others agreed, and that his objection was to the addition made to the creed: let it be removed from the confession of faith and sung anywhere else [[cite:doukas]].

In his own letter to all Orthodox Christians Mark rejects the decree's teaching on the procession of the Spirit, the addition to the creed, unleavened bread, purgatory and the papacy, and tells the faithful to avoid those who signed [[cite:letter]]. The handbook of the Orthodox Church in America calls him Saint Mark of Ephesus and says that he is venerated in the Orthodox Church as one of the Three Pillars of Orthodoxy [[cite:saint]].
