---
name: Moses
role: Prophet and lawgiver of Israel
summary: The Levite who, in the Bible's account, led Israel out of Egypt, received the law at Sinai and brought the people to the edge of Canaan. Jews and Christians name the first five books of the Bible after him.
---

Moses was born to Levite parents in Egypt and brought up by Pharaoh's daughter [[bible:Exod 2:1-10]]. After killing an Egyptian he fled to Midian [[bible:Exod 2:11-15]], and at Horeb he was sent back to bring Israel out [[bible:Exod 3:1-10]]. He stands at the centre of the events from the exodus to the edge of Canaan: the plagues and the crossing of the sea, the covenant and the law at [[place:mount-sinai]], the building of the tabernacle and the forty years in the wilderness. He died on [[place:mount-nebo]] at the age of 120, in sight of the land he was not allowed to enter [[bible:Deut 34:1-7]].

The Bible describes him as a prophet without equal, with whom God spoke "mouth to mouth" [[bible:Num 12:6-8]] [[bible:Deut 34:10]]. The New Testament sets him beside Jesus: "the law was given through Moses. Grace and truth were realized through Jesus Christ" [[bible:John 1:17]]. Hebrews calls him a faithful servant in God's house and Christ the Son over it [[bible:Heb 3:1-6]]. The Bible gives no date for his birth or death and names no pharaoh, and no source from the time outside it mentions him; when he lived depends on the disputed date of [[event:the-exodus]].
