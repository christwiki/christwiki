Joseph Ratzinger was born at Marktl am Inn in Bavaria on 16 April 1927 and ordained a priest in 1951 [[cite:deed]]. He taught theology at Bonn, Münster, Tübingen and Regensburg, and attended the [[event:second-vatican-council|Second Vatican Council]] as an expert adviser to Cardinal Frings [[cite:deed]]. Paul VI made him archbishop of Munich and Freising and a cardinal in 1977, and [[person:john-paul-ii]] made him prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in 1981 [[cite:deed]]. The cardinals elected him pope on 19 April 2005 [[cite:deed]].

As prefect he signed the instructions of 1984 and 1986 on liberation theology [[cite:ln-end]] [[cite:lc-end]]; see [[event:medellin-and-liberation-theology]]. He chaired the commission that prepared the Catechism of the Catholic Church [[cite:fd-2]] and the one that prepared its Latin edition [[cite:laetamur]]; see [[event:catechism-of-the-catholic-church]]. As pope he argued that the council should be read as reform in continuity and not as a break [[cite:curia-2005]].

He also signed the letter of 2001 that told the bishops of the new law reserving cases of the sexual abuse of minors by clerics to his congregation, and as pope he approved its revision in 2010 [[cite:norms]]. That year he wrote to the Catholics of Ireland, telling victims that he was "truly sorry" and the bishops that some of them and their predecessors had failed, at times grievously, to apply canon law to the crime of child abuse [[cite:ireland]]; see [[event:clergy-abuse-crisis]].

On 11 February 2013 he declared that he renounced the ministry of bishop of Rome from 28 February [[cite:declaratio]]; see [[event:benedict-resigns-francis-elected]]. He lived his last years in the Mater Ecclesiae monastery in the Vatican and died on 31 December 2022 [[cite:deed]].
