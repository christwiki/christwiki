Eleazar is known from [[person:josephus]]. He was a kinsman of Menahem, a rebel leader in Jerusalem. When Menahem's party was attacked in the temple and Menahem was killed, Eleazar was among the few who escaped to [[place:masada]], where, in Josephus's words, he "acted the part of a tyrant" afterwards [[cite:escape]]. Josephus calls him the commander of the Sicarii who held the fortress and a descendant of [[person:judas-the-galilean|the Judas]] who had urged the Jews not to submit to the Roman census [[cite:descent]].

When the Romans under [[person:flavius-silva]] had burned the defenders' last wall, Josephus says, Eleazar would neither flee nor let others flee, and in two speeches persuaded his men to kill their families and themselves [[cite:speeches]]. Whether the speeches or the manner of the end can be taken as history is disputed; see the dating note on [[event:fall-of-masada|the fall of Masada]].
