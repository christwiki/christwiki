Arius was a presbyter at [[place:alexandria]] under Bishop [[person:alexander-of-alexandria]]. Socrates says that he took the bishop's teaching on the unity of the Trinity for a revival of Sabellius, and argued against it that a begotten Son must have had a beginning [[cite:socrates]]. Little of his own writing survives. In a letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia he complains that he is persecuted for saying that the Son has a beginning while God has none [[cite:letter]], and Athanasius quotes lines from his *Thalia*, a statement of his views in verse [[cite:thalia]]. Almost everything else is reported by his opponents.

The [[event:council-of-nicaea]] condemned his teaching [[cite:council]] and [[person:constantine]] exiled him [[cite:exile]]. He was later recalled. Socrates, and Athanasius in a letter based on an eyewitness, relate that he died suddenly at [[place:constantinople]] on the day before he was to be received back into communion; both tell it as a judgement on him [[cite:death]] [[cite:athanasius-death]].
