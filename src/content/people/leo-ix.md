The letter sent in Leo's name to [[person:michael-cerularius]] insists that the Roman church is the head of all the churches and is judged by none, and cites the Donation of Constantine as proof of its privileges [[cite:letter]]. Hermann of Reichenau, a contemporary, records that the pope was detained for some time at Benevento, came back to Rome sick as Easter approached and died there in April 1054 [[cite:death]].

The legates nevertheless acted in July on the authority of the Roman see, citing the letters Pope Leo had sent to Michael [[cite:bull]], and the patriarch of Antioch, writing to Constantinople afterwards, advised waiting until a new pope was chosen [[cite:peter]]. See [[event:east-west-schism]].
