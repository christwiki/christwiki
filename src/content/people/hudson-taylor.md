The history of his mission gives his birth as 21 May 1832, the son of James Taylor of Barnsley in Yorkshire [[cite:birth]]. He dates his conversion to an afternoon when he was about fifteen and picked up a gospel tract in his father's library [[cite:conversion]]. He sailed from Liverpool on 19 September 1853 under the Chinese Evangelisation Society and landed at Shanghai on 1 March 1854 [[cite:voyage]]. He later resigned from that society because it was often in debt, which he held to be against Scripture, and went on working at Ningpo without a salary [[cite:resigned]].

Illness sent him back to England, where he worked on a revision of the New Testament in the Ningpo dialect and came to think that a new agency was needed for inland China [[cite:brighton]]. In 1865 he organised the China Inland Mission, and on 26 May 1866 he sailed with its first party in the *Lammermuir* [[cite:founded]]. See [[event:hudson-taylor]].
