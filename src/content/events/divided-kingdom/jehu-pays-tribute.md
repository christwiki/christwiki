dating: |
  Shalmaneser's annals place his defeat of Hazael in his eighteenth regnal year [[cite:obelisk]], and the fuller versions add that he then received the tribute of Jehu [[cite:annals]]. His sixth year is the eponymy of Dayan-Ashur, 853 BC, so the eighteenth is 841 BC [[cite:eponym]]. The caption on the Black Obelisk has no date of its own [[cite:caption]].

  Kings does not tie Jehu's revolt to any outside event. Jehu was king by 841, and Ahab, who was alive in 853 [[cite:kurkh]], was followed by two sons whose reigns Kings gives as two and twelve years [[bible:1 Kings 22:51; 2 Kings 3:1]]. Edwin R. Thiele, *The Mysterious Numbers of the Hebrew Kings* (3rd ed., 1983), argues that Israel then counted both the first and the last part-year of a reign as full years. On that reckoning the two reigns fill the twelve years between the Assyrian dates, and the revolt falls in 841 BC, shortly before the tribute.

Joram, son of [[person:ahab]], had been wounded fighting [[person:hazael]] of Aram at Ramoth-gilead and lay recovering at [[place:jezreel]], where Ahaziah, king of Judah, came to visit him [[bible:2 Kings 8:28-29]]. [[person:elisha]] sent one of the prophets to the army at Ramoth-gilead to anoint [[person:jehu]], one of its commanders, as king, with the charge to destroy the house of Ahab. His fellow officers spread their cloaks under him and proclaimed him king [[bible:2 Kings 9:1-13]].

Jehu drove to Jezreel. He shot Joram with an arrow and had the body thrown onto the field that had been Naboth's. Ahaziah was wounded as he fled and died at Megiddo [[bible:2 Kings 9:16-28]]; Chronicles has him caught in hiding in Samaria [[bible:2 Chr 22:9]]. [[person:jezebel]] was thrown from a window [[bible:2 Kings 9:30-37]]. The guardians of Ahab's seventy sons in [[place:samaria]] sent Jehu their heads; relatives of Ahaziah were killed on the road; and in Samaria Jehu gathered the worshippers of Baal into their temple, had them killed and turned the building into a latrine [[bible:2 Kings 10:1-28]].

Kings gives a divided verdict. Jehu did what the Lord intended to Ahab's house, and his sons would rule to the fourth generation; but he kept the golden calves of Bethel and Dan, and in his days Hazael took all Israel's land east of the Jordan [[bible:2 Kings 10:29-33]]. The prophet Hosea, about a century later, speaks of the blood of Jezreel as a debt still owed by Jehu's house [[bible:Hos 1:4]].

The Assyrian record adds something Kings leaves out. In his eighteenth year [[person:shalmaneser-iii]] crossed the Euphrates, defeated Hazael, shut him up in Damascus and marched on to a headland on the coast. At that time, the annals say, he received the tribute of Tyre, Sidon and Jehu of Israel [[cite:annals]]. The Black Obelisk, set up at [[place:kalhu]] near the end of the reign, shows the scene. A figure bows to the ground before the king, and the caption lists what Jehu sent: silver, gold, gold vessels, tin, staffs and spears [[cite:caption]]. The Assyrian scribes call him "son of Omri", that is, ruler of the land they named after Omri, and say nothing of how he came to the throne.

The revolt ended the dynasty of Omri and the royal cult of Baal, and it founded a dynasty that held the throne for four more generations [[bible:2 Kings 15:12]]. The tribute is the earliest recorded payment by a king of Israel to Assyria. A third account of these deaths, in the name of an Aramean king, survives on [[event:tel-dan-stele]].
