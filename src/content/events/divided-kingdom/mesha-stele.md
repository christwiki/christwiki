dating: |
  The stele gives no year. Kings dates Moab's revolt to the death of Ahab [[bible:2 Kings 1:1; 3:5]], and Ahab was alive in 853 BC [[cite:kurkh]]. Mesha says that Israel held the land of Medeba through Omri's days and half the days of his son, forty years in all, and that he has seen the ruin of Omri's house [[cite:omri]]. That may look back on the fall of the dynasty in 841 BC. The Louvre's record says that it was carved shortly after 842 BC and dates it to the time of Mesha, about 830–805 BC [[cite:louvre]]. The revolt began after 853 BC, and the stele is placed here about 840 BC; it may be a decade or more later.

  The reading of line 31 is disputed. André Lemaire, "'House of David' Restored in Moabite Inscription", *Biblical Archaeology Review* 20/3 (1994), restored the damaged letters as *bt[d]wd*, "House of David". Israel Finkelstein, Nadav Na'aman and Thomas Römer, "Restoring Line 31 in the Mesha Stele: The 'House of David' or Biblical Balak?", *Tel Aviv* 46 (2019), pp. 3–11, read a king's name instead, perhaps Balak. Michael Langlois, "The Kings, the City and the House of David on the Mesha Stele in Light of New Imaging Techniques", *Semitica* 61 (2019), pp. 23–47, defends Lemaire's reading. Matthieu Richelle, "A Re-examination of the Reading BT DWD ('House of David') on the Mesha Stele", *Eretz-Israel* 34 (2021), pp. 152–159, judges that it remains unproven.

Kings says that [[person:mesha]], king of Moab, was a sheep breeder who paid the king of Israel a heavy tribute in lambs and wool, and that he rebelled when [[person:ahab]] died [[bible:2 Kings 3:4-5]]. Joram of Israel marched against him with Jehoshaphat of Judah and the king of Edom, by the desert road through Edom. When the army ran out of water, the prophet [[person:elisha]] promised both water and victory. The allies laid Moab waste and shut Mesha up in Kir-hareseth. There he sacrificed his eldest son on the city wall. Kings then says, "There was great wrath against Israel", and the allies went home [[bible:2 Kings 3:6-27]].

Mesha's own account was found in 1868 at Dhiban, ancient [[place:dibon]] [[cite:find]], on a basalt stele now in the Louvre [[cite:louvre]]. He says that Omri, king of Israel, oppressed Moab for many years because Chemosh, Moab's god, was angry with his land, and that Omri's son meant to do the same. But he, Mesha, has seen the downfall of that son and of his house, and Israel, he says, has perished for ever [[cite:omri]].

He then lists his gains. He took Ataroth, where the men of Gad had lived "from of old", and killed its people [[cite:ataroth]]; Numbers also counts Ataroth among the towns of Gad [[bible:Num 32:34]]. At the god's command he marched by night against Nebo, took it by noon, killed seven thousand people because he had devoted the town to Ashtar-Chemosh, and dragged the vessels of Yahweh before Chemosh [[cite:nebo]]. Chemosh, he says, drove the king of Israel from Jahaz [[cite:jahaz]], and he himself rebuilt towns, cisterns and a road by the Arnon, some of it with Israelite prisoners [[cite:building]].

The two accounts agree that the house of Omri ruled Moab, that Mesha ended that rule, and that Israel did not win it back. They do not describe the same fighting. Kings tells of an invasion by way of Edom that broke off at Kir-hareseth. The stele tells of towns taken on the plateau around Medeba and Dibon [[bible:Josh 13:9]] and names no king of Israel but Omri. Each credits its victories to its own god, and Mesha's devoting of a whole town to his god is the practice that the book of Joshua describes at Jericho [[bible:Josh 6:17-21]].

The stele names Yahweh, the god of Israel, in the words of one of Israel's enemies [[cite:nebo]]. A broken passage near its foot [[cite:line31]] has been read as naming the "House of David"; that reading is contested and is discussed in the note on dating.
