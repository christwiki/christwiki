  Amos dates his words to the reigns of Uzziah of Judah and Jeroboam son of Joash of Israel, "two years before the earthquake" [[bible:Amos 1:1]]. The earthquake was still remembered centuries later [[bible:Zech 14:5]], but no source gives its year. Hosea's heading runs from Uzziah to Hezekiah [[bible:Hos 1:1]]. His first oracle looks ahead to the end of Jehu's dynasty, which came with the murder of Jeroboam's son [[bible:Hos 1:4]] [[bible:2 Kings 15:8-12]], so he too began while Jeroboam was alive.

Under [[person:jeroboam-ii]] the northern kingdom recovered its old borders [[bible:2 Kings 14:25-28]]. The book of Amos describes its leading people as rich and at ease, lying on beds of ivory, and as zealous in their festivals and offerings [[bible:Amos 6:1-6]] [[bible:Amos 5:21-23]].

[[person:amos]] kept flocks and tended sycamore figs at Tekoa in Judah [[bible:Amos 1:1]] [[bible:Amos 7:12-14]]. He went north and announced judgment on Israel for selling the needy, denying justice to the poor and crushing the weak [[bible:Amos 2:6-8]] [[bible:Amos 4:1-3]]. Being chosen, he said, made Israel more answerable, not safer [[bible:Amos 3:1-2]]. In the Lord's name he rejected the worship of the sanctuaries [[bible:Amos 5:21-24]]:

> But let justice roll on like rivers, and righteousness like a mighty stream.

At [[place:bethel]], the king's sanctuary, the priest Amaziah accused him of conspiracy and told him to go home and prophesy in Judah. Amos answered that he was no prophet by trade; the Lord had taken him from following the flock. He repeated that Israel would go into exile [[bible:Amos 7:10-17]].

[[person:hosea]] began under the same king [[bible:Hos 1:1]]. His book says he was told to marry a woman of prostitution. He married Gomer, and their three children were given names of judgment: Jezreel, Lo-Ruhamah and Lo-Ammi, "not my people" [[bible:Hos 1:2-9]]. A second account has him buy an adulterous woman and keep her apart for many days [[bible:Hos 3:1-5]]. The marriage stands for the covenant. Israel has gone after the Baals, and the Lord will win her back and betroth her to himself for ever [[bible:Hos 2:14-20]]. Hosea names the coming master, Assyria, and sets against the judgment the Lord's own reluctance: "How can I give you up, Ephraim?" [[bible:Hos 11:1-9]].

Within a generation Assyria ended the northern kingdom ([[event:fall-of-samaria]]). The New Testament draws on both prophets. Matthew applies "Out of Egypt I called my son" to Jesus and has Jesus quote "I desire mercy, and not sacrifice" [[bible:Matt 2:15]] [[bible:Matt 9:13]]. Paul applies "not my people" to the calling of the gentiles [[bible:Rom 9:24-26]]. At the council in Jerusalem, James cites Amos's promise that David's fallen tent will be rebuilt [[bible:Amos 9:11-12]] [[bible:Acts 15:13-18]].
