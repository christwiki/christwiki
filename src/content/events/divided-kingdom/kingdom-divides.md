  Kings gives no calendar date. The year is reached by counting back from two points that Assyrian records fix for the northern kingdom: Ahab fought at Qarqar in 853 BC [[cite:kurkh]] and Jehu paid tribute in 841 BC [[cite:annals]]. Kings gives the lengths of the two reigns between those kings [[bible:1 Kings 22:51; 2 Kings 3:1]] and of the reigns from Jeroboam to Ahab [[bible:1 Kings 14:20; 15:25, 33; 16:8, 23, 29]].

Kings traces the division back to the reign of [[person:solomon]]. [[person:jeroboam-i]], an Ephraimite whom Solomon had put in charge of the forced labour of the house of Joseph, was met on the road by the prophet Ahijah of Shiloh. Ahijah tore a new cloak into twelve pieces and gave him ten: ten tribes would be taken from Solomon's son, and one left to the house of [[person:david]] for the sake of David and of [[place:jerusalem]] [[bible:1 Kings 11:26-36]]. Solomon tried to kill Jeroboam, who fled to [[person:shoshenq-i|Shishak]], king of Egypt [[bible:1 Kings 11:40]].

When Solomon died, his son [[person:rehoboam]] went to [[place:shechem]], where all Israel had gathered to make him king. The assembly, with Jeroboam back from Egypt, asked him to lighten the heavy yoke his father had laid on them. The older counsellors advised him to agree. He took the advice of the young men he had grown up with and answered: "My father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions" [[bible:1 Kings 12:1-14]].

The northern tribes replied, "What portion have we in David?", and went home. Rehoboam's officer over the forced labour was stoned to death and the king fled to Jerusalem. Israel made Jeroboam king; only Judah, with Benjamin, stayed with the house of David. Rehoboam raised an army to win back the north, but the prophet Shemaiah forbade the war and the men went home [[bible:1 Kings 12:16-24]]. Kings says the outcome came from the Lord, to fulfil Ahijah's word [[bible:1 Kings 12:15]]. Chronicles tells the story in the same terms [[bible:2 Chr 10:1-11:4]].

Jeroboam made Shechem his residence. So that his people would not go up to sacrifice in Jerusalem, he set up two golden calves, one at [[place:bethel]] and one at [[place:dan]], appointed priests who were not Levites and fixed a festival of his own in the eighth month [[bible:1 Kings 12:25-33]]. Chronicles adds that priests and Levites from the north moved to Judah [[bible:2 Chr 11:13-17]]. Kings calls the calves the sin of Jeroboam and measures later kings of Israel by it [[bible:1 Kings 12:30; 16:26; 2 Kings 10:29; 15:9]].

From here the Old Testament follows two kingdoms. Israel, the larger, lasted until [[event:fall-of-samaria]]; Judah, ruled by David's line, lasted until [[event:fall-of-jerusalem]]. Ahijah's oracle says that David's descendants would be humbled, "but not forever" [[bible:1 Kings 11:39]]. Ezekiel later looks for a day when they will no longer be "divided into two kingdoms" and David will be king over them all [[bible:Ezek 37:22-24]].
