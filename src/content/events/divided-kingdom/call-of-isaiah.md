dating: |
  Isaiah dates the vision to the year King Uzziah died [[bible:Isa 6:1]]. No outside source fixes that year. Edwin R. Thiele, *The Mysterious Numbers of the Hebrew Kings* (3rd ed., 1983), puts it at 740/739 BC; W. F. Albright, "The Chronology of the Divided Monarchy of Israel", *Bulletin of the American Schools of Oriental Research* 100 (1945), pp. 16–22, at 742 BC. Because the vision stands in chapter 6 and not at the head of the book, it is also debated whether it was Isaiah's first call or a later commission.

  The meeting with Ahaz came some years afterwards. The attack by Rezin and Pekah is usually dated to 734–732 BC (Mordechai Cogan and Hayim Tadmor, *II Kings*, Anchor Bible, 1988). A summary inscription of Tiglath-pileser III written about 729 BC already counts Ahaz, under the name Jehoahaz, among the kings who had paid him tribute [[cite:tribute]].

In the year that King [[person:uzziah]] died, [[person:isaiah]] son of Amoz saw the Lord seated on a high throne, the hem of his robe filling the temple. Six-winged seraphim called to one another, "Holy, holy, holy, is Yahweh of Armies! The whole earth is full of his glory!" The thresholds shook and the house filled with smoke [[bible:Isa 6:1-4]]. Isaiah cried out that he was undone, a man of unclean lips who had seen the King. A seraph touched his mouth with a coal from the altar and told him that his sin was forgiven. Then he heard the Lord ask whom he should send, and answered, "Here I am. Send me!" [[bible:Isa 6:5-8]]. He was sent to a people who would hear and not understand, until the land lay waste and only a stump was left [[bible:Isa 6:9-13]].

Some years later, in the reign of Uzziah's grandson [[person:ahaz]], Rezin of Damascus and Pekah of Israel marched on [[place:jerusalem]] to put a king of their own on its throne [[bible:Isa 7:1-6]] [[bible:2 Kings 16:5]]. Isaiah met Ahaz by the upper pool and told him to keep calm, because the plan would fail: "If you will not believe, surely you shall not be established" [[bible:Isa 7:3-9]]. Ahaz declined to ask for a sign. Isaiah gave one. A woman would conceive and bear a son and name him Immanuel, and before the child could tell good from evil the lands of the two kings would be deserted [[bible:Isa 7:10-16]]. The Hebrew word for her is *almah*. The ancient Greek translation renders it "a virgin" [[cite:lxx]]. Irenaeus, about AD 180, reports that the Jewish translators Theodotion and Aquila wrote "a young woman", and argues against them [[cite:irenaeus]]. Translations still differ.

Ahaz turned to Assyria. He sent the silver and gold of the temple and the palace to Tiglath-pileser and called himself his servant and son, and the Assyrian king took Damascus and killed Rezin [[bible:2 Kings 16:7-9]]. Tiglath-pileser's own list of tributaries includes Jehoahaz of Judah [[cite:tribute]]. Isaiah warned that the Assyrian flood would sweep on into Judah itself [[bible:Isa 8:5-8]].

The same chapters speak of a child born to sit on David's throne, named "Wonderful, Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace" [[bible:Isa 9:6-7]], and of a shoot from the stock of Jesse on whom the Spirit of the Lord rests, who judges the poor with righteousness and in whose reign the wolf lives with the lamb [[bible:Isa 11:1-9]].

In Isaiah the Immanuel sign is tied to the crisis of Ahaz's own day [[bible:Isa 7:16]]. Matthew, quoting it in the Greek form, says it was fulfilled in the birth of Jesus, and applies the light dawning on Galilee to the start of his ministry [[bible:Matt 1:22-23]] [[bible:Matt 4:13-16]]. John says that Isaiah "saw his glory, and spoke of him" [[bible:John 12:39-41]], Paul cites the root of Jesse [[bible:Rom 15:12]], and the seraphim's cry returns in the worship of heaven in Revelation [[bible:Rev 4:8]].
