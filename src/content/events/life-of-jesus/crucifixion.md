dating: |
  The Gospels do not give the year. It is worked out from the evidence set out below, and two years satisfy it: AD 30 and AD 33. The timeline places the event at AD 30 and shows both.

  **The officials.** Jesus died while Pontius Pilate governed Judea and Caiaphas was high priest [[bible:Matt 26:57; Matt 27:2]]. Josephus says that Pilate succeeded Valerius Gratus, who had appointed Caiaphas [[cite:pilate-arrives]], and that after ten years he was sent to Rome and arrived after Tiberius had died, about when Caiaphas was deposed [[cite:pilate-leaves]]. Tiberius died on 16 March AD 37 [[cite:tiberius-death]]. The limits are therefore about AD 26 and AD 36.

  **The start of the ministry.** Luke dates the preaching of John the Baptist to the fifteenth year of Tiberius [[bible:Luke 3:1-2]], AD 28/29 by the usual count or a year or so earlier by others (see [[event:ministry-of-john-the-baptist]]). Jesus was baptised after that, and John's Gospel then names three Passovers, the last being that of his death [[bible:John 2:13; John 6:4; John 11:55]]. In John, at the first of them the temple has been forty-six years in building [[bible:John 2:20]]; Josephus puts the start of the work in Herod's eighteenth year, about 20/19 BC [[cite:temple]], which points to AD 27 or 28.

  **The day.** All four Gospels put the death on the day before the Sabbath, a Friday [[bible:Mark 15:42; Matt 27:62; Luke 23:54; John 19:31]]. In John that Friday is the day of preparation for the Passover, the 14th of the month Nisan [[bible:John 19:14]]; in the other three the Passover meal has been eaten the evening before, which makes it the 15th [[bible:Mark 14:12-17]]. The Jewish month began with the sighting of the new moon, so the weekday of 14 and 15 Nisan can be calculated for each year. Within Pilate's term a Friday coincided with the 14th of Nisan, or fell within a day of it, in AD 30 (7 April) and AD 33 (3 April). The other years either have no such Friday or, like AD 27 and AD 34, are hard to fit to the rest of the evidence.

  **The two proposals.** AD 30 gives a ministry of about two years beginning in AD 28; so John P. Meier, *A Marginal Jew*, vol. 1 (1991), chapter 11. It suits the forty-six years of John 2:20. AD 33 follows from counting the fifteenth year of Tiberius as AD 29 and allowing three or more years; so Harold W. Hoehner, *Chronological Aspects of the Life of Christ* (1977), and Colin J. Humphreys and W. G. Waddington, "Dating the Crucifixion", *Nature* 306 (1983), pp. 743–746, who give the astronomical calculations. The evidence does not decide between them.

  The place, Golgotha, is described only as outside the city and near it [[bible:John 19:20; Heb 13:12]]; on the traditional site see [[place:golgotha]].

After [[person:pontius-pilate|Pilate]]'s sentence, Mark tells, the soldiers dressed [[person:jesus]] in purple, crowned him with thorns and mocked him as "King of the Jews". Then they led him out [[bible:Mark 15:16-20]]. A passer-by, Simon of Cyrene, was made to carry his cross [[bible:Mark 15:21]]. At [[place:golgotha]], "the place of a skull", near the city of [[place:jerusalem]], they crucified him between two others and cast lots for his clothes [[bible:Mark 15:22-27; John 19:20]]. The charge written above him read "The King of the Jews" [[bible:Mark 15:26]]. John says that it stood in Hebrew, Latin and Greek, and that Pilate refused to alter it [[bible:John 19:19-22]].

Passers-by and the chief priests mocked him [[bible:Mark 15:29-32]]. Luke records the prayer "Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they are doing", a sentence that some ancient manuscripts lack, and a promise to one of the two crucified with him: "today you will be with me in Paradise" [[bible:Luke 23:34, 39-43]]. Women who had followed him from Galilee watched from a distance, among them [[person:mary-magdalene]] [[bible:Mark 15:40-41]]. John places his mother [[person:mary]] beside the cross with "the disciple whom he loved", to whom Jesus entrusted her [[bible:John 19:25-27]]; Irenaeus, about AD 180, identifies that disciple as [[person:john-the-apostle|John]] [[cite:irenaeus]].

From the sixth hour, Mark says, there was darkness over the whole land. At the ninth hour Jesus cried out the first line of Psalm 22, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?", and then, with a loud cry, he died [[bible:Mark 15:33-37; Ps 22:1]]. In Luke his last words are "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit" [[bible:Luke 23:46]], in John "It is finished" [[bible:John 19:30]]. Mark reports that the veil of the temple was torn in two and that the centurion said, "Truly this man was the Son of God!" [[bible:Mark 15:38-39]]. Matthew adds an earthquake and the opening of tombs [[bible:Matt 27:51-53]]. John reports that a soldier pierced Jesus's side with a spear, and appeals to an eyewitness for it [[bible:John 19:33-35]].

Two writers outside the New Testament mention the execution. Tacitus, a Roman senator writing about AD 115–120 and hostile to the Christians, says that "Christus" was put to death by Pontius Pilatus in the reign of Tiberius [[cite:tacitus]]. He does not say where he learned it. A passage in Josephus, written in AD 93–94, says that Pilate condemned Jesus to the cross "at the suggestion of the principal men amongst us". As the manuscripts give it, the passage also calls him the Christ and says that he appeared alive on the third day [[cite:josephus]]. That wording is disputed, and how much of the passage Josephus himself wrote is debated; see [[source:josephus-antiquities]]. So far as the passage is his, it is a second notice from outside the church. Neither writer gives the day or the place.

The death of Jesus stands at the centre of Christian preaching from the first. Paul hands on, as something he had himself received, "that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures" [[bible:1 Cor 15:3]], and he calls the message of "Christ crucified" a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Greeks [[bible:1 Cor 1:23]]. The first Christians read the event through Israel's Scriptures: the Passover lamb, of which no bone was to be broken [[bible:John 19:36; Exod 12:46; 1 Cor 5:7]], and Isaiah's servant who "bore the sin of many" [[bible:Isa 53:12; Luke 22:37]].
