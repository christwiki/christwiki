[[person:archelaus]] had ruled Judea, Samaria and Idumea since the death of his father, Herod. [[person:josephus]] reports that the leading men of Judea and Samaria, together with his own brothers, accused him before [[person:augustus]] of cruelty. The emperor summoned him to Rome, heard the case, banished him to Vienne in Gaul and confiscated his property [[cite:banished]] [[cite:war-banished]]. Cassius Dio records the same sentence in a single line of his history of the year [[cite:dio]].

Augustus did not appoint another member of the family. Josephus writes in one place that the ethnarchy was made into a province and in another that it was attached to the province of Syria [[cite:coponius]] [[cite:annexed]]. A governor of equestrian rank, Coponius, was sent out with the power to inflict the death penalty [[cite:coponius]]. At the same time [[person:quirinius]], a senator and former consul, came to Syria as the emperor's legate with orders to assess property there, and he entered Judea to register what people owned and to sell off the estate of Archelaus [[cite:census]]. Tacitus calls [[place:caesarea-maritima|Caesarea]], on the coast, the capital of Judea, as [[place:antioch]] was of Syria [[cite:caesarea]]; the book of Acts shows the governor holding court there [[bible:Acts 23:33-35]].

The registration meant tax paid directly to Rome. Josephus says that the high priest Joazar persuaded most people to declare their property [[cite:census]]. A man named Judas, from Gamala in Gaulanitis, whom Josephus elsewhere calls [[person:judas-the-galilean]], joined with a Pharisee named Zadok to urge revolt. They called the census the beginning of slavery and taught that God alone should be their ruler [[cite:fourth-sect]] [[cite:coponius]]. These passages do not say how Judas ended. In the book of Acts the Pharisee Gamaliel recalls that Judas of Galilee "rose up in the days of the enrollment", that he perished, and that his followers were scattered [[bible:Acts 5:37]].

Josephus treats this as the beginning of the movement that led to the war of AD 66. He calls it a fourth school of thought beside the Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, and blames it for the destruction that followed [[cite:fourth-sect]]. Two sons of Judas were crucified by a later governor [[cite:sons]], and another, Menahem, was among the leaders of the revolt in Jerusalem [[cite:menahem]].

Judea was now governed by Roman officials. The fifth of them was [[person:pontius-pilate]] [[cite:governors]]. Galilee stayed under [[person:herod-antipas]] [[cite:tetrarchs]]. The question put to [[person:jesus]] in [[place:jerusalem]], whether it was lawful to pay tax to Caesar, belongs to the situation the census created [[bible:Mark 12:13-17]].
