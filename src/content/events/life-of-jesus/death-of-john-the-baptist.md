dating: |
  Neither source dates the execution. The Gospels place it during the Galilean ministry of Jesus, before the feeding of the five thousand [[bible:Mark 6:14-32]] [[bible:Matt 14:12-13]]. If John began to preach in AD 28–29 and Jesus was crucified in AD 30 or 33, the death falls between about AD 28 and 32; the timeline uses c. AD 29. See Harold W. Hoehner, *Herod Antipas* (1972).

  Josephus mentions the death only in looking back. He says that when the Nabataean king Aretas destroyed the army of Antipas, some Jews saw in the defeat a punishment for what Antipas had done to John [[cite:execution]]. That battle was fought not long before Tiberius died in March AD 37, for the Roman reprisal was called off at the news of his death [[cite:war-with-aretas]] [[cite:vitellius]]. Josephus does not say how many years separated the execution from the battle. A minority of scholars infer that John died about AD 35 and date the crucifixion to AD 36: Nikos Kokkinos, "Crucifixion in A.D. 36", in *Chronos, Kairos, Christos*, edited by J. Vardaman and E. M. Yamauchi (1989).

[[person:herod-antipas]], who ruled Galilee and Perea, had long been married to a daughter of Aretas, king of the Nabataean Arabs. [[person:josephus]] relates that on a journey to Rome he stayed with a half-brother, fell in love with that brother's wife, [[person:herodias]], and agreed to marry her and send his own wife away. The Nabataean princess learned of the plan and escaped to her father by way of the border fortress of [[place:machaerus]] [[cite:marriage]]. Herodias was herself a granddaughter of [[person:herod-the-great]] [[cite:marriage]], and Josephus says that in leaving a living husband for his brother she defied the ancestral laws [[cite:herodias]].

The Gospels say that [[person:john-the-baptist]] told the tetrarch, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife", and that Antipas put him in prison because of Herodias [[bible:Mark 6:17-18]] [[bible:Luke 3:19-20]]. The law of [[person:moses]] forbade a man to take his brother's wife [[bible:Lev 18:16; 20:21]].

Mark tells the end at length. Herodias wanted John dead, but Antipas feared him, knowing him to be "a righteous and holy man", and kept him safe. At a banquet on the tetrarch's birthday for his courtiers, officers and the leading men of Galilee, the daughter of Herodias danced, and Antipas swore to give her whatever she asked. Prompted by her mother she asked for the head of John the Baptist on a platter. The tetrarch was distressed but would not break his oath before his guests. A guard beheaded John in the prison, and his disciples came and buried the body [[bible:Mark 6:19-29]]. Matthew tells the same story more briefly and says that Antipas had wanted to kill John but feared the people [[bible:Matt 14:3-12]]. Neither Gospel names the place. Matthew does not name the girl [[bible:Matt 14:6]]. In Mark the manuscripts differ: the translation quoted here reads "the daughter of Herodias herself", while the linked translation follows manuscripts in which the girl is herself named Herodias and is called the tetrarch's daughter [[bible:Mark 6:22]].

Josephus names the place and gives a different motive. He also names a daughter of Herodias by her first marriage, Salome, without connecting her with John's death [[cite:herodias]]. Antipas, he says, feared that John's hold over the crowds might lead to a rising, thought it better to strike first, and sent him in chains to Machaerus, where he was put to death [[cite:execution]]. His account of John says nothing of the banquet or of John's words about the marriage. He also calls the first husband of Herodias simply Herod, where Mark calls him Philip [[cite:herodias]] [[bible:Mark 6:17]]; the "Philip" printed in brackets in the linked translation was added by the translator.

When Antipas later heard of the works of [[person:jesus]], the Gospels report, he said that John, whom he had beheaded, had been raised from the dead [[bible:Mark 6:14-16]]. In Mark and Matthew, Jesus speaks of John as the [[person:elijah]] who had already come and had been treated as his opponents pleased [[bible:Mark 9:11-13]] [[bible:Matt 17:10-13]].
