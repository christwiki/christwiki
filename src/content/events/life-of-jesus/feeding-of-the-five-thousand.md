dating: |
  John says that the Passover was near [[bible:John 6:4]], and Mark mentions green grass [[bible:Mark 6:39]], so the Gospels set the event in spring. In John's outline this is the Passover one year before the one at which Jesus died [[bible:John 11:55]]. With a crucifixion in AD 30 that is the spring of AD 29, the year used here; with a crucifixion in AD 33 it is AD 32. See Harold W. Hoehner, *Chronological Aspects of the Life of Christ* (1977), in the chapter on the duration of Christ's ministry.

  The Gospels do not agree exactly on the place. Luke says that Jesus had withdrawn towards "a city called Bethsaida" [[bible:Luke 9:10]]. Mark has the disciples sent off by boat afterwards "to the other side, to Bethsaida" [[bible:Mark 6:45]]. John puts the scene across the lake from Capernaum and says that boats from Tiberias came near the spot [[bible:John 6:1, 17, 23]]. The first three Gospels speak of a deserted place, and Mark, Matthew and John have Jesus cross the lake to reach it. The exact site is unknown, and the site of Bethsaida is itself disputed; see [[place:bethsaida]].

This is the only miracle of the Galilean ministry told in all four Gospels [[bible:Mark 6:30-44]] [[bible:Matt 14:13-21]] [[bible:Luke 9:10-17]] [[bible:John 6:1-15]]. In Mark the Twelve have just returned from their mission, and [[person:jesus]] takes them away by boat on the [[place:sea-of-galilee|lake]] to a deserted place to rest. Matthew adds that he had just heard of the death of [[person:john-the-baptist]]. The crowds saw them go and followed on foot. Jesus had compassion on them, Mark says, "because they were like sheep without a shepherd", and he taught them; Matthew and Luke say that he healed their sick [[bible:Mark 6:30-34]] [[bible:Matt 14:12-14]] [[bible:Luke 9:10-11]]. Luke sets the scene near [[place:bethsaida]].

As Mark tells it, late in the day the disciples asked him to send the people away to buy food. He answered, "You give them something to eat." They had five loaves and two fish; in John it is [[person:andrew]] who points out a boy carrying five barley loaves and two fish. Jesus had the people sit down on the grass. He took the loaves, looked up to heaven, blessed and broke them, and gave them to the disciples to hand out, and he divided the fish. All ate and were satisfied, and the disciples filled twelve baskets with what was left. The men numbered about five thousand; Matthew adds that there were women and children besides [[bible:Mark 6:35-44]] [[bible:Matt 14:15-21]] [[bible:John 6:5-13]].

John alone reports the reaction. The people said, "This is truly the prophet who comes into the world", and Jesus, seeing that they meant to take him by force and make him king, withdrew to the mountain by himself [[bible:John 6:14-15]]. That night, according to Mark, Matthew and John, the disciples in their boat saw him walking on the sea [[bible:Mark 6:45-52]] [[bible:Matt 14:22-33]] [[bible:John 6:16-21]]. Mark and Matthew also tell of a second feeding, of four thousand people with seven loaves [[bible:Mark 8:1-10, 19-20]] [[bible:Matt 15:32-39]].

In John the crowd finds Jesus the next day at [[place:capernaum]], and he speaks in the synagogue there about the manna their ancestors ate in the wilderness and about himself: "I am the bread of life." He says that the bread he will give is his flesh, for the life of the world. Many of his disciples found the saying hard and left him. When he asked the Twelve whether they too would go, [[person:peter]] answered, "You have the words of eternal life" [[bible:John 6:22-69]].

The story recalls the account in Kings of [[person:elisha]] feeding a hundred men with twenty barley loaves, with some left over [[bible:2 Kings 4:42-44]], and the manna that Exodus says was given in the time of [[person:moses]] [[bible:Exod 16:4, 14-15]]. The actions of taking, blessing, breaking and giving return in the accounts of the Last Supper [[bible:Mark 6:41; 14:22]].
