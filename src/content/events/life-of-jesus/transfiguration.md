Mark and Matthew set the event six days after Peter's confession near Caesarea Philippi [[bible:Mark 8:27-29; Mark 9:2; Matt 17:1]], and Luke "about eight days" after it [[bible:Luke 9:28]]. It stands in all three shortly before Jesus leaves Galilee for Judea and Jerusalem for the last time [[bible:Mark 10:1, 32; Matt 19:1; Luke 9:51]]. The year therefore depends on the year of the crucifixion: about AD 29 if Jesus died in AD 30, about AD 32 if he died in AD 33. By about AD 350 Cyril of Jerusalem names Mount Tabor [[cite:cyril]], and that tradition is followed on the map. Six days after [[person:peter]] had called him the Christ, Mark and Matthew say, [[person:jesus]] took Peter, [[person:james-son-of-zebedee]] and [[person:john-the-apostle]] up a high mountain by themselves [[bible:Mark 8:29; Mark 9:2; Matt 17:1]]. Luke says "about eight days", and that he went up to pray [[bible:Luke 9:28]].

There, the three Gospels report, his appearance changed in front of them. Mark says that his clothing became "glistening, exceedingly white" [[bible:Mark 9:2-3]]. Matthew adds that his face shone like the sun [[bible:Matt 17:2]]. Moses and Elijah appeared and talked with him [[bible:Mark 9:4]]. Only Luke gives the subject: they spoke of his "departure", which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem [[bible:Luke 9:30-31]]. Peter proposed to make three tents, one each for Jesus, Moses and Elijah; Mark remarks that he did not know what to say, because they were very afraid [[bible:Mark 9:5-6]].

A cloud then overshadowed them, and a voice came out of it: "This is my beloved Son. Listen to him" [[bible:Mark 9:7]]. In Matthew the disciples fell on their faces, and Jesus touched them and told them not to be afraid [[bible:Matt 17:6-7]]. When they looked around they saw only Jesus [[bible:Mark 9:8]]. On the way down he ordered them to tell no one what they had seen until the Son of Man had risen from the dead [[bible:Mark 9:9]]. They asked why the scribes said that Elijah must come first. He answered that Elijah had already come and had been treated as people pleased [[bible:Mark 9:11-13]]; Matthew notes that the disciples understood him to mean John the Baptist [[bible:Matt 17:13]].

The Gospels do not name the mountain. Cyril of Jerusalem, teaching about three hundred years later, speaks of the transfiguration "on Mount Tabor" [[cite:cyril]], and [[place:mount-tabor]] in Galilee is the traditional site.

In all three Gospels the scene stands between Jesus's first announcement that he must suffer and be killed [[bible:Mark 8:31]] and his journey to Jerusalem. The voice echoes the one at his baptism [[bible:Mark 1:11]], and the two figures beside him are the lawgiver and the prophet whose return Malachi had promised [[bible:Mal 4:4-5]]. The second letter of Peter appeals to the event as something its author saw and heard: "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty", and "we heard this voice come out of heaven when we were with him on the holy mountain" [[bible:2 Pet 1:16-18]].
