  Luke gives a precise date: the word of God came to John "in the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar" [[bible:Luke 3:1-2]]. Augustus died on 19 August AD 14 [[cite:augustus-death]] and Tiberius succeeded him. Counted from that day, the fifteenth year runs from August AD 28 to August AD 29. If Luke used a calendar whose year began in the autumn or in January and counted the months of AD 14 as the first year, it is AD 27–28 or AD 28. A few scholars count from the powers Tiberius shared with Augustus in his last years and reach AD 26–27. The reckonings are set out in Jack Finegan, *Handbook of Biblical Chronology* (revised edition, 1998), and Harold W. Hoehner, *Chronological Aspects of the Life of Christ* (1977), in the chapter on the commencement of Christ's ministry.

  Luke's other names fit any of these years. Pontius Pilate came to Judea as the successor of Valerius Gratus and stayed ten years, leaving shortly before Tiberius died in March AD 37 [[cite:pilate-arrives]] [[cite:pilate-leaves]] [[cite:tiberius-death]]. Luke does not say how long John preached before he was imprisoned.

Luke opens his account of [[person:john-the-baptist]] with a formal date: the fifteenth year of the emperor [[person:tiberius]], when [[person:pontius-pilate]] governed Judea, [[person:herod-antipas]] was tetrarch of Galilee, and Annas and [[person:caiaphas]] held the high priesthood [[bible:Luke 3:1-2]]. Luke alone tells of John's birth, to the elderly priest Zechariah and his wife Elizabeth, a relative of Mary, and says that he lived in the desert until he appeared in public [[bible:Luke 1:5-25, 36, 57-80]].

Mark and Matthew tell that John preached in the [[place:judean-wilderness|wilderness of Judea]] and along the [[place:jordan-river|Jordan]], proclaiming "the baptism of repentance for forgiveness of sins". People came out from [[place:jerusalem]] and all Judea and were baptised by him in the river, confessing their sins [[bible:Mark 1:4-5]] [[bible:Matt 3:1-6]]. He wore camel's hair with a leather belt and ate locusts and wild honey [[bible:Mark 1:6]], dress that recalled the prophet [[person:elijah]] [[bible:2 Kings 1:8]]. The Gospels apply to him the "voice of one crying in the wilderness" from the book of Isaiah and [[person:malachi]]'s messenger who prepares the way [[bible:Mark 1:2-3]] [[bible:Isa 40:3]] [[bible:Mal 3:1]].

Matthew and Luke give the substance of his preaching. Judgment was near, descent from [[person:abraham]] would protect no one, and every tree that bore no good fruit would be cut down [[bible:Matt 3:7-10]] [[bible:Luke 3:7-9]]. In Luke he tells the crowds to share clothing and food, tax collectors to take no more than was due, and soldiers to extort nothing [[bible:Luke 3:10-14]]. In all four Gospels he points beyond himself to one who is coming, whose sandal he is not fit to untie (in Matthew, to carry) and who will baptise with the Holy Spirit; the first three call that one mightier than John [[bible:Mark 1:7-8]] [[bible:Matt 3:11-12]] [[bible:Luke 3:15-17]] [[bible:John 1:26-27, 33]]. In John's Gospel he denies that he is the Christ, Elijah or the prophet [[bible:John 1:19-23]]; in Matthew, [[person:jesus]] says of him, "this is Elijah, who is to come" [[bible:Matt 11:14]].

[[person:josephus]], writing about AD 93, also describes John. He calls him a good man who told the Jews to practise justice towards one another and piety towards God and so to come to baptism. The washing, Josephus explains, was not to gain pardon for sins but to purify the body once the soul had been cleansed by right conduct. Crowds gathered, and Herod Antipas feared that John's influence might lead to an uprising [[cite:josephus]]. In this passage Josephus says nothing of a message about one who was to come, and he does not connect John with Jesus.

Antipas put John in prison [[bible:Luke 3:19-20]]; see [[event:death-of-john-the-baptist]]. In Matthew, Jesus, who was baptised by him, says that no one born of women is greater than John [[bible:Matt 11:11]]. His following outlived him: the book of Acts tells of disciples at [[place:ephesus]], years later, who knew only "John's baptism" [[bible:Acts 19:1-7]].
