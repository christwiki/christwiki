dating: |
  The fixed point is Jacob's arrival in Egypt. The chronology explained at [[event:call-of-abraham]] puts it in 1876 BC, 430 years before an exodus in 1446 BC [[bible:Exod 12:40-41]] [[bible:1 Kgs 6:1]]. Joseph was then about 39: he had entered Pharaoh's service at 30, and seven years of plenty and two of famine had passed [[bible:Gen 41:46]] [[bible:Gen 41:53-54]] [[bible:Gen 45:6]]. He was sold at 17 [[bible:Gen 37:2]], about 1898 BC, and died at 110 [[bible:Gen 50:26]], about 1805 BC. The timeline shows the span from his sale to his father's arrival.

  No Egyptian text names Joseph or records these events, and Genesis does not name the Pharaoh, so there is no outside anchor. If the exodus is dated to the thirteenth century BC, or if the 430 years include the patriarchs' time in Canaan, as in the Septuagint and Josephus [[cite:lxx]] [[cite:josephus]], the dates come down by about two centuries, and by about four if both are accepted.

[[person:joseph]], a son of [[person:jacob]]'s old age, was his father's favourite, and Jacob gave him a special coat. He dreamed that his brothers' sheaves, and then the sun, moon and eleven stars, bowed down to him, and his brothers hated him [[bible:Gen 37:3-11]]. When he was sent to find them with the flocks, they threw him into a pit and then sold him for twenty pieces of silver to traders on their way to Egypt. They let their father believe that a wild animal had killed him [[bible:Gen 37:12-35]].

In Egypt Joseph was bought by Potiphar, the captain of Pharaoh's guard. Potiphar's wife accused him falsely and he was put in prison [[bible:Gen 39:1-20]]. There he interpreted the dreams of two of Pharaoh's officials [[bible:Gen 40:1-22]]. Two years later he was brought out to interpret Pharaoh's own dreams of seven fat cows eaten by seven thin ones: seven years of plenty would be followed by seven years of famine [[bible:Gen 41:1-32]]. Pharaoh set him over all Egypt. He was thirty years old, and he stored grain in the cities through the good years [[bible:Gen 41:39-49]].

The famine reached Canaan, and Jacob sent ten of his sons to buy grain. Joseph knew them, but they did not know him [[bible:Gen 42:1-8]]. He tested them, kept Simeon as a hostage and demanded to see Benjamin, the youngest [[bible:Gen 42:18-24]]. On their second visit he had his silver cup hidden in Benjamin's sack, and Judah offered to stay as a slave in the boy's place [[bible:Gen 44:1-34]]. Then Joseph made himself known: "God sent me before you to preserve life" [[bible:Gen 45:1-8]].

Jacob came down to Egypt with his whole family, seventy people in all, and they settled as herdsmen in [[place:goshen]] [[bible:Gen 46:5-7]] [[bible:Gen 46:26-34]] [[bible:Gen 47:1-12]]. He lived there seventeen years and was carried back to Canaan for burial [[bible:Gen 47:28]] [[bible:Gen 50:12-13]]. Joseph told his frightened brothers, "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good" [[bible:Gen 50:15-21]]. Before he died he made the Israelites swear to carry his bones out of Egypt when God brought them back to the land promised to Abraham [[bible:Gen 50:22-26]].

Exodus begins from this point: Israel multiplies in Egypt until a new king arises "who didn't know Joseph" [[bible:Exod 1:6-8]], and [[person:moses]] takes Joseph's bones with him at the exodus [[bible:Exod 13:19]]. Psalm 105 and [[person:stephen]]'s speech both retell the story as part of God's dealings with Israel [[bible:Ps 105:16-23]] [[bible:Acts 7:9-16]].
