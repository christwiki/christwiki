Isaac had been born to [[person:abraham]] and Sarah in their old age, and Abraham was living at [[place:beersheba]] in the south [[bible:Gen 21:1-5]] [[bible:Gen 21:33-34]]. Then, Genesis says, "God tested Abraham". He was to take "your son, your only son, whom you love, even Isaac", go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on a mountain that God would show him [[bible:Gen 22:1-2]].

Abraham rose early, split wood, and set out with [[person:isaac]] and two servants. On the third day he saw the place and left the servants behind. Isaac carried the wood; Abraham carried the fire and the knife [[bible:Gen 22:3-6]]. Isaac asked where the lamb for the offering was. Abraham answered, "God will provide himself the lamb for a burnt offering, my son" [[bible:Gen 22:7-8]]. At the place he built an altar, bound Isaac, laid him on the wood and took the knife [[bible:Gen 22:9-10]].

The angel of the Lord called to him from heaven: "Don't lay your hand on the boy or do anything to him. For now I know that you fear God" [[bible:Gen 22:11-12]]. Abraham saw a ram caught by its horns in a thicket and offered it instead of his son [[bible:Gen 22:13-14]]. The angel called a second time. Because Abraham had not withheld his son, God swore by himself to bless him, to make his offspring as many as the stars and the sand, and through his offspring to bless all the nations of the earth [[bible:Gen 22:15-18]]. Abraham returned to Beersheba [[bible:Gen 22:19]].

Genesis does not say where in Moriah the mountain was. Chronicles states that [[person:solomon]] built the temple "at [[place:jerusalem|Jerusalem]] on Mount Moriah" [[bible:2 Chr 3:1]], and [[person:josephus]], in the first century AD, says outright that the mountain of the sacrifice was the one on which the temple later stood [[cite:josephus]].

Hebrews says that Abraham offered Isaac by faith, reckoning that God could raise the dead, and that in a figure he did receive him back from death [[bible:Heb 11:17-19]]. James cites the act as the work that completed Abraham's faith [[bible:Jas 2:21-22]]. Christian writers from the second century read the story as a foreshadowing of the cross. [[person:irenaeus]], about AD 180, compares Isaac carrying the wood with the taking up of the cross, and writes that Abraham gave up his only and beloved son so that God might be pleased to offer his own beloved Son for all Abraham's offspring [[cite:irenaeus]].
