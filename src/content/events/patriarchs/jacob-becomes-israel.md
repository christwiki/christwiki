  Genesis gives Jacob's age at neither scene, but it can be worked out from figures elsewhere in the book. Jacob was 130 when he came to Egypt, in the second year of the famine [[bible:Gen 47:9]] [[bible:Gen 45:6]]. Joseph was then about 39: he had entered Pharaoh's service at 30, and seven years of plenty had followed [[bible:Gen 41:46-47]] [[bible:Gen 41:53-54]]. So Jacob was about 91 when Joseph was born, at the end of his first fourteen years with Laban [[bible:Gen 30:25-26]] [[bible:Gen 31:41]]. That makes him about 77 at Bethel on the way out and about 97 at Peniel on the way back, twenty years later [[bible:Gen 31:38]].

  On the chronology explained at [[event:call-of-abraham]], in which Jacob is born about 2006 BC, the dream at Bethel falls about 1929 BC and the night at Peniel about 1909 BC. These are the Bible's own figures added together. Nothing outside the Bible dates the events, and the other reckonings described there would move both dates by the same amount.

[[person:jacob]] and [[person:esau]] were the twin sons of Isaac and Rebekah. Before their birth Rebekah was told that "the elder will serve the younger" [[bible:Gen 25:21-26]]. Esau, the firstborn, sold Jacob his birthright for a meal [[bible:Gen 25:29-34]], and Jacob, with his mother's help, obtained by deceit the blessing that Isaac meant for Esau [[bible:Gen 27:5-29]]. Esau planned to kill him, and Jacob was sent away to his uncle Laban in [[place:haran]] [[bible:Gen 27:41-45]].

On the road, Genesis says, he slept in the open with a stone under his head. He dreamed of a stairway set between earth and heaven, with the angels of God going up and down on it. The Lord stood above it and gave him the promise made to Abraham and Isaac: the land, descendants like the dust of the earth, and blessing for all the families of the earth [[bible:Gen 28:10-15]]. Jacob set up the stone as a pillar, named the place [[place:bethel]], "God's house", and vowed that if God brought him home in peace the Lord would be his God [[bible:Gen 28:16-22]].

He served Laban for twenty years, married Leah and Rachel, and became the father of eleven sons and a daughter [[bible:Gen 29:15-30]] [[bible:Gen 29:31-30:24]] [[bible:Gen 31:38-41]]. On his way home he heard that Esau was coming to meet him with four hundred men. He sent gifts ahead and sent his family across the ford of the Jabbok [[bible:Gen 32:3-23]]. Left alone, he "wrestled with a man there until the breaking of the day". The man struck Jacob's hip and injured it, but Jacob would not let him go without a blessing. The man said: "Your name will no longer be called Jacob, but Israel; for you have fought with God and with men, and have prevailed" [[bible:Gen 32:24-28]]. Jacob called the place [[place:peniel]], saying, "I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved" [[bible:Gen 32:30]]. He went on limping, and Esau ran to meet him and embraced him [[bible:Gen 32:31-33:4]].

Back in Canaan, Jacob returned to Bethel and built an altar. There, Genesis says, God appeared to him again, confirmed the name Israel and repeated the promise of the land [[bible:Gen 35:6-15]]. Benjamin, his twelfth son, was born soon afterwards [[bible:Gen 35:16-18]]. The twelve sons are the ancestors from whom the tribes of Israel take their names [[bible:Gen 49:28]].

[[person:hosea]] recalls that Jacob "struggled with the angel, and prevailed" and met God at Bethel [[bible:Hos 12:3-4]]. In John's Gospel [[person:jesus]] takes up the dream and speaks of "the angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man" [[bible:John 1:51]].
