[[person:mary-i-of-england]] was proclaimed queen in [[place:london]] on 19 July 1553 [[cite:machyn-accession]]. At the end of November 1554 the Lords and Commons declared themselves "very sorry and repentant of the schism" from Rome and asked to be received back into the unity of the church, and the pope's legate, Cardinal [[person:reginald-pole]], absolved the realm [[cite:repeal]] [[cite:foxe-absolution]]. The same Parliament revived three medieval statutes against heresy, to be in force from 20 January 1555 [[cite:revival]]. Under the statute of 1401, a person convicted of heresy in a bishop's court who refused to abjure, or who relapsed after abjuring, was handed to the sheriff to be burned in public [[cite:comburendo]].

The first to die, Foxe says, was John Rogers, burned at Smithfield in London on 4 February 1555 [[cite:foxe-rogers]]; the diary of the Londoner Henry Machyn records the burning under the same day [[cite:machyn-rogers]]. In May the king and queen wrote to the bishop of London complaining that heretics sent to the bishops were being left in their errors. He was to try to win them back and, if they stayed obstinate, to proceed against them "according to the order of the laws" [[cite:foxe-letter]].

On 16 October 1555 [[person:nicholas-ridley]], bishop of London under [[person:edward-vi]], and [[person:hugh-latimer]], once bishop of Worcester, were burned together at [[place:oxford]] [[cite:foxe-bishops]]. Foxe reports that when the fire was brought Latimer told Ridley to "play the man" and spoke of lighting "such a candle, by God's grace, in England, as I trust shall never be put out"; Latimer died quickly, and Ridley, whose fire was badly laid, slowly [[cite:foxe-oxford]]. [[person:thomas-cranmer]], the former archbishop of Canterbury, signed a recantation in prison that acknowledged the pope as head of the church [[cite:foxe-recantation]]. On 21 March 1556, when he was to repeat it in public before his execution, he renounced what he had signed, and by Foxe's account he held his right hand in the flame so that it burned first [[cite:foxe-cranmer]] [[cite:machyn-cranmer]].

The last to suffer, according to Foxe, were three men and two women burned at [[place:canterbury]] about six days before the queen died [[cite:foxe-last]]. Mary died on 17 November 1558, and Pole, who had been consecrated archbishop of Canterbury the day after Cranmer's death, within days of her [[cite:machyn-cranmer]] [[cite:machyn-deaths]].

The narratives of these deaths cited here come from [[source:foxe-acts-and-monuments]], and it is the work of a partisan. Foxe calls the dead martyrs and their judges papists moved by "implacable despite" [[cite:foxe-last]]; Mary's Parliament had called what they held "errors and heresies" [[cite:revival]]. The first Parliament of [[person:elizabeth-i]] repealed the heresy statutes in 1559 [[cite:supremacy]].
