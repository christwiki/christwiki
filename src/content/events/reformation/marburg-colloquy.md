Landgrave [[person:philip-of-hesse]] called the reformers of Wittenberg and those of the Swiss and south German cities to his court at [[place:marburg]] [[cite:osiander]]. His chancellor opened the public session by urging them to seek God's glory and brotherly unity [[cite:osiander]].

[[person:martin-luther]] and his party arrived on Thursday 30 September 1529 [[cite:osiander]]. The landgrave first had the leaders talk in private pairs, Luther with Johannes Oecolampadius and [[person:philip-melanchthon]] with [[person:huldrych-zwingli]]; four sessions followed before the landgrave and no more than twenty-four hearers [[cite:zwingli]].

The question was how to understand Christ's words "this is my body" [[bible:Matt 26:26]]. Andreas Osiander, who sat with Luther's party, reports that Luther wrote the words on the table in chalk. Zwingli argued from the saying "The flesh profits nothing" [[bible:John 6:63]] that a bodily eating would be useless, and from texts on Christ's likeness to us that his body, like ours, can be in only one place. Luther answered that John 6 does not speak of the Supper and that God can keep a body in one place, in many or in none. Asked for his proof from Scripture, he lifted the cloth and pointed to the chalked words [[cite:osiander]].

No agreement was reached on this point. Osiander says that his side would have accepted the others as brothers if they had confessed that Christ's body is present in the Supper, in whatever manner, and that they would not; they in turn asked to be received as brothers and to share communion, and this was refused. It was then agreed that Luther should set down the chief articles of faith and that all should sign [[cite:osiander]].

Ten theologians signed fifteen articles. Fourteen record agreement, from the Trinity and justification by faith to infant baptism and civil authority [[cite:articles]]. The fifteenth says that both sides hold the sacrament of the altar to be a sacrament of the true body and blood of Christ, and the spiritual partaking of that body and blood to be necessary for every Christian. It then states that they have not agreed at this time whether the true body and blood of Christ are bodily present in the bread and wine, and that each side should show the other Christian love, as far as conscience allows, and pray God for a right understanding [[cite:art15]].

The two sides reported the outcome differently. Luther wrote to his wife that "although we object to be brethren, we wish to live at peace" [[cite:wife]]. Zwingli wrote that Luther had been beaten in the eyes of fair judges, and that the papists could no longer hope to win Luther over now that the two parties agreed on the other doctrines [[cite:zwingli]].

The confession presented at [[place:augsburg]] the next year teaches that the body and blood of Christ are truly present and distributed in the Supper, and rejects those who teach otherwise [[cite:ac10]]; see [[event:augsburg-confession]].
