  The years 1622, 1626 and 1632 are those of the Jesuit writers: Tellez puts the emperor's public profession in 1622 [[cite:tellez-1622]], the oath of obedience on 11 February 1626 [[cite:tellez-1626]], the battle on 27 June 1632 [[cite:tellez-battle]] and the emperor's death in September 1632 [[cite:tellez-death]].

  The Ethiopian record used here is the abridged chronicle of the kings that René Basset translated in 1881, not the longer Chronicle of Susenyos, of which no freely readable copy was found. Basset held that it was compiled probably under Iyasu II (1729–1753) and that from Susenyos onward it copies the annals written at court in each reign [[cite:basset-intro]]. It counts by the years of the reign and by Ethiopian months. It puts the battle on Monday the third of the month Sane in the twenty-fifth year, the proclamation on the fifth of Sane, adding that others say the twenty-first, and the king's death on the tenth of Maskaram in the twenty-seventh year [[cite:chron-proclamation]]. The two reckonings are given here side by side and are not converted.

The Jesuit [[person:pedro-paez]] reached Fremona in Ethiopia, where Portuguese Catholics lived, in May 1603 [[cite:paez-arrival]]. The abridged Ethiopian chronicle says that [[person:susenyos]], who reigned as Seltan Sagad, set his residence at [[place:gorgora]] in the sixth year of his reign, and that in the seventh the disputes about the two natures began [[cite:chron-natures]]. By the account of the Jesuit historian Balthazar Tellez, it was proclaimed that no one should maintain "but one Nature in Christ" and that all should confess two, and the abuna, Simon, posted an excommunication against all who embraced the faith of the Portuguese [[cite:tellez-natures]]. The chronicle records under the eleventh year that Yolyos and the abuna Sem'on died on the same day, says of a later rebel that, like Yolyos, he had risen because of the Franks, and puts the king's residence at Danqaz from the twelfth year [[cite:chron-yolyos]]. Tellez says that Yolyos, the emperor's son-in-law, took arms against those who confessed two natures, that the abuna encouraged his soldiers, and that both were killed [[cite:tellez-yolyos]].

In 1622, Tellez says, Susenyos publicly professed "the Latin Faith", and Páez, who had received him into the church, died soon afterwards at Gorgora [[cite:tellez-1622]]. The chronicle says under the seventeenth year that people perished in Wag because of the Franks, and under the nineteenth it notes the arrival of "Afonsu the patriarch", [[person:afonso-mendes]], and the death, for the same cause, of a rebel who ruled in Shewa [[cite:chron-afonsu]]. On 11 February 1626, by Tellez's account, the emperor knelt before the patriarch with the New Testament in his hands and swore obedience to the pope, and the nobles and clergy after him. It was then proclaimed that no priest or monk should say mass until he had been to the patriarch, because their ordinations were held doubtful, and that all should embrace the Roman faith on pain of death [[cite:tellez-1626]].

The Jesuit Jerónimo Lobo, who served in the mission, writes that when he published the emperor's edict some sixty monks threw themselves from a precipice rather than obey [[cite:lobo-edict]]. In the twenty-fifth year, the chronicle says, before the king left Danqaz for the war, his son [[person:fasilides|Fasil]] told him that everything was desolate and troubled by this belief of the Franks, which was not in the books of their fathers, and asked him to pray as the faith of Alexandria taught [[cite:chron-fasil]].

The chronicle goes on to tell that the king destroyed a rebel army at Wayna Dega and then returned to the faith of Alexandria by a proclamation: that faith was to be restored and his son Fasil was to reign, for he himself was weak, had done wrong and was ill [[cite:chron-proclamation]]. Tellez counts about eight thousand dead in the battle [[cite:tellez-battle]]. He gives the proclamation in these words: "we first gave you this Faith, believing it was good, but innumerable People have been kill'd about it", and so "we restore you the Faith of your Fore-Fathers" [[cite:tellez-proclamation]]. Tellez, who calls Dancaz the emperor's usual residence [[cite:tellez-battle]], says that he died there in September 1632, declaring that he died in the Roman faith [[cite:tellez-death]].

Under Fasilides, the chronicle says, a new patriarch arrived and the Frank Afonsu returned to his country [[cite:chron-fasilides]]. Tellez tells it as an expulsion: the patriarch and the Jesuits were sent to Fremona, which they reached in April 1633 [[cite:tellez-fremona]], and in May 1634 they were put into the hands of the Turks on the coast [[cite:tellez-coast]]. Lobo sums up the mission: Susenyos "called us into his dominions in the year 1625, from whence we were expelled in 1634" [[cite:lobo-summary]].
