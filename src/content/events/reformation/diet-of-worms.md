In 1520 [[person:martin-luther]] published three books that set out his case. *To the Christian Nobility* asked the emperor and the lay rulers to reform the church and denied that only the pope may interpret Scripture or call a council [[cite:nobility]]. *The Babylonian Captivity of the Church* denied that there are seven sacraments [[cite:captivity]]. *The Freedom of a Christian* taught that faith alone justifies [[cite:freedom]].

The bull *Exsurge Domine* of Pope [[person:leo-x]] was not an answer to these books: B. J. Kidd's collection dates it 15 June 1520 [[cite:exsurge-date]], eight days before Luther signed the dedication of the first of them [[cite:nobility-date]]. It condemned forty-one propositions taken from Luther's writings, among them that the treasure from which the pope grants indulgences is not the merits of Christ and the saints, that the Roman pontiff is not by Christ's institution his vicar over all churches, and that purgatory cannot be proved from canonical Scripture [[cite:exsurge-props]]. From such errors, the bull says, it would follow that the church, which the Holy Spirit guides, has always erred, against Christ's promise to be with his disciples to the end of the world. It ordered Luther's books burned, said that he had refused a summons and a safe-conduct, and gave him sixty days to recant [[cite:exsurge-terms]].

On 10 December 1520 Luther burned the bull at [[place:wittenberg]] with the books of canon law [[cite:burning]]. On 3 January 1521 a second bull, dated at St Peter's in [[place:rome]], declared him a heretic and excommunicated him and all who followed or protected him [[cite:decet]].

The emperor [[person:charles-v]] summoned Luther under safe-conduct to the diet at [[place:worms]] [[cite:tocharles]]. On 18 April 1521, asked again whether he would retract his books, Luther answered that unless he was convinced by the testimony of Scripture or by evident reason, since he did not trust popes or councils alone, his conscience was captive to the word of God, and he could not and would not retract anything [[cite:speech]]. In the text edited from the manuscripts and first prints the speech ends *Gott helf mir. Amen*, "God help me. Amen." The words *Ich kan nicht anderst, hie stehe ich*, "I cannot do otherwise, here I stand", stand in only two prints, both from one Wittenberg press, and the editor judged them an addition [[cite:close]].

The next day Charles told the estates that a single monk, "led astray by private judgement", had set himself against the faith held by all Christians for a thousand years, and that he would hear him no more [[cite:charles]]. The Edict of Worms declared Luther cut off from the church and a manifest heretic, forbade anyone to house, feed or help him, and ordered his arrest and the burning of his books [[cite:edict]].

On 12 May Luther described how he had been "snatched" from his companions and brought by night to a castle [[cite:amsdorf]]; the editors of his letters identify it as the [[place:wartburg]]. He later told the elector [[person:frederick-the-wise]] of Saxony that he had hidden for a year in obedience to him [[cite:frederick]].
