  The confession's own text gives only the place and the year. The day comes from letters written at the time. On 27 June 1530, a Monday, Melanchthon wrote to Luther that the confession had been presented "last Saturday" (*proximo Sabbato*) [[cite:saturday]], which was 25 June.

  The "Apology" that Luther read in May is the draft of the confession under its earlier name. B. J. Kidd, *Documents Illustrative of the Continental Reformation* (1911), pp. 255–256, sets out the sequence: Melanchthon sent the draft to Luther on 11 May and Luther answered the elector on 15 May.

The emperor [[person:charles-v]] summoned the estates of the empire to [[place:augsburg]] to deliberate on defence against the Turks and on the division in religion, and asked them to submit their opinions on the second matter in German and Latin. The Lutheran princes answered with a statement of what was taught in their lands [[cite:preface]]. [[person:martin-luther]] was not at the diet [[cite:luther-jonas]]. The text was drawn up by [[person:philip-melanchthon]] [[cite:luther-july]]. In May Luther had read what he calls "Philip's Apology" and told the elector [[person:john-of-saxony|John of Saxony]] that he was delighted with it and could not himself have worded it "so softly and sweetly" [[cite:luther-may]].

On Saturday 25 June 1530 the confession was presented to the emperor [[cite:saturday]]. Luther, passing on what Justus Jonas had written to him, says that it was read by Dr Christian Beyer before the emperor and the princes and bishops of the empire [[cite:luther-july]]. It carries the names of the elector John of Saxony, six other princes and the cities of Nuremberg and Reutlingen [[cite:signers]].

The first twenty-one articles set out teaching. Article 4 says that people "cannot be justified before God by their own strength, merits, or works, but are freely justified for Christ's sake, through faith", when they believe that they are received into favour and that their sins are forgiven for Christ's sake; "this faith God imputes for righteousness in His sight" [[cite:art4]]. Article 7 calls the church the congregation of saints in which the Gospel is rightly taught and the sacraments are rightly administered [[cite:art7]]. Article 10 teaches that the body and blood of Christ are truly present in the Lord's Supper and are distributed to those who eat it [[cite:art10]]. The signers add that this teaching varies in nothing from Scripture, from the catholic church or from the church of Rome as known from its writers, and that the disagreement is about certain abuses [[cite:sum]]. Seven further articles defend changes made in their churches, among them communion in both kinds and the marriage of priests [[cite:abuses]].

The emperor referred the confession to learned men of several nations, and their reply, the Confutation, is dated 3 August 1530 in the edition cited [[cite:confutation-preamble]]. On article 4 it agrees that no one can merit eternal life by his own powers without grace, and it holds it contrary to Scripture to deny that works done with the help of grace are meritorious, though of themselves, it says, our works have no merit and it is God's grace that makes them worthy of eternal life [[cite:confutation-4]]. It rejects the ascription of justification to faith alone [[cite:confutation-6]]. Melanchthon relates that the emperor demanded assent to the Confutation and did not receive the defence, the Apology, which the princes offered in return [[cite:apology]].

The Lutheran princes and cities who published the Book of Concord in 1580 declared that they held to the confession presented in 1530 and to no other [[cite:concord]]. The [[event:peace-of-augsburg]] forbade the emperor and the estates to make war on any estate of the empire on account of the Augsburg Confession [[cite:peace]]. The Joint Declaration confirmed in 1999 recalls that doctrinal condemnations on justification were put forward both in the Lutheran confessions and by the [[event:council-of-trent]], and states that they do not apply to the teaching of either church as presented in it [[cite:jddj]]; see [[event:joint-declaration-on-justification]].
