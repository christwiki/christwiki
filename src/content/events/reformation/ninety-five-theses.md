  Luther's letter to Archbishop Albert is dated from Wittenberg on the vigil of All Saints 1517, that is 31 October, and says that the theses are enclosed [[cite:albert]]. This fixes the day on which he sent them.

Erwin Iserloh, *The Theses Were Not Posted* (1968), argued that no posting took place. Volker Leppin and Timothy J. In 1517 an indulgence granted by Pope [[person:leo-x]] for the building of St Peter's in [[place:rome]] was being preached in the provinces of [[person:albert-of-brandenburg]], archbishop of [[place:mainz]] and Magdeburg [[cite:instructio]] [[cite:albert]]. Albert's instruction to the preachers offered full remission of sins and of the pains of purgatory to those who were contrite, had confessed, prayed in seven churches and gave according to their rank. For souls already in purgatory it offered full remission by way of intercession, and for this the giver need not be contrite or have confessed [[cite:instructio]]. The teaching behind such grants had been stated by Pope Clement VI in 1343: the merits of Christ, with those of Mary and the elect, form a treasure that the popes dispense to the penitent for the remission of temporal punishment [[cite:unigenitus]].

Among the preachers was the Dominican [[person:johann-tetzel]], whom Luther later called the originator of the affair [[cite:preface]]. One of Tetzel's sample sermons has dead parents cry out to their children that a small alms would free them from their torments [[cite:tetzel]].

On 31 October 1517 [[person:martin-luther]], who lectured on theology at [[place:wittenberg]] [[cite:heading]], wrote to Albert [[cite:albert]]. He had not heard the preachers himself, he said, but grieved at what the people believed: that a letter of indulgence made them sure of salvation, and that souls flew out of purgatory as soon as the money fell into the chest [[cite:albert]]. He asked the archbishop to withdraw the instruction, and he enclosed his own "Disputations" [[cite:albert]].

These were ninety-five theses in Latin, announced for debate at Wittenberg [[cite:heading]]. The first says that Christ, in calling for repentance, "willed that the whole life of believers should be repentance" [[cite:t1]]. The pope, Luther argues, can remit only penalties imposed by his own authority or by the canons [[cite:t5]]. Those who say that the soul flies out when the penny rings in the box "preach man" [[cite:t27]]. Every truly repentant Christian has a right to full remission of penalty and guilt, even without letters of pardon [[cite:t36]], and the true treasure of the church is the gospel [[cite:t62]]. The theses do not reject indulgences or the pope: they curse whoever speaks against the truth of apostolic pardons and bless whoever guards against the licence of the preachers [[cite:t71]].

Whether Luther posted the theses in public that day is not certain. His letter to Albert does not speak of it [[cite:albert]]. In 1545 he recalled that he wrote to the archbishop and to his own bishop and, when they ignored him, published the theses [[cite:preface]]. [[person:philip-melanchthon]] wrote in 1546 that Luther affixed them to the church next to the castle at Wittenberg on the day before All Saints [[cite:melanchthon]].

Luther recalled that he was soon denounced to the pope and ordered to Rome [[cite:preface]]. What followed is told under [[event:diet-of-worms]].
