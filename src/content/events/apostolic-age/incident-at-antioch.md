dating: |
  Paul gives no date. He tells of the incident directly after the meeting in Jerusalem [[bible:Gal 2:1-10]] and joins the two only with the words "But when" [[bible:Gal 2:11]], so the order of events is inferred from the order of the telling.

  If the meeting of Galatians 2 is the one described in Acts 15, the incident belongs to the stay of Paul and Barnabas at Antioch that followed it [[bible:Acts 15:30-35]], about AD 49. J. B. Lightfoot, *Saint Paul's Epistle to the Galatians* (10th ed., 1890), in his note on 2:11, places it there. If the meeting of Galatians 2 is the earlier famine visit, the incident can be put before the meeting of Acts 15, with the people "from James" among the men who came down from Judea [[bible:Acts 15:1-2]]. William M. Ramsay, *St. Paul the Traveller and the Roman Citizen* (1895), chapter 7, argues for that order. The dating note on [[event:council-of-jerusalem|the council of Jerusalem]] sets out the wider question. The timeline places the incident after the council and marks the year as approximate.

The account is [[person:paul]]'s own, written to the churches of Galatia [[bible:Gal 1:1-2]]. In the text that the New Revised Standard Version translates he calls the other apostle Cephas, the name that John's Gospel explains as [[person:peter]] [[bible:John 1:42]]; the World English Bible, which is quoted here, prints "Peter" in verses 11 and 14. When Cephas came to [[place:antioch]], Paul says, he ate with the Gentile believers. Then "some people came from James", and he drew back and kept apart, "fearing those who were of the circumcision". The other Jewish believers joined him in what Paul calls hypocrisy, and even [[person:barnabas]] was carried along [[bible:Gal 2:11-13]]. James is [[person:james-the-just|the Lord's brother]], whom Paul has just named among the leaders in Jerusalem [[bible:Gal 1:19]] [[bible:Gal 2:9]].

Paul says that he opposed Cephas "to his face" and spoke to him in front of them all: "If you, being a Jew, live as the Gentiles do, and not as the Jews do, why do you compel the Gentiles to live as the Jews do?" [[bible:Gal 2:11, 14]]. His words pass, with no marked break, into the argument of the whole letter: no one is justified by works of the law, and "if righteousness is through the law, then Christ died for nothing" [[bible:Gal 2:15-21]]. He does not say how Cephas answered or how the matter ended.

Acts, in its account of these years at Antioch, mentions no visit of Peter [[bible:Acts 14:26-15:2]] [[bible:Acts 15:30-40]]. It says that Peter had earlier been criticised in Jerusalem for eating with uncircumcised men [[bible:Acts 11:2-3]]. Of Paul and Barnabas at Antioch it reports a sharp quarrel over whether to take [[person:john-mark]] on their next journey, after which Barnabas sailed for Cyprus and Paul set out with [[person:silas]] [[bible:Acts 15:36-40]].

Later readers explained the passage in different ways. [[person:clement-of-alexandria]], as [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]] reports him, held that this Cephas was not the apostle but one of the seventy disciples who bore the same name [[cite:clement]]. [[person:jerome]] wrote in his commentary on Galatians, as he quotes it in his later reply to Augustine, that Peter did not sin and that Paul did not presume to rebuke one greater than himself: the rebuke was a matter of policy, not a real disagreement. He said that he was following [[person:origen]] and other Greek commentators, who had used this reading to answer Porphyry, a critic who turned the passage against the apostles [[cite:jerome-reply]].

[[person:augustine]] objected in two letters to Jerome, which the translation used here dates to 394 or 395 and to 397. If Paul wrote that Peter was at fault when he was not, he argued, a falsehood stands in Scripture and its authority is shaken throughout; Peter "was truly corrected" and Paul told the truth [[cite:augustine-first]] [[cite:augustine-second]]. Jerome defended himself in 404, in the letter numbered 112 among his own and 75 among Augustine's [[cite:jerome-reply]]. Augustine replied the next year, held to his reading, and named [[person:cyprian]] and [[person:ambrose]] as authorities on his side [[cite:augustine-answer]].
