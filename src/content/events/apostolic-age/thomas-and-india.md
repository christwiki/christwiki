The year shown, AD 52, is the one that the Syro-Malabar Church, one of the churches of the Thomas Christians of Kerala, gives for his landing at Kodungallur, with AD 72 for his death at Mylapore, in a chronology published by its eparchy of Melbourne [[cite:tradition]]; the page states the dates without naming a source for them.

  The oldest account cited here, the *Acts of Thomas*, is anonymous and carries no date. M. R. James, *The Apocryphal New Testament* (1924), assigns it to the third century. He reports that by the weight of authority it was composed in Syriac and that the surviving Greek text is a translation, though he thought it still arguable that the work was first written in Greek. Percy Gardner, *The Coins of the Greek and Scythic Kings of Bactria and India in the British Museum* (1886), likewise speaks of legends of Thomas "dating from the third century". The King Gundaphoros of the Acts bears the name of a king whose coins Gardner dates not later than the middle of the first century AD [[cite:coins]]. On that dating a king of the name reigned in or before the apostle's lifetime. The coins do not mention Thomas.

What is told of the apostle [[person:thomas]] after the New Testament comes from later writings, and they do not agree on where he went. [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]], early in the fourth century, cites [[person:origen]] for a tradition that Parthia was allotted to Thomas [[cite:origen]].

The *Acts of Thomas* send him to India. The apostles divide the world by lot, and India falls to Judas Thomas. He refuses to go, and Jesus sells him as a carpenter to Abbanes, a merchant of "Gundaphoros, the king of the Indians" [[cite:lots]]. Given money to build the king a palace, Thomas spends it on the poor. The king's brother dies, is shown the palace in heaven and returns to life, and both brothers receive the seal from the apostle [[cite:palace]]. In the kingdom of another ruler, Misdeus, four soldiers spear Thomas on a mountain. He is laid in a royal tomb, and one of the brethren later carries his bones away to the West [[cite:death]].

A king Gondophares is known from coins. The British Museum's catalogue of 1886 reports that they were found at Begram and in the Punjab, in the north-west of the subcontinent [[cite:coins]]. The king of the Acts thus bears the name of a ruler of that region, far from Kerala and Mylapore.

In the fourth century Ephrem the Syrian, who spent his last years at [[place:edessa]], has the devil lament: "The Apostle whom I slew in India is before me in Edessa." He speaks of a merchant who brought the bones there [[cite:ephrem]]. Gregory of Nazianzus pairs Thomas with India as he pairs Peter with Judaea [[cite:gregory]].

The evidence for Christians in India is separate from the story of Thomas. Eusebius passes on a report that Pantaenus of Alexandria went to India and found that Bartholomew had preached there before him [[cite:pantaenus]]. Cosmas Indicopleustes, about AD 547, knew of a church in Male, "where the pepper grows", and a bishop at Calliana appointed from Persia [[cite:cosmas-churches]]. He says that the church on Taprobane belonged to Persian Christians settled there [[cite:cosmas-taprobane]]. Copper plates that their editor assigns, from the form of the letters, to the ninth century or the early tenth record a ruler's grant to a church built at Kollam by a man the text calls Maruvan Sapir Iso [[cite:plates]]. None of these passages names Thomas.

The southern tradition appears later among the sources cited here. Marco Polo's book of 1298 says that the body of St Thomas lies in a small town in the province of Maabar, visited by Christian and Muslim pilgrims, and that the Christians who kept the church told how the saint was killed by an arrow shot at a peacock [[cite:polo]]. A chronology published by an eparchy of the Syro-Malabar Church dates his landing at [[place:kodungallur]] to AD 52 and his martyrdom at [[place:mylapore]] to AD 72 [[cite:tradition]].
