  Constantine's summons told the bishops to be at Arles by 1 August and gives no year [[cite:eus-arles]]. The year follows from the order of events. The proconsul's report that opened the case is dated 15 April in Constantine's third consulship, 313 [[cite:aug-anulinus]]. The hearing at Rome under Miltiades met on 2 October [[cite:opt-rome]]; Miltiades died in January 314 and Silvester succeeded him at the end of that month, by the Roman list of bishops drawn up in 354 [[cite:bishops-of-rome]]. The council of Arles wrote to Silvester [[cite:arles-letter]], so its 1 August is that of 314. The manuscripts of Optatus put the Roman hearing in Constantine's fourth consulship, 315, which cannot be right if Miltiades presided. Optatus also calls the day a Friday [[cite:opt-rome]], and 2 October was a Friday in 313, not in 315.

The account of how the division began comes from Optatus, a bishop of [[person:caecilian]]'s communion who wrote some sixty years later against the other side. During the [[event:great-persecution]], he says, some bishops in Africa gave up the Scriptures to the authorities; the Latin word for such men is *traditores*, "handers-over" [[cite:opt-traditores]]. When the persecution ended, Caecilian was elected bishop of [[place:carthage]] and consecrated by Felix of Aptunga. Optatus relates that a party in the city, supported by bishops from Numidia, alleged that Felix had been a *traditor*, declared the consecration void and set up a rival bishop, Majorinus [[cite:opt-caecilian]].

[[person:constantine]], now ruling Africa, sent money to Caecilian's clergy and exempted them from public duties [[cite:eus-money]]. The opponents handed the proconsul a dossier of charges for the emperor [[cite:aug-anulinus]] and asked him for judges from Gaul [[cite:opt-petition]]. Constantine referred the case to Miltiades, bishop of [[place:rome]], with three Gallic bishops [[cite:eus-miltiades]]. By Optatus's account nineteen bishops met at the Lateran on 2 October 313, cleared Caecilian and condemned Donatus, who led the accusers [[cite:opt-rome]].

The losing party complained that the judges had been few and hasty. Constantine then ordered bishops from many provinces to assemble at [[place:arles]] by 1 August [[cite:eus-arles]]. The council reported to Silvester of Rome that the accusers had been "condemned or rejected" [[cite:arles-letter]]. Its canons ruled that clergy proved from public records to have handed over the Scriptures were to be removed, but that ordinations they had performed stood [[cite:arles-traditores]]; that converts from heresy who had been baptised in the name of the Trinity were not to be baptised again, against the custom of the Africans [[cite:arles-rebaptism]]; and that Easter should be kept on one day everywhere and no bishop should consecrate another by himself [[cite:arles-other]].

The accusers appealed from the bishops to the emperor himself. Constantine wrote that they were asking judgement of a man who himself awaited the judgement of Christ [[cite:opt-constantine]]. He heard the case all the same, and Augustine, also an opponent, writing from the records eighty to ninety years later, says that he pronounced Caecilian innocent [[cite:aug-verdict]] and ordered the property of the dissidents' congregations confiscated [[cite:aug-confiscation]]. A proconsular inquiry, Optatus adds, cleared Felix [[cite:opt-felix]].

The division did not end. The party took its name from [[person:donatus-magnus]]; Jerome, no friend of his, writes that he "deceived almost all Africa, and especially Numidia" [[cite:jerome]]. In 406 the Catholic clergy of Hippo were still writing to a Donatist bishop, complaining of attacks by his followers and defending the imperial laws against his party [[cite:aug-later]]. In this case a Christian emperor was asked to settle a dispute between bishops, and did so by convening councils. And a council ruled that an ordination is not undone by the fault of the bishop who performed it, and that baptism in the name of the Trinity is not repeated.
