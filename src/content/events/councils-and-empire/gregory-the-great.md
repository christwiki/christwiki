In 590 a plague killed the bishop of [[place:rome]], and the people chose the deacon Gregory to succeed him. [[person:gregory-i]] came from a senatorial family, had used his wealth to found six monasteries in Sicily and a seventh in Rome, and had served as the pope's representative at Constantinople. He wrote to the emperor asking not to be confirmed, and was seized and consecrated when he tried to hide [[cite:election]] [[cite:bede-life]]. In his letters he mourns the quiet he has lost [[cite:letter-theoctista]].

At the start of his episcopate he wrote the *Pastoral Rule* [[cite:letter-leander]], on who should take up the care of souls, how a pastor should live and how he should teach. It calls the government of souls "the art of arts" [[cite:pastoral]]. When the bishop of Constantinople used the title "universal", Gregory protested that no bishop should claim it [[cite:letter-john]], and he refused it for himself when the bishop of Alexandria addressed him as "Universal Pope": "my honour is the honour of the universal Church" [[cite:letter-eulogius]]. He styled himself instead "servant of the servants of God", as at the head of his letter to the missionaries [[cite:bede-sending]].

Bede relates, as a tradition, that Gregory once saw English boys for sale in Rome and wished to go to their people himself [[cite:bede-life]]. What his own letters show is an order, sent about 595 to his agent in Gaul, to buy English boys to be raised in monasteries [[cite:letter-candidus]]. In 596 he sent [[person:augustine-of-canterbury]], a monk of his own monastery, with a party of monks. They lost heart on the way, and Gregory wrote urging them on [[cite:bede-sending]] [[cite:letter-eulogius]]. They landed on the Isle of Thanet. [[person:aethelberht-of-kent]], king of Kent, whose Frankish wife Bertha was a Christian, heard them in the open air, allowed them to preach and gave them a house at [[place:canterbury]] [[cite:bede-thanet]]. The king was later baptised [[cite:bede-canterbury]]. Gregory told the bishop of Alexandria that more than ten thousand English had been baptised at one Christmas [[cite:letter-eulogius]].

In 601 Gregory sent more helpers, the pallium for Augustine and a plan for two provinces of twelve bishops each [[cite:bede-pallium]]. He urged the king to suppress idols and overthrow temples [[cite:bede-king]]. After the party had left he sent different advice: the temples should be kept and turned into churches, and the old feasts replaced with Christian ones, since whoever climbs to the highest place "rises by degrees or steps, and not by leaps" [[cite:bede-mellitus]]. Augustine set his see in an old Roman church at Canterbury [[cite:bede-see]].

Gregory died in March 604. Bede, whose text gives the year as 605, calls him the apostle of the English [[cite:bede-life]]. The later history of the church he founded is taken up under [[event:synod-of-whitby]].
