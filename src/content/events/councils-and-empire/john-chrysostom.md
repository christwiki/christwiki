  Socrates, who lived in Constantinople through these years, dates the main events by consuls. John was ordained bishop on 26 February in the consulship of Honorius and Eutychian, AD 398 [[cite:ordination]]. He left the city for his final exile on 20 June in the sixth consulship of Honorius, 404 [[cite:statue]], and died at Comana on 14 September in the seventh consulship of Honorius and the second of Theodosius, 407 [[cite:death]]. Socrates gives no date for the synod of the Oak. He places John's second condemnation between Christmas and the Easter before the exile of June 404 [[cite:statue]], so the synod and the first banishment belong to 403 at the latest.

[[person:john-chrysostom]] was born at [[place:antioch]], studied under the rhetorician Libanius, lived for some years as a monk, and was ordained there [[cite:character]] [[cite:reforms]]. In 387 a crowd protesting a new tax pulled down the imperial statues, and the city waited for the punishment of [[person:theodosius-i]] [[cite:riot]]. While the bishop went to plead with the emperor, John preached through Lent to a frightened congregation, until the bishop returned before Easter with a pardon [[cite:statues]] [[cite:flavian]].

When the bishop of [[place:constantinople]] died in 397, the court sent for John, and he was ordained on 26 February 398 [[cite:ordination]]. His supporter Palladius describes what he did with the office: he disciplined clergy who kept women in their houses, cut the bishop's household expenses and gave the money to hospitals, reformed the order of widows, held night services for working people, and preached against the greed of the rich [[cite:reforms]]. Socrates says that his severity and freedom of speech offended many, and that people who did not know him found him haughty [[cite:character]].

Theophilus, bishop of [[place:alexandria]], then came to the capital with a party of bishops. The empress [[person:eudoxia]], angered by a sermon against women that was taken as aimed at her, backed him [[cite:oak]]. A synod of thirty-six bishops met at a place called the Oak, outside [[place:chalcedon]], and summoned John to answer a list of charges. He refused to be judged by his declared enemies and asked for a general council; the synod deposed him for not appearing and sent the emperor a charge of treason, that he had called the empress Jezebel [[cite:oak-palladius]] [[cite:oak]]. He was sent away, and the anger of the people soon forced the court to bring him back [[cite:recall]].

When games were held around a silver statue of Eudoxia set up near his church, John denounced them; Socrates reports that he preached a sermon beginning "Again Herodias raves". A second gathering of bishops ruled that he had resumed his see unlawfully [[cite:statue]]. John's own letter to Innocent, bishop of Rome, tells how soldiers broke into the churches on Easter eve and drove out those about to be baptised, and asks the western bishops to declare the proceedings void [[cite:innocent]]. On 20 June 404 he was taken into exile [[cite:statue]]. Eudoxia died that October [[cite:eudoxia-death]].

He was held first at Cucusus in Armenia, then moved; ordered on to Pityus on the Black Sea, he died on the road at Comana on 14 September 407. Palladius gives his last words as "Glory to God for all things" [[cite:exile]] [[cite:death]]. In 438 his body was brought back to Constantinople and those who had left the church on his account returned to it [[cite:relics]].
