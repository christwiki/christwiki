dating: |
  The acts date the council to the eighth day before the Ides of May in the fourth year of Reccared and the year 627 of the Spanish era [[cite:acts-opening]]. The Spanish era runs 38 years ahead of the Christian era, so the day is 8 May 589. The acts and Isidore's history are paraphrased here from the Latin.

  What is debated is the wording of the creed, not the date. The king's profession and the third anathema teach that the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son [[cite:acts-tomus]] [[cite:acts-anathemas]]. In the creed of Constantinople as the council recited it, the copies of the acts divide. The copy read here, a ninth-century manuscript from Corbie that holds a Frankish revision of the Spanish collection of canons, transcribed for the Monumenta Germaniae Historica, has "from the Father" only, both times the creed is given [[cite:acts-creed]]. Alberto Ferreiro, ["Toletum III / Toledo III"](https://www.konziliengeschichte.org/site/de/publikationen/lexikon/database/4300.html), in the online *Lexikon der Konzilien* (konziliengeschichte.org, January 2025), reports that the edition of J. Vives prints "from the Father and the Son", that the critical edition of the *Hispana* treats those words as a later interpolation in the acts, and that some scholars place their first appearance in the creed at the Eighth Council of Toledo in 653. Ferreiro himself argues that the bishops of 589 had every reason to use them. The same entry notes that the complete acts carry the signature of Leander of Seville, fourth among the bishops, and a sermon he preached for the occasion. The manuscript read here names only the first bishop to sign, Masona of Mérida, and omits the sermon [[cite:acts-signatures]].

The Visigoths who ruled Spain had been Arian Christians since their conversion in the fourth century ([[event:ulfilas]]) [[cite:isidore-arian]], beside a Catholic church that held the faith of Nicaea [[cite:acts-tomus]]. Gregory the Great, who knew Bishop [[person:leander-of-seville]] personally, relates that Leander brought the king's son Hermenegild to the Catholic faith, that the Arian king Leovigild had Hermenegild put to death, and that on his deathbed Leovigild commended his other son, [[person:reccared-i]], to Leander's guidance [[cite:hermenegild]]. Reccared became king in 586 and adopted the Catholic faith at the very start of his reign [[cite:isidore]]. Gregory of Tours says that he first made the bishops of both sides debate before him [[cite:debate]].

On 8 May 589 the king met the bishops of Spain and Gaul at [[place:toledo]] [[cite:acts-opening]]. A written statement of his faith was read out. It confesses the Son as equal and coeternal with the Father and says that the Holy Spirit is to be confessed and preached as proceeding from the Father and the Son. The king presented the Goths, and the Sueves now subject to his kingdom, as an offering to God, accepted the four general councils, and set out the creeds of Nicaea and Constantinople and the definition of Chalcedon. He and Queen Baddo signed [[cite:acts-tomus]]. Gothic bishops, clergy and nobles then abjured Arianism in a series of anathemas, the third of which condemns anyone who does not believe that the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son [[cite:acts-anathemas]].

The second canon ordered that in every church of Spain, Gaul and Galicia the creed of Constantinople should be recited aloud at mass before the Lord's Prayer, after the custom of the Eastern churches [[cite:acts-canon-2]]. Another canon forbade Jews to have Christian wives or concubines, to buy Christian slaves, or to hold public offices that gave them power to punish Christians [[cite:acts-canon-14]]. Pope Gregory wrote to Leander of his joy at the king's conversion [[cite:gregory-leander]].

The creed of 381 says that the Spirit "proceedeth from the Father" [[cite:creed-381]]. The king's profession adds the Son, and a joint Orthodox and Catholic statement of 2003 calls it the earliest use of such language in a creed-like text; it adds that no clear record exists of how the word *Filioque* came to be inserted in the creed itself [[cite:agreed-2003]]. From Spain, the statement says, the creed with the addition spread through Gaul [[cite:agreed-2003]]. The same statement describes the clause as part of the ordinary teaching of the church for Catholics and most Protestants, and as an offence to Eastern Christians both for its theology and because it was added to the creed of an ecumenical council without ecumenical agreement [[cite:agreed-2003-readings]]. The later quarrel is told under [[event:photian-schism]] and [[event:east-west-schism]].
