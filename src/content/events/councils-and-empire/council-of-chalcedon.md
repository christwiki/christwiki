A synod at [[place:constantinople]] under its bishop Flavian had condemned the monk Eutyches, who confessed two natures in Christ before the union and only one after it [[cite:eutyches]]. [[person:leo-i]] of Rome supported Flavian in his Tome, which teaches that each nature keeps its own properties and that both meet in one person [[cite:tome]]. In 449 a council at Ephesus under [[person:dioscorus-of-alexandria]] restored Eutyches and deposed Flavian [[cite:ephesus-449]] [[cite:leo-ephesus]]. Leo called it a meeting "not of judges but of robbers" [[cite:robbers]]. The next emperor, [[person:marcian]], who owed his throne to the empress [[person:pulcheria]] and married her, summoned a new council [[cite:marcian]].

It met in October 451 in the church of the martyr Euphemia at [[place:chalcedon]] [[cite:euphemia]] [[cite:deposition]]. Leo's legates objected that Dioscorus had come to be judged and must not sit as a judge; he was seated in the middle, and the minutes of Ephesus were read; Dioscorus said that he accepted "of two" natures but not "two" [[cite:session-1]]. He did not answer three summonses and was deposed for disregarding the canons and disobeying the council [[cite:deposition]].

The bishops at first refused to write a new statement of faith [[cite:session-2]]. When the Tome was read they cried out that Peter had spoken through Leo and that Leo and Cyril taught the same [[cite:acclamation]]. A first draft did not satisfy the Roman legates, who threatened to leave; the emperor's officials asked whether the bishops followed Dioscorus or Leo, and a committee wrote a new text [[cite:session-5]]. This Definition confesses one and the same Christ, perfect in Godhead and in manhood, "in two natures, unconfusedly, immutably, indivisibly, inseparably", the property of each nature being preserved and united in one person and subsistence [[cite:definition]]. Marcian attended the session that followed [[cite:session-6]].

Canon 28 gave the see of Constantinople, as the imperial city, privileges equal to those of old Rome and the rank next after it [[cite:canon-28]]. The Roman legates, absent when it was voted, protested [[cite:session-16]]. Leo welcomed the council's judgment on the faith but annulled this canon as contrary to those of Nicaea: a city could be royal, he wrote, without being an apostolic see [[cite:leo-marcian]] [[cite:leo-pulcheria]]. The Eastern churches kept the canon and renewed it in 692 [[cite:trullo-36]].

The Greek and Latin churches received the Definition; Gregory the Great later wrote that he revered the four councils like the four Gospels [[cite:four-councils]]. Many in Egypt and Palestine refused it; Alexandria rose against the bishop appointed in place of Dioscorus, and he was later killed [[cite:alexandria]]. A historian of that side honours Dioscorus as a confessor and confesses Christ as one nature from two [[cite:zachariah]]; Cyril had written that the incarnate nature of the Word is, after the union, conceived of as one [[cite:cyril]]. The Coptic, Syriac, Armenian and Ethiopian churches, with others, form the Oriental Orthodox family [[cite:churches]]. They condemn the teaching of Eutyches, receive the first three councils and keep Cyril's wording [[cite:agreed-1990]]. Modern agreements are told under [[event:christological-declarations]].
