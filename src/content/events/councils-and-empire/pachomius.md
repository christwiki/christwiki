dating: |
  No source gives a year for the foundation. The Life says that Pachomius was twenty when he was conscripted, during Constantine's war against Maxentius [[cite:life-youth]], which ended at the [[event:battle-of-the-milvian-bridge|battle of the Milvian Bridge]] in October 312; that he was baptised on his release and then lived with the hermit Palamon [[cite:life-baptism]]; and that only afterwards did he settle at Tabennisi [[cite:life-call]]. The foundation therefore falls some years after 313. The date of about 320 used here is an estimate from that sequence.

  The Life dates his death to 14 Pachon, that is 9 May, soon after Easter, and names no year [[cite:life-death]]. Modern scholarship places it in 346: see Philip Rousseau, *Pachomius: The Making of a Community in Fourth-Century Egypt* (1985).

The ancient Life says that [[person:pachomius]] was born to pagan parents in the Thebaid, in Upper Egypt, and that at twenty he was conscripted and shipped away with other recruits. In a city where they were held under guard, local people brought them food. He was told that they were Christians, and he vowed to serve their God if he were set free [[cite:life-youth]]. On his discharge he went to the village of Chenoboskion, was baptised, and attached himself to an old hermit named Palamon [[cite:life-baptism]].

Some time later, praying at an abandoned village called [[place:tabennisi]], he heard a voice telling him to stay and build a monastery, because many would come to him. The Life adds that an angel gave him a tablet with a rule for those he would teach, and that he built a small cell on the spot [[cite:life-call]]. Palladius, writing about 420, also tells of an angel who gave him the rule on a tablet of brass [[cite:palladius-rule]]. His brother John joined him, and he began to enlarge the buildings for the numbers he expected [[cite:life-brother]]. Three men came first, then many more. He set the more able in charge of the others, and since he would not have any of the monks hold clerical office they called in priests from nearby villages for the Eucharist [[cite:life-disciples]].

The rule that Palladius reports has the monks living three to a cell inside one enclosure, eating together, wearing a common habit, working according to their strength and meeting for set prayers through the day and night [[cite:palladius-rule]]. [[person:jerome]], who translated the community's rules into Latin about 404, describes houses of about forty monks, each under a housemaster and each grouped by a trade; thirty or forty such houses made a monastery, and all the monasteries had one head and assembled twice a year [[cite:jerome-order]]. A monastery was built across the river for Pachomius's sister; other women joined her, and they kept the same rule [[cite:life-sister]] [[cite:palladius-women]].

The Life tells how [[person:athanasius]] came to the monasteries on a tour of the Thebaid, and how Pachomius hid among the monks to avoid being ordained [[cite:life-athanasius]]. It says that he died of an illness on 9 May, shortly after Easter [[cite:life-death]]. Palladius gives seven thousand monks living under the rule in his own day, thirteen hundred of them in the chief house [[cite:palladius-numbers]].

The Life itself begins from the hermits: Antony in the inner desert and the solitaries of Nitria [[cite:life-antony]]. See [[event:antony-in-the-desert]]. Pachomius, in Jerome's words, was among those who first laid the foundations of the *coenobia*, the monasteries of the common life [[cite:jerome-origin]].
