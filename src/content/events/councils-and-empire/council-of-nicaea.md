According to Socrates, [[person:arius]], a presbyter of [[place:alexandria]], objected to the teaching of his bishop, [[person:alexander-of-alexandria]]. If the Father begot the Son, he argued, the Son had a beginning, and there was a time when he was not [[cite:soc-arius]]. In his own words, "the Son has a beginning, but … God is without beginning" [[cite:arius-letter]]. Alexander excommunicated him, and other bishops took sides [[cite:soc-arius]]. [[person:constantine]] sent [[person:hosius-of-cordoba]] to Alexandria with a letter urging peace [[cite:soc-hosius]]. When that failed he summoned the bishops to [[place:nicaea]] [[cite:vc-summons]].

No minutes of the council survive; it is known from its own documents and from what participants wrote afterwards. [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea]] describes bishops from Mesopotamia to Spain; the bishop "of the imperial city", too old to travel, sent presbyters [[cite:vc-summons]], whom Socrates names as presbyters of Rome [[cite:soc-date]]. Counts of the bishops differ: Eusebius says more than 250 [[cite:vc-number]], Eustathius of Antioch about 270 [[cite:eustathius]], [[person:athanasius]] "three hundred more or less" and later 318 [[cite:ath-number]] [[cite:ath-318]]. By Eusebius's account Constantine opened the sessions in the palace and joined in the debate [[cite:vc-sessions]].

Eusebius told his own church that he had offered a creed and that the emperor urged its acceptance with one word added, *homoousios*, "of one substance" [[cite:soc-council]]. Athanasius, who attended as Alexander's deacon [[cite:soc-council]], says the bishops wanted to use only the words of Scripture, found that Arius's supporters could take each phrase in their own sense, and so wrote that the Son is "from the essence" of the Father and "one in essence" with him [[cite:ath-terms]]. The creed calls the Son "begotten, not made" and "of one substance" with the Father, and condemns those who say there was a time when he was not [[cite:creed]]. The council condemned Arius and two Libyan bishops who stood by him [[cite:letter]], and the emperor exiled Arius [[cite:soc-council]].

The bishops also agreed that all churches should keep Easter on the same day and no longer follow the Jewish reckoning [[cite:letter]] [[cite:vc-easter]]. Of their twenty canons, the fourth requires a bishop to be appointed by the bishops of his province, at least three being present and the rest consenting in writing, with confirmation left to the metropolitan [[cite:canon4]]. The sixth lets "the ancient customs" stand by which the bishop of Alexandria has authority over Egypt, Libya and Pentapolis, "since the like is customary for the Bishop of Rome also", and keeps the privileges of [[place:antioch]] and the other provinces [[cite:canon6]]. The seventh gives the bishop of [[place:jerusalem]] a place of honour by ancient custom, saving the rights of the metropolis [[cite:canon7]], and the twentieth has prayer said standing on Sundays and in the days of Pentecost [[cite:canon20]].

The creed did not end the dispute: Athanasius records a series of rival formulas issued by later councils [[cite:ath-creeds]]. The longer creed recited today is linked with the [[event:council-of-constantinople-i]] of 381 [[cite:creed-381]]. Chalcedon reaffirmed the creed of Nicaea [[cite:chalcedon]]. The main traditions appeal to Nicaea in their own documents: the Catholic Church [[cite:catechism]], the Eastern and Oriental Orthodox [[cite:orthodox]], the Church of the East [[cite:church-of-the-east]], and the churches of the Reformation in their confessions [[cite:augsburg]] [[cite:articles]].
