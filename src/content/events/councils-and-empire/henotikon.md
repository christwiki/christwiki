After [[event:council-of-chalcedon|Chalcedon]] the people of [[place:alexandria]] were divided between those who wanted Dioscorus back and those who upheld Proterius, the bishop appointed in his place [[cite:proterius]]. A generation later, when the see fell vacant, the emperor [[person:zeno]] expelled John, who had obtained it, and restored Peter, surnamed Mongus, on condition that he sign a statement which the emperor addressed to the Alexandrians and that he admit the party of Proterius to communion [[cite:alexandria]]. Evagrius and the Syriac chronicle of Zachariah both say that the statement was framed on the advice of [[person:acacius-of-constantinople]], bishop of [[place:constantinople]] [[cite:acacius]] [[cite:zachariah-henotikon]]. It is known as the Henotikon, the instrument of union.

The Henotikon declares that the only creed is that of the 318 fathers of Nicaea, confirmed by the 150 at Constantinople and followed at Ephesus. It anathematises Nestorius and Eutyches alike, receives the twelve chapters of Cyril of Alexandria, and confesses Christ as consubstantial with the Father in his Godhead and with us in his manhood, "one and not two", whose miracles and sufferings are those of a single person. It does not use the language of one nature or of two. Of Chalcedon it says only that anyone who has held another opinion, "whether at Chalcedon or in any synod whatever", is anathematised [[cite:henotikon]].

Peter signed [[cite:acacius]], and so did Peter the Fuller, restored to the see of Antioch [[cite:fuller]]. The chronicle of Zachariah, written from the side opposed to Chalcedon, reports that clergy and monks in Egypt broke with Peter because neither the Henotikon nor the bishops' letters contained a clear anathema of the council and of Leo's Tome [[cite:separatists]]. Under the next emperor, Anastasius, Evagrius says, Chalcedon was neither openly proclaimed nor rejected by all: some bishops held to every word of it, some anathematised it, others kept to the Henotikon, and the emperor removed those who disturbed the practice of their region in either direction [[cite:anastasius]].

[[place:rome]] refused the arrangement. Pope Simplicius protested in July 482 against giving Alexandria to Peter, whom he called a companion of heretics [[cite:simplicius]]. His successor Felix sent two bishops to Constantinople to demand that Chalcedon be upheld and Peter expelled; they held communion with Peter's party instead and were deposed on their return [[cite:legates]]. On 28 July 484 Felix declared Acacius removed from his office and from catholic communion, charging him among other things with supporting Peter, whom Acacius had himself once reported to Rome as condemned [[cite:sentence]] [[cite:sentence-date]]. Acacius, Evagrius reports from Zachariah, did not accept the sentence [[cite:legates]]. In 494 [[person:gelasius-i]] wrote to Anastasius that there are two powers by which this world is chiefly ruled, "the sacred authority of the priests and the royal power", and that in things divine the emperor bows his head before the leaders of the clergy [[cite:gelasius]].

Embassies from Pope [[person:hormisdas]] to Anastasius achieved nothing; the *Liber Pontificalis* has the emperor answer that he wished to give commands, not to receive them [[cite:lp-anastasius]]. Justin, who succeeded Anastasius [[cite:justin]], asked Rome for legates [[cite:lp-reunion]]. On 28 March 519 John, bishop of Constantinople, signed the profession they brought [[cite:formula-date]]. It accepts the four councils and the letters of Leo, anathematises Acacius by name along with Dioscorus, Timothy Aelurus and the two Peters, and promises that those separated from the communion of the apostolic see shall not be named at the liturgy [[cite:formula]]. The Roman account says that there was then harmony from East to West [[cite:lp-reunion]]. Evagrius says that in the first year of Justin, [[person:severus-of-antioch]], who anathematised Chalcedon daily, was ordered to be arrested and fled his see [[cite:severus]]; what followed is told under [[event:jacob-baradaeus]].
