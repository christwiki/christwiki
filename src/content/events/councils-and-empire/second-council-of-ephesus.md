A synod at Constantinople under its bishop [[person:flavian-of-constantinople]], acting on a charge brought by Eusebius of Dorylaeum, had deposed the archimandrite [[person:eutyches]], who confessed that Christ was from two natures before the union but allowed only one nature after it [[cite:eutyches]]. [[person:leo-i]] of Rome, in the letter to Flavian known as the Tome, called that statement absurd [[cite:tome]]. The emperor [[person:theodosius-ii]] summoned a council to meet at [[place:ephesus]] on 1 August 449 to end the controversy [[cite:summons]]. Evagrius, who accepted Chalcedon, says that [[person:dioscorus-of-alexandria]] was made president through Chrysaphius, a court official hostile to Flavian, and that those who had judged Eutyches were to attend without a vote [[cite:president]].

The first session is described by both sides. Flavian wrote to Leo that Dioscorus had the statement of Eutyches read, declared him orthodox and restored him, and then moved the condemnation of Flavian and of Eusebius; that Leo's letters were not read; and that when he appealed to the Roman see soldiers surrounded him [[cite:appeal]]. Leo learned from his deacon Hilarus, who had escaped, that his legates had protested in the council [[cite:leo-44]]. When the minutes were read at Chalcedon in 451 they showed that Leo's letter had not been read. Dioscorus said that he had twice proposed it, and bishops who had signed said that they had done so under compulsion, with soldiers around them [[cite:minutes]]. The Syriac chronicle that goes under the name of Zachariah, written by opponents of Chalcedon, says that the acts of the trial of Eutyches were examined, that Flavian and Eusebius were removed, and that Eutyches was received because he presented a recantation and confessed the true faith [[cite:zachariah]].

The minutes of a later session survive in Syriac. On 22 August, in the church called Mary, the council heard that the Roman envoys would not attend, since their commission covered only the case of Eutyches, and that Domnus of Antioch was ill [[cite:acts-date]] [[cite:acts-envoys]]. It deposed Ibas of Edessa [[cite:acts-ibas]]; Theodoret of Cyrrhus, whom Dioscorus charged with writing against Cyril of Alexandria [[cite:acts-theodoret]]; and Domnus himself, as opposed to Cyril and to the [[event:council-of-ephesus|council of 431]] and a follower of Nestorius [[cite:acts-domnus]].

Theodosius confirmed the council by an edict, which says that Flavian and Eusebius had revived the error of Nestorius and that they, Domnus and Theodoret had been deposed as unworthy of their office [[cite:edict]]. In the letters that follow it he speaks of two holy and ecumenical synods held by his will and orders all bishops to subscribe [[cite:edict-letters]]. Leo, with a synod at Rome, asked him to leave everything as it had been until a larger council could meet in Italy [[cite:leo-44]]. In July 451 Leo wrote to the empress Pulcheria of "that meeting, not of judges but of robbers, at Ephesus" [[cite:robbers]]; his Latin word is *latrocinium* [[cite:robbers-latin]]. The name "Robber Council", or Latrocinium, which the English editor of the acts of Chalcedon uses in his summaries [[cite:chalcedon]], is therefore Leo's; the council's own minutes call it holy and ecumenical [[cite:acts-envoys]]. At [[event:council-of-chalcedon|Chalcedon]] the presiding officials ruled that Flavian had been unjustly deposed, and the bishops deposed Dioscorus [[cite:minutes]].

Those who did not receive Chalcedon wrote of the council differently. The Syriac chronicle from their side counts it as the second synod of Ephesus [[cite:zachariah]], and the Syriac copy of its acts, made in 535, ends by naming "the holy and God-loving Dioscorus" [[cite:colophon]]. The Coptic Synaxarium, the book of commemorations read in the liturgy of the Coptic Orthodox Church, keeps his day on 7 Tout and calls him "the great champion of Orthodoxy" [[cite:synaxarium]]. The same tradition rejected the teaching ascribed to Eutyches: the chronicle says that he rejected the truth of the body taken from the Virgin [[cite:zachariah-eutyches]]. In 1990 a joint commission of the Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches condemned the Eutychian heresy together and recommended that each side lift its condemnations of the other's councils and fathers [[cite:agreed-1990]].
