Peace prayers had been held in the Nikolaikirche in [[place:leipzig]] every Monday since 20 September 1982, by the account of its pastor, [[person:christian-fuhrer]] [[cite:fuhrer-1]]. The theologian Richard Schröder, looking back in 2014, says that seven people came to the first, and that Führer and the superintendent never cancelled or moved one in eight years of pressure from the state [[cite:schroder-origins]]. He also says that the prayers were no idyll. The political groups that used them saw the church as a protected space and a stage, and in 1988 the superintendent shut those groups out of shaping the prayers [[cite:schroder-conflict]].

In 1989 the police blocked the approaches to the church, and on 7 October, Führer recalled, hundreds were arrested in front of it [[cite:fuhrer-1]]. He had asked the other city churches to hold prayers as well, so that as many people as possible would hear the message of nonviolence [[cite:fuhrer-2]].

For Monday 9 October there is the report that the Leipzig head of the state security service sent to Berlin. Prayers were held from 17:00 to 18:00 in four churches in the centre, the Nikolaikirche, the Thomaskirche, the Michaeliskirche and the Reformed church, with more than 5,000 people inside and thousands more outside [[cite:stasi-100]]. Führer later put the number in the churches at about 8,000 [[cite:fuhrer-2]]. The regional bishop, Johannes Hempel, had told the district authorities that he would come in person to call for calm and nonviolence and for dialogue between state and citizens, and he spoke in all four churches [[cite:stasi-hempel]]. Three appeals are annexed to the report. One, signed by three church-based groups, asked the demonstrators to refrain from all violence and the security forces not to answer peaceableness with violence [[cite:stasi-annex2]]. Another, in the name of members and supporters of the New Forum, called for dialogue in place of violence [[cite:stasi-annex3]]. The third came from six Leipzig citizens, the conductor Kurt Masur, a pastor, a cabaret artist and three secretaries of the ruling party's district leadership. They promised to work for dialogue and asked for calm, and their appeal was broadcast on the Leipzig radio and the city's loudspeakers [[cite:stasi-annex1]].

From 18:35 to 20:30, the report goes on, an unauthorised demonstration of 50,000 to 60,000 people moved from the forecourt of the Nikolaikirche round the city ring [[cite:stasi-100]]. Among the chants it lists are "We are the people" [[cite:stasi-100]] and "No violence" [[cite:stasi-101]]. There were no attacks on the security forces, and the measures prepared to prevent and break up a demonstration were not applied, because of the overall situation and by a central decision [[cite:stasi-101]].

A chronology of 1989 that the European Parliament published twenty years later records that Erich Honecker resigned on 18 October and that the Berlin Wall was breached on 9 November [[cite:ep-october]]. In Romania, according to the same chronology, protests broke out at Timișoara on 16 December in response to the government's attempt to evict the pastor [[person:laszlo-tokes]], and spread to Bucharest [[cite:ep-december]].
