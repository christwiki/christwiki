The Orthodox churches had been preparing a general council since a conference at Rhodes in 1961, under a rule that every text must be agreed unanimously [[cite:towards]]. In January 2016 their primates decided to hold it in June at the Orthodox Academy of [[place:crete]], and took off the agenda the questions on which they had not agreed, among them how autocephaly is proclaimed [[cite:towards]].

In the first half of June four churches announced that they would not take part [[cite:russia]]. The Bulgarian synod asked on 1 June for a postponement, objecting among other things that topics it thought important were missing from the agenda and that texts could not be edited during the council, and resolved not to attend otherwise [[cite:bulgaria]]. The synod of Antioch said that it would not take part while its communion with the Patriarchate of Jerusalem remained broken in a dispute over jurisdiction [[cite:antioch]]. The Georgian synod asked for postponement until unity was reached and resolved that its delegation could not attend; its objections included the drafts on marriage and on relations with other Christians [[cite:georgia]]. Patriarch [[person:kirill-of-moscow]] had met Pope Francis at Havana that February [[cite:havana]]. His church's synod held that the absence of even one recognised autocephalous church was an insurmountable obstacle, supported postponement, and resolved that its delegation could not take part in a council held without the consent of several churches [[cite:russia]].

The council opened on Pentecost Sunday, 19 June, with a liturgy at Heraklion [[cite:bulletin]]. [[person:bartholomew-i]] of Constantinople signed its encyclical as chairman, followed by the primates of Alexandria, Jerusalem, Serbia, Romania, Cyprus, Greece, Poland, Albania, and the Czech Lands and Slovakia [[cite:signatures]]. It issued six documents, on fasting, relations with other Christians, autonomy, the diaspora, marriage and the church's mission, with an encyclical and a message [[cite:documents]].

The encyclical says that the Orthodox Church comprises fourteen autocephalous churches and that autocephaly must not operate at the expense of unity [[cite:enc-5]]. Among later councils "of universal authority" it names that of 1484, convened, it says, to refute the unionist [[event:council-of-florence|Council of Florence]] [[cite:enc-3]]. The document on other Christians states that the Orthodox Church is the one, holy, catholic and apostolic church, whose unity cannot be disturbed, and that she nonetheless "accepts the historical name" of other churches and confessions not in communion with her [[cite:relations]]. Dialogue with them, the encyclical adds, has never meant compromise in matters of faith [[cite:enc-20]].

The council's message calls the proclamation of Orthodox unity its first priority and records a proposal that such a council meet every seven or ten years [[cite:message]]. The Russian synod had judged that the ground for convening it, the consent of all the primates, was absent [[cite:russia]]. The question of autocephaly had been left off the agenda [[cite:towards]]; it returned within three years over [[event:autocephaly-of-ukraine|Ukraine]].
