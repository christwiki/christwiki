From about 1910 a publishing company in [[place:chicago]] issued twelve volumes of essays under the title *The Fundamentals: A Testimony to the Truth* [[cite:fund-1]] [[cite:fund-12]]. The foreword to the first says that two Christian laymen bore the cost because they believed the time had come for "a new statement of the fundamentals of Christianity", and that the series would be sent to every pastor, missionary, theological teacher and student and Sunday school superintendent in the English-speaking world whose address could be found [[cite:fund-1]]. That volume contains essays on the virgin birth, the deity of Christ and the Holy Spirit, and a history of the higher criticism of the Bible [[cite:fund-1]]. [[person:r-a-torrey]] belonged to the committee that supervised the series and edited its last two volumes; by then, the two laymen state, nearly three million copies had been printed [[cite:fund-12]].

On 21 May 1922 [[person:harry-emerson-fosdick]] preached at the First Presbyterian Church in New York a sermon entitled "Shall the Fundamentalists Win?" [[cite:fosdick-title]]. People who called themselves Fundamentalists, he said, meant to drive men and women of liberal opinions out of the evangelical churches [[cite:fosdick-4]]. He named the tests he saw them setting: belief in the virgin birth as historical fact, in Scriptures dictated without error, in an atonement by substitution, and in Christ's return on the clouds to set up a millennium [[cite:fosdick-4]]. He did not ask that these opinions be given up; he asked whether anyone had the right to deny the Christian name to those who differed about them [[cite:fosdick-6]], and he called for tolerance from liberals as well [[cite:fosdick-11]].

[[person:j-gresham-machen]], who taught New Testament at Princeton Theological Seminary, published *Christianity and Liberalism* in February 1923 [[cite:machen-title]]. He traced liberal religion in all its forms to naturalism, the denial that God's creative power entered into the origin of Christianity [[cite:machen-2]]. Liberalism, he argued, "not only is a different religion from Christianity but belongs in a totally different class of religions" [[cite:machen-7]], and he called a separation of the two parties in the church "the crying need of the hour" [[cite:machen-160]].

Fosdick had said in 1922 that Fundamentalists had tried to put on the statute books of a whole state laws against the teaching of modern biology [[cite:fosdick-6]]. In 1925 Tennessee made it unlawful to teach in its public schools any theory that denies the story of the divine creation of man as taught in the Bible, and John Scopes, a teacher at [[place:dayton-tennessee]], was indicted under the act [[cite:scopes-309]]. On the seventh day of the trial Clarence Darrow, for the defence, examined William Jennings Bryan, who was acting with the prosecution, on his study of the Bible [[cite:scopes-284]] [[cite:scopes-contents]]. The jury found Scopes guilty and the judge fined him $100 [[cite:scopes-313]].

The word "modernism" was already in use in the Catholic Church: in the encyclical *Pascendi* of 1907 Pius X had condemned under that name a body of opinions held by some Catholic clergy and laity [[cite:pascendi-2]], called it "the synthesis of all heresies" [[cite:pascendi-39]], and ordered a council of vigilance in every diocese to watch for it [[cite:pascendi-55]].
