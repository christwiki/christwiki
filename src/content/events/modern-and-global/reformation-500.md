[[person:martin-luther]] sent his Ninety-five Theses to the archbishop of Mainz on 31 October 1517 [[cite:fctc-40]]; see [[event:ninety-five-theses]]. So says *From Conflict to Communion*, the report that the international Lutheran–Catholic dialogue commission published in 2013 to prepare for the five-hundredth anniversary [[cite:fctc-1]]. It observed that 2017 would be the first centenary of the Reformation to fall in the ecumenical age [[cite:fctc-7]]. The point, it said, was "not to tell a different history, but to tell that history differently" [[cite:fctc-16]]. It spoke of a dawning awareness that the struggle of the sixteenth century is over, and proposed five imperatives, the first being always to begin from the perspective of unity [[cite:fctc-238]].

On 31 October 2016 the anniversary year was opened [[cite:statement-thanks]] with a common prayer in the cathedral of [[place:lund]] in Sweden [[cite:lwf-report]], the city where the Lutheran World Federation held its first assembly in 1947 [[cite:lwf-history]]. [[person:francis]] and Cardinal Kurt Koch took part for the Catholic Church, and for the federation its president, Bishop [[person:munib-younan]] of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in Jordan and the Holy Land, and its general secretary, Martin Junge [[cite:lwf-report]]. The federation called it the first joint Catholic–Lutheran commemoration of the Reformation at the global level [[cite:lwf-report]].

In his sermon Francis acknowledged "with gratitude" that the Reformation had helped give Scripture a more central place in the church's life [[cite:homily]]. Luther's question, how to find "a propitious God", was in his words the decisive question for our lives, and Luther's "by grace alone" a reminder that God always takes the initiative [[cite:homily]].

During the service Francis and Younan signed a joint statement [[cite:lwf-report]]. It says that through fifty years of dialogue and through shared service Catholics and Lutherans have learned that what unites them is greater than what divides them [[cite:statement-thanks]]. It gives thanks for the spiritual and theological gifts received through the Reformation, and confesses and laments that Lutherans and Catholics have wounded the visible unity of the church [[cite:statement-conflict]]. It commits both to growth in communion rooted in baptism and to removing the remaining obstacles to full unity, and it speaks of the pain of those who share their whole lives but cannot share the Eucharist [[cite:statement-witness]].

The federation's report calls the Joint Declaration of 1999 a key milestone on the way to Lund [[cite:lwf-report]]; see [[event:joint-declaration-on-justification]]. Differences remained: at the press conference that followed, Koch named the church, the Eucharist and ministry as the three open questions of the dialogue [[cite:lwf-press]].
