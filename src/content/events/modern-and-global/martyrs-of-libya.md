The Vatican's Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity gives this account. On 15 February 2015 twenty-one young men were put to death on the coast of Libya for their Christian faith. Twenty were Egyptian Copts and one came from Ghana. They were workers who had been kidnapped in the city of [[place:sirte]] in December 2014 and January 2015 by militiamen claiming to belong to the "Islamic State", and they were beheaded on a beach in the western part of the city [[cite:note]]. The man from Ghana, the note says, was not a Coptic Christian; asked to deny Christ, he answered, "Their God is my God" [[cite:note]]. The Dicastery prints the twenty-one names, ending with his: Matthew Ayariga [[cite:names]].

On 16 February [[person:francis]], at the end of an address to the Moderator and representatives of the Church of Scotland, turned to his native tongue to speak of what he had read that day, the execution of "those twenty-one or twenty-two Coptic Christians". They were killed, he said, simply for being Christians, and it made no difference whether such victims were Catholics, Orthodox, Copts or Protestants: "Their blood confesses Christ." He called this an "ecumenism of blood" [[cite:francis-2015]].

A notice of the Coptic Orthodox Diocese of Los Angeles, dated 21 February, reports that [[person:tawadros-ii]], Pope of Alexandria, announced that day the inclusion of "the 21 Coptic New Martyrs of Libya" in the Synaxarium of the Coptic Orthodox Church, to be commemorated each year on 8 Amshir, which corresponds to 15 February [[cite:synaxarium]]. The Dicastery's note adds that the Holy Synod of that church confirmed the entry of their names and in 2017 made 8 Amshir a yearly day of contemporary martyrs [[cite:note]]. Neither report is the Coptic church's own act: the pope's announcement is known here through a diocese's notice, the synod's decision through the Dicastery's booklet. By the same note, the remains were found in a mass grave on the Libyan coast in 2017; those of the twenty Egyptians were returned to the Coptic church in May 2018, and those of Matthew Ayariga were brought to Egypt in September 2020 [[cite:note]]. They rest in a church built for them in the village of Al-Our, near Samalut, from which thirteen of the men came [[cite:note]].

On 11 May 2023 Tawadros was in [[place:rome]] for the fiftieth anniversary of the meeting of [[person:paul-vi]] and [[person:shenouda-iii]] [[cite:note]] [[cite:anniversary]], for which see [[event:christological-declarations|the common declaration of 1973]]. Francis thanked him for the gift of a relic of the martyrs and announced that, "with Your Holiness’ consent, these 21 martyrs will be included in the Roman Martyrology as a sign of the spiritual communion uniting our two Churches" [[cite:martyrology]]. The first commemoration in the Catholic Church was kept on 15 February 2024 with an ecumenical prayer in St Peter's Basilica, in which the vicar general of a Coptic Orthodox diocese took part [[cite:commemoration]].
