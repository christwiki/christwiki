The [[source:augsburg-confession|Augsburg Confession]] of 1530 taught that people cannot be justified before God by their own strength, merits or works, but are freely justified for Christ's sake through faith [[cite:ac-4]]. The Council of Trent, in its session of January 1547, condemned anyone who said that the sinner is justified by faith alone in the sense that nothing else is required to cooperate in obtaining the grace of justification and that he need not be prepared and disposed by the movement of his own will [[cite:trent-canon-9]]. The Joint Declaration opens by saying that the condemnations on both sides were still in force and still divided the churches [[cite:jd-1]].

The declaration sums up the dialogue held since the [[event:second-vatican-council|Second Vatican Council]] [[cite:jd-13]]. At its centre is a common confession: people are accepted by God "by grace alone", in faith in Christ's saving work and not because of any merit of their own, and receive the Holy Spirit, who renews their hearts and equips and calls them to good works [[cite:jd-15]]. It claims "a consensus in basic truths" of the doctrine, calls the remaining differences of language, theological elaboration and emphasis acceptable, and concludes that the teaching of each church as presented in the declaration does not fall under the other's sixteenth-century condemnations [[cite:jd-40-41]]. It says that it does not cover all that either church teaches about justification [[cite:jd-5]], and it lists questions still to be clarified, among them the church, authority, ministry and the sacraments [[cite:jd-43]].

The Lutheran World Federation reported in June 1998 that 89 of its 124 member and associate churches had replied, 80 with a yes and five with a no; the hardest points were the doctrine as criterion, sin in the justified, and good works [[cite:lwf-responses]]. Its Council voted to affirm the agreements [[cite:lwf-vote]] on 16 June 1998, and the Catholic response followed on 25 June [[cite:ocs-2]]. That response accepted the consensus in basic truths but held that some positions still diverged [[cite:response-declaration]]. It called the Lutheran formula "at the same time righteous and sinner", as the declaration explains it, not acceptable to Catholics [[cite:response-1]], and judged that some differences were matters of substance [[cite:response-5]].

An Annex answered these questions [[cite:ocs-2]]. It says that Lutherans and Catholics can together understand the Christian as at once righteous and sinner to the extent it sets out, despite their different approaches [[cite:annex-2a]]. With it, Cardinal Edward Cassidy and Bishop [[person:walter-kasper]] for the Catholic Church, and Bishop [[person:christian-krause]] and seven other officers for the federation, signed a statement confirming the declaration "in its entirety"; it adds that further dialogue is needed to reach full church communion [[cite:ocs]]. The signing took place at [[place:augsburg]] on 31 October 1999, and in Rome that day [[person:john-paul-ii]] called it a "milestone" [[cite:angelus]].

Other communions later associated themselves with it or affirmed it. The World Methodist Council declared at Seoul in 2006 that the declaration's common understanding corresponds to Methodist doctrine [[cite:methodist]]. The Anglican Consultative Council in 2016 welcomed and affirmed its "substance" [[cite:anglican]]. The World Communion of Reformed Churches affirmed its common statements in 2017 [[cite:reformed]].
