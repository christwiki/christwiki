In 1915 the Ottoman Empire was at war with Russia, France and Britain [[cite:frus-1400]]. On the nights of 24 and 25 April numerous Armenians were arrested in [[place:constantinople]], among them doctors, journalists, writers, clergy and members of parliament, and most were sent into the interior [[cite:lepsius-38]]. The German embassy heard of about 500 arrests [[cite:lepsius-38]]; the American ambassador, [[person:henry-morgenthau]], cabled of more than a hundred [[cite:frus-1395]]. The Armenian Patriarch, Archbishop [[person:zaven-i-der-yeghiayan|Zaven]] [[cite:lepsius-34]], asked the Grand Vizier and the Minister of the Interior for the reason and was told that leading men were being removed from the capital as a precaution against disorder [[cite:lepsius-38]].

On 24 May the Allied governments declared that Armenians were being massacred "with the connivance and often assistance of Ottoman authorities", spoke of crimes "against humanity and civilization", and said they would hold the members of the Ottoman government personally responsible [[cite:frus-1398]]. The Grand Vizier resented this as interference with his government's rights over its own subjects [[cite:frus-1399]].

On 10 July Morgenthau reported a "systematic attempt to uproot peaceful Armenian populations" by arrest, torture and deportation "from one end of the Empire to the other", with rape, pillage and murder, directed from Constantinople in the name of military necessity [[cite:frus-1400]]. He gave the cause as he understood it: Armenian volunteers had joined the Russian army and some had helped the Russians at Van, and vengeance was being taken on people most of whom were innocent [[cite:frus-1400]]. In August he wrote of people "herded like cattle into the arid and hostile wastes of the interior" [[cite:frus-1406]], and in September that the "destruction of Armenian race in Turkey is progressing rapidly" [[cite:frus-1410]]. The Minister of the Interior had told him that Protestant and Catholic Armenians would be exempted; Morgenthau reckoned them at a tenth of the whole, the rest belonging to the Armenian national ("Gregorian") church [[cite:frus-1408]].

A German missionary sister who came through [[place:deir-ez-zor]], a town in the Syrian desert, in the summer of 1915 found its inn crowded with deported Armenians, most of them women and children, who had had no bread for days [[cite:bb-145]]. The British collection of testimony published in 1916 also reports on the Christians it calls Nestorians, Syrians or Assyrians: Turkish troops and Kurdish tribesmen held their villages around Urmia in Persia from January to May 1915 [[cite:bb-99]], and in June attacked Hakkari, where the house of the patriarch Mar Shimun at Qudshanis was plundered and burned and its people fled to Persia [[cite:bb-169]].

In December Pope Benedict XV told the cardinals that utter ruin hung over Armenia [[cite:benedict]], and the Patriarch told the German embassy that Armenians who had come from Anatolia were still being arrested and expelled from the capital [[cite:lepsius-214]]. Johannes Lepsius, who edited the German documents, writes that a law of 1 August 1916 abolished the Armenian patriarchate of Constantinople and removed its office to Jerusalem [[cite:lepsius-intro]].

The International Association of Genocide Scholars holds that this was genocide against Armenians, Assyrians and Greeks [[cite:iags]]. The United States House of Representatives has used the word, and it stands in the title of a motion laid before the German Bundestag [[cite:hres]] [[cite:bundestag]]. The government of Turkey rejects it: it acknowledges the loss of many Armenian lives, describes the measures as a wartime relocation, and denies that there was a plan to destroy the Armenians [[cite:mfa]].
