dating: |
  Exodus dates the setting up of the tabernacle to the first day of the first month of the second year after the departure from Egypt [[bible:Exod 40:17]], and Numbers has the camp leave Sinai seven weeks later [[bible:Num 10:11-12]]. The calendar year is counted from [[event:the-exodus]] and is as uncertain as that date: about 1445 BC if the exodus is put at about 1446 BC on the strength of [[bible:1 Kgs 6:1]] (Bryant G. Wood, "The Rise and Fall of the 13th-Century Exodus-Conquest Theory", *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 48, 2005), or the year after an exodus of about 1260 BC under Ramesses II (Kenneth A. Kitchen, *On the Reliability of the Old Testament*, 2003). The biblical books are the only source for the tabernacle, and no remains of it are known. No Egyptian record of the exodus is known, and many scholars doubt that an exodus on the scale the Bible describes took place; the note on [[event:the-exodus]] sets out the question. The timeline places the event at the earlier date and shows both.

During the forty days on [[place:mount-sinai]], Exodus says, [[person:moses]] was told to collect gold, silver, bronze, fine cloth, skins and acacia wood from all who were willing to give. "Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them", after the pattern shown to him [[bible:Exod 25:1-9]]. At its centre was to be the ark, a chest of acacia wood overlaid with gold, holding the tablets of the covenant. On it lay a cover of pure gold with two cherubim, and from above that cover God would speak with Moses [[bible:Exod 25:10-22]].

After the affair of the golden calf the work was carried out. The people brought more than was needed and had to be told to stop [[bible:Exod 36:3-7]]. Bezalel of the tribe of Judah and Oholiab of the tribe of Dan directed the craftsmen [[bible:Exod 35:30-35]], who made the tent, the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars and the priests' vestments as Moses had been instructed [[bible:Exod 39:32-43]].

On the first day of the first month of the second year the tabernacle was set up. Moses put the tablets in the ark and the ark behind the veil [[bible:Exod 40:17-21]]. The book of Exodus ends with the cloud covering the tent: "Yahweh's glory filled the tabernacle", and Moses could not go in. From then on the lifting of the cloud was the signal to break camp [[bible:Exod 40:34-38]].

[[person:aaron]] and his four sons had been named as priests [[bible:Exod 28:1]]. Moses washed them, clothed Aaron in the high priest's vestments and anointed him, and they remained at the entrance of the tent for seven days [[bible:Lev 8:1-13]] [[bible:Lev 8:33-36]]. On the eighth day Aaron offered his first sacrifices and blessed the people, and, Leviticus says, fire came out from before the Lord and consumed the offering on the altar [[bible:Lev 9:1-7, 22-24]]. Two of his sons, Nadab and Abihu, died for offering "strange fire" [[bible:Lev 10:1-3]]. Leviticus then lays down that the high priest may pass behind the veil only once a year, on the tenth day of the seventh month. On that day he brings blood to make atonement for the sanctuary and the people, and a second goat is sent into the wilderness carrying their sins [[bible:Lev 16:1-22]] [[bible:Lev 16:29-34]].

The tent and its ark went with Israel through the wilderness and were finally brought into [[event:solomons-temple|Solomon's temple]] [[bible:1 Kgs 8:4-11]]. The letter to the Hebrews describes the tabernacle and the Day of Atonement and reads them as a figure of Christ, who "entered in once for all into the Holy Place" [[bible:Heb 9:1-12]].
