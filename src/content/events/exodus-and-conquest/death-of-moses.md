dating: |
  Deuteronomy dates the farewell speeches to the first day of the eleventh month of the fortieth year after the exodus [[bible:Deut 1:3]]. Moses is mourned for thirty days [[bible:Deut 34:8]], and Joshua crosses the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month [[bible:Josh 4:19]], so the death falls near the end of the fortieth year.

  The calendar year is forty years after [[event:the-exodus]] and shares its uncertainty: about 1406 BC if the exodus is put at about 1446 BC from [[bible:1 Kgs 6:1]] (Bryant G. Wood, "The Rise and Fall of the 13th-Century Exodus-Conquest Theory", *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 48, 2005), or about 1220 BC if it is put about 1260 BC under Ramesses II (Kenneth A. Kitchen, *On the Reliability of the Old Testament*, 2003). No Egyptian record mentions Moses or the exodus, and many scholars doubt that an exodus on the scale the Bible describes took place; the note on [[event:the-exodus]] sets out the question. The timeline places the event at the earlier date and shows both.

In the fortieth year after the exodus, with Israel camped east of the Jordan in the land of Moab, [[person:moses]] addressed the people for the last time [[bible:Deut 1:1-5]]. The book of Deuteronomy is presented as these speeches. In them he restates the law and makes a covenant in Moab "in addition to the covenant which he made with them in Horeb" [[bible:Deut 29:1]]. It binds everyone present, from the leaders to the woodcutters and water-carriers, and also "those who are not here with us today" [[bible:Deut 29:10-15]]. He sets before them life and death, blessing and curse, and urges them to choose life [[bible:Deut 30:15-20]].

Moses, now a hundred and twenty years old, told the people that he would not cross the Jordan, and charged [[person:joshua]] in the sight of all Israel to be strong and courageous [[bible:Deut 31:1-8]]. He had earlier laid his hands on Joshua before the priest Eleazar and the whole community [[bible:Num 27:18-23]]. Deuteronomy says that Moses wrote "this law" and gave it to the priests and elders, to be read aloud to all Israel every seventh year at the feast of tents, and that the book was placed beside the ark as a witness [[bible:Deut 31:9-13]] [[bible:Deut 31:24-26]]. He taught the people a song and pronounced a last blessing on Israel [[bible:Deut 31:22, 30]] [[bible:Deut 33:1]].

He had asked to be allowed to cross and had been refused [[bible:Deut 3:23-28]]; the reason given in the command to go up the mountain is his failure at the waters of Meribah [[bible:Deut 32:48-52]]. He went up from the plains of Moab to [[place:mount-nebo]], to the top of Pisgah, opposite Jericho, and was shown the whole land, from Gilead and Dan to the western sea and south to Zoar. "I have caused you to see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there" [[bible:Deut 34:1-4]]. Moses died there in the land of Moab and was buried in a valley opposite Beth-peor, "but no man knows where his tomb is to this day". Israel wept for him thirty days [[bible:Deut 34:5-8]].

The book closes with the judgment that no prophet has since arisen in Israel like Moses, "whom Yahweh knew face to face" [[bible:Deut 34:10-12]]. Earlier in the book Moses promises a prophet like himself [[bible:Deut 18:15-18]], and in Acts Peter applies that promise to Jesus [[bible:Acts 3:12, 22]]. In the Gospels Moses appears with Elijah at the transfiguration [[bible:Luke 9:28-31]]. The New Testament names the first part of the Scriptures after him, "the law of Moses" [[bible:Luke 24:44]].
