  Exodus sets the arrival at Sinai in the third month after the departure from Egypt [[bible:Exod 19:1]], so these events fall in the year of [[event:the-exodus]] and share its uncertainty. That year is about 1446 BC if the 480 years of [[bible:1 Kgs 6:1]] are counted literally (Bryant G. Wood, "The Rise and Fall of the 13th-Century Exodus-Conquest Theory", *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 48, 2005), or about 1260 BC if the store city Raamses [[bible:Exod 1:11]] was built for Ramesses II (Kenneth A. Kitchen, *On the Reliability of the Old Testament*, 2003). There is no evidence from outside the Bible for the events at Sinai. No Egyptian record of the exodus is known, and many scholars doubt that an exodus on the scale the Bible describes took place; the note on [[event:the-exodus]] sets out the question. In the third month after leaving Egypt the Israelites camped in the wilderness in front of [[place:mount-sinai]] [[bible:Exod 19:1-2]]. Through [[person:moses]], Exodus says, God offered them a covenant: if they kept it, they would be his own possession among all peoples, "a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation". The people answered that they would do all that the Lord had spoken [[bible:Exod 19:3-8]].

On the third day, the account goes on, there was thunder and lightning, a thick cloud and the sound of a trumpet, and the mountain smoked and shook [[bible:Exod 19:16-19]]. God then spoke the Ten Commandments, which begin by naming him as the one who brought Israel out of Egypt [[bible:Exod 20:1-17]]. The people were afraid and asked Moses to speak to them in God's place [[bible:Exod 20:18-21]]. Moses wrote down the words, built an altar, read "the book of the covenant" aloud and sprinkled the blood of the sacrifices on the altar and on the people as "the blood of the covenant" [[bible:Exod 24:3-8]]. He then went up into the cloud on the mountain for forty days and forty nights [[bible:Exod 24:15-18]] and was given two stone tablets "written with God's finger" [[bible:Exod 31:18]].

While he was away the people asked [[person:aaron]] for gods to go before them. Aaron made a calf from their gold earrings and built an altar in front of it [[bible:Exod 32:1-6]]. Moses pleaded for the people on the mountain [[bible:Exod 32:11-14]], came down, broke the tablets at the sight of the calf and destroyed it [[bible:Exod 32:19-20]]. About three thousand men were killed by the Levites [[bible:Exod 32:26-28]]. Moses offered to be blotted out himself if the people could not be forgiven [[bible:Exod 32:30-32]]. He cut two new tablets and went up again, and there, the text says, the Lord passed before him and proclaimed himself "a merciful and gracious God, slow to anger" [[bible:Exod 34:1-7]]. After another forty days the words of the covenant, "the ten commandments", were written on the new tablets [[bible:Exod 34:27-28]].

Deuteronomy repeats the commandments. It has Moses tell the next generation that the covenant at Horeb, its name for the mountain, was made "with us, even us, who are all of us here alive today" [[bible:Deut 5:1-22]]. The churches number the ten differently: the Catholic Catechism notes that Catholics and Lutherans follow Augustine's division, and the Orthodox and Reformed that of the Greek Fathers [[cite:catechism]].

Jeremiah promised a new covenant, unlike the one made when Israel was led out of Egypt [[bible:Jer 31:31-34]]. The New Testament takes up both the promise and the words of Moses: at the Last Supper Jesus calls the cup "my blood of the new covenant" [[bible:Matt 26:27-28]], and the letter to the Hebrews contrasts the fire and trumpet of Sinai with Mount Zion and "Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant" [[bible:Heb 12:18-24]].
