dating: |
  Josephus dates the senate's decree to the consulship of Domitius Calvinus and Asinius Pollio, which is 40 BC [[cite:senate]]. He dates the capture of Jerusalem to the consulship of Marcus Agrippa and Caninius Gallus, which is 37 BC, and says that the siege began in "the third year" after the decree [[cite:siege]] [[cite:end]]. These are the dates generally accepted: see Emil Schürer, *The History of the Jewish People in the Age of Jesus Christ*, revised by G. Vermes and F. Millar, vol. 1 (1973), section 14.

  His other figures do not all agree with them. He places the decree in the 184th Olympiad, which ended in the summer of 40 BC [[cite:senate]], and he says that Jerusalem fell twenty-seven years to the day after Pompey took it, which counted exactly gives 36 BC [[cite:end]]. Cassius Dio puts the capture under the consuls of 38 BC [[cite:dio]]. W. E. Filmer, "The Chronology of the Reign of Herod the Great", *Journal of Theological Studies* 17 (1966), pp. 283–298, and Andrew E. Steinmann, "When Did Herod the Great Reign?", *Novum Testamentum* 51 (2009), pp. 1–29, argue from such details for 39 and 36 BC. The question bears on the date of Herod's death; see [[event:death-of-herod]].

  Josephus is the only full source. He wrote more than a century later and drew on Nicolaus of Damascus, whom he accuses of shaping his account to gratify Herod [[cite:antipater]].

[[person:herod-the-great]] was the son of Antipater, an Idumean and the friend and adviser of the high priest [[person:hyrcanus-ii]] [[cite:antipater]]. Mark Antony, who controlled the Roman East, appointed Herod and his brother Phasael tetrarchs and put the public affairs of the Jews in their hands [[cite:tetrarch]].

In 40 BC a Parthian army overran Syria. It brought with it Antigonus, the son of [[person:aristobulus-ii]], who had promised the Parthians money in return for the throne [[cite:parthians]]. Hyrcanus and Phasael were seized. Antigonus had Hyrcanus's ears cut off, which barred him from the high priesthood, and Phasael killed himself. Herod escaped from [[place:jerusalem]] by night with his family [[cite:flight]]. He made his way by Egypt and Rhodes to [[place:rome]] [[cite:voyage]].

There Antony and Octavian, the future [[person:augustus]], both took his side. The senate declared Antigonus an enemy of Rome, because he had accepted the kingdom from the Parthians, and voted to make Herod king. Herod left the senate house walking between Antony and Octavian, and the decree was laid up on the Capitol with a sacrifice [[cite:senate]] [[cite:senate-war]]. [[person:josephus]] says that Herod had not expected it: he had meant to ask for the throne for his wife's young brother, who was of the Hasmonean family [[cite:senate]].

It took three years to make the decree good. In the spring of the third year Herod laid siege to Jerusalem [[cite:siege]] together with Gaius Sosius, whom Antony had made governor of Syria [[cite:dio]]. During the siege he married Mariamne [[cite:siege-war]], a granddaughter of both Hyrcanus and Aristobulus [[cite:flight]]. The city fell after some five months, and the Roman soldiers killed without regard to age or sex. Antigonus surrendered to Sosius [[cite:siege]] [[cite:siege-war]]. Herod kept the foreign troops out of the sanctuary and paid them from his own money to stop the looting [[cite:restraint]]. Antigonus was taken in chains to Antony and beheaded [[cite:axe]]. Cassius Dio adds that he was first bound to a cross and flogged, which no king had suffered before at Roman hands [[cite:dio]]. "And thus", Josephus writes, "did the government of the Asamoneans cease", and it passed to a man "of no more than a vulgar family" [[cite:end]].

Herod owed his crown to Rome and kept it by loyalty to whoever ruled there. Tacitus sums it up: Antony gave him the throne, and Augustus enlarged his power [[cite:tacitus]]. He is the King Herod of the Gospels: Matthew sets the birth of [[person:jesus]] in his days [[bible:Matt 2:1]], and Luke begins his account of the births of [[person:john-the-baptist]] and Jesus in them [[bible:Luke 1:5]].
