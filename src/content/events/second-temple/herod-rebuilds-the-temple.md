dating: |
  In the *Antiquities* Josephus puts the start of the work in the eighteenth year of Herod's reign [[cite:speech]]. Counted from his capture of Jerusalem in 37 BC, that is 20/19 BC. In the earlier *Jewish War* he says the fifteenth year [[cite:fifteenth]], which on the same count would be 23/22 BC. The *Antiquities* is the fuller account and its date is the one generally followed: see Emil Schürer, *The History of the Jewish People in the Age of Jesus Christ*, revised by G. Vermes and F. Millar, vol. 1 (1973), section 15.

  John's Gospel has people in Jerusalem say that the temple has been forty-six years in building [[bible:John 2:20]]. Reckoned from 20/19 BC, that points to about AD 27 or 28, and it is one of the figures used to date the ministry of Jesus. The exact year depends on how Herod's regnal years and the forty-six years are counted.

  Josephus is the main source for the rebuilding. He wrote about a century after it began, but he was himself a priest [[cite:priest]], and by his own account the last of the work was finished only in his lifetime [[cite:finished]].

In the eighteenth year of his reign [[person:herod-the-great]] announced that he would rebuild the temple in [[place:jerusalem]]. [[person:josephus]] gives him a speech: the temple built by the exiles who returned from [[place:babylon]] was lower than Solomon's, because the Persian kings had fixed its measurements, and now that he had peace, wealth and the friendship of [[place:rome]] he would make good the lack [[cite:speech]]. The people feared that he would pull the old building down and never finish the new. He promised to demolish nothing until everything was ready. He assembled a thousand wagons and ten thousand workmen, and he had priests trained as masons and carpenters [[cite:preparations]], since only priests could enter the inner courts. Herod himself, Josephus notes, never went into them, "because he was not a priest" [[cite:barrier]].

The new sanctuary stood on new foundations and was built of white stone, with a golden vine spreading above its doors [[cite:sanctuary]]. Its front was plated with gold that flashed in the rising sun, and from a distance the building looked "like a mountain covered with snow" [[cite:gold]]. The innermost room, the Holy of Holies, was empty [[cite:holies]]. Around the sanctuary Herod enclosed an area twice the size of the old one and edged it with colonnades [[cite:fifteenth]]. The royal portico along the south side had 162 columns in four rows [[cite:stoa]]. Foreigners could go no further than the outer court. A low stone barrier marked the limit, with notices in Greek and Latin forbidding any foreigner to pass it on pain of death [[cite:barrier]] [[cite:barrier-war]].

The priests finished the sanctuary itself in a year and six months, and the colonnades and outer courts took eight years [[cite:duration]]. The work as a whole went on far longer. In John's Gospel people say, "It took forty-six years to build this temple!" [[bible:John 2:20]]. Josephus reports that it was finished only at the end of the governorship of Albinus, in the early 60s AD, when more than eighteen thousand workmen were left without employment [[cite:finished]]. A few years later the Romans burned it; see [[event:destruction-of-the-second-temple]].

This is the temple in which the Gospels and Acts are set. The child [[person:jesus]] is brought to it [[bible:Luke 2:27]], and as a man he walks in its colonnades at the feast of Dedication [[bible:John 10:22-23]]. A disciple marvels at its stones, and Jesus answers that not one will be left on another [[bible:Mark 13:1-2]]. The barrier against foreigners lies behind the riot in which [[person:paul]] was arrested, on the charge that he had brought Greeks into the temple [[bible:Acts 21:27-29]].
