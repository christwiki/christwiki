Winfrid, as Willibald says he was first called, was an English monk of the monastery of Nursling, near Winchester [[cite:early]] [[cite:rome]]. He travelled to [[place:rome]] [[cite:rome]], and on 15 May 719 Pope [[person:gregory-ii]] gave him a written commission to preach to the peoples still held by paganism and to baptize by the Roman rite [[cite:commission]]. The letter already calls him [[person:boniface|Boniface]]. Willibald, who wrote his Life within a few years of his death, says that he worked first in Thuringia, then for three years under Willibrord in Frisia, then in Hesse [[cite:rome]].

In 722 the pope called him back and consecrated him bishop on 30 November [[cite:consecration]]. Boniface swore on the tomb of Peter to uphold the Catholic faith and its unity, to be loyal in all things to Peter, to the pope and to the good of his church, to have no communion with bishops who lived against the rules of the fathers, and to correct them or report them to the pope [[cite:oath]]. Gregory commended him to the Frankish ruler Charles Martel, under whose protection he returned to Hesse [[cite:consecration]].

There, Willibald says, many still sacrificed to trees and springs. At [[place:geismar]] Boniface set about felling an oak of great size which the pagans called by the name of Jupiter. A crowd stood by cursing him. When he had cut only a little way, the tree crashed down as if blown from above and broke into four pieces, and those who had cursed believed. From the timber he built an oratory dedicated to Peter [[cite:oak]]. The papal chronicle of the time says in a single sentence that Gregory, through Bishop Boniface, preached the word of salvation in Germany and turned that people to Christ [[cite:lp]].

Pope Gregory III sent him the pallium of an archbishop. He divided Bavaria into four dioceses [[cite:archbishop]], and in 742 reported to Pope Zacharias that he had founded three more sees and that the Franks had held no synod for more than eighty years [[cite:zacharias]]. Under Charles's sons Carloman and Pepin he held synods to restore the canons, and he was given the see of [[place:mainz]] [[cite:synods]]. In 751 he told the pope of a monastery he had founded in the forest by the river [[place:fulda]], under the Rule of Benedict, where he wished to be buried [[cite:fulda]].

In old age he appointed his disciple Lul to succeed him and went back to Frisia. On the morning he had set for confirming the newly baptized, an armed band attacked his camp by the river Bordne, the Boorne in Friesland. He forbade his companions to fight, and they were killed with him. The attackers broke open his chests looking for gold and found books [[cite:synods]] [[cite:death]]. His body was taken by way of Utrecht and Mainz to Fulda [[cite:burial]].

His oath and his letters to four popes bound the churches he founded, and the Frankish church he helped to reform, to the see of Rome.
