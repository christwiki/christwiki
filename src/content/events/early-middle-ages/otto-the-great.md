In 960 Italy was in the hands of King Berengar and his son Adalbert, and Pope [[person:john-xii]], hard pressed by them, sent a deacon and a secretary across the Alps to ask the German king [[person:otto-i]] to come and free the Roman church [[cite:envoys]] [[cite:appeal]]. Otto had his young son made king and entered Italy. Berengar and his family shut themselves up in their castles [[cite:italy]].

He kept Christmas at Pavia and went on to [[place:rome]]. There, says a chronicler close to his court, he was received with the acclamation of the whole Roman clergy and people and was named and consecrated emperor and Augustus by Pope John [[cite:coronation]]. Liudprand of Cremona, who served Otto, adds that he was anointed by the pope, restored the church's property and gave rich gifts, and that the pope and the leading Romans swore on the body of St Peter never to help Berengar and Adalbert [[cite:appeal]].

On 13 February Otto issued a privilege for the Roman church [[cite:dated]]. It confirms to St Peter and to his vicar John the city and duchy of Rome, the lands of Ravenna and the other territories granted by Pippin and Charlemagne [[cite:lands]]. It also reserves the emperor's authority. The clergy and nobles of Rome are to swear that papal elections will be canonical; the man elected is not to be consecrated until he has made a promise before the emperor's envoys; and envoys of pope and emperor are to report each year on how justice is done [[cite:elections]].

The alliance did not last. According to Liudprand, John soon turned to Adalbert, and Otto came back to Rome [[cite:breach]]. A synod in St Peter's, with the emperor present, heard the Roman clergy accuse the pope of perjury, simony, adultery and much else; when John refused to appear he was declared deposed, and Leo, the chief notary of the Roman church, was chosen in his place [[cite:synod]]. The Romans rose against Otto and brought John back, and he died soon afterwards [[cite:end]]. Liudprand writes as the emperor's advocate, and John's side of the story is not heard in his account.

The coronation renewed in the West the imperial title first given to Charlemagne ([[event:coronation-of-charlemagne]]), now held by the German kings. Its terms also gave the emperor a say in who became pope, which the reformers of the next century set out to end ([[event:investiture-controversy]]).
