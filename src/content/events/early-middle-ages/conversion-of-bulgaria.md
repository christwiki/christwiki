  No source read for this entry gives the day or the year of the baptism. The Annals of St Bertin say under 864 that the Bulgar ruler had promised to become a Christian [[cite:promise]]. Under 866 they say that he, who had thought of becoming a Christian in the year before, received baptism, and they go on in the same entry to the revolt and the embassies [[cite:revolt]]. Photius, in a letter assigned to 867, writes that the Bulgars had held the Christian faith for less than two years when clergy from the West arrived [[cite:encyclical]]. The Byzantine history that tells the story gives no year and sets it among the events of the regency of the empress Theodora [[cite:sister]].

  Together these put the baptism between 864 and 866. G. Waitz, editing the annals in 1883, notes that it took place "as it seems" in 864 [[cite:revolt]], and the editor of John VIII's letters gives the same year [[cite:return]]. The pope's answer to Boris is headed AD 866 in the translation used here [[cite:heading]].

  Nor does any of them name the place. The annalist speaks of the king's palace and of the gates of a city [[cite:revolt]]. The timeline sets the event at [[place:pliska]] by convention.

In 864, a Frankish annalist records, King Louis the German marched to meet the khagan of the Bulgars, who had promised that he wished to become a Christian [[cite:promise]]. When the baptism came, it came from [[place:constantinople]]. A Byzantine court history compiled in the next century tells that the sister of [[person:boris-i|Boris]] had become a Christian as a captive in the imperial city and pressed the faith on him after her return, that a famine made him call on the God of the Christians, and that he was baptized by a bishop sent from the capital and named Michael after the emperor [[cite:sister]]. It adds, as something people tell, that a monk and painter named Methodius painted the Last Judgment for him, and that the sight led him to be baptized at dead of night [[cite:painter]].

[[person:photius]], patriarch of Constantinople, wrote to the convert as to a spiritual son. His letter recites the creed, describes the seven general councils one by one, likens what Michael had done to the deeds of Constantine the Great, and continues with counsels for a Christian ruler [[cite:letter]].

The annalist reports that the Bulgar nobles, angered by the baptism, raised the people to kill their king. He went out against them with forty-eight men, seven clerics with burning candles appeared before him, and the rebels fell to the ground in terror; he put fifty-two of the nobles to death and let the rest go unharmed [[cite:revolt]]. Boris's own account survives in the reply of Pope [[person:nicholas-i]], who repeats it to him: the leaders were put to the sword with all their children. To have killed children who had no part in the plot, the pope answers, was a sin, and the captured parents should have been spared as well; Boris is to do penance [[cite:executions]].

Boris then looked west. He asked Louis the German for a bishop and priests, and sent his son and many nobles to [[place:rome]] with gifts, questions about the faith and a request for clergy [[cite:embassies]]. Nicholas answered in 106 chapters. Several deal with what Greeks had told the Bulgars: that they must not bathe on Wednesdays and Fridays, must stand in church with their hands held to the breast, must not take communion without a belt. Boris had told him that Greeks, Armenians and others were teaching different things in his country [[cite:customs]]. Those who refused Christianity and kept to their idols, the pope wrote, were to be reasoned with and shunned, but not forced to believe [[cite:force]]. Asked whether the Bulgars might have a patriarch, he offered a bishop consecrated at Rome and in time an archbishop [[cite:archbishop]].

Photius protested to the eastern patriarchs that men from the West had fallen on a people Christian for less than two years [[cite:encyclical]], and Bulgaria became part of [[event:photian-schism|his quarrel with Rome]]. By 878 it was Rome that complained. In April of that year Pope [[person:john-viii]] wrote to Boris that he grieved to see him deceived, urged him not to follow the Greeks and asked him to return to Saint Peter [[cite:return]]. He told the Greek bishops and clergy in Bulgaria, whom he called invaders, to leave within thirty days or lose their office [[cite:expel]].
