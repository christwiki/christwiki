dating: |
  The minutes head the first session with the seventh day of November in the ninth indiction and the twenty-seventh year of Constantine IV [[cite:opening]]. The ninth indiction ran from September 680 to August 681, so the day is 7 November 680.

  The Liber Pontificalis, written at Rome, has the legates enter Constantinople on 10 November of the ninth indiction and places their reception in the Trullus on 22 November [[cite:lp]]. Neither date agrees with the council's own documents: the emperor's letter of 10 September already reports that the legates have come [[cite:sacra]], and the minutes open on 7 November [[cite:opening]]. Louis Duchesne set this out in the notes to his edition (*Le Liber pontificalis*, vol. 1, 1886, [p. 356, notes 14 and 16](https://archive.org/details/duchesne01/page/n646/mode/1up)): the arrival cannot be later than 10 September 680, and the Roman writer gathers under 22 November what the minutes spread over sessions held from 7 November 680 to March 681. The timeline follows the acts.

  In the text cited, the date of the emperor's letter stands in the heading of the Latin version only, and its regnal figures, the twenty-eighth year and the twelfth after the consulship, differ from the twenty-seventh and thirteenth in the heading of the first session [[cite:sacra]] [[cite:opening]].

The [[event:council-of-chalcedon]] had taught that Christ is one person in two natures. In the seventh century a further question divided the churches of the empire: whether Christ had one will and one "operation", or two. At the opening session the Roman legates said that for some forty-six years bishops of [[place:constantinople]], Sergius, Pyrrhus, Paul and Peter, with Cyrus of Alexandria, had taught one will and one operation; their opponents answered that they had this teaching from the councils and the fathers, from those same bishops, and from Honorius, a former pope of Rome [[cite:session1]].

The teaching had been resisted before. Theophanes relates that the monk [[person:maximus-the-confessor|Maximus]] debated the deposed patriarch Pyrrhus in Africa and then went to Rome, where he urged Pope Martin to hold a synod; it affirmed two wills in Christ. The emperor Constans II had Martin and Maximus brought to Constantinople and exiled, and had Maximus's tongue cut out and his hand cut off [[cite:maximus]] [[cite:constans]].

Constans's son [[person:constantine-iv]] changed course and asked Rome to send delegates [[cite:constans]] [[cite:sixth]] [[cite:lp]]. Pope [[person:agatho]] sent bishops, priests and monks with two letters, his own and one from a Roman synod of 125 bishops [[cite:session1]] [[cite:lp]]. His letter sets out the doctrine of "two natural wills, and two natural operations" and claims that the Roman church "has never erred from the path of the apostolic tradition" [[cite:agatho]]. A letter of the emperor to George, patriarch of Constantinople, dated 10 September in its Latin version, says that the envoys have now come to him and handed over the two letters, and tells George to summon his metropolitans and bishops to the capital [[cite:sacra]]. The Liber Pontificalis dates the legates' entry into the city to 10 November and their reception in the Trullus to 22 November [[cite:lp]]. The minutes date the first session to 7 November 680, in the hall of the palace called the Trullus, with the emperor presiding [[cite:opening]]. Macarius, patriarch of Antioch, who would not give up the doctrine of one will, was deposed [[cite:lp]].

At its thirteenth session the council condemned Sergius, Cyrus, Pyrrhus, Paul, Peter and Theodore of Pharan, and with them Honorius, "because of what we found written by him to Sergius, that in all respects he followed his view" [[cite:sentence]]. Its definition repeats the faith of Chalcedon and adds that there are in Christ

> two natural wills and two natural operations indivisibly, inconvertibly, inseparably, inconfusedly.

The human will is not opposed to the divine but follows it, as in the saying "not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me" [[bible:John 6:38]] [[cite:definition]]. The bishops told the emperor that "Peter spoke through Agatho", and addressed the pope as bishop of "the first see of the Universal Church" [[cite:address]] [[cite:letter]].

Theophanes counts 289 bishops and calls this the sixth ecumenical council [[cite:sixth]]. Both the Greek East and the Latin West received it as such: a council at Constantinople in 692 confirmed its faith [[cite:trullo]], and the Catholic Church cites it under the same title [[cite:catechism]].
