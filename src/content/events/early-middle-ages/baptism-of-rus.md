dating: |
  The Primary Chronicle places the capture of Cherson, the baptism and the baptism of Kyiv in the year 6496 from creation, AD 988 [[cite:cherson]]. It was compiled more than a century later, and its years for this period have to be checked against writers outside Rus'.

  Yahya of Antioch dates the proclamation of the rebel Bardas Phokas to 14 September 987 and puts the agreement with the Rus', including the promise of baptism, and the arrival of their troops after it [[cite:yahya]]; he dates the rebel's death in battle at Abydos to 13 April 989 [[cite:abydos]]. Leo the Deacon, after telling of Phokas's death, says that the fall of Cherson to the Rus' was foretold by pillars of fire in the northern sky, and goes on to a comet and an earthquake [[cite:leo]]. S. H. Cross and O. P. Sherbowitz-Wetzor, *The Russian Primary Chronicle: Laurentian Text* (1953), notes 93–94, following V. G. Vasil'evsky, match these signs with ones that Yahya dates to April and July 989. They place the agreement in the winter of 987–988, the fall of Cherson between April and July 989, and Vladimir's baptism after it, in 989 or 990. Yahya's order of telling, in which the bishops are sent before the troops arrive, would put the baptism in 988. The timeline shows the chronicle's year and the year of the fall of Cherson. Where Vladimir was baptized remains open; the chronicler knew of at least three places.

Christianity had reached the Rus' before [[person:vladimir-the-great]]. In 867 [[person:photius]], patriarch of Constantinople, wrote that they had accepted a bishop and pastor [[cite:photius]]. [[person:olga-of-kyiv]], whom the chronicle calls Vladimir's grandmother [[cite:envoys]], was baptized at Constantinople; the Primary Chronicle tells it as a tale in which she outwits the emperor [[cite:olga]], and a German annalist records under 959 that envoys of Helena, queen of the Rus', who had been baptized at Constantinople, asked King Otto for a bishop [[cite:helena]]. Her son and her people stayed pagan [[cite:olga]].

The chronicle of [[place:kyiv]], written over a century after the event, tells Vladimir's conversion as a deliberate choice. In 986, it says, he was visited in turn by Muslim Bulgars, by Germans sent by the pope, by Jewish Khazars and by a Greek scholar, and questioned each about their faith [[cite:faiths]]. The next year he sent ten men to see how each people worshipped. Those who came back from Constantinople said that in the Greeks' church they had not known whether they were in heaven or on earth [[cite:envoys]].

In 988, the chronicle continues, Vladimir besieged the Greek city of [[place:chersonesus]] on the Black Sea and took it by cutting its water supply. He demanded the emperors' sister Anna in marriage; they answered that she could not marry a pagan. She came with priests, and Vladimir, who had been struck with blindness, was baptized by the bishop of Cherson, recovered his sight and married her. The chronicler notes that others placed the baptism at Kyiv or at Vasil'ev [[cite:cherson]]. Back in Kyiv he had the idols destroyed and the image of Perun dragged to the river, and summoned the whole city to the Dnieper, where the people were baptized by the priests from Cherson and those of the princess. He built churches where the idols had stood and sent the children of leading families to be taught letters [[cite:kyiv]].

Writers closer to the events tell of politics. Yahya of Antioch says that [[person:basil-ii]], threatened by the revolt of Bardas Phokas, asked the king of the Rus' for help; the king married the emperor's sister on condition that he and his people be baptized, and the emperor sent metropolitans and bishops who baptized them [[cite:yahya]]. Psellus mentions the "Scythian" troops who then fought for Basil [[cite:psellus]], and Leo the Deacon, after telling of the rebel's death, names the capture of Cherson among disasters foretold by signs in the sky [[cite:leo]]. As the translators of the chronicle reconstruct it from these writers, the alliance came first and the attack on Cherson later, the reverse of the chronicle's order.

Rus' thus took its bishops from Constantinople and its books in Slavonic, the legacy of [[event:cyril-and-methodius]] [[cite:slavonic]]. The chronicle itself, written after [[event:east-west-schism]], has the Greek teacher warn Vladimir against the Latins [[cite:faiths]].
