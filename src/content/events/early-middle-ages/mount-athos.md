He says that the church of the Lavra had been under construction for less than four months when news came that Nikephoros Phokas had been proclaimed emperor [[cite:building]]. The translators of the typikon put that accession in 963, the year by which the foundation is usually dated, and note that the saint's Lives place the building somewhat differently. They date the charter of John Tzimiskes to 971–972 and Athanasius's own typikon to 973–975.

Monks were living on [[place:mount-athos]] before the Lavra was built. When [[person:athanasius-the-athonite]] came there from Mount Kyminas, it was the *protos*, the first among the monks, and the elders who assigned him a place for his cell, as their custom was [[cite:building]].

Athanasius tells the story of the foundation himself, some ten years later. The general [[person:nikephoros-ii-phokas]], whom he had known at Kyminas, wished to become a monk. While commanding the campaign against Crete he summoned Athanasius to the island and pressed him to build a lavra beside his cell on Athos, where the general would join him when the war was over. Athanasius, who wanted solitude, resisted [[cite:patron]]. After the victory Nikephoros sent a monk with a letter and six pounds (*litrai*) of gold. Cells were built for him and a church was begun [[cite:building]].

Less than four months later Nikephoros was proclaimed emperor. Athanasius left the church half built, went to the capital and reproached him. The emperor swore that he still meant to keep his vow and told him to finish the work, and he paid part of the cost [[cite:building]]. Athanasius describes the labour of clearing rock and forest on a headland with no harbour, a hundred miles from the mainland by his reckoning [[cite:site]]. By the emperor's charter, which he quotes, the Lavra was to have eighty monks with Athanasius as superior; once the emperor too was dead the monks were to choose their own superior from among themselves; and the house was never to be given to any person or made subject to another monastery, but to remain "free and self-governing" [[cite:chrysobull]].

A large endowed community among hermits brought conflict. After Nikephoros's death the *protos* and another monk went to the emperor John Tzimiskes with complaints against Athanasius. The emperor sent Euthymios, a monk of the Stoudios monastery, who sat for a week with all the superiors, found both parties blameless and then drew up rules for the whole Mountain [[cite:inquiry]]. Among them: monks are not to trade in land for profit, wine is not to be sold to laymen, and boys and eunuchs are not to be admitted. The charter was signed by the emperor, the *protos*, Athanasius and the other superiors [[cite:rules]].

Between them the two documents show a self-governing monastery under the emperor's protection, hermits beside it, and a yearly assembly of the superiors with the *protos* [[cite:chrysobull]] [[cite:inquiry]].
