Greek and Latin Christians had quarrelled before, over the pope's claim to judge a patriarch of Constantinople and over the words "and from the Son" in the creed ([[event:photian-schism]], [[event:third-council-of-toledo]]). By 1054 the pope was no longer named in the liturgy at [[place:constantinople]]. The patriarch [[person:michael-cerularius]] believed this had been so for centuries [[cite:diptychs]]; Peter, patriarch of Antioch, answered that he had heard the name read there forty-five years earlier [[cite:memory]].

In 1053 a letter in the name of Pope [[person:leo-ix]] answered Greek attacks on the Latin use of unleavened bread. It declared that the Roman see is judged by no one and cited the Donation of Constantine in proof [[cite:claims]]. Three legates followed, led by [[person:humbert-of-silva-candida]]. In 1054 the emperor received them; the patriarch would not meet them [[cite:legation]]. Leo had died at Rome that April, before the legates acted [[cite:death]].

On Saturday 16 July the legates laid a document on the altar of Hagia Sophia and walked out, shaking the dust from their feet [[cite:legation]]. The bull finds the city, in its rulers and leading citizens, most Christian and orthodox. It charges Michael and his supporters with a list of heresies, among them rebaptizing Latins, letting priests marry and cutting from the creed the procession of the Spirit from the Son, and pronounces anathema on Michael, on two named associates and on their followers, unless they repent [[cite:bull]].

The synod's edict answers in kind. In words borrowed from Photius [[cite:photius]], it speaks of men from the darkness of the West who condemned the orthodox for wearing beards, for receiving communion from married priests and for refusing to alter the creed [[cite:reply]]. It holds that the three were not sent by the pope at all but came on their own, with forged seals [[cite:impostors]]. It anathematizes the document and those who wrote it or advised it; the emperor ordered it burnt, and the patriarch kept the original [[cite:sentence]].

The synod read the bull as an attack on the whole orthodox church [[cite:reply]], but its own sentence fell only on the document, its authors and their helpers [[cite:sentence]], whom it held to have acted without the pope [[cite:impostors]]. Peter of Antioch, who also rejected the addition to the creed, urged Michael to treat the Latins as brothers, to be lenient where the faith was not at stake [[cite:brothers]] and to write to the next pope [[cite:nextpope]]. The estrangement hardened later. After the sack of Constantinople in 1204 Innocent III wrote that the Greek church now had reason to detest the Latins "more than dogs" ([[event:sack-of-constantinople]]) [[cite:sack]]. In 1965 the pope and the ecumenical patriarch declared that the censures of 1054 had been aimed at persons, not at the churches, and were not meant to break communion. They removed them from memory, and added that this did not end the differences between the two churches ([[event:lifting-of-the-anathemas]]) [[cite:declaration]].
