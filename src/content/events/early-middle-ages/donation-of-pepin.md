When [[person:stephen-ii]] became pope in 752 the Lombard king Aistulf held [[place:ravenna]], the centre of the emperor's province in Italy, and was pressing on [[place:rome]]. Stephen's Roman biographer says that Aistulf demanded a yearly tax of one gold coin a head from the Romans. An envoy came from the emperor at Constantinople with orders for Aistulf, and nothing followed; Stephen wrote to the emperor again, asking him to come and free Italy. Seeing that no help would come from the empire, Stephen wrote in secret to [[person:pepin-the-short|Pepin]], king of the Franks [[cite:lp-threat]].

In October 753 the pope left Rome, and on 6 January 754 Pepin met him near the royal estate of Ponthion. The king dismounted, bowed to the ground and walked beside the pope's horse like a groom. Stephen begged him in tears to take up the cause of St Peter and of the Roman state, and Pepin swore to restore the exarchate of Ravenna and the rights and places of that state [[cite:lp-promise]]. At Saint-Denis the pope anointed Pepin and his two sons as kings of the Franks, and at Quierzy the Frankish nobles agreed to the campaign [[cite:lp-journey]] [[cite:annals]].

Pepin forced the Alpine passes and shut Aistulf up in Pavia. Aistulf swore to hand back Ravenna and other cities, then broke his word and besieged Rome for three months. Pepin came again in 756 [[cite:lp-war]] [[cite:annals-756]]. An envoy of the emperor reached him near Pavia and asked that Ravenna and the cities of the exarchate be returned to imperial rule. Pepin refused. He had gone to war, he swore, for no man's favour but for love of St Peter and the pardon of his sins [[cite:lp-envoys]].

Aistulf yielded. Pepin issued a written donation of the cities to St Peter, the Roman church and all future popes, which the biographer says was still kept in the church's archive [[cite:lp-envoys]]. Abbot Fulrad collected the keys of the cities and laid them with the deed at the tomb of Peter. The list runs from Ravenna, Rimini, Pesaro and Fano to Urbino, Gubbio and Comacchio [[cite:lp-donation]]. The Frankish annals agree: Pepin took Ravenna with the Pentapolis and the whole exarchate and handed it over to St Peter [[cite:annals-756]]. Neither source gives the text of the deed. Charlemagne renewed his father's promise at Rome in 774 [[cite:lp-774]].

With this grant the bishop of Rome became the ruler of a territory in central Italy, and the papacy looked to the Frankish kings, not to the emperors, for protection. It is a different thing from the "Donation of Constantine", a text in which Constantine is made to give Pope Silvester "the city of Rome and all the provinces, districts and cities of Italy or of the western regions" [[cite:constantine]]. That document is a forgery made centuries after Constantine, as Lorenzo Valla showed in 1440 [[cite:valla]].
