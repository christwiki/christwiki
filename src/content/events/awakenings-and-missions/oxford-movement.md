On 14 July 1833 [[person:john-keble]] preached the assize sermon at St Mary's in [[place:oxford]] [[cite:start]] [[cite:preached]]. He asked by what signs a nation that had counted itself part of Christ's church might be judged to be turning from God, and whether apostasy was too hard a word for a people that threw off the church's restraint [[cite:sermon1]]. Churchmen, he said, should answer with intercession and remonstrance and should go on obeying the state [[cite:sermon2]]. R. W. Church, who lived in Oxford through these years, connects the sermon with the government's suppression of ten Irish bishoprics [[cite:bishoprics]]. [[person:john-henry-newman]] wrote that he always kept the day as the start of the movement [[cite:start]].

That September Newman began, on his own initiative, a series of *Tracts for the Times* [[cite:began]] [[cite:bishoprics]]. The first asks the clergy on what they will rest their authority if the state casts the church off, and answers that it rests on their "apostolical descent": the bishops who ordained them stand in succession from the apostles [[cite:tract1]]. [[person:edward-pusey]], a professor and canon of Christ Church, contributed a tract on fasting dated 21 December 1833, and Newman says that he gave the movement "a position and a name" [[cite:pusey]].

Tract 90, dated 1841, turned to the Thirty-nine Articles of the [[event:elizabethan-settlement]]. Newman argued that they were "not uncatholic" and could be signed by those who aimed at being "catholic in heart and doctrine" [[cite:t90-intro]], and that they should be taken in the most catholic sense they would admit [[cite:t90-end]]. Church quotes the resolution of 15 March 1841 in which the heads of the Oxford colleges declared that such interpretations were "evading rather than explaining the sense" of the Articles [[cite:censure]]. Newman agreed to stop the Tracts, and he says that for three years one bishop after another spoke against him in their charges [[cite:stopped]]. Pusey's *Life* prints the sentence of 2 June 1843 by which the vice-chancellor suspended him from preaching in the university for two years after a sermon on the Eucharist [[cite:suspended]].

On 8 October 1845 Newman wrote from Littlemore that he meant to ask a Passionist priest that night for admission into "the One Fold of Christ" [[cite:received]]. Pusey stayed: his *Life* says that the claim of the catholic church, which called Newman to submit to Rome, kept Pusey steadfast in the Anglican communion, and that the work the three friends had begun had "changed the whole aspect of the English Church" [[cite:after]].
