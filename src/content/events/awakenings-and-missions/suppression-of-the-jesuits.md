On 21 July 1773 [[person:clement-xiv]] issued at [[place:rome]] the brief *Dominus ac Redemptor*, dated from St Mary Major in the fifth year of his pontificate [[cite:date]]. It recalls religious orders that earlier popes had dissolved, among them the Templars, suppressed by Clement V in 1312 [[cite:templars]]. Those popes, the brief says, acted from the fullness of their power, without a judicial trial and without letting the orders answer the charges against them [[cite:no-trial]].

The brief then turns to the Society of Jesus (see [[event:society-of-jesus]]). It says the Society was founded for the salvation of souls and the conversion of heretics and unbelievers [[cite:founded]], and that Paul III approved it in 1540 [[cite:paul-iii]]. Almost from the beginning, it goes on, there was discord inside the Society and between it and other orders, the secular clergy, universities and princes [[cite:discord]]. Among the quarrels it lists is the dispute over rites used in some places that departed from those approved by the universal Church [[cite:rites]] (compare [[event:chinese-rites-controversy]]). It reports that the kings of France, Spain, Portugal and the Two Sicilies had expelled the Jesuits from their realms [[cite:kings]] and had pressed first Clement XIII and then Clement XIV to suppress the Society altogether [[cite:petition]].

Clement declares that the Society can no longer bear the fruit for which it was founded and that lasting peace can hardly be restored to the Church while it exists, and by his apostolic power he abolishes it, with its offices, houses, schools, colleges and constitutions [[cite:suppress]]. The authority of the general and the other superiors passes to the local bishops, and no more novices may be received [[cite:authority]]. Members not yet ordained are released from their simple vows [[cite:simple-vows]]. Ordained members may enter another order or live as secular clergy under the bishops, supported from the revenues of their former houses [[cite:priests]]. The suppression extends to the missions [[cite:missions]]. Clergy are forbidden to speak or write about the suppression and its causes without the pope's leave [[cite:silence]].

The bull of [[person:pius-vii]] that undid the brief tells what followed. Former Jesuits living as secular priests in the Russian Empire asked to remain united in one body, and the emperor Paul I recommended them to the pope in a letter of 11 August 1800 [[cite:russia]]. In March 1801 Pius allowed them to form a congregation of the Society of Jesus within Russia [[cite:russia-1801]], and in 1804, at King Ferdinand's request, he extended the grant to the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies [[cite:sicilies]]. On 7 August 1814 [[cite:restored-date]], citing requests from archbishops, bishops and other distinguished persons [[cite:requests]], he extended it to the Papal States and to all other states [[cite:extend]] and set aside *Dominus ac Redemptor* so far as it stood in the way [[cite:derogate]].

Clement's brief rests the suppression on the peace of the Church and on other reasons which, he writes, he keeps in his own mind [[cite:suppress]]. Forty-one years later Pius restored the Society by the same fullness of apostolic power [[cite:extend]].
