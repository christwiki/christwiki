  The church's Articles and Covenants give the day of its organization as the sixth day of April 1830 [[cite:dc20-date]], and Smith's history has the same date [[cite:organized]].

  The sources differ on the place. Smith's manuscript history puts the meeting in the house of Peter Whitmer and heads the revelation received there "Given at Fayette, Seneca Co N Y" [[cite:organized]]. His sketch of 1842 says that the church was first organized in the town of Manchester, Ontario County [[cite:manchester]]. The editors of the Joseph Smith Papers, in their note to that sentence of the 1842 text, say that the earliest sources place the meeting at Fayette and call the Manchester reading inaccurate; they suggest that it may go back to William W. Phelps, one of the editors of the Book of Commandments, who added "given in Manchester" to the revelation of 6 April 1830 [[cite:manchester]]. The church's present edition heads that revelation as given at Fayette, in the home of Peter Whitmer [[cite:dc21-heading]]. The timeline follows the manuscript history.

[[person:joseph-smith]] says in the history he began in 1838 that in his fifteenth year, at Manchester in the state of New York, there was "an unusual excitement on the subject of religion" among Methodists, Presbyterians and Baptists [[cite:youth]]; see [[event:second-great-awakening|the Second Great Awakening]]. In the spring of 1820, he writes, he went into the woods to ask God which church to join and saw two Personages in a pillar of light, one of whom called the other his beloved Son; he was told to join none of the sects, "for they were all wrong" [[cite:vision-1838]]. He left other accounts: one written about 1832 says "I saw the Lord", who told him that his sins were forgiven [[cite:vision-1832]]; a sketch printed in 1842 speaks of "two glorious personages" [[cite:vision-1842]].

The same history says that in 1823 a messenger named Moroni told him of a book written on gold plates, that he was given the plates in 1827, and that he translated them [[cite:plates]]. The title page of the Book of Mormon, with the imprint Palmyra, 1830, calls it an abridgment of the record of the people of Nephi, written "to the convincing of the Jew and Gentile that Jesus is the Christ, the Eternal God" [[cite:title-page]]. At the end of the volume three men state that an angel showed them the plates [[cite:three-witnesses]], and eight more that Smith showed them plates with "the appearance of gold", which they handled [[cite:eight-witnesses]].

On 6 April 1830, the history says, Smith and five others met at the house of Peter Whitmer to organize a church, and a revelation given that day at [[place:fayette-new-york|Fayette]] called Smith "a seer, a translator, a prophet, an apostle of Jesus Christ" [[cite:organized]]. The church's Articles and Covenants order baptism by immersion "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost", by one who has authority from Jesus Christ [[cite:dc20-baptism]]. A revelation that the church dates to the same month, given for some who wished to join without rebaptism, says that to be baptized even a hundred times avails nothing [[cite:dc22]]. In 1842 Smith wrote that the Latter-day Saints believe in God the Eternal Father, in his Son Jesus Christ and in the Holy Ghost, in the Bible as the word of God "as far as it is translated correctly", and in the Book of Mormon as the word of God [[cite:articles]].

Smith and his brother Hyrum were shot by a mob in the jail at Carthage, Illinois, on 27 June 1844 [[cite:dc135]]. An epistle of December 1847, signed by [[person:brigham-young]] as president on behalf of the Twelve Apostles [[cite:epistle-signed]], reports that members of that council and 143 pioneers reached the Great Basin in the latter part of July 1847 [[cite:epistle-journey]] and chose the site of a city there, to be called [[place:salt-lake-city|Great Salt Lake City]] [[cite:epistle-city]].

One of the church's revelations calls it "the only true and living church upon the face of the whole earth" [[cite:dc1]], and its First Presidency and Twelve Apostles declared in 2020 that it is "Christ’s New Testament Church restored" [[cite:restored]]. Documents of three other churches do not accept its baptism. Asked in 2001 whether baptism conferred by the Latter-day Saints is valid, the Catholic Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith answered "Negative", with the approval of [[person:john-paul-ii]] [[cite:cdf]]. A leaflet of the ecumenical office of the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) calls Mormonism "a new religious tradition distinct from the historic apostolic tradition of the Church" and advises that people who come from it be received on profession of faith and baptized [[cite:pcusa]]. A page of the United Methodist Church's website says that the Latter-day Saints baptize with the words Father, Son and Holy Spirit but mean such different things by them that this "is not what we consider Christian baptism" [[cite:umc]].
