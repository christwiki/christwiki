In 1722 a few families who had left Moravia for the sake of their faith were given leave to build on the estate of Count [[person:nikolaus-von-zinzendorf]] in Upper Lusatia [[cite:founding]]. The carpenter Christian David, who had led them out, and his companions felled the first tree for the first house on 17 June, on a wild slope below the hill called the Hutberg, and the count's steward named the place [[place:herrnhut]], "the Lord's Watch" [[cite:founding]]. The church that grew there holds that these settlers were the descendants and survivors of the ancient Church of the Brethren [[cite:preface]].

The settlement drew people of different opinions, and by 1727 it was divided. Zinzendorf drew up statutes, which the Lutheran pastor of the parish and the leading inhabitants approved. On 12 May 1727 he spoke to the whole community for three hours, the statutes were read, and each inhabitant gave his hand in pledge [[cite:pledge]]. The statutes declare that Herrnhut is to remain in love with the children of God in all religious persuasions and to judge none. They count as a genuine brother one who confesses that he owes his awakening and salvation wholly to the mercy of God in Christ and who shows by his whole life that he is determined to be delivered from sin, and they say that such a person is not to be despised for holding sectarian or defective opinions [[cite:statutes]].

On 13 August the congregation walked to the parish church at Berthelsdorf for the Lord's Supper. The Herrnhut diary, as the church's history quotes it, says that on the way those who had been estranged embraced one another, that during the hymns it could hardly be told whether the people were singing or weeping, and that they went home at noon "with feelings of holy extacy" and spent the following days "learning the lesson of love" [[cite:august]]. Two weeks later twenty-four men and twenty-four women agreed to divide the hours of day and night among them by lot, so that prayer should not cease [[cite:intercession]].

The same history traces the missions to Zinzendorf's schooldays at [[place:halle]], where he heard of the Tranquebar mission in Francke's house, and to his visit to Copenhagen in 1731, where Brethren of his household met Anthony, a black servant from St Thomas in the West Indies who had been baptized in Denmark and who described the condition of the slaves on the island [[cite:halle]]. [[person:leonard-dober]] offered himself, writing to the congregation that he was ready to become a slave in order to tell them of the Saviour. He set out on foot with David Nitschmann on 21 August 1732 [[cite:dober]], and they reached St Thomas on 13 December [[cite:arrival]].
