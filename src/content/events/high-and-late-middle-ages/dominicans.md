[[person:dominic]] was subprior of the cathedral canons of Osma in Spain. Travelling with his bishop, Diego, he met in the south of France the abbots whom the pope had sent to preach against the Albigensians, whom the church held to be heretics. Jordan of Saxony, who knew Dominic and wrote some twelve years after his death, reports the bishop's advice to them: the heretics won people by a show of gospel poverty, and would not be answered by preachers who came with horses and baggage. Diego sent his own retinue home, and the preachers went on foot, without money [[cite:diego]].

Diego died, and a crusade was preached against the Albigensians. Dominic stayed and preached, almost alone, for nearly ten years [[cite:alone]]. As the Lateran council of 1215 drew near, two men of [[place:toulouse]] joined him. One, Peter Seila, gave houses in the city, and Bishop Fulk assigned the group a sixth of the diocese's tithes for books and food [[cite:toulouse]].

Dominic went with Fulk to the council to ask [[person:innocent-iii]] to confirm an order that would be called, and would be, an order of Preachers. The pope told him to return and choose, with his brothers, a rule already approved [[cite:council]]; the council itself forbade new religious orders and required new houses to take an approved rule [[cite:lateran13]]. They chose the Rule of Augustine, with stricter customs added, and resolved to hold no landed property [[cite:council]]. In the summer of 1216 they were given the church of Saint Romanus; they were about sixteen. Innocent died, and Dominic went to his successor [[cite:church]].

On 22 December 1216, at [[place:rome]], [[person:honorius-iii]] addressed a privilege to Dominic, prior of Saint Romanus at Toulouse, and his brothers. It took their church under papal protection and confirmed there the life of canons under the Rule of Augustine [[cite:bull]]. A month later he wrote to them as "preachers" and charged them to preach the word of God in season and out of season [[cite:preachers]].

In 1217 Dominic told the brothers, few as they were, that he would scatter them through the world. Some went to Paris, others to Spain and, in 1218, to [[place:bologna]] [[cite:sent]]. The first general chapter met at Bologna in 1220, with Jordan present, and ruled that the order would keep neither property nor fixed revenues [[cite:chapter]]. Dominic fell ill and died at Bologna the next year; the bishop of Ostia, the future [[person:gregory-ix]], conducted the funeral [[cite:death]].

Among the first brothers sent to Paris were two who went to study [[cite:sent]]. Ten years after Dominic's death Gregory IX was commissioning Dominican friars to seek out heretics [[cite:ille]]; see [[event:papal-inquisition]].
