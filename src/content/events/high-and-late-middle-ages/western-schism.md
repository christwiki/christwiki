[[person:gregory-xi]] died at [[place:rome]] in March 1378, and the cardinals met in conclave in the palace where he had died. They chose Bartholomew, archbishop of Bari, who took the name [[person:urban-vi]], and in a letter of 8 May they announced that they had elected him freely and unanimously and had crowned him at Easter in St Peter's [[cite:letter-of-may]].

In August cardinals who had elected him declared the election void. Armed Romans, they now said, had surrounded and entered the palace and threatened to kill them unless they chose a Roman or an Italian, and they had named the archbishop out of fear [[cite:cardinals]]. [[person:catherine-of-siena]] wrote to three Italian cardinals that this was false: they had chosen Urban canonically, done him reverence and asked favours of him, and had now made an antipope [[cite:catherine]]. The English chronicler Knighton records that the cardinals who left Urban went to [[place:avignon]] and set up Robert of Geneva as Pope Clement, the [[person:clement-vii-of-avignon|Clement VII]] of the Avignon line, and that kings and kingdoms adhered some to one pope and some to the other [[cite:knighton]].

Each line continued. Asked by the king of France for a remedy, the University of Paris proposed three: that both claimants resign, that they accept arbitration, or that a general council decide [[cite:paris]]. In 1409 the cardinals called a council at [[place:pisa]], which declared that their united college had the right to summon it and cited both claimants to hear its judgment [[cite:pisa]]. The modern editor of the decrees of Constance says that Pisa elected Alexander V as a third pope [[cite:intro]]; his successor John XXIII names him as his predecessor [[cite:convocation]]. Neither of the older claimants gave way, so that the Council of Constance had three to deal with: John XXIII, Gregory XII of the Roman line and Benedict XIII of the Avignon line [[cite:three]].

Constance, summoned by John XXIII, deposed him on 29 May 1415 after he had fled the city [[cite:three]]. On 4 July it accepted the resignation of Gregory XII, made through his proxy Carlo Malatesta, having first allowed itself to be convoked afresh in Gregory's name [[cite:gregory]]. On 26 July 1417 it deposed Benedict XIII [[cite:benedict]]. On 11 November Cardinal Oddo Colonna was elected and took the name [[person:martin-v]] [[cite:martin]].

The schism was ended by a council that had judged popes. What that implied for the authority of councils and popes is taken up in [[event:council-of-constance]].
