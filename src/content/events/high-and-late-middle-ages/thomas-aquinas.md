[[person:thomas-aquinas]] came of a noble family. William of Tocco, a Dominican who gathered the evidence for his canonization more than forty years after his death, tells how he was sent as a boy to study at [[place:naples]], joined the Friars Preachers there, and was seized and held by his family before they let him go back [[cite:youth]]. The order sent him to Cologne to study under [[person:albert-the-great]]. His fellow students, Tocco says, called the silent young man "the dumb ox", and Albert is reported to have said that his bellowing in teaching would be heard through the whole world [[cite:albert]]. On Albert's recommendation he went to teach at [[place:paris]], where he became a master. Among his books Tocco names a Summa of theology in three parts [[cite:paris]].

The Summa opens by asking whether any teaching is needed beyond philosophy. Thomas answers that human beings are directed to an end beyond the grasp of reason, and that even what reason can discover about God would be known only by a few, after a long time and with many errors; a teaching revealed by God was therefore necessary for salvation [[cite:need]]. Theology argues from the articles of faith and treats Scripture as its proper authority, but it also uses philosophy, because "grace does not destroy nature but perfects it" [[cite:reason]]. The second question gives five ways of proving that God exists: from motion, from efficient causes, from possibility and necessity, from the degrees found in things, and from the governance of the world [[cite:ways]].

In the Third Part Thomas treats the eucharist with the word that stands in the creed of the Fourth Lateran Council [[cite:lateran]]. The whole substance of the bread is changed into the whole substance of Christ's body, a change that "can be called transubstantiation", while the appearances of bread and wine remain [[cite:eucharist]].

He did not finish. Bartholomew of Capua testified in 1319 that after saying Mass at Naples Thomas wrote and dictated nothing more, leaving the Summa in the treatise on penance. Pressed by his companion Reginald, he answered that all he had written seemed to him straw beside what he had seen and what had been revealed to him [[cite:straw]]. Tocco has a similar story [[cite:tocco-end]]. Summoned to a council, Thomas fell ill on the road and died on 7 March 1274 [[cite:straw]] [[cite:death]]. The editors of the English translation note that the missing questions were supplied afterwards from his commentary on the Sentences [[cite:supplement]].

In 1879 Pope Leo XIII called Thomas "chief and master" of the scholastic doctors, recalled that the Council of Trent laid the Summa on the altar beside Scripture, and asked the Catholic bishops to restore his teaching [[cite:leo]].
