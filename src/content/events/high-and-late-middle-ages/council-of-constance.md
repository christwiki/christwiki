The council met at [[place:constance]] on the summons of John XXIII, the pope of the line begun at Pisa [[cite:session1]]. When he fled in March 1415 it declared that his departure did not dissolve it [[cite:session3]], and on 6 April, in the decree Haec sancta, it declared that it "has its authority directly from Christ" and that everyone, "including also the pope", is bound to obey it in what concerns the faith, the ending of the schism and the reform of the church [[cite:session5]] [[cite:haec-sancta]]. It then deposed two of the three claimants and accepted the resignation of the third (see [[event:western-schism]]). In October 1417, in the decree Frequens, it ordered that general councils be held in future at fixed intervals, in the end every ten years [[cite:frequens]]. A month later [[person:martin-v]] was elected [[cite:election]].

[[person:jan-hus]], a priest and master of [[place:prague]], came to give an account of his faith; [[person:sigismund]], king of the Romans, had issued him a safe-conduct, dated 18 October 1414, which took him under the protection of the empire and ordered that he be let pass, stay and return freely [[cite:safe-conduct]]. Hus reached Constance on 3 November [[cite:arrival]]. Within weeks he was a prisoner, and the Bohemian lord John of Chlum posted protests on the church doors that he had been seized under the king's safe-conduct [[cite:chlum]]. He was heard in public on three days in June 1415 [[cite:hearings]]. Afterwards he wrote that Sigismund's word was as little to be trusted as his safe-conduct [[cite:letter63]], and on 1 July he declared that he would not abjure articles he had never held, nor those taken from his books, lest he offend against the truth [[cite:declaration]].

On 6 July the council condemned thirty articles from his writings, declared him a heretic, degraded him from the priesthood and gave him up to the secular power [[cite:sentence]]. Peter of Mladoňovice, who was there, reports that Hus told the assembly he had come freely, with the safe-conduct of the king then present [[cite:plea]]. A paper crown painted with devils was set on his head, Sigismund ordered him taken away, and he was led to a meadow outside the town. Chained to the stake, he refused a last offer of his life in return for recanting and died singing "Christ, Son of the living God, have mercy on me". His ashes were thrown into the Rhine [[cite:burning]]. In September the council decreed that a safe-conduct given by an emperor or king does not prevent a competent judge from trying a case of heresy [[cite:session19]].

The two decrees on councils remain contested. Martin V said at the last session that he approved what the council, acting as a council, had decided in matters of faith [[cite:session45]]. Pius II forbade appeals from a pope to a council [[cite:execrabilis]], and the First Vatican Council rejected the idea that a council is an authority above the Roman pontiff [[cite:vatican1]]. The modern editor of the decrees notes that the standing of the sessions held before Martin's election is still disputed [[cite:intro]]. In 1999 [[person:john-paul-ii]] praised the moral courage of Hus and expressed "deep regret" for the "cruel death" inflicted on him [[cite:jp2]].
