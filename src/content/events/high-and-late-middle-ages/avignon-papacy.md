Boniface VIII (see [[event:unam-sanctam]]) was followed by Benedict XI, who died at Perugia in July 1304. The cardinals, shut up there for more than nine months and divided into two parties, agreed to choose a man from beyond the Alps. In June 1305 they elected the archbishop of Bordeaux, who became [[person:clement-v]]. The Florentine chronicler Giovanni Villani tells, as a thing done in secret, that [[person:philip-iv-of-france]] had met the archbishop beforehand and exacted promises. By this election, Villani writes, "the papacy reverted to foreigners, and the court went beyond the mountains" [[cite:election]].

Villani speaks of the pope's court at [[place:avignon]] from 1308 [[cite:court1308]] [[cite:court1310]]. Clement died in 1314 on the road to Bordeaux; Villani, no friend of the Gascon pope, calls him greedy for money and a simoniac [[cite:clement-death]]. His successor John XXII, elected in 1316 after a vacancy of two years, had been bishop of Avignon [[cite:john]].

The poet Petrarch, living there, called the city "the Babylon of the West". "Here reign the successors of the poor fishermen of Galilee," he wrote; "they have strangely forgotten their origin." He described men loaded with gold and clad in purple, palaces and fortified heights, and parchments turned by a leaden seal into nets to catch Christians [[cite:petrarch]].

[[person:catherine-of-siena]], a Dominican tertiary [[cite:scudder-life]], wrote to [[person:gregory-xi]] in a different tone, as a daughter to a father. "Come, come, and resist no more the will of God that calls you," she wrote; the vicar of Christ should rest in his own place, the seat of Peter [[cite:come]]. "Be manly in my sight, and not timorous." He should return, raise the standard of the cross and give the church good priests [[cite:manly]]. "Up, father, like a man!" [[cite:up]]. In 1376 Florence, then at war with the pope, sent her to Avignon as its envoy [[cite:scudder-life]] [[cite:scudder-events]].

Gregory returned to Italy and entered [[place:rome]] in January 1377. He died in March 1378, and by September of that year the cardinals had declared the election of his successor invalid and chosen another pope [[cite:scudder-events]]; see [[event:western-schism]].
