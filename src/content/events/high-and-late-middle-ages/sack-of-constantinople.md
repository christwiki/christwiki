Envoys of the French barons who had taken the cross hired a fleet from [[place:venice]]. The doge, [[person:enrico-dandolo]], offered ships and provisions for 85,000 marks, and fifty galleys in return for half of all conquests [[cite:treaty]]. Too few crusaders came, and 34,000 marks stayed unpaid. The doge offered to defer the debt if the army helped Venice retake Zara [[cite:debt]]. A Cistercian abbot forbade the attack in the pope's name, because the people of Zara were Christians. The city was taken all the same, and [[person:innocent-iii]] afterwards absolved the barons [[cite:zara]].

At Zara came an offer on behalf of Alexius, son of a dethroned emperor. If the army restored him, he would bring the empire back to obedience to Rome, from which it had long been separated (see [[event:east-west-schism]]), pay 200,000 marks and support the crusade [[cite:offer]]. The fleet sailed to [[place:constantinople]], and Alexius was crowned beside his father [[cite:crowned]]. Then Greeks and crusaders fell out. A courtier, Mourzuphles, seized the throne and strangled Alexius, and the clergy with the army declared the war just, because of the murder and because the Greeks had withdrawn from obedience to Rome [[cite:murder]]. Robert of Clari, a knight in the ranks, reports that the bishops called the Greeks enemies of God and promised absolution, in the pope's name, to all who attacked them [[cite:sermons]].

Franks and Venetians agreed beforehand how to divide booty and empire [[cite:pact]]. On a Monday in April 1204 they scaled the walls. Mourzuphles fled in the night, and men in the crusader camp set a fire that burned through the next day [[cite:capture]]. Villehardouin writes that so much booty had never been won in any city since the creation of the world. It was shared between Franks and Venetians, and Baldwin of Flanders was elected emperor [[cite:booty]].

The Byzantine historian Niketas Choniates tells what this meant inside the city: icons trampled, relics thrown out, the consecrated bread and wine spilled, the altar of Hagia Sophia broken up and shared out, pack animals led into the sanctuary to carry off its silver, a prostitute seated on the patriarch's throne [[cite:choniates]]. Innocent III wrote to his legate in July 1205 that the crusaders had spared neither age nor sex, had violated women, nuns among them, and had stripped the churches of altar plate, crosses and relics. The Greek church, he wrote, now detested the Latins "more than dogs", and with reason [[cite:innocent]].

The conquest stood. Innocent's council of 1215 spoke of the Greeks as returning to Roman obedience and ranked a patriarch of Constantinople, who was to receive his pallium from the pope, next after Rome [[cite:lateran]]. In 2004 [[person:john-paul-ii]], receiving the Ecumenical Patriarch [[person:bartholomew-i]], named April 1204 among the memories that weigh on the two churches and said he shared Innocent's indignation and sorrow [[cite:jp2]].
