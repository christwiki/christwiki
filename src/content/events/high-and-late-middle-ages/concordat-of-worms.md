The popes since [[person:gregory-vii]] had condemned the investiture of bishops by lay hands ([[event:investiture-controversy]]), and yet, as Pope Paschal II wrote in February 1111, a bishop-elect in the German kingdom was not consecrated until the king had invested him [[cite:paschal-feb]]. Paschal proposed a way out. Bishops, his privilege says, had become servants of the royal court because they held cities, duchies, counties, mints and tolls from the crown; these were to go back to the king, who would give up investiture [[cite:paschal-feb]] [[cite:ekkehard-1111]]. The chronicler Ekkehard of Aura reports that when the terms were read out in [[place:rome]] the princes protested furiously at the loss to the churches and to themselves, and that the pope was then kept under guard by the king's men [[cite:ekkehard-1111]]. A second privilege, of April, granted [[person:henry-v-emperor]] investiture with staff and ring [[cite:paschal-apr]].

The settlement came eleven years later under Pope [[person:callixtus-ii]]. Ekkehard describes a general assembly at [[place:worms]] in September 1122, where the princes worked for a week or more to make peace. The pope's legates, led by the bishop of Ostia, received the emperor and his army back into communion. Two charters were exchanged and read out in a field by the Rhine because of the size of the crowd, and the bishop of Ostia celebrated mass and gave the emperor the kiss of peace and communion [[cite:ekkehard-1122]].

In his charter Henry gives up "all investiture through ring and staff", grants canonical election and free consecration in all the churches of his kingdom and empire, and promises to restore the possessions of the Roman church and of other churches lost in the conflict [[cite:emperor]]. In return Callixtus grants that the bishops and abbots of the German kingdom shall be elected in the emperor's presence, without simony or violence. If an election is disputed, the emperor is to support the party with the better right, on the advice of the metropolitan and the bishops of the province. The one elected receives the regalia from the emperor by the sceptre and owes him what is due for them. In the other parts of the empire the bishop is consecrated first and receives the regalia within six months [[cite:pope]] [[cite:ekkehard-charters]].

By these terms investiture with ring and staff no longer passes through the ruler's hands [[cite:emperor]], while the regalia, the cities, counties, tolls and other rights that a bishop holds from the crown, still do [[cite:paschal-feb]] [[cite:pope]]. The council that Callixtus held at the Lateran in 1123 repeated the rules of the reform: no one is to be ordained for money, no one consecrated bishop without canonical election, and no lay person is to dispose of the church's affairs or goods [[cite:lateran]].
