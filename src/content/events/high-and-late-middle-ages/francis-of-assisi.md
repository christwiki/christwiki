dating: |
  The date is that of the journey to Rome, and no early source gives its year. Celano counts by years from Francis's conversion: he finished repairing the Portiuncula in the third year, and twenty years had passed when he died in October 1226 [[cite:years]]. That puts the hearing of the Gospel about 1208 or 1209 and the approval by Innocent III soon after.

  Editors have chosen between two years. Paschal Robinson, *The Writings of Saint Francis of Assisi* (1906), p. 25, gives 23 April 1209 and notes that Paul Sabatier's *Vie de S. François d'Assise* put the event in the summer of 1210. A. G. Ferrers Howell, *The Lives of S. Francis of Assisi by Brother Thomas of Celano* (1908), p. xxiii, also gives 1210. The later dates are firm: the bull confirming the Rule is dated 29 November in the eighth year of Honorius III, 1223 [[cite:bull]], and Celano dates the death to Sunday 4 October 1226 [[cite:death]].

Near the end of his life [[person:francis-of-assisi]] described his own beginning. When he was "in sin" the sight of lepers was bitter to him; the Lord led him among them, he showed them mercy, and what had been bitter became sweet. Soon afterwards he "left the world" [[cite:testament]]. His first biographer, Thomas of Celano, writing within three years of his death, tells how he sold cloth and a horse, offered the money to the priest of the ruined church of San Damiano outside [[place:assisi]], and, when his father brought him before the bishop, stripped naked and handed back his clothes [[cite:renounce]].

He repaired San Damiano and, later, the chapel of Saint Mary at the Portiuncula. There, Celano says, he heard the Gospel in which Christ sends out his disciples without gold, wallet, shoes or a second tunic, and cried out that this was what he wanted. He put on one rough tunic with a cord [[cite:gospel]]. Others joined him. Francis writes that no one showed him what to do, but that the Most High revealed to him that he should live "according to the form of the holy Gospel"; he had it written down in a few words, and the pope confirmed it [[cite:testament]]. Celano adds the detail: Francis went to [[place:rome]] with his brothers, and [[person:innocent-iii]] granted their request, blessed them and sent them to preach repentance [[cite:rome]]. Francis named them the Lesser Brothers, the Friars Minor [[cite:name]].

At San Damiano a community of women began under [[person:clare-of-assisi]], a noblewoman of Assisi whom Francis had turned to this life [[cite:clare]].

On 29 November 1223 [[person:honorius-iii]] confirmed the brothers' Rule [[cite:bull]]. It binds them to observe the Gospel in obedience, without property and in chastity, forbids them to accept money or to make any house or place their own, and promises obedience to the pope [[cite:rule]].

Celano reports that two years before his death, at the hermitage of La Verna, Francis saw in a vision a man like a seraph fixed to a cross, and that the marks of nails then appeared in his hands and feet and a wound in his side [[cite:stigmata]]. While ill at San Damiano he composed in Italian the praises of God for brother sun, sister moon and "our sister, the bodily death" [[cite:canticle]]. He died at the Portiuncula in October 1226 [[cite:death]]. [[person:gregory-ix]] canonized him at Assisi less than two years later [[cite:canonized]].

The Rule forbade the brothers to preach in any diocese against its bishop's will, and had them ask the pope for a cardinal to be the order's governor, protector and corrector [[cite:rule-end]]. In his Testament Francis forbade the brothers to seek privileges from the Roman curia or to add glosses to the Rule [[cite:testament]].
