dating: |
  *On the Unity of the Church* belongs to 251, the year from which the Roman list of bishops dates Cornelius [[cite:cornelius]], against whom Novatian was consecrated and whose envoys were turned away at Carthage [[cite:envoys]]. The minutes of the council of eighty-seven bishops give only the day, 1 September [[cite:council]]. It belongs to the dispute with Stephen. Cyprian was sent into exile on 30 August 257 [[cite:acts]], and Stephen was dead before 6 August 258, the day on which his successor Xystus was executed, as Cyprian reported at the time [[cite:xystus]] [[cite:xystus-death]]. The latest 1 September that fits is therefore that of 256, the year usually given; the heading "258" printed with the linked translation cannot be right. Cyprian's exile and death are dated by the consuls of the year in the record of his trial: 30 August 257 and 14 September 258 [[cite:acts]].

  Chapter 4 of the treatise exists in two versions. Maurice Bévenot concluded from the manuscripts that Cyprian wrote both, the one that speaks of a primacy in 251 and the other as a revision during his dispute with Stephen (*St. Cyprian's De Unitate chap. 4 in the Light of the Manuscripts*, Rome, 1937).

In 251 the church at [[place:rome]] chose Cornelius as bishop [[cite:cornelius]]. [[person:eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]] reports that the presbyter [[person:novatian]] held that Christians who had sacrificed could never be received back, and that a synod of sixty bishops condemned him; he quotes a letter of Cornelius which says that Novatian had himself consecrated as a rival by three bishops brought to Rome for the purpose [[cite:novatian]]. Novatian's envoys were turned away at [[place:carthage]] [[cite:envoys]]. [[person:cyprian]], who faced a breakaway group of his own there [[cite:carthage]], then wrote *On the Unity of the Church*.

Christ gave the keys to Peter first, Cyprian argues, although all the apostles received equal power, in order to show that unity begins from one [[bible:Matt 16:18-19]] [[cite:peter]]. The bishops hold that unity in common: "The episcopate is one, each part of which is held by each one for the whole" [[cite:episcopate]]. Whoever leaves the church leaves salvation: "He can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother" [[cite:mother]].

The chapter on Peter survives in two versions. One adds that a primacy is given to Peter and asks whether a man who deserts the chair of Peter can think that he is in the church; the other lacks those sentences, which the editor of the translation linked here prints in his notes and calls spurious [[cite:versions]]. Pope [[person:leo-xiii]], in an encyclical of 1896, quotes the chapter in support of the Catholic teaching that Christ chose Peter and his successors as the principle and centre of the church's unity [[cite:catholic]]. [[person:john-calvin|Calvin]], in the *Institutes*, quotes it to show that Christ gave the keys to one man not to set him above the others but to commend the unity of the church, the other apostles being Peter's equals in honour and power [[cite:reformed]].

Writing to Cornelius, Cyprian called Rome "the throne of Peter" and "the chief church whence priestly unity takes its source" [[cite:carthage]]. Yet he held that baptism given outside the church was void, since there is "no salvation out of the Church", and that converts from heresy must be baptised [[cite:outside]]. [[person:stephen-i]] of Rome ruled that nothing should be changed from what had been handed down: such converts were to be received by the laying on of hands. Cyprian called this an error [[cite:stephen]]. Eusebius quotes Dionysius of Alexandria as saying that Stephen had written that he would not hold communion with the bishops of Cilicia, Cappadocia and Galatia, who followed the same practice [[cite:eusebius]]. On 1 September 256 eighty-seven African bishops voted for Cyprian's position, after he had told them that none of them set himself up as "a bishop of bishops" [[cite:council]]. Firmilian of [[place:caesarea-in-cappadocia|Caesarea in Cappadocia]] attacked Stephen for boasting of his succession from Peter [[cite:firmilian]].

Under the emperor Valerian Cyprian was exiled in August 257 and beheaded near Carthage on 14 September 258 [[cite:acts]]. In 314 the Council of Arles decided against the African custom of rebaptising [[cite:arles]]. His sentences on the one church and the one episcopate have been quoted on opposite sides since, as the appeals of Calvin and Leo XIII show [[cite:reformed]] [[cite:catholic]].
