dating: |
  The *Life* gives ages, not years. Antony was about eighteen or twenty when his parents died and he gave his property away [[cite:family]], about thirty-five when he left the tombs for the deserted fort across the river [[cite:tombs]], and nearly a hundred and five when he died [[cite:death]]. Jerome's Chronicle places his death in the nineteenth year of Constantius, AD 356 [[cite:jerome]]. Counting back, he was born about 251, renounced his property about 270, shut himself in the fort about 285 and came out of it about 305. These years are only as exact as the round ages Athanasius gives. The years shown are those of this first withdrawal, to the tombs near his village and then to the fort by the Nile. The move to the inner mountain, the second place listed, came much later: it followed the persecution under Maximinus and the martyrdom of Peter, bishop of Alexandria [[cite:alexandria]], which Eusebius puts in the ninth year of the persecution, about 311 [[cite:peter]].

[[person:antony-the-great]] was born in Egypt to Christian parents who owned good land. They died when he was about eighteen or twenty and left him with a young sister. Some months later he went into church as the Gospel was being read and heard the words of Jesus to the rich man: "If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me" [[bible:Matt 19:21]]. He took the words as spoken to himself, gave his land to the villagers, sold his goods for the poor, and placed his sister with a community of Christian women [[cite:family]].

[[person:athanasius]] says that there were then few monasteries in Egypt and that no monk yet knew the deep desert; those who wished to live in this way stayed near their own villages. Antony began like them, learning from an old solitary nearby and working with his hands [[cite:village]]. He then moved to the tombs outside the village, where the *Life* describes him beaten by demons and comforted by a vision [[cite:tombs]]. At about thirty-five he crossed the river to a deserted fort and shut himself in, receiving bread twice a year [[cite:fort]]. The *Life* gives the fort no name; Palladius, about 420, calls Antony's settlement by the river [[place:pispir]] [[cite:pispir]].

After nearly twenty years his friends broke down the door. He came out, Athanasius says, in sound health and even temper, healed the sick, reconciled enemies and persuaded many to take up the solitary life, so that "the desert was colonised by monks" [[cite:emerges]]. During the persecution under Maximinus he went to [[place:alexandria]] to serve the confessors [[cite:alexandria]]. Finding himself pressed by visitors, he travelled three days into the inner desert with a party of Saracens and settled on what the *Life* calls the inner mountain [[cite:mountain]], which later tradition names [[place:mount-colzim]]. He died there at a great age [[cite:death]].

The account given here comes from one book, which Athanasius wrote for monks abroad who had asked how Antony lived, and which he offers them as a pattern [[cite:purpose]]. A generation after Antony's death [[person:augustine]] heard at [[place:milan]] how two imperial officials at Trier had come upon the *Life* in a cottage, read it, and given up the emperor's service that same day, and he says that until then Antony's name had been unknown to him [[cite:augustine]].
