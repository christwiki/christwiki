dating: |
  Genesis gives the creation no date, and no source outside the Bible bears on it. The genealogies that follow give each ancestor's age at the birth of his son [[bible:Gen 5:3-32]] [[bible:Gen 11:10-26]], and readers since antiquity have added these up to count the years from Adam. Josephus does so in the first century AD [[cite:josephus]].

  The totals depend on the text used. In the Hebrew text Adam is 130 years old when Seth is born [[bible:Gen 5:3]]; in the Greek Septuagint he is 230, and the two differ in the same way for most of the names that follow [[cite:lxx]]. Christians also differ on whether the days and the genealogies are meant to yield a chronology at all. The timeline places the event first and assigns no year.

Genesis begins: "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth" [[bible:Gen 1:1]]. The earth is at first formless and empty, dark and covered with water [[bible:Gen 1:2]]. Then, over six days, God speaks and things come to be: light; the sky; dry land, seas and plants; sun, moon and stars; sea creatures and birds; land animals and, last, human beings [[bible:Gen 1:3-27]]. Humankind is made in God's image, male and female, and is told to be fruitful and to rule over the other living things [[bible:Gen 1:27-28]]. God sees that everything he has made is "very good" [[bible:Gen 1:31]]. On the seventh day he rests, and he blesses that day and makes it holy [[bible:Gen 2:1-3]].

From its fourth verse the second chapter tells how the first man and woman were made. God forms [[person:adam|the man]] from the dust of the ground and breathes life into him [[bible:Gen 2:7]], plants a garden in Eden and puts him there to cultivate and keep it [[bible:Gen 2:8-15]]. The man may eat from every tree except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; on the day he eats from that one he will die [[bible:Gen 2:16-17]]. He names the animals, but none of them is a partner for him, so God makes [[person:eve|a woman]] from his rib and brings her to him [[bible:Gen 2:18-23]]. The narrator adds that this is why a man leaves his parents and joins his wife, and the two become "one flesh" [[bible:Gen 2:24]].

Later Scripture returns to these chapters often. The Sabbath commandment rests on the six days and the seventh [[bible:Exod 20:8-11]]. A psalm says that the heavens were made by the Lord's word [[bible:Ps 33:6-9]]. [[person:jesus]] quotes both chapters when he is asked about divorce [[bible:Matt 19:3-6]]. John's Gospel opens with the words "In the beginning" and says that all things were made through the Word [[bible:John 1:1-3]], and the letter to the Colossians, which bears [[person:paul]]'s name, says that all things were created through Christ and for him [[bible:Col 1:15-17]].

Christians have read the six days in more than one way since antiquity. [[person:basil-of-caesarea]], preaching on Genesis in the fourth century, took the first day as a day and a night of twenty-four hours [[cite:basil]]. [[person:augustine]], early in the fifth century, wrote that what kind of days these were is very hard, perhaps impossible, to conceive [[cite:augustine]]. The creed of 381, used in both East and West, opens with what these chapters teach: it confesses God as "maker of heaven and earth and of all things visible and invisible" [[cite:creed]].
