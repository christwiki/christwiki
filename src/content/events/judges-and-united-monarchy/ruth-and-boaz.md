  The book dates its story only to "the days when the judges judged" [[bible:Ruth 1:1]]. Its closing genealogy makes Boaz the great-grandfather of David [[bible:Ruth 4:21-22]], who became king about 1010 BC ([[event:david-becomes-king]]). Three generations before David points to the late twelfth or early eleventh century BC. The year shown is a round figure, not a date given by any source.

  When the book itself was written is debated. It explains the handing over of a sandal as a custom of "former time" [[bible:Ruth 4:7]], so it looks back on the events from some distance. Robert L. Hubbard, *The Book of Ruth* (1988), argues for the early monarchy; Frederic W. Bush, *Ruth, Esther* (1996), argues from its language for the years around the exile.

The book of Ruth is set "in the days when the judges judged". A famine drove Elimelech of [[place:bethlehem]] to Moab with his wife [[person:naomi]] and their two sons. Elimelech died there. The sons married Moabite women, Orpah and [[person:ruth]], and after about ten years both sons died as well [[bible:Ruth 1:1-5]].

Naomi set out for home and told her daughters-in-law to go back to their own families. Orpah went. Ruth refused: "where you go, I will go; and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God" [[bible:Ruth 1:6-17]]. The two women reached Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest [[bible:Ruth 1:22]].

Ruth went to glean behind the reapers, a right the law gave to the poor and the foreigner [[bible:Lev 19:9-10]]. She came by chance to the field of [[person:boaz]], a wealthy relative of Elimelech. He had heard what she had done for Naomi, and he told his men to leave grain for her [[bible:Ruth 2:1-16]]. Later Naomi sent Ruth by night to the threshing floor where Boaz was winnowing barley. There she asked him to act as the family's near kinsman, the relative who had the right to redeem its land [[bible:Ruth 3:1-13]] [[bible:Lev 25:25]].

At the town gate, before ten elders, Boaz offered that right to a closer relative. The man gave it up when he heard that Ruth, the dead man's widow, came with the land, "to raise up the name of the dead on his inheritance". Boaz then bought Elimelech's land and took Ruth as his wife [[bible:Ruth 4:1-10]]. She bore a son, Obed, and the women of the town said, "A son is born to Naomi" [[bible:Ruth 4:13-17]].

The book ends with a genealogy: Obed was the father of Jesse, and Jesse the father of [[person:david]] [[bible:Ruth 4:17-22]]. Deuteronomy shuts Moabites out of the assembly of Israel [[bible:Deut 23:3]]; this book makes a Moabite woman the great-grandmother of King David. Matthew's genealogy of [[person:jesus]] names her, "Boaz became the father of Obed by Ruth" [[bible:Matt 1:5]], and Luke's runs through Boaz, Obed and Jesse as well [[bible:Luke 3:32]].
