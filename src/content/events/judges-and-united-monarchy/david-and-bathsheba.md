dating: |
  Samuel gives no year. The story is set in David's years at Jerusalem, during the war with Ammon [[bible:2 Sam 10:1-7]] [[bible:2 Sam 11:1]]; those years run from about 1003 to about 970 BC, reckoned as [[event:david-becomes-king|David becomes king]] explains.

  The next generation narrows the span. Solomon was born to Bathsheba after the first child had died [[bible:2 Sam 12:24]]. He reigned forty years [[bible:1 Kings 11:42]], and his son Rehoboam was forty-one at his own accession [[bible:1 Kings 14:21]], so on these figures Rehoboam was born about a year before Solomon became king. Solomon was therefore already a father when he became king about 970 BC, which puts his own birth in the 980s BC at the latest, and the events told here come before it. The year shown, about 995 BC, is a placing within that span and is not given by any source; it is a conventional placing, marked as traditional like the date of [[event:covenant-with-david|the covenant with David]]. If the forty-year reigns are round numbers, as the note on David's accession allows, the margin is wider.

The story opens during the war with Ammon. In the spring [[person:david]] sent Joab and the army to ravage Ammon and besiege Rabbah, and stayed in [[place:jerusalem]] [[bible:2 Sam 11:1]]. One evening, from the roof of his house, he saw a woman bathing. He was told that she was [[person:bathsheba]], daughter of Eliam and wife of [[person:uriah-the-hittite]]. He sent for her and lay with her, and she sent him word that she was pregnant [[bible:2 Sam 11:2-5]].

David recalled Uriah from the siege and told him to go down to his house. Uriah would not eat, drink and lie with his wife while the ark and the army were camped in the field. He slept at the door of the king's house, and again did not go home when David made him drunk [[bible:2 Sam 11:6-13]]. David sent him back with a letter for Joab: Uriah was to be put where the fighting was fiercest and left to be struck down. He was killed under the wall of Rabbah with others of David's men [[bible:2 Sam 11:14-17, 24]]. When Bathsheba had mourned her husband, David brought her to his house. She became his wife and bore a son. The chapter ends: "the thing that David had done displeased Yahweh" [[bible:2 Sam 11:26-27]].

Samuel says that the Lord sent [[person:nathan]] to David. The prophet told him of a rich man with many flocks who took a poor man's one ewe lamb to feed a guest. David said that the man deserved to die. Nathan answered, "You are the man." Because David had killed Uriah and taken his wife, the sword would never leave his house, and what he had done in secret would be done to his own wives in the sight of all Israel [[bible:2 Sam 12:1-12]]. David said, "I have sinned against Yahweh." Nathan replied that the Lord had put away his sin and that he would not die, but that the child would [[bible:2 Sam 12:13-14]]. The child fell ill, and David fasted and lay on the ground; on the seventh day it died [[bible:2 Sam 12:15-18]]. Bathsheba bore David another son, [[person:solomon]] [[bible:2 Sam 12:24-25]].

Later in the book David's son Absalom drives him out of Jerusalem and takes his father's concubines in a tent on the palace roof, "in the sight of all Israel" [[bible:2 Sam 15:13-14]] [[bible:2 Sam 16:21-22]].

The biblical writers treat the episode differently. Kings judges that David did what was right all his life "except only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite" [[bible:1 Kings 15:5]]. Chronicles tells of the siege of Rabbah, with David remaining in Jerusalem, and passes straight to the city's capture [[bible:1 Chr 20:1-3]]. The heading of Psalm 51 assigns that prayer of repentance to David "when Nathan the prophet came to him" [[bible:Ps 51:1-4]]. Matthew's genealogy of Jesus names Solomon's mother only as the woman who had been Uriah's wife [[bible:Matt 1:6]].
