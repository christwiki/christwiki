dating: |
  Samuel sets the oracle after David had moved into his house of cedar in Jerusalem and had been given "rest from all his enemies" [[bible:2 Sam 7:1-2]]. That puts it after the capture of the city about 1003 BC ([[event:david-takes-jerusalem]]) and before David's death about 970 BC, dates reckoned back from later reigns that Assyrian records fix ([[event:david-becomes-king]]). No year is given, and the books of Samuel are not arranged strictly in order of time. The year shown, about 1000 BC, is a conventional placing early in the Jerusalem years.

  A date late in the reign is also possible. The cedar house was built by craftsmen sent by Hiram of Tyre [[bible:2 Sam 5:11]]. Josephus says that the temple was begun in Hiram's eleventh year [[cite:hiram-antiquities]] or, where he cites Tyrian records, his twelfth [[cite:hiram-apion]]; Kings puts it in Solomon's fourth year [[bible:1 Kings 6:1]]. If those figures are right, Hiram came to the throne only seven or eight years before Solomon did, and the house and the oracle belong to David's last decade.

When [[person:david]] was settled in his palace at [[place:jerusalem]], he said to the prophet [[person:nathan]] that he lived in a house of cedar while the ark of God stayed in a tent. Nathan told him to do what he had in mind [[bible:2 Sam 7:1-3]]. That night, the account says, the word of the Lord came to Nathan with a different answer. The Lord had moved about in a tent since the exodus and had never asked any leader of Israel for a house of cedar [[bible:2 Sam 7:4-7]].

The oracle then turns on the word "house". The Lord had taken David from following the sheep to be prince over Israel, and now "Yahweh will make you a house". After David's death a son of his will succeed him and will build a house for the Lord's name. "I will be his father, and he will be my son." If he does wrong he will be punished, but the Lord's favour will not be taken from him as it was from [[person:saul]]. "Your house and your kingdom will be made sure forever before you. Your throne will be established forever" [[bible:2 Sam 7:8-16]].

The chapter does not use the word covenant. David's last words do: "he has made with me an everlasting covenant" [[bible:2 Sam 23:5]]. In Psalm 89 the Lord says, "I have made a covenant with my chosen one, I have sworn to David, my servant" [[bible:Ps 89:3-4]]. The texts differ on whether the promise has conditions. Samuel and Psalm 89 say that the sins of David's sons will be punished but the promise will stand [[bible:Ps 89:30-37]]. Psalm 132 and the prayer of [[person:solomon]] in Kings make the throne depend on their obedience [[bible:Ps 132:11-12]] [[bible:1 Kings 8:25]]. Chronicles repeats the oracle without the clause about punishment [[bible:1 Chr 17:11-14]].

Kings explains by this promise why David's family kept part of the kingdom when it was torn from Solomon's son [[bible:1 Kings 11:11-13]]. Psalm 89 laments a time when the king's crown lay in the dust, and asks where the Lord's sworn faithfulness to David has gone [[bible:Ps 89:38-49]]. Prophets spoke of a king still to come from David's family: a child on David's throne, a shoot from the stock of Jesse, a righteous Branch [[bible:Isa 9:6-7]] [[bible:Isa 11:1-2]] [[bible:Jer 23:5-6]].

The New Testament applies the promise to [[person:jesus]]. In Luke the angel tells Mary that God will give her son "the throne of his father, David", and that his kingdom will have no end [[bible:Luke 1:32-33]]. Peter says at Pentecost that David knew of God's oath and spoke of the resurrection of the Christ [[bible:Acts 2:29-32]]. Paul calls Jesus a descendant of David "according to the flesh" [[bible:Rom 1:3]], and Hebrews quotes "I will be to him a Father" as said of the Son [[bible:Heb 1:5]]. Augustine, in the early fifth century, argued that the promise was not fulfilled in Solomon and pointed to Christ [[cite:augustine]].
