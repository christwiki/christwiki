In Luke, when the messengers of [[person:john-the-baptist|John]] have gone, [[person:jesus]] speaks to the crowds about John: a prophet and much more than a prophet. "This is he of whom it is written", he says, with the words about the messenger [[bible:Luke 7:24-27]]. Luke remarks that all the people and the tax collectors, who had been baptised with John's baptism, declared God to be just, and that the Pharisees and the lawyers, who had not been baptised by him, rejected God's purpose [[bible:Luke 7:29-30]].

The quotation has the same wording as in Matthew [[bible:Luke 7:27; Matt 11:10]]. The passage in Malachi, and the form in which the Gospels have the words, are described under [[quotation:mal-3-1-in-matt-11-10]].
