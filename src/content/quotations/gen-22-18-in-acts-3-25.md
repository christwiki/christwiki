At the end of his speech in Solomon's porch [[person:peter]] tells his hearers that they are the children of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with their fathers, "saying to Abraham", and the promise follows. God raised up his servant, he says, and sent him to them first, to bless them [[bible:Acts 3:11-12, 25-26]]. The fathers are "our fathers" in the World English Bible and "your" ancestors in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead; those words are left out above [[bible:Acts 3:25]].

Genesis has the promise more than once. In the first, to Abram, "all the families of the earth" are to be blessed in him [[bible:Gen 12:1-3]]. After the binding of [[person:isaac]] it is all the nations of the earth, and the offspring of [[person:abraham]] is named [[bible:Gen 22:9-18]]. The same is said to Isaac [[bible:Gen 26:1-4]]. The quotation in Acts has the families of the first and the offspring of the others [[bible:Acts 3:25]].

The translations differ on Genesis 22:18, and its words are not given above. In the World English Bible the nations "will be blessed by" Abraham's offspring. In the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition they gain blessing for themselves by it [[bible:Gen 22:18]]. The ancient Greek translation has "in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed" [[cite:lxx]].

[[person:paul]] quotes the promise to Abraham in Galatians, and argues from the word "offspring" [[bible:Gal 3:8, 16]].
