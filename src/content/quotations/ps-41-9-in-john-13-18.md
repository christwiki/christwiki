In John, when [[person:jesus]] has washed the disciples' feet and taken his place again, he says that he is not speaking of all of them. He knows whom he has chosen, but the Scripture is to be fulfilled, and he quotes a line about one who eats bread with him [[bible:John 13:12, 18]]. He adds that he tells them before it happens, so that when it happens they may believe [[bible:John 13:19]]. After this he says that one of them will betray him [[bible:John 13:21]]. In Mark's account of the supper Jesus says that the betrayer is one who is eating with him, without a quotation [[bible:Mark 14:18]].

In the psalm a man lies ill. His enemies ask when he will die, and his own close friend, whom he trusted and who shared his bread, has lifted his heel against him [[bible:Ps 41:4-9]]. The speaker has said, "I have sinned against you", and he asks to be raised up so that he may repay them [[bible:Ps 41:4, 10]].

John's quotation takes the line about the bread and the heel and leaves out the words about the friend who was trusted [[bible:Ps 41:9; John 13:18]]. The translations word the line about the bread differently. Where the World English Bible has "bread with me" in both passages, the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has "my bread" [[bible:Ps 41:9; John 13:18]].
