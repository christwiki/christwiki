In John's account of the evening of the supper, [[person:jesus]] tells the disciples that the world will hate them as it hated him, and that those who saw his works have hated both him and his Father. This happened, he says, so that the word written "in their law" may be fulfilled [[bible:John 13:2, 5; 15:18-25]]. The words are found in the Psalms [[bible:Ps 35:19; 69:4]]. In another place John has Jesus cite a psalm as written "in your law" [[bible:John 10:34; Ps 82:6]].

Two psalms have the phrase, and in each the speaker prays to be rescued [[bible:Ps 35:17; 69:1]]. In Psalm 35 he asks that his enemies may not rejoice over him, and that those who hate him without a cause may not wink their eyes. In Psalm 69 he says that those who hate him without a cause are more than the hairs of his head [[bible:Ps 35:19]] [[bible:Ps 69:4]]. John's sentence names neither psalm [[bible:John 15:25]].

John quotes Psalm 69 elsewhere: when Jesus drives the traders out of the temple, the disciples remember its line about zeal for God's house [[bible:John 2:14-17; Ps 69:9]]. In the same psalm the speaker says that God knows his foolishness and that his sins are not hidden [[bible:Ps 69:5]].
