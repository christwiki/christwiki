Matthew tells how [[person:jesus]], coming near to [[place:jerusalem]], sends two disciples to fetch a donkey and her colt, and says that all this was done to fulfil what was spoken through the prophet [[bible:Matt 21:1-5]]. He does not name the prophet. The opening words, an order to tell the daughter of Zion, match a line of Isaiah [[bible:Isa 62:11]]. The rest is from the book of Zechariah, where the verse opens with a call to her to rejoice [[bible:Zech 9:9]]. John quotes the passage more briefly for the same event, and adds that the disciples did not understand these things at first [[bible:John 12:14-16]].

In Zechariah the poem has two lines for the animal: "riding on a donkey, even on a colt, the foal of a donkey" [[bible:Zech 9:9]]. In Matthew the disciples bring the donkey and the colt, and Jesus sits "on them". In Mark and in John there is one animal [[bible:Matt 21:7; Mark 11:7; John 12:14]]. Matthew's quotation leaves out the line that calls the king "righteous, and having salvation" in the World English Bible, "triumphant and victorious" in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead [[bible:Zech 9:9; Matt 21:5]].

In the next verse of Zechariah chariot and horse are cut off: by a speaker who says "I" in the World English Bible; by the king, "he", in the ancient Greek translation and in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition. The battle bow is cut off too [[bible:Zech 9:10]] [[cite:lxx]]. The verse goes on to peace for the nations and a rule from sea to sea [[bible:Zech 9:10]].
