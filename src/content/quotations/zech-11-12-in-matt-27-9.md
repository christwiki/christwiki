Matthew tells how [[person:judas-iscariot|Judas]], seeing that [[person:jesus]] is condemned, brings the thirty pieces of silver back to the chief priests and elders, throws them down in the sanctuary and hangs himself. The priests say that the price of blood may not go into the treasury, and buy the potter's field with it as a burial place for strangers. Matthew says that what was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was then fulfilled [[bible:Matt 27:3-10]].

Thirty pieces of silver are in the book of Zechariah. There the speaker, who has been told to shepherd a flock marked for slaughter, asks for his wages and is weighed thirty pieces of silver ("shekels" in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition), and at the Lord's word he throws them, in the house of the Lord [[bible:Zech 11:4, 12-13]]. Where the money is thrown, the versions differ: "to the potter" in the World English Bible, "into the furnace" in the ancient Greek translation [[cite:lxx]], "into the treasury" in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead [[bible:Zech 11:13]].

[[person:jeremiah]], whom Matthew names, goes down to a potter's house, is told to buy a potter's jar and to break it in the valley of the son of Hinnom, and buys a field at [[place:anathoth]] for silver [[bible:Jer 18:1-4]] [[bible:Jer 19:1-2, 10-11]] [[bible:Jer 32:6-9]].

Acts tells of the field differently. Judas himself obtains a field with the reward of his wickedness, falls headlong and bursts open, and the field comes to be called the field of blood. [[person:peter]]'s speech there quotes two passages from the Psalms about him [[bible:Acts 1:16-20; Ps 69:25; 109:8]].
