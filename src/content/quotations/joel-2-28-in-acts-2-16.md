Acts tells that on the day of Pentecost those who were together in one place were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages, and that some who heard them said they were full of new wine. [[person:peter]], standing with the eleven, answers that these people are not drunk: "this is what has been spoken through the prophet Joel", and five verses follow [[bible:Acts 2:1-4, 13-21]]. He does not say that it is fulfilled. Later in the speech he says that [[person:jesus]], raised and exalted, has poured out what they see and hear [[bible:Acts 2:32-33]].

In the book of Joel the passage follows a promise that the people will eat and be satisfied and praise the name of the Lord [[bible:Joel 2:26-27]]. Afterward, the Lord says, he will pour out his Spirit on all flesh. There will be wonders in the heavens and on the earth before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes, and whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved, for there will be those who escape in Mount Zion and in [[place:jerusalem]] [[bible:Joel 2:28-32]].

Peter's quotation differs from Joel in several places. It begins "in the last days", where Joel has "afterward", in the Hebrew text as modern translations render it and in the ancient Greek translation alike [[bible:Acts 2:17; Joel 2:28]] [[cite:lxx]]. It adds "says God", and after the servants it adds that they will prophesy [[bible:Acts 2:17-18; Joel 2:28-29]]. Its day of the Lord is "great and glorious", as in the Greek translation; the Hebrew text, as modern translations render it, has "great and terrible" [[bible:Acts 2:20; Joel 2:31]] [[cite:lxx]]. And it stops in the middle of the last verse of the passage, before Mount Zion and Jerusalem [[bible:Acts 2:21; Joel 2:32]].

[[person:paul]] quotes the last line, about calling on the name of the Lord, in Romans [[bible:Rom 10:13]].
