John has just said that the unbelief of those who saw the signs of [[person:jesus]] fulfilled a word of Isaiah. He goes on, "For this cause they couldn't believe, for Isaiah said again", and quotes a second passage [[bible:John 12:37-40]]. The word "fulfilled" stands before the first quotation. The second has an introduction of its own, without that word [[bible:John 12:38-39]]. John then says that Isaiah saw his glory and spoke of him [[bible:John 12:41]].

The passage is from the vision in which [[person:isaiah]] sees the Lord on a throne and is sent to his people [[bible:Isa 6:1, 8-10]]. There the prophet is told to make the people's heart fat, their ears heavy and their eyes shut; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has "mind" for "heart" and "dull" for "fat" [[bible:Isa 6:10]]. In the ancient Greek translation the people have closed their own eyes [[cite:lxx]]. In John's form someone else has done it, "He has blinded their eyes and he hardened their heart", and the ears are not mentioned [[bible:John 12:40]].

Matthew quotes the same passage in the Greek form, as a saying of Jesus about the crowds to whom he speaks in parables [[bible:Matt 13:2, 10-15]] [[cite:lxx]]: see [[quotation:isa-6-9-in-matt-13-14]]. John goes on to say that many did believe, even among the rulers [[bible:John 12:42]].
