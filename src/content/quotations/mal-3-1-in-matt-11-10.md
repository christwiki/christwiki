In Matthew, when the disciples whom [[person:john-the-baptist|John]] sent from prison have gone, [[person:jesus]] speaks to the crowds about John. He calls him a prophet and more than a prophet, "for this is he, of whom it is written", and the quotation follows [[bible:Matt 11:2, 7-10]]. Jesus goes on to say that John, if they are willing to receive it, is Elijah who is to come [[bible:Matt 11:14]].

In the book of [[person:malachi]] the Lord says that he sends his messenger to prepare the way "before me", and that the Lord whom the people seek will come suddenly to his temple [[bible:Mal 3:1]]. The book closes with the promise that Elijah the prophet will be sent before the day of the Lord comes [[bible:Mal 4:5]].

In the Gospel the words are said to someone. The messenger goes "before your face" and prepares "your way" [[bible:Matt 11:10]]. A sentence of Exodus has that form, where an angel is sent ahead of those addressed, to keep them on the way [[bible:Exod 23:20]]; in the ancient Greek translation it reads "I send my angel before thy face" [[cite:exodus]].

Mark has the quotation at the opening of his Gospel, in the World English Bible in the same words, and Luke has it in the same speech of Jesus [[bible:Mark 1:2; Luke 7:24-27]]: see [[quotation:mal-3-1-in-mark-1-2]] and [[quotation:mal-3-1-in-luke-7-27]].
