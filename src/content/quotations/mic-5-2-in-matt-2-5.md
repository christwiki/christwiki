Matthew tells how [[person:herod-the-great|Herod]], troubled by the question of the wise men, gathers the chief priests and scribes and asks where the Christ is to be born. They answer, "In [[place:bethlehem]] of Judea", and give the words of the prophet as their reason [[bible:Matt 2:1-6]]. The quotation is spoken by them, not by the narrator, and nothing is said here to be fulfilled [[bible:Matt 2:5-6]].

The words are from the book of Micah, which Matthew does not name [[bible:Mic 5:2; Matt 2:5]]. In Micah, Bethlehem Ephrathah is small among the clans of Judah, and yet one who is to rule in Israel will come from it. The lines that follow speak of him as a shepherd who will be great to the ends of the earth, and of Assyria coming into the land [[bible:Mic 5:2-5]].

Matthew's form differs from Micah's. Where Micah calls Bethlehem small, Matthew has "in no way least" [[bible:Mic 5:2; Matt 2:6]]; the ancient Greek translation, too, has the town few in number [[cite:lxx]]. Matthew has "land of Judah" where Micah has Ephrathah. His last line, about one who will shepherd "my people, Israel", is like what the tribes say to [[person:david]] at Hebron: that the Lord told him he would be shepherd of his people Israel [[bible:2 Sam 5:1-2]].

John reports that some in the crowd objected to [[person:jesus]] on this ground: Scripture said that the Christ comes from Bethlehem, and he was from Galilee [[bible:John 7:40-42]].
