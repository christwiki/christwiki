[[person:paul]] ends his speech in the synagogue at Antioch of Pisidia with a warning: let his hearers beware that what is said in the prophets does not come upon them. The quotation follows, about a work done in their days that they will not believe [[bible:Acts 13:14-16, 38-41]]. He names no prophet. The words are in the book of Habakkuk [[bible:Hab 1:5]].

In Habakkuk the prophet cries out to the Lord about violence and asks how long he will not hear. The answer is that a work is being done in their days that they would not believe if they were told: the Lord is raising up the Chaldeans, who march through the breadth of the earth to seize dwellings that are not theirs [[bible:Hab 1:1-6]].

The opening differs between the versions. The Hebrew text, as modern translations render it, tells the hearers to look among the nations. The ancient Greek translation addresses "ye despisers", who are to wonder "and vanish" [[bible:Hab 1:5]] [[cite:lxx]]. Paul's quotation has scoffers who are to wonder and perish [[bible:Acts 13:41]]. In Habakkuk the World English Bible, like the Greek translation and like Acts, has the speaker doing the work; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has a work that "is being done", and those words are left out above [[bible:Hab 1:5; Acts 13:41]] [[cite:lxx]].
