John tells how the soldiers who have crucified [[person:jesus]] divide his garments into four parts, one for each soldier, and cast lots for his coat, which is woven in one piece and which they do not want to tear. He says that this was to fulfil the Scripture, and adds that this is what the soldiers did [[bible:John 19:23-25]]. The other three Gospels, in the translations used here, tell of the dividing and the lots without a quotation [[bible:Mark 15:24; Matt 27:35; Luke 23:34]].

The verse of the psalm has two lines, garments divided and lots cast for clothing [[bible:Ps 22:18]]. John's story has an act for each: the garments are shared out, and the coat is drawn for [[bible:John 19:23-25]].

The psalm is the prayer of a man who is mocked and surrounded, who asks God not to be far off, and who goes on to praise God in the assembly [[bible:Ps 22:6-8, 16-19, 22-24]]. Its opening words are those that Jesus cries from the cross in Mark and in Matthew [[bible:Ps 22:1; Mark 15:34; Matt 27:46]].

[[person:justin-martyr|Justin]], writing about AD 155–165, tells Trypho that the psalm speaks of the cross and that those who crucified Jesus parted his garments by lot, and he reproaches him: "this very Psalm you maintain does not refer to Christ" [[cite:justin]].
