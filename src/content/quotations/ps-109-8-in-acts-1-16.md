This is the second of two quotations that [[person:peter]] gives from the Psalms after saying that the Scripture about [[person:judas-iscariot|Judas]] had to be fulfilled [[bible:Acts 1:15-16, 20]]. From it he draws what is to be done: one of the men who went about with them, from the baptism of John to the day [[person:jesus]] was taken up, must become a witness with them of his resurrection. Two are put forward, lots are cast, and Matthias is counted with the eleven apostles [[bible:Acts 1:21-26]].

In Psalm 109 the line stands among curses on one man: may his days be few, may another take his office, may his children be fatherless [[bible:Ps 109:8-9]]. The translations differ on whose curses they are. The World English Bible gives them as the words of the speaker, who has been repaid with hatred for his love. The New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, introduces them with "They say", as the words of his accusers [[bible:Ps 109:4-6]].

In the ancient Greek translation the office is that "of overseer" [[cite:lxx]], and the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has that word in Acts as well [[bible:Acts 1:20]]. The first of Peter's two quotations has its own entry: [[quotation:ps-69-25-in-acts-1-16]].
