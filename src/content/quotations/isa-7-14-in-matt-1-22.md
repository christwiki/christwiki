Matthew tells how an angel explains to [[person:joseph-of-nazareth|Joseph]] in a dream that [[person:mary]] has conceived by the Holy Spirit, and says that all this happened to fulfil what the Lord had spoken through the prophet [[bible:Matt 1:20-23]]. He does not name the prophet. The words are those of the sign that [[person:isaiah]] announces to King [[person:ahaz]] [[bible:Isa 7:10-14]].

In Isaiah the sign is given when two kings have gone up to attack [[place:jerusalem]], and it has a term: before the child knows how to refuse evil and choose good, the land of those two kings will be forsaken [[bible:Isa 7:1-2, 14-16]]. Matthew applies the name Immanuel, which he translates "God with us", to the child whom Joseph is told to name [[person:jesus]] [[bible:Matt 1:21-23]].

The ancient Greek translation of Isaiah calls the mother "a virgin" [[cite:lxx]], and Matthew's quotation has that word [[bible:Matt 1:23]]. Whether "virgin" is the right rendering was disputed early. In the *Dialogue* of [[person:justin-martyr|Justin]], written about AD 155–165, the Jew Trypho answers that the Scripture has "the young woman" and that the whole prophecy refers to [[person:hezekiah]] [[cite:trypho]]. [[person:irenaeus]], about AD 180, reports that Theodotion and Aquila, whom he calls Jewish proselytes, translated "a young woman", and he argues against them [[cite:irenaeus]]. Translations still differ. The World English Bible has "the virgin" in Isaiah; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has "the young woman" [[bible:Isa 7:14]].
