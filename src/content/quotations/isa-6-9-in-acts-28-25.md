Acts ends in [[place:rome]]. [[person:paul]], living there with a soldier who guards him, calls together the leaders of the Jews, and on an appointed day speaks to those who come to his lodging, from morning until evening, about the kingdom of God and about [[person:jesus]], from the law of Moses and from the prophets. Some are persuaded and some do not believe. As they leave, disagreeing among themselves, Paul says one thing more: the Holy Spirit spoke rightly through Isaiah the prophet, and the passage about hearing without understanding follows. He adds that the salvation of God is sent to the Gentiles, and that they will listen [[bible:Acts 28:16-17, 23-28, 30-31]].

The translations differ on whose fathers the words were spoken to, and the introduction is left out of the words given above. The World English Bible has "our fathers"; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has "your" [[bible:Acts 28:25]].

The passage is from the vision in which [[person:isaiah]] is sent to his people [[bible:Isa 6:8-10]]. As in Matthew, the quotation has the people's heart grown dull and their eyes closed by themselves, which is the form of the ancient Greek translation [[bible:Acts 28:27; Matt 13:15]] [[cite:lxx]]. The passage, and the difference between the Hebrew and the Greek, are described under [[quotation:isa-6-9-in-matt-13-14]]. John quotes it too: [[quotation:isa-6-10-in-john-12-39]].
