In the speech at Pentecost [[person:peter]] says that God raised [[person:jesus]] of Nazareth, whom they had crucified, because it was not possible that death should hold him. "For David says concerning him", and four verses of a psalm follow [[bible:Acts 2:14, 22-28]]. Peter then argues from the tomb. [[person:david]] died and was buried, and his tomb is with them to this day; being a prophet, he spoke of the resurrection of the Christ, whose soul was not left in Hades [[bible:Acts 2:29-31]].

In the psalm a speaker who takes refuge in God says that he keeps the Lord always before him and will not be moved, that his body rests secure, that God will not give him up to Sheol, and that God shows him the path of life [[bible:Ps 16:1, 8-11]].

The words of the psalm are not given above, because the translations differ on the line that Peter's argument turns on. The World English Bible has "neither will you allow your holy one to see corruption". The New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has "the Pit" where that has "corruption" [[bible:Ps 16:10]]. The ancient Greek translation has "to see corruption" [[cite:lxx]], and in Acts the Holy One is not to see decay, or corruption [[bible:Acts 2:27]]. In the verse before, where Acts and the Greek have the tongue rejoicing, the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition has the soul in the psalm [[bible:Ps 16:9; Acts 2:26]] [[cite:lxx]].

Paul uses the same line in his speech at Antioch of Pisidia: [[quotation:ps-16-10-in-acts-13-35]].
