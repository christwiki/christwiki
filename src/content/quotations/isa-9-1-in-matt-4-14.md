Matthew tells how [[person:jesus]], after [[person:john-the-baptist|John]] has been arrested, leaves [[place:nazareth]] and settles at [[place:capernaum]] by the lake, "in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali", and says that this was to fulfil what was spoken through [[person:isaiah]] the prophet [[bible:Matt 4:12-16]]. His quotation is shorter than the passage in Isaiah. It keeps the names of the lands and the image of light [[bible:Isa 9:1-2; Matt 4:15-16]].

The oracle in Isaiah sets a "former time", in which Zebulun and Naphtali were brought into contempt, against a latter time of glory [[bible:Isa 9:1]]. Kings tells how, in the days of Pekah, Tiglath-pileser of Assyria took Galilee and "all the land of Naphtali" and carried the people away to Assyria [[bible:2 Kings 15:29]].

The lines that follow in Isaiah speak of a yoke that is broken and of a child born to sit on the throne of [[person:david]] [[bible:Isa 9:4-7]]. Matthew's quotation stops before them. The next thing he tells is that Jesus began to preach that the kingdom of heaven was at hand [[bible:Matt 4:16-17]].

The translations of Isaiah differ in two places. For the latter time the World English Bible has a past tense, "he has made it glorious", where the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, has a future. In the next verse that translation has "deep darkness" where the World English Bible, like Matthew, has the "shadow of death" [[bible:Isa 9:1-2; Matt 4:16]].
