In John, at the feast of the Dedication in [[place:jerusalem]], [[person:jesus]] says, "I and the Father are one", and the Jews take up stones to stone him: for blasphemy, they say, because he, being a man, makes himself God. He answers with a question: "Isn't it written in your law, 'I said, you are gods?'" If those to whom the word of God came were called gods, and the Scripture cannot be broken, how can they say that he whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world blasphemes when he says, "I am the Son of God"? [[bible:John 10:22-23, 30-36]]

The line is from a psalm, which is here called law [[bible:Ps 82:6; John 10:34]]. In the psalm God judges among the gods. He charges them with judging unjustly and showing partiality to the wicked. They are called gods and sons, or children, of the Most High, and are told that they will nevertheless die like men [[bible:Ps 82:1-2, 6-7]]. The psalm gives them no other name [[bible:Ps 82:1-8]]. In the argument in John they are those "to whom the word of God came" [[bible:John 10:35]].

Jesus quotes the first half of the verse. The second half calls them sons, or children, of the Most High [[bible:Ps 82:6; John 10:34]].
