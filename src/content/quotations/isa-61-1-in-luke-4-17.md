Luke tells how [[person:jesus]] comes to [[place:nazareth]], where he had been brought up, goes into the synagogue on the Sabbath and stands up to read. The book of the prophet Isaiah is given to him and he finds the place. When he has read he gives the book back, sits down and says: "Today, this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing" [[bible:Luke 4:16-21]]. Here the words about fulfilment are spoken by Jesus, of what he has just read. Luke goes on to tell that those in the synagogue wondered at his words, and in the end were filled with anger and drove him out of the town [[bible:Luke 4:22, 28-30]].

The passage is from Isaiah 61, where a speaker says that the Spirit of the Lord is on him and that the Lord has anointed him and sent him to bring good news, to bind up the broken-hearted and to proclaim liberty to captives, the year of the Lord's favour, "and the day of vengeance of our God" [[bible:Isa 61:1-2]]. The reading in Luke ends with the year of the Lord and does not have the day of vengeance [[bible:Luke 4:19; Isa 61:2]].

The reading has the "recovery of sight to the blind" of the ancient Greek translation, where the Hebrew text, as modern translations render it, speaks of release for prisoners [[cite:lxx]] [[bible:Isa 61:1; Luke 4:18]]. It also has a line that Isaiah 61 does not have, about setting free the oppressed; a line of Isaiah 58 speaks of letting the oppressed go free [[bible:Luke 4:18; Isa 61:1-2; 58:6]]. Those who receive the good news in Isaiah are "the humble" in the World English Bible and the oppressed in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition; in Luke they are "the poor" [[bible:Isa 61:1; Luke 4:18]].

The translations of Luke differ in one line, left out of the words given above. The World English Bible has "to heal the broken hearted"; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, does not have it [[bible:Luke 4:18]].
