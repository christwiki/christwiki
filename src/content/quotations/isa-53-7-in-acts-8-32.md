Acts tells how [[person:philip-the-evangelist|Philip]] is sent to the road that goes down from [[place:jerusalem]] to [[place:gaza]], and meets a man of Ethiopia, a eunuch and an official of the queen, sitting in his chariot and reading the prophet Isaiah. Philip asks whether he understands what he is reading. Acts gives the passage, two verses. The official asks whether the prophet says this about himself or about someone else, and Philip, beginning from this Scripture, tells him about [[person:jesus]] [[bible:Acts 8:26-35]]. Of Philip's answer Acts reports only that [[bible:Acts 8:35]].

The verses are from the passage about the servant in Isaiah 53. He is oppressed and does not open his mouth, like a lamb led to the slaughter and a sheep silent before its shearers [[bible:Isa 52:13; 53:7]]. The verse that follows is not given above, because the two translations of Isaiah render it differently. In the World English Bible the servant is "taken away by oppression and judgment", and the verse asks who of his generation considered it; in the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, he is taken away through injustice, and the question is about his future [[bible:Isa 53:8]]. The ancient Greek translation reads "In his humiliation his judgment was taken away" [[cite:lxx]], and the World English Bible has those words in Acts [[bible:Acts 8:33]].

Who the servant is has been read in more than one way; the readings are set out under [[event:prophecies-of-return|the prophecy of the suffering servant]]. Matthew quotes a verse of the same chapter: [[quotation:isa-53-4-in-matt-8-17]].
