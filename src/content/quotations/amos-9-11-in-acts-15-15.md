Acts tells that when the apostles and elders met in [[place:jerusalem]] over the Gentile believers, [[person:peter]] spoke, [[person:barnabas]] and [[person:paul]] reported what God had done among the nations through them, and [[person:james-the-just|James]] answered. Simeon, he says, has told how God first visited the nations to take from them a people for his name. "This agrees with the words of the prophets. As it is written", and the quotation follows. His judgment is that those of the Gentiles who turn to God are not to be troubled [[bible:Acts 15:1-19]].

In the book of [[person:amos]] the Lord says that in that day he will raise up the fallen tent of David and build it as in the days of old, "that they may possess the remnant of Edom, and all the nations who are called by my name". The verses before speak of the sifting of the house of Israel among the nations, and the verses after of Israel planted again on its land [[bible:Amos 9:8-15]].

James's quotation differs from the Hebrew text as modern translations render it. Where that has the remnant of Edom possessed, the ancient Greek translation has "the remnant of men" and all the Gentiles, who "may earnestly seek me" [[bible:Amos 9:12]] [[cite:lxx]]. In Acts, too, the rest of mankind is to seek the Lord [[bible:Acts 15:17]]. The quotation opens "After these things I will return", where Amos has "In that day" [[bible:Acts 15:16; Amos 9:11]].

The translations of Acts differ on how the quotation ends, and the ending is left out of the words given above. The World English Bible ends "says the Lord, who does all these things", and adds that all God's works are known to him from eternity. In the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, the Lord makes these things known "from long ago" [[bible:Acts 15:17-18]].
