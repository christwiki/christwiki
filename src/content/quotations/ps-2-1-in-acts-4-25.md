Acts tells that [[person:peter]] and John, released by the council with threats, went to their own people and reported what had been said to them, and that all raised their voice to God together. Their prayer quotes the opening of a psalm as words that God spoke through [[person:david]], and applies it: in this city [[person:herod-antipas|Herod]] and [[person:pontius-pilate]], with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered against God's holy servant [[person:jesus]], whom he anointed [[bible:Acts 4:13, 21-28]]. The prayer names rulers, Gentiles and peoples, as the psalm names kings, rulers, nations and peoples [[bible:Acts 4:25-27; Ps 2:1-2]].

In the psalm the nations and their kings set themselves against the Lord and his anointed. He who sits in the heavens laughs at them, and says that he has set his king on Zion, his holy hill [[bible:Ps 2:1-6]].

The translations of Acts differ on the words that introduce the quotation, which are left out above. The World English Bible has "by the mouth of your servant, David"; the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, adds that God spoke by the Holy Spirit, and puts the verbs of the quotation in the past [[bible:Acts 4:25-26]].

The decree that follows in the psalm, "You are my son", is quoted in the speech of Paul at Antioch of Pisidia: [[quotation:ps-2-7-in-acts-13-32]].
