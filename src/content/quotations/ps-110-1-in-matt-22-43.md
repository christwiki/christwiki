In Matthew, [[person:jesus]] asks the assembled Pharisees what they think of the Christ: whose son is he? They say, "Of David". He asks how [[person:david]] "in the Spirit" can then call him Lord, quotes the opening of a psalm, and asks: "If then David calls him Lord, how is he his son?" No one was able to answer him [[bible:Matt 22:41-46]]. As Jesus puts the question, David is the speaker of the psalm and the one he calls "my Lord" is the Christ [[bible:Matt 22:42-45]].

In the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition, to which the links here lead, and in the ancient Greek translation the psalm is headed with the name of David; the World English Bible as served for the words quoted here omits the headings of the psalms [[bible:Ps 110:1]] [[cite:lxx]]. In it the Lord tells the one addressed to sit at his right hand until his enemies are made a footstool, promises him rule from Zion among his enemies, and swears that he is a priest for ever in the order of Melchizedek [[bible:Ps 110:1-4]]. The translations differ in two details. In Matthew the World English Bible has the enemies made "a footstool for your feet", and the other has them put "under your feet"; in the psalm the first writes "my Lord" and the second "my lord" [[bible:Matt 22:44; Ps 110:1]].

Mark and Luke tell of the same question: [[quotation:ps-110-1-in-mark-12-36]], [[quotation:ps-110-1-in-luke-20-42]]. In Acts, [[person:peter]] quotes the verse at Pentecost, says that David did not ascend into the heavens, and applies it to Jesus, raised and exalted [[bible:Acts 2:1, 14, 32-36]]. The psalm's verse about Melchizedek is taken up under [[event:melchizedek-blesses-abram|Melchizedek blesses Abram]].
