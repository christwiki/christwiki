In Matthew, [[person:jesus]] asks the assembled Pharisees what they think of the Christ: whose son is he? They say, "Of David". He asks how [[person:david]] "in the Spirit" can then call him Lord, quotes the opening of a psalm, and asks: "If then David calls him Lord, how is he his son?" No one was able to answer him [[bible:Matt 22:41-46]]. The argument takes two things as agreed: that David is the speaker of the psalm, and that the one he calls "my Lord" is the Christ [[bible:Matt 22:42-45]].

The psalm has the name of David in its heading, in the Hebrew and in the ancient Greek translation [[bible:Ps 110:1]] [[cite:lxx]]. In it the Lord tells the one addressed to sit at his right hand until his enemies are made a footstool, promises him rule from Zion among his enemies, and swears that he is a priest for ever in the order of Melchizedek [[bible:Ps 110:1-4]].

Mark and Luke tell of the same question: [[quotation:ps-110-1-in-mark-12-36]], [[quotation:ps-110-1-in-luke-20-42]]. In Acts, [[person:peter]] quotes the verse at Pentecost, says that David did not ascend into the heavens, and applies it to Jesus, raised and exalted [[bible:Acts 2:14, 32-36]]. The psalm's verse about Melchizedek is taken up under [[event:melchizedek-blesses-abram|Melchizedek blesses Abram]].
