# Sources first cited for events c. 1782–1950

Reviewer batch S06. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · dating · src/content/sources/tikhon-messages.md
- **Text:** "his message of 15 February 1922 on famine relief and the removal of church valuables. The dates of 1917 and 1918 are in the Julian calendar."
- **Problem:** The record marks only the 1917–1918 dates as Julian, so a reader takes 15 February 1922 as a civil (Gregorian) date. It is not: the message is dated 15 (28) February 1922, Old Style / New Style, and it answers a decree of the All-Russian Central Executive Committee which the message itself dates 13 (26) February. Read as New Style, the message would precede the decree it protests against. The record also contradicts the event and person entries, which cite "Message of 15 (28) February 1922".
- **Should be:** "his message of 15 (28) February 1922 … All four dates are in the Julian calendar, which the Patriarch went on using after the state changed to the Gregorian in February 1918; the New Style dates are 4 December 1917, 31 December 1917, 1 February 1918 and 28 February 1922." (Also front matter `summary`: "his message of 15 (28) February 1922".)
- **Evidence:** azbyka.ru/otechnik/Tihon_Belavin/poslanie-o-pomoshhi-golodayushhim-i-ob-iz-jatii-tserkovnyh-tsennostej/ — headed "Послание от 15/28 февраля 1922 г.", closing date "15 февраля 1922 г.", text refers to the VTsIK decision of 13/26 February; `src/content/events/modern-and-global/russian-revolution-and-the-church.md` line 47 and `src/content/people/tikhon-of-moscow.md` line 21 both give "15 (28) February 1922".
- **Confidence:** high

### B · completeness · src/content/sources/lepsius-deutschland-und-armenien.md
- **Text:** "Germany was the ally of the Ottoman Empire, and these are the working papers of its diplomats. The selection is the editor's, and the long introduction is his own account."
- **Problem:** The record presents the printed texts as the diplomats' papers, with only the selection being editorial. It omits the best-known fact about this edition: comparison with the originals in the Political Archive of the German Foreign Office (Wolfgang Gust, published at armenocide.net from 2000 and in *Der Völkermord an den Armeniern 1915/16*, 2005; English 2014) showed that many documents were abridged or altered before printing, chiefly to remove passages bearing on German knowledge and co-responsibility; whether the Foreign Office handed Lepsius doctored copies or Lepsius cut them himself is debated. A source record that is asked to state "what is restored rather than preserved" and any "serious authenticity debate" has to say this.
- **Should be:** Add: "The texts as printed are not always the texts in the files. Wolfgang Gust's comparison with the originals in the Foreign Office archive (2000; printed edition 2005) found omissions and changes in many documents, most of them removing passages on Germany's own role; the Foreign Office used the volume in its defence at the peace negotiations. A passage cited from Lepsius should be checked against the original where Gust prints it." Also `written.start` is 1915 while `display` says "Documents of 1914–1918".
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge, confirmed by web search (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Gust; Berghahn's description of W. Gust, ed., *The Armenian Genocide: Evidence from the German Foreign Office Archives*, 2014: Lepsius's volume had text "deleted or manipulated", Gust published "the wording of the original documents … as well as the individual manipulations" on armenocide.net in March 2000).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/jefferson-circular-to-the-governors-1792.md
- **Text:** `edition: "Text of The Papers of Thomas Jefferson, vol. 23, at Founders Online …"` with `url: https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-27-02-0774`
- **Problem:** The edition statement and the link disagree. Founders Online identifiers carry the volume number: `01-27-…` is volume 27 of the Papers (1 September–31 December 1793), whose supplement prints documents omitted from earlier volumes. Volume 23 covers January–May 1792 by date, but this circular is not printed there.
- **Should be:** "Text of The Papers of Thomas Jefferson, vol. 27 (supplement of documents of 1764–1793), at Founders Online …" — confirm the page numbers on the record's "Source" line.
- **Evidence:** Web search: Founders Online catalogues "Circular to the Governors of the States, 1 March 1792" as Jefferson/01-27-02-0774, while documents of March 1792 printed in vol. 23 carry ids 01-23-02-…; the record's note refers to an illustration "in this volume". The page itself returned no text to the fetch tool, so the printed page range was not read.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/sources/catherine-booth-female-ministry.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1859, circa: true, display: "1859, revised later"`; "London, Morgan and Chase, 23 pages, transcribed by the Victorian Women Writers Project of Indiana University, which dates the pamphlet 1859."
- **Problem:** The text read is the revision, not the pamphlet of 1859. The pamphlet of December 1859 was called *Female Teaching: or, The Rev. A. A. Rees versus Mrs. Palmer* (second edition 1861); *Female Ministry; or, Woman's Right to Preach the Gospel* (Morgan and Chase) is the shortened, less polemical version usually dated 1870. The record's own second paragraph says the preface describes it as a revision, so the front-matter date of 1859 and the summary ("Catherine Booth's pamphlet arguing…", with no date) let 1870 wording be quoted as her words of 1859.
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1859, end: 1870, display: "first published 1859 as Female Teaching; this revised text c. 1870"`, and say in the body that quotations give the wording of the revision.
- **Evidence:** Web search: crivoice.org/cbooth.html ("originally published in London in 1859 under the title Female Teaching and republished in 1861 … a less confrontational version was published 1870 under the title Female Ministry"); Salvation Army history blog (historycollections.blogs.sas.ac.uk) dates *Female Ministry* 1870. Library catalogues differ ([1859?]/[1870]), hence medium.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/liddon-life-of-pusey.md
- **Text:** `author: Henry Parry Liddon`; `written: start: 1894, circa: true`; "The four-volume life of Edward Pusey by Henry Parry Liddon."
- **Problem:** Liddon died in September 1890 with the book unfinished. It was edited and completed from his drafts by J. O. Johnston and R. J. Wilson (with W. C. E. Newbolt for the last volume) and came out over five years: volumes 1–2 in 1893, volume 3 in 1894, volume 4 in 1897. The record gives one author and one approximate year and says nothing of the posthumous editing, which matters for weighing the narrative (the fourth volume in particular is largely the editors' work).
- **Should be:** `author: Henry Parry Liddon, completed and edited by J. O. Johnston and R. J. Wilson`; `written: start: 1893, end: 1897`; one sentence in the body: "Liddon died in 1890; the work was finished from his papers by its editors and published 1893–1897."
- **Evidence:** Web search: Marsh's Library catalogue (web.marshlibrary.ie/catalogue/Record/16901): "edited and prepared for publication by J. O. Johnston and Robert J. Wilson … London: Longmans, Green, and Co., 1893–7 … v. 4 co-edited by W. C. E. Newbolt"; Project Canterbury's index (anglicanhistory.org/pusey/liddon/, opened) gives only "Longmans, 1894" for the whole set, which is where the record's date comes from. Liddon's death in 1890 is own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/booth-in-darkest-england.md
- **Text:** `author: William Booth`; "It is the founder's own statement … Its figures are his estimates, drawn from the writers he names."
- **Problem:** The record treats the book as Booth's unaided work. Its authorship is a known question: the journalist W. T. Stead put Booth's material into literary shape (Stead said he did the "hack-work" himself while holding that the ideas were the General's; Frank Smith and Susie Swift of the Army also supplied material). Booth's preface itself acknowledges "valuable literary help from a friend of the poor" who was "not in any way connected with the Salvation Army", without naming him. The brief for source records asks that disputed or assisted authorship be acknowledged.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: "Booth's preface acknowledges literary help from a friend outside the Army; this was the journalist W. T. Stead, who by his own account shaped Booth's material into the book, so the scheme is Booth's but the wording cannot always be taken as his own."
- **Evidence:** Web search: the preface wording is as quoted (Project Gutenberg no. 475; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Darkest_England_and_the_Way_Out); Stead's letter to C. F. Aked (attackingthedevil.co.uk) on his "hack-work"; Christian History Institute, "In Darkest England", names Smith, Swift and Stead as helpers.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/owen-history-of-the-bible-society.md
- **Text:** "For the two years before that meeting he was not himself a witness; he first heard of the plan, he says, from the circular that announced the meeting."
- **Problem:** Owen's own next paragraph qualifies this. He says that "early in the summer of 1803" Joseph Hughes sent him two copies of his Essay (the pamphlet that proposed the society), asking him to give one to the Bishop of London; Owen passed it on but, "regarding it as utterly chimerical, he took little pains either to understand or to recommend it". So he had heard of the project nine months before the circular; what he dates to the circular is his becoming "first acquainted with the plan".
- **Should be:** "…he was not himself a witness. He says that Hughes sent him the Essay in the summer of 1803, that he thought the project chimerical and paid it little attention, and that he first became acquainted with the plan through the circular that announced the meeting."
- **Evidence:** archive.org/stream/historyoforiginf01owen (vol. 1, chap. 1, just after the text of the circular): "It was … through the medium of the circular above-described, that the writer of this History became first acquainted with the plan…"; then "Early in the summer of 1803, the author received from Mr. Hughes … two copies of the Essay…". The record's other details were confirmed in the same scan (meeting 7 March 1804; Owen declines and Pratt is appointed 12 March; Pratt resigns and Owen succeeds 23 April; Charles in London December 1802; no Welsh girl).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/sources/barruel-history-of-the-clergy.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1794, display: "London, 1794 (English translation of the French original)"`; "The book appeared within two years of the events"
- **Problem:** `written` gives the year of the English translation, not of the work. Barruel's French original, *Histoire du clergé pendant la Révolution françoise*, was printed in London by Coghlan in 1793, within a year of the September massacres of 1792. The record never gives the date of the original, and "within two years" understates how close the book is to the events.
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1793, display: "London, 1793 (French); English translation 1794"`; body: "The French original appeared in 1793, within a year of the events; the English translation cited here followed in 1794."
- **Evidence:** Web search of library catalogues (National Library of Australia nla.cat-vn156314; Texas A&M and others, ESTC T80487): *Histoire du clergé pendant la Révolution françoise: ouvrage dédié à la nation angloise*, Londres, J. P. Coghlan, 1793, preliminary matter dated 10 August 1793; the record of the English version notes "First published in 1793 as 'Histoire du clergé'".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · consistency · src/content/sources/gospel-of-thomas.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 300, end: 500, display: "Coptic copy of the 4th or 5th century"`; "names Greek papyri from Oxyrhynchus that preserve related sayings"
- **Problem:** (1) The sister record `nag-hammadi-codices.md` dates the same books to the "4th century AD" (300–400) and reports the first editors' judgment "no later than the second half of the fourth century"; this record allows the fifth century for one of them. Dated papyri reused in the bindings of the find (Codex VII: AD 341, 346, 348) are why the codices are placed in the mid to later fourth century. (2) `written` is the date of the copy only, and the body gives no date for the Greek fragments. P.Oxy. 1, 654 and 655 are dated on palaeographic grounds to about AD 200–250, which is the firm evidence that the collection existed, in Greek, at least a century before the Coptic copy; without that a reader may take "c. 300–500" as the date of the work.
- **Should be:** `display: "Coptic copy of the 4th century; Greek fragments of c. 200–250"` (and `end: 400`, as in the codices record); add to the body: "Three Greek papyri from Oxyrhynchus (P.Oxy. 1, 654, 655), dated to the first half of the third century, preserve parts of about twenty of the sayings, so the collection is at least that old."
- **Evidence:** `src/content/sources/nag-hammadi-codices.md` (front matter and body); own knowledge for the cartonnage dates (J. W. B. Barns, G. M. Browne, J. C. Shelton, *Nag Hammadi Codices: Greek and Coptic Papyri from the Cartonnage of the Covers*, 1981) and for the dating of the Oxyrhynchus fragments (Grenfell and Hunt; H. W. Attridge in Layton, ed., *Nag Hammadi Codex II,2–7*, 1989). gospels.net's manuscript page was not reachable through the fetch tool.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/gregory-xvi-in-supremo-apostolatus.md
- **Text:** `summary: "… Gregory XVI condemns the trade in Black Africans and all enslavement, and forbids any Catholic, cleric or lay, to defend it as permissible."`
- **Problem:** The summary states one reading as the letter's plain sense. How far the letter reaches was contested at once: in the United States Bishop John England (letters to Secretary of State John Forsyth, 1840–41) and the bishops at Baltimore held that it condemned the slave trade and the act of reducing free people to slavery, not the holding of people already enslaved, and that reading governed Catholic practice in the slave states. The operative clause forbids anyone "unjustly to molest, despoil of their goods or reduce to slavery" Indians, Blacks and others, and to defend "that traffic" as lawful; it does not pronounce on existing slave-holding. The body's last sentence is careful ("names both the traffic and the act of reducing people to servitude"); the summary ("all enslavement") is not.
- **Should be:** Summary: "…condemns the trade in Black Africans and the reducing of anyone to slavery, and forbids any Catholic to defend the traffic as permissible." Body: add that American bishops of the time read it as leaving domestic slavery untouched, and that the date is 3 December 1839 (the record gives only the year).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the text (papalencyclicals.net/greg16/g16sup.htm, operative paragraph) and of England's *Letters to the Hon. John Forsyth on the Subject of Domestic Slavery* (1844). Not re-opened in this audit.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/bundestag-drucksache-18-8613.md
- **Text:** `kind: law`; "A motion laid before the German Bundestag in 2016 … The text is a motion of three parliamentary groups dated 31 May 2016."
- **Problem:** The record stops at the tabling of the motion and never says what became of it. The Bundestag adopted it on 2 June 2016 with one vote against and one abstention, which is why it counts as the parliament's statement; a motion merely "laid before" a parliament would be weak evidence of "how a state describes the events". It is a resolution, not a statute, so `kind: law` also overstates it.
- **Should be:** Add: "The Bundestag adopted the motion on 2 June 2016 with one vote against and one abstention. It is a resolution of the parliament and has no force of law." (The date in `written.display` could then read "tabled 31 May 2016; adopted 2 June 2016".)
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Plenarprotokoll 18/173, 2 June 2016). Not opened in this audit.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/reichskonkordat-1933.md
- **Text:** "The concordat has 34 articles and a final protocol."
- **Problem:** The treaty as signed on 20 July 1933 had a third part that the record does not mention: a secret annex (Geheimanhang) regulating the position of clergy and seminarians in case Germany reintroduced general military service, which the Versailles treaty then forbade. It was not published with the concordat and is absent from the text the record links, which is the reason to say so.
- **Should be:** Add: "A secret annex, not printed in the text linked here, provided for the treatment of clergy and students of theology if general conscription were reintroduced."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (the annex is printed in the documentary editions of the concordat, e.g. A. Kupper, *Staatliche Akten über die Reichskonkordatsverhandlungen 1933*, 1969, and L. Volk, *Kirchliche Akten…*, 1969). Not opened in this audit.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/slavery-abolition-act-1833.md
- **Text:** "The Act for the abolition of slavery throughout the British colonies, in force from 1 August 1834."
- **Problem:** The record repeats the Act's title as a description of its reach. Section 64 excepts "the Territories in the Possession of the East India Company", Ceylon and St Helena, and the Act itself sets later starting dates for the Cape of Good Hope (1 December 1834) and Mauritius (1 February 1835). "Throughout the British colonies … from 1 August 1834" is therefore too absolute.
- **Should be:** "…in force from 1 August 1834 in the West Indian colonies (later at the Cape and Mauritius); section 64 excepts the East India Company's territories, Ceylon and St Helena."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of 3 & 4 Will. IV c. 73, ss. 1, 12 and 64 (the linked legislation.gov.uk text). Not re-opened in this audit.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

The wiki has 44 events between 1782 and 1950 and the 172 source records of this batch serve them well; the source records themselves are, with the exceptions above, accurate to the day, page and count wherever I tested them. What is missing is whole subjects, and with them their primary sources. I grepped `src/content/{events,people,places,threads,eras,sources}` for each term in quotation marks below and found no entry (0 files) unless noted.

- **The churches and the Jews under National Socialism; the Shoah.** "Holocaust", "Shoah", "Aryan" – 0; "Jewish/Jews" occurs in no event after 1900 except `second-vatican-council`. The entries on Barmen, Bonhoeffer and *Mit brennender Sorge* never mention the Aryan paragraph, the persecution of the Jews or the debate about Pius XII ("Pius XII" appears only for 1950 and China). First-rank sources absent: the Aryan paragraph and the Pastors' Emergency League pledge (1933), Bonhoeffer's "The Church and the Jewish Question" (1933), the Confessing Church memorandum to Hitler (1936), *Summi Pontificatus* (1939), Pius XII's Christmas message of 1942, the Darmstadt Statement (1947). This is the most serious gap of the period; it belongs in a separate event (1933–45) and in a sentence each in the three existing ones.
- **Biblical criticism and Darwin.** "Darwin", "Strauss", "Wellhausen", "Essays and Reviews", "Harnack", "Revised Version" – 0 ("Westcott" once, in a source). The nineteenth-century crisis over Scripture and science, which the entries on *The Fundamentals*, *Pascendi* and Vatican I presuppose, has no entry. Sources: Strauss, *Das Leben Jesu* (1835); *Essays and Reviews* (1860); Darwin, *Origin of Species* (1859); Westcott and Hort's Greek Testament and the Revised Version (1881); Leo XIII, *Providentissimus Deus* (1893); Holy Office, *Lamentabili* (1907) – the last is the companion of *Pascendi*, which has a record.

## Method note
All 172 records were read in full. The batch contains no `[[bible:…]]` tokens; the six passages that record bodies name in prose (1 Sam 12:23 for Keble; Exod 3:7–8 for Jones; Dan 8:13–14 for Miller; 1 Cor 11:5, 1 Cor 14:34–35 and Gal 3:28 for Catherine Booth) were read in the WEB and fit what the records say. Every date, article/canon/vote count, imprint and "public domain" statement was compared with what I know of the document; about 30 claims that were precise or looked doubtful were then checked on the web (16 pages opened: azbyka.ru ×2, archive.org full texts of Baillie's Schleiermacher, Anderson, Owen vol. 1 ×4 and the Millennial Star, ekd.de, vatican.va, anglicanhistory.org, persee.fr, gospels.net, de/en.wikipedia; about 18 web searches). Confirmed among others: Anderson's credit of the Civil Constitution translation to J. H. Robinson; Baillie's facing-page editions and §§8–9 / §§3–4; Owen's dates of 7 and 12 March and 23 April 1804; the General Epistle's 143 pioneers, 14 April and 23 December 1847; the EKD's "erste Vollsitzung" at Stuttgart; Francis's Namugongo wording; Huppenkothen's six years; Gutenberg no. 10633 being Clarkson's 1839 edition.
