# Events: Awakenings and Missions, 1792–1891

Reviewer batch E15. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · dating · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/uganda-martyrs.md
- **Text:** front matter `date: start: 1885, end: 1886`; summary "In 1885 and 1886 King Mwanga of Buganda has Christian converts killed"; dating note "None of the documents cited here records a death in 1887"
- **Problem:** The killings honoured as the Martyrs of Uganda ran to January 1887: the last of the twenty-two canonized Catholic martyrs, Jean-Marie (John Mary) Muzeyi, was beheaded on 27 January 1887. That is why the Church of England calendar (and every standard account) dates them 1885–7. The dating note treats "1885–7" as an unexplained oddity and the end year, summary and narrative stop at 1886; the entry also says Paul VI canonized "twenty companions" without saying that one of them died in 1887. The cited Uganda Episcopal Conference page has a life of "St John Mary Kiwanuka Muzeeyi" (the text on the live page breaks off before the death), so the note's statement is only true because of a truncated web page.
- **Should be:** `end: 1887`; summary "Between 1885 and 1887 …"; in the dating note: "The last of the twenty-two canonized Catholic martyrs, Jean-Marie Muzeyi, was killed on 27 January 1887, which is why the calendars give 1885–7." A sentence giving the usual count (22 Catholics canonized; 23 Anglicans commemorated) would also answer the reader's obvious question.
- **Evidence:** https://uecon.org/index.php/uganda-martyrs (lists Muzeeyi among the 22; his life is cut off mid-sentence); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Martyrs ("22 Catholic and 23 Anglican converts … executed between 31 January 1885 and 27 January 1887"; list row for Jean-Marie Muzeyi "27 January 1887"); Common Worship calendar "Martyrs of Uganda, 1885–7 and 1977".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/russian-mission-to-alaska.md
- **Text:** "He died there on 13 December 1837 [[cite:herman-death]]."
- **Problem:** The day is stated in the wiki's own voice on the strength of the Valaam centenary history of 1894 (a hagiographic compilation written 57 years later). The day is not secure: the Russian-American Company manager's dispatch to the bishop of Irkutsk, used by modern research (S. Korsun), gives 15 November 1837 (Julian), and the Orthodox Church in America's own life admits that "some records" give an earlier death date with burial about a month later, so 13 December may be the burial. The year is not affected.
- **Should be:** Attribute and flag: "The Valaam history says that he died there on 13 December 1837; a company dispatch of the time points to 15 November, and 13 December may be the day of burial." Same wording for `people/herman-of-alaska.md` (which already attributes the date to the Valaam history).
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_of_Alaska (text and note: "Although his official hagiography states that he died on December 13, 1837, this was a mistake. The correct date is based on the dispatch by the company manager Kupreyanov to the bishop of Irkutsk. See Korsun, p. 146"); oca.org life for 13 December ("some records … state that he died on November 28th and was buried on December 26th"). I did not open Korsun.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/taiping-rebellion.md
- **Text:** "Hong was proclaimed emperor of a new dynasty of Taiping, 'Great Peace', in 1851 [[cite:dynasty]]." (also dating note: "his proclamation as emperor of the new dynasty at Yung-gnan in 1851")
- **Problem:** "Emperor" is Brine's English gloss (p. 124 has "proclaimed first emperor of the new dynasty of Taeping"), not Hong's title. Hong took the title Tianwang, "Heavenly King", and the movement expressly reserved the imperial term *di* for God; the state was the Taiping Tianguo, "Heavenly Kingdom of Great Peace", which is the entry's own title. The same entry later uses "Kan-wang, the Shield King" without ever saying that Hong himself was the Heavenly King (only the person entry's summary does), and the place of the proclamation (Brine: Yong'an, autumn 1851; most modern accounts: Jintian, 11 January 1851) is given only in the dating note.
- **Should be:** "In 1851 Hong was proclaimed ruler of a new dynasty, the Taiping ('Great Peace') Heavenly Kingdom, with the title Heavenly King (Tianwang); Brine calls him its 'first emperor' [[cite:dynasty]]." Likewise in `people/hong-xiuquan.md` ("He was proclaimed emperor of a new dynasty of Taiping in 1851").
- **Evidence:** Brine p. 124 as scanned (archive.org taepingrebellio00bringoog: "…gnan, where he was proclaimed first emperor of the…"); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Xiuquan ("declared himself the Heavenly King of the Heavenly Kingdom of Peace"; founding declared 11 January 1851 at Jintian); Roberts's 1862 letter quoted in the entry itself calls him by no imperial title.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/first-vatican-council.md
- **Text:** "On 20 September Rome fell [[cite:rome-fell]]." (end of the narrative of the council)
- **Problem:** The entry never says how the council ended. After the fall of Rome Pius IX suspended it indefinitely by the bull *Postquam Dei munere* of 20 October 1870; it was never reconvened or formally closed, and most of its prepared agenda (including the schema on the church and the bishops) was left undone — the reason Vatican II took up the episcopate, which the entry's last sentence alludes to without explanation. The entry also omits that the Franco-Prussian War broke out the day after the definition (19 July), which is why the bishops dispersed.
- **Should be:** Add one sentence: "On 20 October 1870 Pius IX suspended the council indefinitely (*Postquam Dei munere*); it never met again." cited to the bull.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (the bull is in the Acta Pii IX and at vatican.va in Latin/Italian); grep of the entry and of `second-vatican-council.md` for "suspend/adjourn/prorog" finds nothing.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/bible-society.md
- **Text:** "'without Note or Comment'" / threads: scripture-and-canon (whole entry stops at the First Report of 1805)
- **Problem:** For a wiki with a "scripture and canon" thread, the Society's most consequential decision about the canon is missing: the Apocrypha controversy of 1825–26, in which (under pressure from the Scottish auxiliaries, led by Robert Haldane) the Society resolved that its funds should be used only for the canonical books and no longer for Bibles containing the Apocrypha. The Edinburgh and Glasgow societies seceded anyway, and the rule is the main reason English Protestant Bibles since then are normally printed without the Apocrypha. Nothing else in the wiki covers it (grep "Apocrypha" finds only king-james-bible, westminster-assembly and ancient entries; king-james-bible has no word on the later dropping of the books). The entry also gives no hint of the scale the Society reached.
- **Should be:** A closing paragraph citing the Society's resolutions of 1826 (printed in its 22nd Report, 1826, and in Canton's/Browne's histories) on the exclusion of the Apocrypha.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; grep of `src/content/events` for "Apocrypha controversy|without the Apocrypha" returns nothing. (The entry is right not to tell the Mary Jones story: Owen, the cited source, does not have it.)
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/latter-day-saints.md
- **Text:** whole entry (narrative runs 1820–1847, then modern statements on baptism)
- **Problem:** Two things a careful reader would regard as essential are absent. (1) Plural marriage: the revelation recorded in 1843 (Doctrine and Covenants 132), its public announcement in 1852, the conflict with the United States over it, and the Manifesto of 1890 (Official Declaration 1) ending it — the best-known issue between the church and other Americans in the period this era covers; the word does not occur in the entry (grep "polygam|plural marriage" in the wiki finds only the Kimbangu entry). (2) The church's own name, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, is never given, nor that after 1844 the movement divided (the body that followed Young to Utah; the Reorganized church of 1860, now Community of Christ), so "the church" in the last paragraph is undefined. The reason the other churches give for not accepting the baptism (a different doctrine of God/the Trinity) is stated for the Methodists but not for the CDF answer, which gave no reasons in the response itself.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence on D&C 132 and Official Declaration 1 (both are in the already-cited `doctrine-and-covenants` source), name the church in full once, and note the division after Smith's death.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; D&C 132 and Official Declaration 1 at churchofjesuschrist.org; grep as stated.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/william-carey.md
- **Text:** "Carey embarked with his family on 13 June 1793 [[cite:embarked]]. On 7 October 1805 the missionaries, by then settled at Serampore in Bengal, agreed on a statement…"
- **Problem:** The entry jumps from the embarkation to 1805 and says nothing of what Carey did in India, which is what the title ("…and the Modern Missionary Movement") and the `scripture-and-canon` thread lead the reader to expect: arrival in Bengal in November 1793; the move in January 1800 to the Danish settlement of Serampore because the East India Company would not admit missionaries (this explains "by then settled"); the partnership with Joshua Marshman and William Ward, who are not named; the first convert, Krishna Pal, baptized December 1800; and above all the translations — the Bengali New Testament printed at Serampore in 1801, the first of some forty versions the press issued. `people/william-carey.md` has none of this either.
- **Should be:** Two or three cited sentences (the Periodical Accounts vols. 1–2 and the Serampore "Memoirs of the translations" give all of it at first hand).
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; read both files in full; grep "Bengali|Marshman" finds only this entry's Form-of-Agreement paragraph.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/awakenings-and-missions/hidden-christians-of-japan.md
- **Text:** dating note: "Marnas prints the notification of the Supreme Council itself, dated in his text the second day of the second month, which he gives as 22 March 1873; that does not agree with Petitjean's letters, and no exact day is given here."
- **Problem:** The conflict the note leaves open can be resolved. The notification is Dajōkan (Council of State) Proclamation No. 68, issued on 24 February 1873 (Japan had adopted the Gregorian calendar on 1 January 1873, so no lunar conversion applies). That agrees with Petitjean ("toward the end of February") and shows Marnas's "22 March" to be a mistaken conversion.
- **Should be:** Add to the dating note: "The notification is Council of State Proclamation No. 68 of 24 February 1873, which agrees with Petitjean's letter." and, if wanted, give the day in the narrative.
- **Evidence:** Japanese government (MLIT) multilingual text "The Lifting of the Ban on Christianity" (mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R1-00791.html: "On February 24, 1873, the Meiji government issued Grand Council of State Proclamation No. 68, ordering the removal of the official signpost prohibiting Christianity"); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Public_Notices. Found by web search; I did not open the Japanese text of the proclamation.
- **Confidence:** medium
