# Events: Primeval History, Patriarchs, Exodus and Conquest

Reviewer batch E01. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

Batch: 19 event entries, eras primeval-history, patriarchs, exodus-and-conquest. No A- or B-level defect was found: the Bible citations, the chronological arithmetic and the named scholarship all checked out. What follows are C-level imprecisions and omissions.

## Findings

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/patriarchs/call-of-abraham.md
- **Text:** "they settled on the way at [[place:haran]], and there Terah died [[bible:Gen 11:31-32]]. … Then, Genesis says, the Lord spoke to [[person:abraham|Abram]]"
- **Problem:** Order of narrative is given as order of events. On Genesis's own figures Terah is 70 when Abram is born (Gen 11:26), Abram leaves Haran at 75 (Gen 12:4, the figure the dating note relies on) and Terah dies at 205 (Gen 11:32), so Terah is alive for some 60 years after the departure. It is Stephen (Acts 7:4) who puts the departure "when his father was dead"; the entry notes Stephen's other difference (the place of the call) but not this one.
- **Should be:** "…settled at Haran. Genesis records Terah's death there at 205 [[bible:Gen 11:32]] and next tells of the call" (no "Then"); and add to the Stephen paragraph that Acts 7:4 places the move to Canaan after Terah's death, whereas the ages in Gen 11:26, 11:32 and 12:4 leave Terah alive when Abram departs (the Samaritan Pentateuch reads 145 for Terah's age at death).
- **Evidence:** WEB read locally: Gen 11:26 "Terah lived seventy years, and became the father of Abram, Nahor, and Haran"; Gen 11:32 "two hundred five years"; Gen 12:4 "seventy-five"; Acts 7:4 "when his father was dead". Samaritan reading: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** medium (Gen 11:26 need not mean Abram was the son born in Terah's 70th year, but that is the plain reading and the wiki flags such tensions elsewhere)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/patriarchs/call-of-abraham.md
- **Text:** "The Greek Septuagint makes the 430 years cover the time in Canaan as well as in Egypt [[cite:lxx]]; Josephus counts in the same way … and Paul sets the law 430 years after the promise [[bible:Gal 3:17]]."
- **Problem:** The note presents the short sojourn as resting on the Greek text, Josephus and Paul only. It omits the internal Hebrew evidence that the same reckoning appeals to, and that the long count has to explain: Gen 15:13 gives 400 years, not 430; Gen 15:16 promises a return "in the fourth generation"; and Exod 6:16-20 gives only Levi – Kohath – Amram – Moses, with lifespans (137, 133, 137) that cannot fill 430 years since Kohath was already born at the descent (Gen 46:11). The Samaritan Pentateuch also has the longer reading of Exod 12:40.
- **Should be:** Add one sentence: "Genesis 15 speaks of 400 years and of a return in the fourth generation [[bible:Gen 15:13-16]], and Exodus lists only four generations from Levi to Moses [[bible:Exod 6:16-20]]; the Samaritan text of Exod 12:40 agrees with the Greek."
- **Evidence:** WEB read locally (Gen 15:13-16; Exod 6:16-20; Exod 12:40). Brenton Exod 12:40 opened at ebible.org/eng-Brenton/EXO12.htm ("in the land of Egypt and the land of Chanaan"). Samaritan reading: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high for the biblical data; medium on whether the maintainers want it in the note

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/the-exodus.md
- **Text:** "Those who hold to a historical exodus defend one of two dates."
- **Problem:** A false absolute. Other dates are defended in the literature the note itself relies on: Hoffmeier's article (n. 60) cites Gary Rendsburg, "The Date of the Exodus and the Conquest/Settlement: The Case for the 1100s", *Vetus Testamentum* 42 (1992), pp. 510–527; and a link with the expulsion of the Hyksos in the sixteenth century BC goes back to Josephus (*Against Apion* 1) and still has advocates. The same absolute is repeated on the era page ("place it either about 1446 BC … or in the thirteenth century BC").
- **Should be:** "Most of those who hold to a historical exodus defend one of two dates."
- **Evidence:** Hoffmeier, JETS 50 (2007), p. 236 n. 60, read in the PDF at etsjets.org (files_JETS-PDFs_50_50-2_JETS_50-2_225-247_Hoffmeier.pdf). Josephus/Hyksos: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/the-exodus.md
- **Text:** "The first takes the figure in Kings literally: Solomon began the temple in his fourth year, "in the four hundred and eightieth year" after Israel came out of Egypt [[bible:1 Kgs 6:1]]."
- **Problem:** The whole early date (and every date of the patriarchs era, via call-of-abraham) hangs on this one figure, and the note does not say that the figure itself is not textually uniform: the Septuagint reads "four hundred and fortieth year" at the same verse. The wiki reports the Septuagint variant at Exod 12:40 but not this one. Nor does it mention that Paul's speech in Acts 13:19-21 implies a longer interval (about 450 years before or including the judges, then Saul's 40), or that the Judges figures add up to more than 480 allows (the wiki says so only in age-of-the-judges).
- **Should be:** Add: "The Greek Septuagint reads 440 years in this verse [cite Septuagint, 3 Kingdoms 6:1], and the figures of Judges and of Acts 13:19-21 add up to more than 480; see [[event:age-of-the-judges]]."
- **Evidence:** Brenton, Kings III 6:1, opened at ebible.org/eng-Brenton/1KI06.htm: "in the four hundred and fortieth year after the departure of the children of Israel out of Egypt". WEB Acts 13:19-21 read locally.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/covenant-at-sinai.md
- **Text:** "at the Last Supper Jesus calls the cup "my blood of the new covenant" [[bible:Matt 26:27-28]]"
- **Problem:** "New" stands in the WEB at Matt 26:28 (it follows the Majority Text) but not in the NRSVUE to which the citation links ("my blood of the covenant"); the earliest manuscripts of Matthew and Mark lack the word. This is the case the authoring guide rules out (quoted translation and linked translation differ in substance), and the sentence uses the quotation precisely to tie Jesus' words to Jeremiah's "new covenant".
- **Should be:** Cite Luke 22:20 or 1 Cor 11:25, where both translations read "the new covenant in my blood"; or keep Matthew and write "my blood of the covenant", echoing Exod 24:8.
- **Evidence:** WEB Matt 26:28 read locally: "this is my blood of the new covenant". NRSVUE/critical text of Matt 26:28 without "new": own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/covenant-at-sinai.md
- **Text:** "Deuteronomy repeats the commandments."
- **Problem:** A mild harmonisation of parallel texts. The two versions differ, most visibly in the Sabbath commandment: Exod 20:11 grounds it in the six days of creation (as the creation entry notes), Deut 5:15 in the deliverance from Egypt.
- **Should be:** "Deuteronomy gives the commandments again, with some differences: there the Sabbath is grounded in the deliverance from Egypt and not in the six days of creation [[bible:Deut 5:6-21]] [[bible:Deut 5:15]]."
- **Evidence:** WEB Deut 5:12-15 and Exod 20:8-11 read locally.
- **Confidence:** high (that the texts differ); low priority

### C · completeness · src/content/events/primeval-history/the-flood.md
- **Text:** "A Mesopotamian flood story [[cite:gilgamesh]] survives on a tablet copied in the seventh century BC [[cite:tablet]]"
- **Problem:** The dating note gives only the date of the Nineveh copy. A reader will take the Mesopotamian story to be a seventh-century text, although the tradition is a thousand years older: the wiki's own source record says the standard epic dates from about 1100 BC and follows the older poem of Atrahasis, whose tablets were copied in the seventeenth century BC. That is the fact that matters for the question the note raises ("how it is related to Genesis").
- **Should be:** "…survives on a tablet copied in the seventh century BC; the story is much older, and is told already in the Babylonian poem of Atrahasis, known from tablets of the seventeenth century BC."
- **Evidence:** src/content/sources/epic-of-gilgamesh.md (body); Atrahasis tablets dated to the reign of Ammi-saduqa: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/primeval-history/the-fall.md
- **Text:** "It is [[person:paul]] who states the meaning that became central for Christians. Sin came into the world through one man, he writes, and death through sin"
- **Problem:** The entry moves from Genesis straight to Paul and to Revelation's "old serpent". Two books of the wiki's own Bible (deuterocanon) make both moves before Paul: Wisdom 2:23-24 says that "death entered into the world by the envy of the devil", the earliest identification of the serpent with the devil, and Sirach 25:24 that "the beginning of sin came from a woman. Because of her, we all die". Without them Paul's reading looks unprepared.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence before the Paul sentence citing [[bible:Wis 2:23-24]] and [[bible:Sir 25:24]].
- **Evidence:** WEB (with deuterocanon) read locally: Wis 2:24; Sir 25:24.
- **Confidence:** medium (an addition, not an error)

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/patriarchs/joseph-and-israel-in-egypt.md
- **Text:** "Jacob came down to Egypt with his whole family, seventy people in all … and was carried back to Canaan for burial [[bible:Gen 47:28]] [[bible:Gen 50:12-13]] … [[person:stephen]]'s speech both retell the story … [[bible:Acts 7:9-16]]"
- **Problem:** The passage of Acts cited as retelling the story differs from Genesis on both points just stated, and the entry does not say so: Acts 7:14 counts seventy-five people (the Septuagint figure at Gen 46:27), and Acts 7:16 has the patriarchs laid at Shechem in a tomb Abraham bought, where Genesis has Jacob buried at Machpelah near Hebron (Gen 50:13) and Jacob as the buyer at Shechem (Josh 24:32). The call-of-abraham entry does flag Stephen's differences; this one silently lets him "retell".
- **Should be:** Add: "Stephen follows the Greek text in counting seventy-five [[bible:Acts 7:14]], and places the burial at Shechem [[bible:Acts 7:16]]."
- **Evidence:** WEB read locally: Gen 46:27 "seventy"; Acts 7:14 "seventy-five souls"; Acts 7:16; Josh 24:32. Brenton Gen 46:27 opened at ebible.org/eng-Brenton/GEN46.htm: "seventy-five souls".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/patriarchs/joseph-and-israel-in-egypt.md
- **Text:** "He was sold at 17 [[bible:Gen 37:2]], about 1898 BC"
- **Problem:** Gen 37:2 says that Joseph was seventeen when he was tending the flock and brought a bad report of his brothers; Genesis does not give his age at the sale. The start year of the entry (-1898) rests on reading the opening age as the age at the sale.
- **Should be:** "Genesis introduces him at 17 [[bible:Gen 37:2]]; if the sale followed in the same year, it falls about 1898 BC".
- **Evidence:** WEB Gen 37:2 read locally.
- **Confidence:** high (minor)

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/wilderness-years.md
- **Text:** "Aaron died on Mount Hor, leaving the priesthood to his son Eleazar [[bible:Num 20:22-29]]" and, in the dating note, "Aaron dies on the first day of the fifth month of the fortieth year [[bible:Num 33:38]]"
- **Problem:** A parallel account that differs is passed over: Deut 10:6 says that Aaron died and was buried at Moserah, where Eleazar succeeded him; in the itinerary of Numbers 33 Moseroth lies seven stations before Mount Hor (Num 33:30-38).
- **Should be:** Add a clause: "Deuteronomy places his death at Moserah [[bible:Deut 10:6]]."
- **Evidence:** WEB read locally: Deut 10:6 "traveled from Beeroth Bene Jaakan to Moserah. There Aaron died, and there he was buried"; Num 33:30-38.
- **Confidence:** high (low priority)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/covenant-renewal-at-shechem.md
- **Text:** "The second assembly was at [[place:shechem]] … [[bible:Josh 24:1]]"
- **Problem:** The place, which is in the entry's title, is not uniform in the textual tradition: the Septuagint reads Shiloh (Selo) at Josh 24:1 and again at 24:25. The wiki elsewhere records Septuagint variants that affect its entries; here it treats Shechem as undisputed.
- **Should be:** One sentence in the body or the dating note: "The Greek Septuagint places the assembly at Shiloh [cite Septuagint, Joshua 24:1, 25]; the Hebrew text has Shechem."
- **Evidence:** Brenton Joshua 24:1 opened at ebible.org/eng-Brenton/JOS24.htm: "And Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Selo". Verse 25 in the Greek: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · consistency · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/merneptah-stele.md
- **Text:** summary: "It is the earliest known mention of Israel outside the Bible."
- **Problem:** The summary is more absolute than the entry. The body says "No undisputed older mention of Israel outside the Bible is known", and the dating note reports a disputed earlier candidate (the Berlin block read by Görg). The authoring guide lists "the earliest mention" in summaries as a defect. The same sentence stands in the summary of src/content/sources/merneptah-stele.md and in the era summary of exodus-and-conquest.
- **Should be:** "It is the earliest undisputed mention of Israel outside the Bible."
- **Evidence:** The entry's own body and dating note; Hoffmeier, JETS 50 (2007), pp. 241–242, read in the PDF (he reports and rejects Görg's reading).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/exodus-and-conquest/death-of-moses.md
- **Text:** summary: "In the plains of Moab Moses renews the covenant with the generation born in the wilderness, writes down the law and hands the leadership to Joshua."
- **Problem:** The summary states in the wiki's own voice what the body carefully attributes ("Deuteronomy says that Moses wrote 'this law'"). That Moses wrote the law is the disputed point of Pentateuchal authorship, which the wiki nowhere discusses. The authoring guide applies attribution to summaries. The summaries of jacob-becomes-israel, joseph-and-israel-in-egypt, the-tabernacle, wilderness-years and covenant-renewal-at-shechem are likewise unattributed narrative, though without a disputed claim of this weight.
- **Should be:** "Deuteronomy tells how, in the plains of Moab, Moses renews the covenant …, writes down the law and hands the leadership to Joshua."
- **Evidence:** docs/authoring-guide.md ("The rule of attribution applies to the summary too"); the entry's body.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

- **The conquest and settlement of Canaan after Jericho is not covered at all.** The wiki goes from the fall of Jericho straight to Joshua's farewell ("When the wars were over"). Missing: Ai and Achan (Josh 7–8), Gibeon and the southern campaign (Josh 9–10), the burning of Hazor (Josh 11), the allotment and the tabernacle at Shiloh (Josh 13–21), and the different picture of Judges 1. This is also where the archaeological argument about Israel's origins is actually fought (et-Tell/Ai unoccupied in the Late Bronze Age; the destruction of Late Bronze Hazor, which Wood's and Hoffmeier's articles both discuss; the hill-country settlements of Iron Age I). The exodus note gives it one sentence (Finkelstein/Silberman, Dever). Grepped: "Hazor" (only age-of-the-judges and places/megiddo), "\bAi\b" (only places/bethel), "Gibeon" (only Shishak entries), "Yadin|Ben-Tor" (unrelated). Belongs as an event "The Conquest and Settlement of Canaan" between crossing-the-jordan and covenant-renewal-at-shechem.
- **The Amarna letters (about 1350 BC).** No hit for "Amarna", "Habiru|Apiru". They are the main contemporary written evidence for Canaan between the two proposed exodus dates (Egyptian rule over the Canaanite city-kings, Jerusalem under Abdi-Heba, the 'Apiru), used by both sides (Wood, p. 489, for the early date; the Egyptian control of Canaan as the standing objection to it). At least a paragraph in the exodus dating note, better a source record.
- **Egyptian evidence bearing on the sojourn.** The exodus note says only that no Egyptian text mentions the exodus. It does not mention the evidence the thirteenth-century case rests on beyond the name Raamses: Semitic settlement in the eastern Delta (Tell el-Dab'a/Avaris; "Hyksos" and "Avaris" appear only in places/pi-ramesses or not at all), or Egyptian texts on Asiatic labourers and border traffic. No hit for "Hyksos|Manetho", "Shasu|Soleb" (the "Shasu of Yhw" lists of Amenhotep III, usually taken as the earliest occurrence of the divine name outside the Bible).
- **Genesis 4–5 and 12–36 are thin.** Cain and Abel appear only in passing (the-fall, people/adam, people/eve). The destruction of Sodom (Gen 18–19; used in Luke 17, 2 Peter 2, Jude) has no treatment: "Sodom" occurs only in the Melchizedek entry. Hagar and Ishmael are told only inside covenant-with-abraham. Balaam (Num 22–24, and the Deir Alla inscription that names him) has no hit at all.
- **Authorship and composition of the Pentateuch.** No entry or note states the traditional ascription to Moses and the modern critical account (grepped "Wellhausen", "documentary hypothesis", "Mosaic authorship", "Astruc|Graf|Kuenen": nothing; "Pentateuch" only in the Septuagint and Vulgate entries; "higher criticism" only in the-fundamentals). For a wiki that marks disputed authorship of New Testament letters, this is a first-rank omission; it belongs in the dating notes of creation or death-of-moses and as a nineteenth-century event.
- **Chronologies built on Genesis.** The creation note says readers "have added these up" and cites Josephus, but the reckonings that shaped Christian timekeeping are not named: the Byzantine era of the world (5509 BC; the wiki uses its year-numbers in the Theophanes entries without explaining them), the Jewish era (3761 BC) and Ussher's 4004 BC (grep "Ussher": nothing).
- **Minor, inside existing entries:** tower-of-babel does not say that the city's own name, Bab-ili, means "gate of god" and that Genesis's "confusion" is a play on the Hebrew verb; creation and the-flood mention no Mesopotamian creation text, and Atrahasis appears only in a source record; crossing-the-jordan gives Jericho's archaeology well but not the later Italian–Palestinian excavations (1997 onwards).

## Method note

About 225 Bible passages were read in the local WEB (every citation carrying a number, age, name, date, sequence or quotation; the rest checked from knowledge of the text); three quotations that differ from the local copy (Gen 14:18, 15:5, 22:2) were checked at bible-api.com, match the WEB served there, and are not reported. The chronological arithmetic (966 + 480, + 430, the patriarchs' ages, Jacob's and Joseph's ages, the 215-year count, Caleb's seven years, Josephus's 25 years) was recomputed and is right; person front matter (Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph) agrees with it.

About 40 pages opened: Hoffmeier 2007 and Wood 2005 (JETS PDFs; every page reference in the dating notes confirmed: pp. 232, 236–237, 239, 241–242, 243; Wood p. 476), Bruins and van der Plicht 1995 (Cambridge PDF: "not destroyed by ca. 1400 BC, as Wood (1990) suggested"), Petrie's *Six Temples* (pp. 10, 13, 28, 30) and Breasted's *Ancient Records* III §§ 602–617 in the archive.org full text, the Global Egyptian Museum record, Josephus (eight passages at lexundria.com), Basil, Augustine (2), Irenaeus (2), Cyril, Trent, Augsburg Confession, Confession of Dositheus, Catechism 1333 and 2066, Calvin 4.18.2, Herodotus 1.179, Brenton's Septuagint (Gen 5, 11, 46; Exod 12; Josh 24; 3 Kgdms 6), the Livius Gilgamesh text. All said what the entries claim.

Not opened (accepted from own knowledge): Thiele 1983, Kitchen 2003, Thompson 1974, Van Seters 1975, Finkelstein/Silberman 2001, Dever 2003, Garstang 1940, Kenyon 1957, Wood in *BAR* 1990, Hasel in *BASOR* 1994 (authors, titles and years are correct as far as I know); the Israel Antiquities Authority description of 11Q13; the British Museum record K.3375; Herodotus 1.181 (eight stages) was not re-read.
