# Events: Apostolic Age

Reviewer batch E05. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

Batch: the 26 event entries of `apostolic-age`. Overall result: no category-A error found. Every Bible quotation matches the World English Bible, the itineraries and the Acts/Galatians comparisons are told correctly, and the named scholarship that could be checked exists and says what is claimed. What follows is one B and a set of C items (imprecisions, cross-entry inconsistencies and omissions).

## Findings

### B · scriptural · src/content/events/apostolic-age/first-clement.md
- **Text:** "it begins by apologising that troubles at Rome have delayed an answer on matters the Corinthians had raised"
- **Problem:** This rests on the 1867 ANF rendering "the points respecting which you consulted us". The Greek (περὶ τῶν ἐπιζητουμένων παρ' ὑμῖν πραγμάτων) is rendered by Lightfoot "the matters of dispute that have arisen among you" (so also Lake, Holmes, Ehrman); on that reading the letter does not say that Corinth asked Rome anything. Whether Corinth appealed or Rome wrote unasked is exactly what the entry's last paragraph reports as disputed between Rome and the Orthodox patriarchs, so one translator's choice is here stated as the letter's plain content.
- **Should be:** "it begins by apologising that troubles at Rome have delayed its attention to the matters in dispute at Corinth. (The older translation linked here has 'the points respecting which you consulted us'; the letter does not otherwise say that the Corinthians had written to Rome.)"
- **Evidence:** newadvent.org/fathers/1010.htm ch. 1 (ANF: "…the points respecting which you consulted us"); earlychristianwritings.com/text/1clement-lightfoot.html 1:1 ("somewhat tardy in giving heed to the matters of dispute that have arisen among you").
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/events/apostolic-age/destruction-of-the-second-temple.md
- **Text:** "Titus had the city and the temple levelled, leaving three towers and part of the western wall [[cite:razed]]"
- **Problem:** Josephus says Titus spared the three towers "and so much of the wall as enclosed the city on the west side", as a camp for the garrison. That is the western stretch of the city wall, not a part of it and not the temple's retaining wall; "part of the western wall" will be read by most readers as the Western Wall of the temple mount.
- **Should be:** "leaving three towers and the stretch of the city wall on the west side, to shelter the garrison".
- **Evidence:** lexundria.com/j_bj/7.1-7.3/wst (BJ 7.1–2).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/destruction-of-the-second-temple.md
- **Text:** "he blames the rebels for the city's ruin and clears Titus of burning the temple … The surviving text of Tacitus breaks off before the city falls."
- **Problem:** The entry gives only Josephus's version of Titus's part. The one ancient account that contradicts it is left out: Sulpicius Severus, Chronicle 2.30, says Titus held a council and with others judged that the temple should be destroyed (a passage often held, since Bernays, to derive from the lost part of Tacitus's Histories). The wiki already has the source record `sulpicius-severus-chronicle`.
- **Should be:** Add one attributed sentence: "Sulpicius Severus, about AD 400, reports the opposite: that Titus in council decided the temple should be destroyed (Chronicle 2.30)."
- **Evidence:** newadvent.org/fathers/35052.htm, 2.30: "Titus is said, after calling a council, to have first deliberated whether he should destroy the temple… others and Titus himself thought that the temple ought specially to be overthrown".
- **Confidence:** high (that the passage exists and is omitted)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/council-of-jerusalem.md
- **Text:** "One view identifies the visit of Galatians 2 with the meeting of Acts 15 … Another identifies it with the famine visit"
- **Problem:** The note presents two options as the field. A third, held by much critical Pauline chronology, puts the conference of Galatians 2 after the mission to Macedonia and Achaia and equates it with the visit of Acts 18:22 (John Knox, *Chapters in a Life of Paul*, 1950; Robert Jewett, *A Chronology of Paul's Life*, 1979; Gerd Lüdemann, *Paul, Apostle to the Gentiles*, 1984, a book the wiki itself cites in `claudius-expels-the-jews`). On that view the "about AD 48–49" used across the batch as the fixed end of the 14/17 years (also in `conversion-of-paul`) moves to about 50–52. The note says the answer "affects the dating of his whole career" but omits the reading that changes it most.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence naming this third identification and one work for it.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge of Knox, Jewett and Lüdemann (not re-opened in this task; verify titles and years before citing).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/apostolic-age/earliest-christian-letters.md
- **Text:** "Rainer Riesner, *Paul's Early Period* … (1998), dates it [Galatians] to 48, before 1 Thessalonians"
- **Problem:** Riesner does place Galatians before 1 Thessalonians, but a review reports that he leaves its date open between 48 (before the council) and 50 (after it). "Dates it to 48" is firmer than the book appears to be. (All other Riesner dates used in the batch — conversion 31/32, journey 45–47, council 48, Corinth 50–51, Ephesus 52–55, arrest 57, Rome 59 and 60–62 — agree with the Denver Journal review's summary.)
- **Should be:** "Riesner … holds Galatians to be earlier than 1 Thessalonians, in 48 or at latest 50".
- **Evidence:** Themelios review (thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/pauls-early-period/): "it is regrettable that Riesner leaves such an important matter as the dating of Galatians undecided between AD 48 (before the Jerusalem council) and AD 50"; Denver Journal review: "the letter to the Galatians and the Apostolic Council in 48". The book itself was not opened.
- **Confidence:** low

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/writing-of-the-gospels.md
- **Text:** dating note: "Most scholars put Mark first, close to the year 70 … Brown … puts Matthew at 80–90 … he sets the final form of John between 90 and 110."
- **Problem:** Three Gospels get a date and a named work; Luke gets none, although the note cites Luke 21:20–24 as a datable passage and the entry's range "65–100" covers it. The entry also never says that Luke has a second volume, Acts, on whose date Luke's depends (the pre-70 case of Robinson and Bernier rests largely on Acts ending before Paul's death), and it alludes to "how the Gospels depend on one another" without naming the majority solution (Mark used by Matthew and Luke, with a second sayings source).
- **Should be:** Add Luke's usual date with a reference (e.g. Brown's *Introduction*, which puts Luke about 85, give or take five to ten years — check the page), one sentence on Acts, and one on Markan priority.
- **Evidence:** entry text; grep of the wiki shows no treatment of the date of Luke or Acts elsewhere (`people/luke.md` discusses authorship only).
- **Confidence:** high (omission); the Brown figure for Luke is from memory

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/philip-in-samaria.md
- **Text:** "an Ethiopian eunuch, an official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians" (and the title "an Ethiopian Official")
- **Problem:** Nothing tells the reader that "Ethiopia" in Greek usage of the time is the kingdom of Kush (Meroë, in today's Sudan), not modern Ethiopia, and that "Candace" is the title of its queens, which Greek and Roman writers took for a name (Pliny, Natural History 6.186; Strabo 17.1.54). Without it the story is easily connected with the later church of Aksum, which the wiki treats separately.
- **Should be:** One attributed sentence, e.g. "Pliny says that Candace was the name borne by the queens of Meroë in succession; the 'Ethiopia' of Acts is that Nile kingdom."
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandake ("Contemporary Greek and Roman sources treated it, incorrectly, as a name"; Pliny cited). `grep -rE "Mero|Kush|Candace"` finds only this entry.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/thomas-and-india.md
- **Text:** "A king Gondophares is known from coins … Gardner dates not later than the middle of the first century AD"
- **Problem:** The king's date rests in the entry on a coin catalogue of 1886 alone. The standard evidence is the Takht-i-Bahi inscription (found 1872), dated in the 26th year of "Guduvhara" and year 103 of an era, which places his accession about AD 19–21 and shows him reigning about AD 45–46. It is the one dated document for the king the Acts of Thomas names and is what makes "in or before the apostle's lifetime" checkable.
- **Should be:** Add the inscription as a source and a sentence in the dating note; keep the remark that it does not mention Thomas.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (the Wikipedia page for the inscription returned no text through the proxy); verify the reading and era before citing.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/martyrdom-of-peter-and-paul.md
- **Text:** "The Roman writer Gaius, at the same period, offered to show the 'trophies' of the apostles at the Vatican and on the road to Ostia"
- **Problem:** The entry is purely literary. It omits the one piece of material evidence a careful reader expects: the excavations under St Peter's (1940–1949) uncovered a small second-century shrine in the Vatican necropolis, commonly identified with Gaius's "trophy". It also gives Origen (third century) as the source for the head-downward crucifixion, though the story is told earlier in the Acts of Peter (later second century).
- **Should be:** Add a sentence on the excavated shrine with its publication as source, and note the Acts of Peter as the earlier witness to the manner of Peter's death.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; grep for "necropolis|aedicula|scavi|Acts of Peter" finds nothing in the wiki.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · consistency · src/content/events/apostolic-age/martyrdom-of-peter-and-paul.md
- **Text:** front matter `display: c. AD 64–68`; dating note "the fourteenth year of Nero [[cite:jerome]], which is AD 67–68"
- **Problem:** `people/paul.md` gives Paul's death as "c. AD 64–67" while this entry and `people/peter.md` give 64–68, and the entry itself says Jerome's date for both apostles is 67–68.
- **Should be:** One range in all three (64–68 follows from the entry's own reasoning).
- **Evidence:** `src/content/people/paul.md` front matter (`died: start 64, end 67`), `people/peter.md` (64–68).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · consistency · src/content/events/apostolic-age/the-didache.md
- **Text:** `display: c. AD 50–120`; "The timeline shows the whole range"
- **Problem:** The wiki's own source record for the Didache dates it "Between about AD 50 and 150; most often placed near AD 100". The event's upper limit of 120 is Niederwimmer's date, not the limit of scholarly proposals.
- **Should be:** Align the two (either 50–150 in the event, or explain in the source record why 150).
- **Evidence:** `src/content/sources/didache.md` front matter.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/apostolic-age/revelation-to-john.md
- **Text:** "When Irenaeus discussed the beast's number, one of the names he weighed was *Lateinos*"
- **Problem:** The number is mentioned without its text (Rev 13:18 is never cited) and without the reading most commentators now give it, the name "Nero Caesar" written in Hebrew letters, which also explains the variant 616 that Irenaeus himself knows and rejects (Against Heresies 5.30.1). That reading is part of the case for the early date and for the Domitianic one (Nero returning), so the dating note is thinner without it.
- **Should be:** Cite Rev 13:18, state the figure, and add one attributed sentence on the Nero reading and the 616 variant.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; Irenaeus 5.30.1 (same New Advent page the entry links for 5.30.3).
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

- **The Jerusalem church between Pentecost and Stephen (Acts 3–5)** — the healing at the temple gate, the two hearings before the council, Gamaliel's advice, the common fund, Ananias and Sapphira. No event covers it; `pentecost` stops at Acts 2:47 and `martyrdom-of-stephen` starts at Acts 6. Grepped "Sapphira" (0 files), "Gamaliel" (only in `judea-becomes-a-roman-province` and two people pages).
- **The rest of the New Testament as writings** — Acts, Hebrews, James, 1–2 Peter, 1–3 John, Jude. The era has entries for Paul's letters, the Gospels and Revelation, but none for these; Hebrews gets one clause ("names no author"), the date and authorship of Acts are nowhere discussed, and the Pastorals are only listed as disputed. Grepped "Epistle of James|letter of James", "Johannine|1 John", "Luke-Acts", "Pastoral".
- **Judaism after 70 and the separation of church and synagogue** — Yohanan ben Zakkai and Yavneh, the end of the temple tax into the *fiscus Judaicus* (the tax itself is mentioned in the temple entry), the Jewish-Christian groups later called Ebionites and Nazoraeans. Nothing between the fall of the temple and Bar Kokhba. Grepped "Jamnia|Yavneh|Jabneh" (0), "Ebionite" (0), "Birkat" (0), "fiscus" (0).
- **Domitian and the Christians** — Flavius Clemens and Domitilla (Dio 67.14), Hegesippus on the grandsons of Jude (Eusebius 3.19–20). Two entries of this batch (`revelation-to-john`, `first-clement`) hang their dates on a supposed Domitianic persecution, but the evidence for and against it is set out only on the person page `domitian`.
- **John at Ephesus and the end of the apostolic generation** — Irenaeus on John living until Trajan, the link to Polycarp and Papias. Present only on `people/john-the-apostle.md`; there is no event, and Papias (cited in two entries) has no entry of his own.
- **Mark and the beginnings of Christianity in Egypt** (Eusebius 2.16) — grep "Mark.{0,30}Alexandria" finds nothing in events, people or places; the first Egyptian material in the wiki is Origen (203).
- **Thinly covered inside existing entries:** the synoptic relationship (Markan priority, the sayings source) in `writing-of-the-gospels`; the other apostles' traditions (Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew) apart from Thomas.

## Method note

All 26 files read in full. All 304 `[[bible:…]]` citations were pulled from the local WEB; every quoted phrase (about 95) was machine-matched against the cited verses with no mismatch, and about 80 passages carrying names, numbers, sequences and Acts-versus-Paul comparisons were printed and read against the sentences they support; the remaining citations were checked against my knowledge of the text without printing them. About 35 web pages were opened: Josephus (Antiquities 18.237, 19.274–275, 19.328–331, 19.343–352, 20.100–104, 20.197–203; War 2.261–263, 2.409–410, 6.93–94, 7.1–3), Cassius Dio 60, Suetonius Claudius 25 with Rolfe's note, Seneca Ep. 104 with Gummere's note, 1 Clement (ANF and Lightfoot), Eusebius book 2, Augustine Letters 28, 40, 82, Jerome *De viris* 1 and 5, Ephrem, Acts of Thomas, Cosmas 3 and 11, Gregory of Nazianzus Or. 33, the 1848 Encyclical §§ 11, 13, 14, the Michigan and Chester Beatty pages for P46, Oliver's Hesperia note (PDF), and two reviews of Riesner. Not opened: Riesner, Brown, Carson–Moo, Collins, Cohen, Cotton, Eck, Roth, Magness, Welborn, Herron, Slingerland, Campbell, Schürer and Gardner themselves (titles, years and positions agree with what I know of them); Tacitus and Irenaeus were checked from memory only.
