# People first appearing c. 1380–1650

Reviewer batch P04. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/people/reginald-pole.md
- **Text:** "died within days of the queen in November 1558" (summary); "He died at Lambeth in November 1558. The diarist Henry Machyn puts his death on 19 November, two days after that of Mary I; Foxe says he died the day after the queen"
- **Problem:** The entry offers only two discordant dates (19 and 18 November) and never gives the date that is actually accepted: Pole died on the evening of 17 November 1558, the same day as Mary, about twelve hours after her. The summary's "within days of the queen" therefore misstates the standard chronology. Machyn is quoted correctly (I read the diary: "The xix day of November ded be-twyn v and vj in the morning my lord cardenall Polle at Lambeth"), but he is an outlier, and two disagreeing sources are reported with the accepted date left out. The same wording ("within days of her") stands in `events/reformation/marian-persecutions.md`.
- **Should be:** "died at Lambeth on 17 November 1558, a few hours after the queen" (the contemporary report of his household, followed by all modern reference works), keeping Machyn's 19 November and Foxe's "the day after" as attributed variants. A `died` month/day of 17 November can then be added.
- **Evidence:** british-history.ac.uk Machyn, pp. 169–184 (text as quoted above); en.wikipedia.org "Reginald Pole": "12 March 1500 – 17 November 1558 … Pole died in London … on 17 November 1558, at about 7:00 pm, nearly 12 hours after Queen Mary's death"; own knowledge (ODNB gives the same day).
- **Confidence:** high (that 17 November is the accepted date); medium (that the entry should be rated B rather than C, since each statement is correctly attributed)

### C · dating · src/content/people/john-wycliffe.md
- **Text:** `died: {start: 1384, confidence: traditional}`; "John Foxe, in the sixteenth century, gives his death at Lutterworth on St Silvester's day, 31 December, under the year 1384"
- **Problem:** The label understates the security of the date. That Wycliffe died in the last days of December 1384 is not a later tradition: Thomas Walsingham, a chronicler of his own time, records the paralytic stroke that struck him on St Thomas of Canterbury's day (29 December) and his death from it, and the year is undisputed. It is labelled `traditional` only because the entry cites Foxe, written 180 years later, as its sole witness. (The exact day, 31 December, rests on Foxe's source tradition and on the fifteenth-century testimony of John Horn reported by Thomas Gascoigne, which puts the stroke on Holy Innocents' day, 28 December, and the death on St Silvester's day; the day is generally accepted.)
- **Should be:** `died: {start: 1384, confidence: firm}` (the front matter gives only the year), with Walsingham cited beside Foxe: *Historia Anglicana*, ed. Riley (Rolls Series), vol. 2, pp. 119–120 — entered there under "A.D. 1385" because Walsingham's year begins at Christmas, which is worth saying in the entry. The same applies to the last paragraph of `events/high-and-late-middle-ages/wycliffe-bible.md`.
- **Evidence:** archive.org `thomaewalsingham02wals` OCR, pp. 119–120, which I read: "Die Sancti Thomae, Cantuariensis Archiepiscopi et Martyris, organum diabolicum … Johannes de Wiclif … repente judicio Dei percussus, sensit paralysim omnia membra sua generaliter invasisse … moriens desperavit" (margin: "A.D. 1385", "Death of … Wiclif"); en.wikipedia.org "John Wycliffe" ("c. 1328 – 31 December 1384"; stroke on 28 December 1384). I did not open Gascoigne.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/people/tokugawa-ieyasu.md
- **Text:** "In January 1614 his government issued the proclamation" (summary); "The proclamation of 27 January 1614 speaks in the ruler's voice"
- **Problem:** The day is Satow's nineteenth-century conversion and is stated here as plain fact (the event's dating note at least attributes it to Satow). Modern scholarship dates the text, drafted by the monk Konchiin (Ishin) Sūden, to the 22nd/23rd day of the twelfth month of Keichō 18, i.e. 31 January / 1 February 1614; 27 January (also found in Pagès and Boxer) does not correspond to the date in Sūden's own register. The entry also does not say that the text was written by Sūden and issued under the seal of the reigning shogun Hidetada, Ieyasu having retired in 1605 (the event's dating note mentions Hidetada, citing Murdoch; the person page calls it flatly "his" government's).
- **Should be:** "The proclamation that Satow dates 27 January 1614 (modern studies: 31 January or 1 February, Keichō 18/12/22–23), drafted for Ieyasu by the monk Sūden and issued under the name of his son, the shogun Hidetada, …". The same note belongs in `sources/ieyasu-edict-1614.md` and the event `japan-martyrs`.
- **Evidence:** WebSearch results: Presses de l'Inalco (N. Kouamé) entry on the text, quoting Sūden's *Ikoku nikki* for Keichō 18/12/22 = 31 January 1614; K. Triplett's translation (De Gruyter) placing it in early 1614 as a notice under Hidetada; Samurai-Archives chronology (Keichō 18/12/23). I read snippets only, not the full studies.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/people/nikon.md
- **Text:** "The council that confirmed his corrected books in 1667 refers to him as the former patriarch"; summary "…the council of 1666–1667, which also speaks of him as the former patriarch"
- **Problem:** The reader is not told why he is "former". Nikon left the patriarchal throne in July 1658 after his breach with the tsar, and the same council, with the patriarchs of Alexandria and Antioch, tried and deposed him on 12 December 1666 and sent him to the Ferapontov monastery as a simple monk. So the council that upheld his reforms condemned the man; this, the best-known irony of the affair, is missing from both the person page and the event. The role "Patriarch of Moscow, 1652–1666" also hides the fact that he did not act as patriarch after 1658. No `born`/`died` (1605–1681).
- **Should be:** One cited sentence: withdrew from Moscow in 1658; deposed by the council in December 1666 (acts in *Materialy* / Paul of Aleppo is not a witness for this; Paisius Ligarides's account or the council's sentence should be cited).
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; the event text I read has no sentence on the deposition.
- **Confidence:** high (facts), medium (exact day 12 December)

## Method note

All 126 files were read in full. Every front-matter `born`/`died` year, month and day was checked against my own knowledge and, where there was any doubt, against Wikipedia; all are correct, and no altName belongs to another person and no namesake confusion was found (links to the Fredericks, Ferdinands, Henrys, Charleses, Pauls, Piuses and to Thomas Cromwell were traced through the wiki). The batch has one Bible citation (Rom 1:17, verified in the WEB). Weekday/date arithmetic was recomputed for nine dated statements (e.g. 11 Oct 1424 a Wednesday, 26 Jan 1589 a Sunday, 28 Jan 1547 a Friday, 6 July 1553 a Thursday, 22 March 1429 Tuesday in Holy Week): all hold. About 25 cited passages were opened and read in the archive.org OCR text, on british-history.ac.uk, projectwittenberg.org or ccel.org (Thomas à Kempis's chronicle and Lives, Quicherat vol. 2, Allen's *Briefe Historie*, Wriothesley vols 1–2, Tellez, Coleridge's Xavier vols 1–2, Luther's Correspondence vols 1–2, Lea vol. 1, Foxe vol. 7, Walsingham vol. 2, Kidd's *Documents*, Melanchthon's Life of Luther, Machyn, Pascal's letter 17): every attribution I tested matches its source, so the findings are almost all omissions and labels rather than misreadings. Project Gutenberg was down for maintenance, so the Gutenberg-hosted sources (Murray's Joan of Arc, Bradford, Knox, Marguerite de Valois, Ignatius's Autobiography, Morga, Peter Martyr, Saint-Simon) could not be opened; the Greek/Latin/Czech/Russian sources (Doukas, Sphrantzes, Kritoboulos, Mladoňovice, Höfler, Muravyov, *Materialy*) and the Spanish and Portuguese ones were judged from knowledge only. Walsingham's notice of Wycliffe's death was read in the Rolls Series OCR; Gascoigne was not. For the 1614 Japanese edict only search snippets of the modern studies were seen.
