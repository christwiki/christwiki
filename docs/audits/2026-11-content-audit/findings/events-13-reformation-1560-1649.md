# Events: Reformation, 1560–1649

Reviewer batch E13. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.



## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/events/reformation/thirty-years-war.md
- **Text:** "A month later [[person:innocent-x]] declared null and void for ever every article of the two treaties that harmed the Catholic religion"
- **Problem:** "A month later" is the date written on the brief (26 November 1648), not the date it was issued. *Zelo domus Dei* was drawn up and published only in August 1650 and backdated to 1648; the entry (and its dating note, "The pope's brief is dated 26 November 1648") presents the dating clause as the date of the act.
- **Should be:** "In a brief dated 26 November 1648, but published only on 20 August 1650, Innocent X declared null and void …"; add to the dating note that the brief was backdated (M. F. Feldkamp, "Das Breve 'Zelo domus Dei' vom 26. November 1648. Edition", *Archivum Historiae Pontificiae* 31 (1993), pp. 293–305; K. Repgen, ibid. 33 (1995), pp. 315–333).
- **Evidence:** de.wikipedia.org "Zelo domus Dei" (opened): "wurde am 20. August 1650 … veröffentlicht. Mit dem auf den 26. November 1648 zurückdatierten päpstlichen Breve …", citing Feldkamp and Repgen; en.wikipedia "Pope Innocent X": "In 1650 Innocent X issued the papal brief Zelo Domus Dei … and backdated it to 1648".
- **Confidence:** high

### B · historical · src/content/events/reformation/japan-martyrs.md
- **Text:** "Rules issued with it made Buddhist priests responsible for vouching for the religion of their parishioners [[cite:edict-1614]]."
- **Problem:** The entry states in its own voice what Satow believed in 1878. The "fifteen rules" Satow prints (the *Gojōmoku shūmon danna ukeai no okite*, dated Keichō 18) are held by modern scholarship to be a forgery that began to circulate about 1735; temple certification (terauke) was built up in the 1630s–1660s, not in 1614. The sentence is therefore an anachronism presented as fact (same wording in `sources/ieyasu-edict-1614.md`).
- **Should be:** Either delete the sentence, or: "Satow printed with it fifteen rules for Buddhist priests, dated to the same year; these are now regarded as a forgery of the eighteenth century, and the system of temple certificates dates from the 1630s onward."
- **Evidence:** TASJ vol. 6 text opened (archive.org transactionsasi30japagoog): Satow says the edict "was accompanied by a set of fifteen rules intended to guide the priests who were to guarantee the orthodoxy of their parishioners"; rule 2 etc. refer to objectors to temple contributions. en.wikipedia "Danka system" (opened), following Tamamuro Fumio: the document "appeared around 1735 … Dated 1613 … it is demonstrably a forgery, probably created by the temples themselves … believed genuine even by most Meiji period historians". (The rules also name the Fuju-fuse and Hiden sects, banned decades after 1614 — own knowledge.)
- **Confidence:** medium-high

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/japan-martyrs.md
- **Text:** "On 27 January 1614 the government of [[person:tokugawa-ieyasu]] issued a proclamation"
- **Problem:** 27 January 1614 is Satow's conversion of "the twelfth month of Keichō 18" (no day given). The statement drafted by Konchiin Sūden is normally dated Keichō 18.12.23 and issued under Hidetada's seal, i.e. 1 February 1614 (New Style); 27 January (Boxer also uses it) corresponds to 12.18. The narrative gives one day as fact although the dating note itself shows the day rests on Satow alone.
- **Should be:** "In the twelfth month of Keichō 18 (late January / 1 February 1614) …", or keep 27 January but attribute it ("which Satow dates 27 January 1614") and add that Japanese sources date the issue to the 23rd day of the twelfth month (1 February 1614).
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (Sūden's *Ikoku nikki*: drafted night of 12.22, issued 12.23); not re-verified online. Keichō 19.1.1 = 9 February 1614, so 12.23 = 1 February.
- **Confidence:** low-medium

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/jesuits-in-ethiopia.md
- **Text:** dating note: "Tellez puts … the battle on 27 June 1632 … [the chronicle] puts the battle on Monday the third of the month Sane … The two reckonings are given here side by side and are not converted."
- **Problem:** The two are not parallel expressions of one date; they disagree by twenty days, and the note hides this by declining to convert. 3 Sane of that year is 28 May Julian = 7 June 1632 Gregorian, which was a Monday, exactly as the chronicle says; 27 June 1632 (Gregorian) was a Sunday. The usual scholarly date for Wayna Dega is 7 June 1632, so "27th" in the 1710 English abridgement of Tellez is probably a misprint or error for "7th". Likewise 5 Sane = 9 June and 21 Sane = 25 June for the proclamation, and 10 Maskaram = 17 September 1632 for the death.
- **Should be:** Convert the chronicle's dates (battle Monday 3 Sane = 7 June 1632 N.S.; proclamation 5 or 21 Sane = 9 or 25 June; death 10 Maskaram = 17 September) and say that Tellez's English text prints 27 June for the battle, which does not agree with the chronicle's weekday.
- **Evidence:** Tellez 1710 text opened (archive.org travelsofjesuits00alme): "On the 27th of June 1632, his Scouts brought him Word …"; "The next Day the Slain appear'd to be about 8000." Calendar arithmetic: Sane 1 = 26 May Julian = 5 June Gregorian in 1632; 7 June 1632 Gregorian = Monday (1 Jan 1632 Gregorian was a Thursday).
- **Confidence:** medium (arithmetic high; that Tellez's Portuguese original reads "7" not checked)

### C · historical · src/content/events/reformation/teresa-of-avila.md
- **Text:** "They kept the Carmelite rule in the form given in 1248"
- **Problem:** 1248 is Teresa's own figure (Life 36.27: "given in the year 1248, in the fifth year of the pontificate of Innocent IV"), but the entry gives it in the wiki's voice. Innocent IV's revision of the rule (*Quae honorem Conditoris*) is dated 1 October 1247, which is indeed in his fifth year (elected June 1243).
- **Should be:** "in the form which, she says, was given in 1248 under Innocent IV (the bull is dated 1 October 1247)".
- **Evidence:** ccel.org/ccel/teresa/life/life.viii.xxxvii.html opened, §27 quoted above; date of *Quae honorem Conditoris* from own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/events/reformation/synod-of-diamper.md
- **Text:** "The acts count 813 members, 133 of them priests and 660 lay representatives"
- **Problem:** The figure reproduces Geddes's English ("133 Priests, besides Deacons and Sub-Deacons … and 660 Procurators"), but 133 + 660 is not 813, and the standard figure from Gouvea/the Portuguese acts is 153 priests and 660 laymen (= 813). "133" is almost certainly a misprint in Geddes that the entry passes on.
- **Should be:** "813 members, 153 of them priests (Geddes's translation prints 133) and 660 lay representatives".
- **Evidence:** Geddes text opened (archive.org historyofchurcho00gedd, p. 423): "The Synod consisted of 81[3], viz. 133 Priests, besides Deacons and Sub-Deacons, and others of the Clergy, and 660 Procurators of the People". en.wikipedia "Synod of Diamper" quoting the Gorgias Encyclopedic Dictionary of the Syriac Heritage: "attended by 153 local priests and 660 lay representatives".
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/union-of-brest.md
- **Text:** "The union was proclaimed at a synod at [[place:brest]] in October 1596 … some of the Ruthenian bishops had not accepted the union … Prince [[person:konstanty-ostrogski]], palatine of Kiev, stayed outside it."
- **Problem:** Two synods sat at Brest in October 1596. The opponents (Ostrogski, the bishops Gedeon Balaban of Lviv and Michael Kopystensky of Przemyśl, with Nicephorus, protosyncellus of Constantinople, and Cyril Lucaris for Alexandria) held their own council in the town, rejected the union and declared the uniting bishops deposed, while the uniate synod deposed and excommunicated Balaban and Kopystensky. The entry tells only the uniate synod and only through papal documents; the Orthodox side appears merely as a prince who "stayed outside". For an event the wiki elsewhere treats with a statement from each confession, the rival council is essential.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence on the counter-synod and the mutual depositions (with a primary source, e.g. the protestation of the Orthodox assembly of 9 October 1596 O.S., or the acts printed in *Akty Zapadnoi Rossii* vol. 4), and name the two bishops who refused.
- **Evidence:** Catholic Encyclopedia, "Union of Brest" (newadvent.org/cathen/15130a.htm, opened): Orthodox "sent … Nicephorus, the protosyncellus of the Patriarch of Constantinople, and Cyril Lucaris … held a conciliabulum …"; "Bishops Gideon Balaban, of Lemberg, and Michael Kopystenski, of Przemysl, having declared themselves opposed to the union, were deposed and excommunicated … The enemies of the union published, on 9 October, a protest".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/union-of-brest.md
- **Text:** dating note: "For the proclamation at Brest this entry follows the Holy See's own reckoning, 16 October 1596"
- **Problem:** 16 October (New Style) = 6 October Old Style is the day the bishops assembled; the declaration of union was read in St Nicholas's church on 9 October O.S. (19 October N.S.), which is the date carried by the synod's own act. John Paul II's letter does say "solemnly proclaimed … on 16 October 1596", so the entry reports it correctly, but the note should not leave the impression that 16 October is the documented day of the proclamation, and it does not say which calendar is meant although the articles are dated "by the old calendar".
- **Should be:** "The synod met from 6 October 1596 (Old Style; 16 October New Style) and the act of union was read on 9/19 October; John Paul II's letter gives 16 October for the proclamation."
- **Evidence:** Catholic Encyclopedia, "Union of Brest" (opened): "About 6 October the metropolitan … met at Brest … the Ruthenian bishops, on 9 October … read the declaration of the Ruthenian episcopate accepting the union". vatican.va apostolic letter of 12 Nov 1995, §1 (opened): "solemnly proclaimed at Brest-Litovsk … on 16 October 1596".
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/moscow-patriarchate.md
- **Text:** "In May 1590 a council at Constantinople confirmed what Jeremias had done. … Jeremias, the patriarchs of Antioch and Jerusalem and eighty-one metropolitans, archbishops and bishops signed it"
- **Problem:** The story stops at 1590. The council of 1590 met without the patriarch of Alexandria (the see was vacant; Meletius Pegas, once elected, objected that Jeremias had acted without the other patriarchs), and it was the council of Constantinople of February 1593, with Meletius present, that gave the full assent of the four eastern patriarchs and again fixed Moscow in the fifth place (Moscow had asked for a higher one). The wiki's own source record for the 1589 charter links "the Constantinople acts of 1590 and 1593", but the event never mentions 1593. The entry also gives only the Russian court's version of the negotiation; the Greek participants' accounts (Hierotheos of Monemvasia, who says Jeremias was pressed and acted against his advice; Arsenios of Elasson) are not mentioned, and the 81 signatures are reported in the wiki's voice from the Russian translation although the genuineness of many signatures on the 1590 charter has been questioned on palaeographic grounds (B. L. Fonkich).
- **Should be:** Add: "A second council at Constantinople in 1593, at which Meletius Pegas of Alexandria was present, confirmed the patriarchate and its fifth rank." Attribute the signature count ("the Russian record says that …") and note in one sentence that the Greek metropolitan Hierotheos of Monemvasia, who travelled with Jeremias, tells the negotiation differently.
- **Evidence:** `src/content/sources/moscow-patriarchate-charter-1589.md` (link label "Constantinople acts of 1590 and 1593"); Muravyov text opened at azbyka.ru (records the tsar's letters to "Meletius of Alexandria, newly installed in place of the deceased Silvester", confirming Alexandria had no part in 1590); 1593 council, Hierotheos and Fonkich from own knowledge (not opened).
- **Confidence:** high for the 1593 council; medium-low for the remark on the signatures

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/st-bartholomews-day-massacre.md
- **Text:** "The French authorities gave other accounts. A proclamation in Normandy on 28 August put the slaughter in Paris down to the private quarrel between Guise and the admiral"
- **Problem:** Two things a careful reader expects are missing. (1) The king's own change of account: after first blaming the Guise feud (royal letters of 24 August, echoed in Matignon's proclamation), Charles IX declared before the Parlement of Paris on 26 August that what had been done was by his command, to forestall a Huguenot conspiracy; the entry jumps from the 28 August proclamation to the October decree and so hides that the Crown itself took responsibility within two days. (2) The reaction of Rome: Gregory XIII had a *Te Deum* sung, went in procession to San Luigi dei Francesi, struck a medal ("Ugonottorum strages 1572") and had Vasari paint the scenes in the Sala Regia. This is the best-known confessional aftermath of the event and is absent from the whole wiki (grep "Gregory XIII": no hits).
- **Should be:** Add the king's declaration of 26 August (primary: the Parlement's register or the royal declaration of 28 August 1572) and one sentence on Gregory XIII's thanksgiving and medal; a sentence on Protestant reaction (e.g. Geneva's fast, Elizabeth's reception of the French ambassador) would balance it.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; CSP Foreign vol. 10 nos. 545, 583, 584, 622 opened (they match what the entry reports and contain neither point).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/westminster-assembly.md
- **Text:** summary: "The Church of Scotland approves the Confession in 1647, and the Scottish Parliament ratifies it in 1690." Body: "The Confession was presented to Parliament as the Assembly's 'humble advice' and printed in 1647."
- **Problem:** The entry never says what the body that called the Assembly did with its work. The English Parliament did not adopt the Confession as presented: it required Scripture proofs (1647) and in June 1648 passed it only in an amended form, as "Articles of Christian Religion", leaving out chapters 30 and 31 (church censures; synods) and parts of 20 and 24. The Scottish Parliament first ratified the Confession on 7 February 1649 (an act rescinded in 1661), so 1690 was a re-ratification. The Assembly's other documents — the Directory for the Public Worship of God (which Parliament substituted for the Book of Common Prayer in January 1645), the Form of Presbyterial Church-Government and the Larger Catechism — are not mentioned, and the Independents' dissent ("Apologeticall Narration", 1644) and the later use of the Confession by Congregationalists (Savoy 1658) and Baptists (1677/1689) are absent.
- **Should be:** Add: "Parliament approved the doctrinal chapters, with alterations, in June 1648 under the title *Articles of Christian Religion*; the Scottish Parliament ratified the Confession in February 1649 and again in 1690." Mention the Directory (1645) and the Larger Catechism (1648) beside the Shorter.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (Commons Journal, 20–21 June 1648; Acts of the Parliaments of Scotland, 7 Feb. 1649; ordinance of 4 January 1645 for the Directory); grep of the wiki: "Directory" occurs only in the Covenant's phrase, "Savoy Declaration" and "London Confession" not at all.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/first-baptists.md
- **Text:** "Smyth baptized first himself, then [[person:thomas-helwys]], then the rest" / title "The First Baptist Congregations"
- **Problem:** Three things needed to read the entry rightly are missing. (1) The baptism of 1609 was by pouring, not immersion; immersion was adopted by English Baptists only about 1641. A modern reader will assume the opposite. (2) The outcome of the split: Smyth and most of the group applied to join the Waterlander Mennonites of Amsterdam (Smyth died in 1612; his followers were received in 1615), and Helwys, after addressing the king, was imprisoned in Newgate and was dead by 1616. (3) The entry describes only the General (Arminian) Baptists — it notes that the confession teaches general redemption and falling from grace — without saying that the Calvinist Particular Baptists arose separately in London in the 1630s (confession of 1644), the line from which most later Baptists descend. The wiki mentions Particular Baptists only in the William Carey material.
- **Should be:** Add a closing sentence or two on Smyth's application to the Mennonites, Helwys's imprisonment, and the separate rise of the Particular Baptists and of immersion (1638–1644).
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (Smyth's "Corde credimus" and the Mennonite records in Burrage, *Early English Dissenters*; First London Confession 1644); grep "Particular Baptist", "Waterlander", "Mennonite", "London Confession".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/galileo-affair.md
- **Text:** "The minutes record that the pope then had Bellarmine warn Galileo to give up the opinion, that the commissary of the Holy Office ordered him not to hold, teach or defend it in any way, and that Galileo promised to obey" … "he had obtained leave to print it without mentioning the order of 1616"
- **Problem:** The entry presents the commissary's personal injunction of 26 February 1616 as plain record. That unsigned minute is the most disputed document of the case: in 1633 Galileo answered that he remembered only Bellarmine's oral warning and produced Bellarmine's signed certificate of 26 May 1616, which says only that he had been told the Copernican doctrine "cannot be defended or held" (no "teach in any way", no injunction, no abjuration). The sentence of 1633 quotes both documents. Leaving the certificate and Galileo's defence out makes the 1633 charge look uncontested. The entry has no dating/scholarship note where the long debate on the minute's regularity could be flagged.
- **Should be:** Add: "At his trial Galileo produced a certificate Bellarmine had given him on 26 May 1616, stating only that he had been notified that the opinion could not be defended or held, and said he did not remember the commissary's further order (deposition of 12 April 1633 and the certificate, both in Opere 19; locate the pages in the scan)." Optionally a note that historians dispute whether the minute of 26 February records an order properly given.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge of the trial documents (Favaro, *Opere* 19: deposition of 12 April 1633 and Bellarmine's certificate; the sentence itself recites the certificate); not re-opened online in this audit.
- **Confidence:** high (that the certificate and defence exist and are omitted)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/regnans-in-excelsis.md
- **Text:** title "…and the Execution of Catholic Priests under Elizabeth I"; "By the Catholics' own catalogue, it adds, not above sixty had been executed in twenty-five years"
- **Problem:** The only figure for the executions is the government's polemical one of 1583, and the narrative ends in 1584. Most of the executions came afterwards, under the act of 1585 that made the mere presence of a seminary priest treason (the wiki tells that act under `elizabethan-settlement`, but this entry — whose title promises the executions — gives no sense of their scale or duration: roughly 120–130 priests and about 60 lay people between 1577 and 1603, 1588 being the worst year). The context that prompted the bull, the rising of the northern earls in late 1569, which the bull was meant to support and which had already failed, is also absent, as is the name of the pope (Gregory XIII) who gave the explanation of 1580.
- **Should be:** One sentence in the dating/scholarship note or the last paragraph on the total and the period (naming the list used, e.g. the catalogue of the martyrs beatified and canonised, or a named study), and one clause on the northern rising of 1569.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; *Execution of Justice* 1675 reprint opened on archive.org: "exceed not for these [twen]ty five years space, above the number of threescore … in the time of Queen Mary … almost the number of four hundred" (the entry reports this correctly).
- **Confidence:** medium (omission; exact totals vary by list)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/thirty-years-war.md
- **Text:** "The peace treaty of 1648 describes what followed … In 1629 Ferdinand ordered the return of every bishopric …"
- **Problem:** The narrative goes from the defenestration (1618) straight to 1629 by way of the treaty's preamble. It leaves out the Bohemian election of the Calvinist Frederick V and the battle of the White Mountain (8 November 1620) with the forced re-Catholicisation of Bohemia that followed — the outcome of the revolt with which the entry opens — and it never says that Catholic France fought on the side of Sweden and the German Protestants from 1635, which is the main reason the war cannot be read as simply confessional. The 1624 "normal year" is also given without its chief exception: the emperor's hereditary lands (art. 5 §§38–41 give only limited concessions to Silesian and Lower Austrian Protestants), so Bohemia and Austria stayed closed to Protestant worship.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence on White Mountain and its consequence for Bohemian Protestantism, a clause on France's entry in 1635, and "except in the Habsburg hereditary lands" after the 1624 rule.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; grep "White Mountain" in `src/content`: no hits.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/synod-of-dort.md
- **Text:** "The synod deposed the ministers it had cited until they should repent, and barred from the ministry anyone who refused to subscribe; the States General confirmed its judgment"
- **Problem:** The civil side of the outcome is missing. The synod could meet only after Maurice of Nassau's coup of 1618 against the States of Holland; Johan van Oldenbarnevelt, the Remonstrants' protector, was beheaded on 13 May 1619, four days after the foreign delegates were dismissed, and Hugo Grotius was sentenced to life imprisonment; some two hundred Remonstrant ministers were deprived and about eighty banished, until toleration returned after 1625. The entry reads as a purely doctrinal proceeding. (Grep: "Oldenbarnevelt", "Grotius" — no hits in the wiki.)
- **Should be:** One or two sentences on Oldenbarnevelt, Grotius and the banishment of Remonstrant ministers, with a primary source (e.g. the sentence on Oldenbarnevelt, or the Remonstrants' own *Acta et scripta synodalia*, 1620).
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; grep of `src/content`.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/matteo-ricci-in-china.md
- **Text:** "In May 1600 [[person:matteo-ricci]], Pantoja and a Chinese Jesuit brother left Nanjing by boat"
- **Problem:** The entry begins in 1600 and never says that Ricci had entered China in 1583 with Michele Ruggieri and worked for eighteen years at Zhaoqing, Shaozhou, Nanchang and Nanjing, changing from the dress of Buddhist monks to that of the literati — the background without which the "catechism that argues from the Chinese classics" and the scholars' conversions are not intelligible.
