# Sources first cited for events c. 1558–1782

Reviewer batch S05. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/sources/constantinople-synodal-charter-1590.md
- **Text:** "According to the Russian record the charter was signed by Jeremias … and was delivered in Moscow in June 1590 by Dionysius, metropolitan of Tarnovo."
- **Problem:** The charter is dated at Constantinople in May 1590 (7098); it cannot have reached Smolensk the same month and Moscow in June 1590. Dionysius of Tarnovo brought it a year later, in 1591. The "May 1590" in Muravyov's digest is a slip that Muravyov's own sentence exposes ("two years after the patriarch's departure", i.e. after May 1589), and the record repeats it as the date of delivery. The same wrong year is used as the locator in `events/reformation/moscow-patriarchate.md` ("in Muravyov, under June 1590").
- **Should be:** "…and was brought to Russia by Dionysius, metropolitan of Tarnovo, who reached Smolensk in May 1591 and delivered it to the tsar in June 1591. (Muravyov's digest prints 'May 1590' for his arrival, although it places it 'two years after the departure of the patriarch'.)"
- **Evidence:** azbyka.ru Muravyov ch. 5, section "Пришествие Митрополита Тырновского Дионисия": "Два года спустя после отшествия Патриарха, в Мае 1590 года, воеводы Смоленские повестили… что приехал из города Тырнова Митрополит Дионисий"; the same chapter has the tsar's reply letters name Meletius as the *newly installed* patriarch of Alexandria (elected August 1590) and Dionysius himself say the council met partly because "the Alexandrian had died". orthochristian.com/111637.html: "The tomos was delivered to Moscow in May 1591 by Metropolitan Dionysiy of Tarnovo, and handed to Tsar Feodor Ivanovich on June 20."
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/constantinople-synodal-charter-1590.md
- **Text:** "signed by Jeremias, Joachim of Antioch and Sophronius of Jerusalem and by forty-two metropolitans, nineteen archbishops and twenty bishops"
- **Problem:** The record reports the signature count from the Russian record without noting (a) that the see of Alexandria was vacant and did not sign, which is why a second council was held in February 1593 with Meletius Pegas present, and (b) that the authenticity of many of the signatures on the Greek original has been challenged palaeographically (B. L. Fonkich argued that a large part were written by a few hands). The brief asks records to flag serious authenticity debates.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: the patriarch of Alexandria did not sign (the see was vacant; Dionysius's own letter says so), a further council of 1593 confirmed the act with all four eastern patriarchs represented, and the genuineness of many of the 1590 signatures is disputed in modern scholarship (Fonkich).
- **Evidence:** Muravyov ch. 5 (Dionysius's letter: "для того еще собрал всех Архиереев и Патриархов, что Александрийскаго не стало"); azbyka.ru/dokumenty-ob-uchrezhdenii-moskovskogo-patriarhata lists the 1593 act as a separate document. Fonkich's signature study: own knowledge, not re-located online in this session — check B. L. Fonkich, "Акт Константинопольского собора 1593 г. об основании Московского патриархата" / his study of the 1590 gramota before citing.
- **Confidence:** medium for (a), low for (b)

### B · dating · src/content/sources/zelo-domus-dei.md
- **Text:** `written.display: "26 November 1648"`; "The brief is dated at Rome, at St Mary Major, on 26 November 1648"
- **Problem:** The date on the brief is not the date of issue. The brief was published only on 20 August 1650 (as an official placard print of the Camera Apostolica) and bears the earlier date 26 November 1648; the record gives no hint of this, and `events/reformation/thirty-years-war.md` consequently says the pope acted "a month later".
- **Should be:** `display: "dated 26 November 1648; published 20 August 1650"` and a sentence in the body: "The brief carries the date 26 November 1648 but was not published until 20 August 1650; until then the only public papal objection was the protest of the nuncio Fabio Chigi at Münster in 1648, which the brief itself recalls."
- **Evidence:** de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelo_domus_Dei: "Das Breve Zelo domus Dei wurde am 20. August 1650 durch Papst Innozenz X. … veröffentlicht. Mit dem auf den 26. November 1648 zurückdatierten päpstlichen Breve…"; en.wikipedia "Pope Innocent X": issued 1650, "backdated to 1648". The standard study, cited from memory and to be confirmed before use, is K. Repgen, "Der päpstliche Protest gegen den Westfälischen Frieden und die Friedenspolitik Urbans VIII." (Historisches Jahrbuch 75, 1956).
- **Confidence:** high (publication in August 1650); medium on whether the text was drafted in 1648 or later

### B · dating · src/content/sources/ieyasu-edict-1614.md
- **Text:** `author: Tokugawa Ieyasu`; `display: "27 January 1614 (twelfth month of Keichō 18)"`; "Satow, who translated it, gives the date as 27 January 1614"
- **Problem:** Satow's 1878 conversion is stated in the front matter as the date. By the Japanese record the statement on the expulsion of the padres (Bateren tsuihō no fumi) was drafted for Ieyasu by the Zen monk Konchiin (Ishin) Sūden and promulgated under the seal of the shogun Hidetada on Keichō 18/12/23, which is 1 February 1614; a ban order had gone out on 12/19 = 28 January. 27 January corresponds to neither day. The record names neither Sūden as the drafter nor Hidetada's seal (it only reports Murdoch 1903 that it "appeared under the name of Hidetada").
- **Should be:** `author: "Drafted by Konchiin Sūden for Tokugawa Ieyasu; issued under the seal of the shogun Tokugawa Hidetada"`; `display: "Keichō 18, 12th month, 23rd day (1 February 1614); Satow gives 27 January"`, with the body saying that Satow's date differs from the day given in the Japanese record. The event `japan-martyrs` ("On 27 January 1614 the government of Tokugawa Ieyasu issued…") needs the same correction.
- **Evidence:** ja.wikipedia 禁教令 (citing Gonoi Takashi, 日本キリスト教史, 1990, pp. 150–151): "慶長18年12月19日（新暦1614年1月28日）、重ねて禁教令が布告される。12月23日には、臨済宗の僧である以心崇伝が起草した「伴天連追放之文」に、将軍徳川秀忠が捺印して全国に公布された". Arithmetic: 12/19 = 28 Jan, so 12/23 = 1 Feb 1614.
- **Confidence:** medium-high (date and drafter); the record's attribution of 27 January to Satow is itself accurate

### B · dating · src/content/sources/bradford-of-plymouth-plantation.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1646, circa: true, display: "written over many years; parts dated by the author to about 1645–1646"`; "so that much of the work was written a generation after the first events"; "The date given above for the writing rests on those internal marks"
- **Problem:** Bradford states in the manuscript itself when he began: "these scribled writings (which was aboute y^e year 1630, and so peeced up at times of leasure afterward)". The first book (England, Leiden, the voyage, the landing) is therefore about ten years after the events, not a generation; the annals were written in the mid-1640s and the list of passengers at the end is dated 1650 ("this year 1650"). The record overlooks the author's own date and so overstates the gap for the part of the work the wiki cites most (the voyage and compact).
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1630, end: 1650`, display "begun about 1630 (Bradford's own note); annals written c. 1644–1650". Body: "Bradford says in a note added to the first book that he began 'aboute ye year 1630'; the second book was written in the mid-1640s (he remarks under 1621 that the peace 'hath now continued this 24 years'), and the closing list of passengers is dated 1650."
- **Evidence:** Project Gutenberg 24950 (mirror gutenberg.pglaf.org/2/4/9/5/24950/24950-8.txt), "A late observation, as it were by the way, worthy to be Noted": "when I first begane these scribled writings (which was aboute y^e year 1630…)"; passenger list: "his sone Love lived till this year 1650".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/sources/cyril-lucaris-eastern-confession.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1631, display: "Dated at Constantinople, January 1631"`; `author: Published under the name of Cyril Lucaris`; "The Synod of Jerusalem held that Cyril did not write it"
- **Problem:** Two things a reader needs are missing. (1) The Confession first appeared in Latin at Geneva in March 1629 (with French, English and German versions the same year); the text dated January 1631 is the later Greek version with the four added questions, printed at Geneva in 1633. The record's single date hides that the confession had been in print for two years. (2) On authorship the record gives only the synod's denial. Lucaris never disowned it publicly, synods at Constantinople in 1638 and Jassy in 1642 condemned it (the first naming him), and most modern historians accept it as his; an autograph Greek manuscript is preserved at Geneva. As it stands the record reads as if pseudonymity were the open, likelier option.
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1629, end: 1631`, display "Latin, Geneva 1629; Greek text with four questions dated January 1631, printed Geneva 1633". Add: "Lucaris did not repudiate it in public; a council at Constantinople condemned it together with him in 1638. Most modern scholars regard it as his own work (the Greek text survives in his hand at Geneva), while the Synod of Jerusalem and part of later Orthodox scholarship deny it."
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia "Cyril Lucaris": "In 1629 in Geneva the Eastern Confession of the Christian Faith was published in Latin… In 1633 it was published in Greek. The Council of Constantinople in 1638 anathematised both Cyril I and the Eastern Confession"; Schaff, Creeds of Christendom, vol. 1 §15 (ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds1.v.v.html): "first written by him in Latin, 1629, and then in Greek, with an addition of four questions and answers, 1631, and published in both languages at Geneva, 1633". Geneva autograph: own knowledge (Bibliothèque de Genève, MS grec 37–38) — verify the shelf-mark before citing.
- **Confidence:** high for (1); medium for the wording of (2)

### C · dating · src/content/sources/alexander-vii-ad-sanctam-beati-petri-sedem.md
- **Text:** "from the constitution *Regiminis apostolici*, which it dates 15 February 1664"
- **Problem:** The 1911 Denzinger's "1664" is the year of the Incarnation as written in the bull (year beginning 25 March); by the modern calendar *Regiminis apostolici* was given on 15 February 1665, and that is the date in current reference works (and in later Denzinger editions). The record, and `events/awakenings-and-missions/jansenism.md`, pass the 1664 on without the conversion — the same Old Style trap the wiki handles carefully elsewhere.
- **Should be:** "…which it dates 15 February 1664; the bull is dated by the year of the Incarnation, and by modern reckoning the day is 15 February 1665."
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiminis_Apostolici: "Signature date 15 February 1665"; Denzinger–Hünermann no. 2020 heading (own knowledge).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/ethiopian-abridged-chronicle.md
- **Text:** `display: "Compiled probably 1729–1753 from court annals"`; "probably under Iyasu II (1729–1753)"
- **Problem:** The reign dates are those of Basset's 1881 chronology and are given without that qualification, also in the front matter. Iyasu II reigned from 19 September 1730 to 27 June 1755.
- **Should be:** "probably under Iyasu II (1730–1755; Basset has 1729–1753)", and `written: start: 1730, end: 1755`.
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyasu_II: "Reign 19 September 1730 – 27 June 1755".
- **Confidence:** medium (I did not open Basset's introduction in Journal asiatique 17 to see the years he prints)

### C · historical · src/content/sources/mourts-relation.md
- **Text:** "The book was printed in London in 1622 from papers carried back on the first ship to return."
- **Problem:** The first ship to return was the Mayflower herself, which sailed for England in April 1621. The papers (including the letter dated 11 December 1621 that the record cites) went home on the Fortune, which had arrived in November 1621 and left in mid-December.
- **Should be:** "…from papers carried back on the Fortune, which left Plymouth in December 1621."
- **Evidence:** Bradford, Of Plymouth Plantation, under 1621: "This ship (caled y^e Fortune) was speedily dispatcht away, being laden with good clapbord…"; the letter itself is dated 11 December 1621, eight months after the Mayflower's departure.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/allen-briefe-historie.md
- **Text:** summary: "Its author headed the seminary at Douai that trained priests for England"
- **Problem:** When Allen wrote in 1582 the college he headed was at Rheims, where it had moved from Douai in 1578 (it returned in 1593). The wiki's own record `allen-defence-of-english-catholics` has Campion consulting Allen "at Rheims", and the treason indictment quoted there names "Rome and Rheims".
- **Should be:** "Its author had founded the English seminary at Douai (then at Rheims) that trained priests for England".
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (English College, Douai: founded 1568, at Rheims 1578–1593); internal: allen-defence-of-english-catholics.md.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/frois-martyrdom-of-nagasaki.md
- **Text:** `language: Latin (the version printed in the Acta Sanctorum)`; "Latin text headed 'Martyrii historia auctore Ludovico Froes'"
- **Problem:** The record never says what language Fróis wrote in or how the Latin came about, so a reader takes the Latin for his text. Fróis wrote his relation in Portuguese (dated 15 March 1597, addressed to the general Claudio Acquaviva); it was first printed in an Italian translation by Gasparo Spitilli (Rome: Zannetti, 1599), and the Bollandists' Latin is a translation of that. The wording cited is therefore at two removes from the author, as the wiki says elsewhere of Lobo and De Thou.
- **Should be:** `language: Portuguese (read here in the Latin version of the Acta Sanctorum, made from the Italian translation printed at Rome in 1599)`, and one sentence in the body saying so, with the letter's own date, 15 March 1597.
- **Evidence:** Kyoto University Rare Materials Digital Archive, rmda.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/en/item/rb00033976 (catalogue of *Relatione della gloriosa morte di XXVI. posti in croce… mandata dal P. Luigi Frois alli 15. di Marzo al R. P. Claudio Acquaviva… fatta in Italiano dal P. Gasparo Spitilli*, Rome 1599), as returned by web search; I did not open the Acta Sanctorum preface to see what the Bollandists say of their Vorlage.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/fox-journal.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1694, display: "First printed 1694"`; "George Fox's own account … first edited for the press in 1694, three years after his death."
- **Problem:** The record gives only the year of printing and does not state when the text was composed or how far the printed Journal is Fox's wording. Fox dictated the main narrative about 1675–76 (to his stepson-in-law Thomas Lower), a quarter-century or more after the events of 1647–1660 for which the wiki cites it; and the 1694 text (which both the 1852 edition and Jones's abridgement follow) was prepared by Thomas Ellwood for a committee of Friends, who revised, omitted and smoothed the dictated manuscript. Neither the gap nor the editor is mentioned.
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1675, end: 1694`, display "dictated c. 1675–76; edited by Thomas Ellwood and printed 1694"; add: "The printed text was prepared after Fox's death by Thomas Ellwood, who revised the dictated manuscript; the narrative of the 1640s and 1650s was set down some twenty-five years afterwards."
- **Evidence:** Jones's preface to the abridgement the record links (ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.ii.html): "his first editors, in 1694… contented themselves with improving the diction, introducing some system into the spelling, and cutting out an occasional anecdote… The original manuscript [the Spence MS] is a little livelier, fresher and more graphic than any published edition". Dictation c. 1675–76 and Ellwood as editor: own knowledge (Spence MS, ed. N. Penney, Cambridge 1911).
- **Confidence:** medium-high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/saint-simon-memoirs.md
- **Text:** "Saint-Simon writes of the court as one who was there" (no `written` field)
- **Problem:** The record states no date of composition and so hides the gap. Saint-Simon wrote the Mémoires in their final form between about 1739 and 1749, thirty to forty years after the destruction of Port-Royal (1709) for which he is cited, working in part from Dangeau's journal; they were not printed in full until 1829–30. The record's standard elsewhere ("written long afterwards", "a later narrative") is not applied here.
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1739, end: 1749, circa: true`, display "written c. 1739–1749; first printed in full 1829–1830", and a sentence stating that the account of 1709 was written some thirty years later.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (standard datings in the Boislisle and Coirault editions).
- **Confidence:** medium-high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/spiritual-regulation-1721.md
- **Text:** `author: Issued in the name of Peter I of Russia`
- **Problem:** The record does not name the man who wrote the text. The Regulation was drafted for Peter in 1718–1720 by Feofan Prokopovich, then bishop of Pskov — the wiki has a person entry for him (`people/feofan-prokopovich.md`), but the source record, which the brief expects to state authorship, is silent, and the arguments of Part 1 (why a college is better than one man) are his.
- **Should be:** `author: "Feofan Prokopovich (drafter); issued in the name of Peter I of Russia"`, with a sentence in the body: "The text was drafted by Feofan Prokopovich, bishop of Pskov, and amended by the tsar (the closing note records his corrections of 11 February 1720)."
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (P. V. Verkhovskoi, *Uchrezhdenie Dukhovnoi kollegii i Dukhovnyi reglament*, 1916; J. Cracraft, *The Church Reform of Peter the Great*, 1971).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/sources/formula-of-concord.md
- **Text:** "the creeds, the Augsburg Confession and Luther's catechisms are received as witnesses to how Scripture has been understood"
- **Problem:** The list of secondary norms in the "Summary Content, Rule and Standard" is given as if complete but drops two of the documents the Formula names: the Apology of the Augsburg Confession and the Smalcald Articles (and the Formula specifies the "first, unaltered" Augsburg Confession, a qualifier that was the point at issue with the Philippists). The authoring guide warns against paraphrases of confessions that drop terms.
- **Should be:** "…the three creeds, the first, unaltered Augsburg Confession, its Apology, the Smalcald Articles and Luther's two catechisms are received as witnesses…"
- **Evidence:** Epitome, Summary Content, Rule and Standard, §§3–5 (bookofconcord.org/epitome/): "the first, unaltered Augsburg Confession… together with its Apology, and the Articles composed at Smalcald in the year 1537… the Small and Large Catechisms of Dr. Luther".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/paul-of-aleppo-travels-of-macarius.md
- **Text:** "Paul wrote in Arabic as a visitor from the Greek-speaking East"
- **Problem:** Self-contradictory and inaccurate. Paul and his father Macarius III ibn al-Zaʿim were Arabic-speaking Syrians of the patriarchate of Antioch; the patriarchate belonged to the Greek (Byzantine-rite) Orthodox communion, but it was not Greek-speaking, and Paul repeatedly notes his own and his father's imperfect Greek. The record also gives no dates for the journey or the writing (the visit to Muscovy was 1654–56, the whole journey 1652–59).
- **Should be:** "Paul wrote in Arabic, as an Arabic-speaking cleric of the Greek Orthodox patriarchate of Antioch who visited Muscovy in 1654–1656…"
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; the record's own `language: Arabic`.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/sources/fell-womens-speaking-justified.md
- **Text:** "the objection drawn from 1 Corinthians 14:34–35 and 1 Timothy 2:11–12 … reads Paul's command of silence as addressed to women who were in confusion"
- **Problem:** "Paul's command" in the wiki's own voice covers 1 Timothy 2:11–12, a letter whose authorship the authoring guide lists as disputed and not to be asserted. It is Fell who treats both passages as Paul's.
- **Should be:** "…reads the commands of silence, both of which she takes as Paul's, as addressed to…" (or "the command of silence in 1 Corinthians and 1 Timothy").
- **Evidence:** docs/authoring-guide.md, "Disputed authorship asserted"; 1 Tim 2:11–12 and 1 Cor 14:34–35 read in the WEB.
- **Confidence:** medium (a policy point, not a factual error)

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/rules-for-the-king-james-translators.md
- **Text:** (no `author` field) "The rules survive in copies… They carry no date of their own. The date given here is that of the Hampton Court conference of January 1604, after which the work was set in hand."
- **Problem:** The record names no issuing authority. The rules are regularly attributed to Richard Bancroft, bishop of London, acting for the king while Canterbury was vacant; and the volume the record cites gives a closer date than "after January 1604": Pollard's introduction (pp. 47–48) quotes Bancroft's letter of 30 June 1604 telling the Cambridge company that the king approves the choice of translators and that they are to begin at once, and prints his circulars of 31 July 1604 (doc. LX, pp. 331–335) on the "four and fifty" learned men appointed.
- **Should be:** `author: "Attributed to Richard Bancroft, bishop of London, for James I"`; body: "Bancroft's letters of 30 June and 31 July 1604, printed in the same volume, show the companies chosen and ordered to begin by the summer of 1604; the rules belong to that stage."
- **Evidence:** archive.org OCR of Pollard, Records of the English Bible: "by June 30 Bancroft, Bishop of London, with whom (in the vacancy of the see of Canterbury) the King communicated, was able to write: His Majesty being made acquainted with the choice of all them to be employed in the translating of the Bible…"; doc. LX A "From Fulham the 31st of July, MDCIV". The attribution of the rules to Bancroft is own knowledge (Pollard does not name their drafter on pp. 53–55).
- **Confidence:** medium (date evidence high; attribution to Bancroft medium)

## Completeness of this stretch

Primary sources of first-rank importance for c. 1558–1782 with no record in `src/content/sources/` (checked with `ls src/content/sources | grep -i …` and `grep -rl` over `src/content` for each name):

- **Galileo, *Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems* (1632)** — the book the 1633 sentence condemns; the wiki has the trial file, Bellarmine's letter and the Letter to Christina, but not the work itself (nor Copernicus's *De revolutionibus*, 1543, with Osiander's preface). Belongs with `galileo-affair`. (grep "Dialogo|Dialogue Concerning|De revolutionibus": no source.)
- **Belgic Confession (1561) and Second Helvetic Confession (1566)** — with the Heidelberg Catechism and Dort the confessional standards of the continental Reformed churches; Dort itself subscribed the Belgic. Neither name occurs anywhere in the content. Belong with `heidelberg-catechism` / `synod-of-dort`.
- **The Thirty-nine Articles are present, but not the Book of Common Prayer of 1559/1662 or the Act of Uniformity of 1662** (the Great Ejection, the legal background of the Toleration Act and of Fox's and Bunyan's imprisonments). grep "1662": only Nantes/Pascal. Nor the **Millenary Petition (1603)**, which the Barlow record presupposes ("agents for the millenary plaintiffs").
- **Roman Catechism (1566), the bull *Quo primum* and the Missal of 1570, the Sixto-Clementine Vulgate (1592), and *Inter gravissimas* (1582, the Gregorian calendar)** — the implementing documents of Trent. The wiki converts Old Style/New Style dates in dozens of entries but has no record (and no event) for the calendar reform that created the difference. grep "Quo primum", "Inter gravissimas": 0 hits; "Roman Catechism": one passing mention in a modern entry.
- **Geneva Bible (1560) and Douay–Rheims (1582/1609–10)** — the Protestant and Catholic English Bibles the KJV preface answers; the King James records mention the Geneva only in rule 14.
- **Peter Mogila's Orthodox Confession (1640, approved at Jassy 1642/Constantinople 1643)** — the other great seventeenth-century Orthodox confession, and one of the "two synods" the Jerusalem acts invoke against Lucaris. grep "Mogila|Mohyla": 0.
- **The Stoglav (1551) and Nikon's service books (Sluzhebnik 1655, Skrizhal 1656), and the council of 1656** — the 1667 acts and the 1971 act annul or cite them, but only the 1667 decree has a record; the Old Believer side has only Avvakum (no Solovki petitions).
- **Clement XI, *Ex illa die* (1715)** — the decisive papal ruling on the Chinese rites is read only through Benedict XIV's quotation of it; it deserves its own record, as does Maillard de Tournon's Nanjing decree (1707).
- **Declaration of the Clergy of France (the four Gallican Articles, 1682)**, and Febronius (1763) / the Josephine **Patent of Toleration (1781)** — the stretch ends in 1782 with no document of Gallicanism, Febronianism or Josephinism. grep "Gallican": one unrelated hit; "Patent of Toleration": 0.
- **Savoy Declaration (1658), Cambridge Platform (1648), Second London Baptist Confession (1677/1689), Roger Williams's *Bloudy Tenent* (1644), the Maryland Toleration Act (1649), Bunyan's *Pilgrim's Progress* (1678)** — Congregational and Baptist standards and the classic texts of dissent; none is named anywhere in the content. Belong with `first-baptists`, `westminster-assembly`, `pilgrims-at-plymouth`, `toleration-act`.
- **Warsaw Confederation (1573), Edict of Torda (1568), Racovian Catechism (1605)** — the east-central European toleration acts and the Socinian standard; 0 hits for each. The only "Socin-" hit is a person page.
- **Jesuit sources for the Americas** (the *Jesuit Relations* of New France; documents of the Paraguay reductions) and **the founding of Propaganda Fide (*Inscrutabili*, 1622) with its 1659 instruction to the vicars apostolic** — the wiki's 1645/1656 decrees come from Propaganda/the Holy Office but its foundation is undocumented. 0 hits for "Jesuit Relations", "Brébeuf", "Paraguay", "Inscrutabili".
- **Beginnings of biblical criticism: Spinoza, *Tractatus theologico-politicus* (1670); Richard Simon, *Histoire critique du Vieux Testament* (1678); the Reimarus fragments published by Lessing (1774–78)** — 0 hits for all three; nothing in this stretch documents the rise of historical criticism that later entries presuppose.
- Thin coverage inside existing records: the **Elizabethan Thirty-nine Articles and settlement** have statutes but no Convocation or Injunctions record; **Hooker's *Laws of Ecclesiastical Polity*** is absent; the **Dort** dossier has no Acta of the synod itself beyond the Canons (only Hales and the British report); **Westphalia** has no record for the nuncio Chigi's protest, which (not *Zelo domus Dei*) was the contemporaneous papal objection.

## Method note
All 138 records read in full. Bible citations: the batch has no `[[bible:…]]` tokens; the three passages named in prose (Deut 32:35; 1 Cor 14:34–35; 1 Tim 2:11–12) were read in the WEB. About 30 web pages/texts opened and searched, among them: Muravyov ch. 5 (azbyka.ru), Theiner *Vetera monumenta* vol. 3 OCR (docs. 198, 199, 229 and *Magnus Dominus*: all dates and contents confirmed), Morga (Gutenberg mirror: the 24/26 figures confirmed), Bradford (Gutenberg mirror), the Arminius dedication (CCEL), Teresa *Life* ch. 32 (CCEL), the Articles of Brest (Fordham), the Edict of Nantes (fr.wikisource: counts, brevets and registration dates confirmed), the Edict of Potsdam and Letter of Majesty (GHDI), John Paul II's 1992 address and 1995 letter (vatican.va), and Wikipedia/reference pages for cross-checks (Zelo domus Dei, Regiminis apostolici, Iyasu II, Lucaris, 禁教令, Fortune). Statute citations, regnal years, Old/New Style conversions, Denzinger numbers and Schaff page ranges were checked from knowledge and found consistent.
Not checked: the Wayback copy of the Philotheus edition (archive.org tunnel failed), so the manuscript shelf-mark Q.XVII.15 and the "Roman/Russian" variant are unverified; Basset's introduction, Le Gobien, the Acta Sanctorum preface to Fróis, Pollard's and Cardwell's page ranges, and the Fonkich study of the 1590 signatures were not opened. www.gutenberg.org was down for maintenance during the audit (mirror used).
