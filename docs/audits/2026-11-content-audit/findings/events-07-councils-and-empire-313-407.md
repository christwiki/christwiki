# Events: Councils and Empire, 313–407

Reviewer batch E07. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### C · dating · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/church-of-the-holy-sepulchre.md
- **Text:** "The translators of Egeria note that an old Armenian lectionary places the feast of the dedication on 23 September"
- **Problem:** The sentence reports the McClure–Feltoe footnote faithfully (it does print "September 23"), but that figure is the reading of one manuscript (Paris arm. 44, used by Conybeare) and is regarded as a copyist's slip. The Armenian Lectionary in its better witness (Jerusalem 121, ed. Renoux), like the Georgian lectionary and the later calendars, keeps the Encaenia on 13 September, with the feast of the Cross on the 14th. As the only day mentioned in the dating note, "23 September" misleads.
- **Should be:** "The old Armenian lectionary of Jerusalem places the feast of the dedication on 13 September (one manuscript, the one quoted by Egeria's translators, reads 23 September); Eusebius does not give the day."
- **Evidence:** archive.org full text of McClure & Feltoe, note to ch. 48: "In the Old Arm. Lect. we have: 'September 23, dedication of the holy places…'" (so the wiki quotes its source rightly). Translation of the Armenian Lectionary entry at users.ox.ac.uk/~mikef/durham/armen.html: "The 13th September, dedication of the Holy Places of Jerusalem… " with the note that Paris 44 assigns 23 September, "probably a copyist's error".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/council-of-nicaea.md
- **Text:** front matter `month: 5`, `confidence: firm`; dating note "20 May in the consulship of Paulinus and Julian … The day comes from Socrates … so the timeline gives only the month."
- **Problem:** The year is firm; the month is not. The note gives only Socrates's date and omits the other dated document: the heading under which the Nicene creed was read at Chalcedon dates it to 19 June 325 (XIII Kal. Iul., 19 Daisios). Because of that, the opening is placed by some in late May and by others in June (with 20 May possibly being the convocation date). A month in the front matter with `firm` and no mention of the June date overstates what is known.
- **Should be:** Keep 325 firm; either drop `month` or add to the note: "The copy of the creed read at Chalcedon in 451 is dated 19 June 325; the sessions therefore ran from late May or June into July, and it is disputed whether Socrates's 20 May is the day the council opened."
- **Evidence:** Socrates 1.13 (CCEL page opened: "in the consulate of Paulinus and Julian, on the 20th day of May"); the 19 June date of the creed is in the Acts of Chalcedon, session 2 (own knowledge; cf. the Catholic Encyclopedia art. "Nicaea, First Council of", which reconciles "20 May" with "the drawing up of the symbol [on] 19 June", and fourthcentury.com "Nicaea from the Sources 4", both seen in search results).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/pachomius.md
- **Text:** "The Life says that Pachomius was twenty when he was conscripted, during Constantine's war against Maxentius [[cite:life-youth]], which ended at the … battle of the Milvian Bridge in October 312"
- **Problem:** The Latin Life (the version cited) does name Maxentius, but the dating note then uses that as a real synchronism. Egypt in 312 belonged to Maximinus Daia, not Constantine, so recruits from the Thebaid cannot have been levied for the war against Maxentius; the Greek and Coptic Lives do not name Maxentius, and modern studies (Veilleux, *Pachomian Koinonia* I; Rousseau, *Pachomius*, the book the note itself cites) put the conscription in Maximinus Daia's war against Licinius, 312/313. The conclusion "some years after 313" is unaffected, but the note presents an anachronism of the Latin translator as the anchor.
- **Should be:** Add: "The Latin Life names Maxentius; Egypt was then under Maximinus Daia, and scholars take the war to be his campaign against Licinius in 312–313 (Rousseau, *Pachomius*, 1985). Either way the release falls in 313."
- **Evidence:** PL 73 text (archive.org OCR): "…tyrannum Maxentium gerente proelium … Pachomius annos natus viginti detentus" confirms the wiki's report of the Latin; the editor's own *notatio* in PL 73 already debates whether the war was "Maxentiano" or "Liciniano". The scholarly placing under Maximinus Daia is own knowledge; I did not open Rousseau or Veilleux.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/julian-the-apostate.md
- **Text:** "He told the high priest of Galatia to found hostels for strangers, since \"the impious Galilaeans support not only their own poor but ours as well\" [[cite:arsacius]]."
- **Problem:** The letter to Arsacius (Wright 22 = Bidez 84) is transmitted only by Sozomen (5.16) and its authenticity is disputed; it has been argued to be a Christian forgery. The entry quotes it as Julian's own words without qualification, while the same programme is attested in the undisputed "Letter to a Priest".
- **Should be:** "In a letter to the high priest of Galatia preserved by Sozomen, whose authenticity is disputed, Julian urges …"; and/or cite the Fragment of a Letter to a Priest (289A–291D, 305B–D) for the same point.
- **Evidence:** Peter Van Nuffelen, "Deux fausses lettres de Julien l'Apostat (la lettre aux Juifs, Ep. 51 [Wright], et la lettre à Arsacius, Ep. 84 [Bidez])", *Vigiliae Christianae* 56 (2002), pp. 131–150 (bibliographic record confirmed via search; argument from own knowledge).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/julian-the-apostate.md
- **Text:** "After Constantine's death, Socrates says, the soldiers killed the emperor's brother Dalmatius"
- **Problem:** The linked NPNF translation reads "the younger brother Dalmatius", which the entry has taken as Constantine's brother. Socrates's Greek (τὸν νέον Δαλμάτιον) means "the younger Dalmatius", i.e. the Caesar Dalmatius, Constantine's nephew, whom Socrates has just introduced as the son "who bore his own name" (cf. Socrates 2.25, where the Caesar Dalmatius is killed by the soldiers). The entry also leaves unsaid what a reader needs most here: Julian's own father, Julius Constantius, was killed in the same purge (Julian, *Letter to the Athenians* 270C–D), which is why he and Gallus were "orphans".
- **Should be:** "…the soldiers killed the younger Dalmatius, Constantine's nephew, and the lives of Julian and his brother Gallus, now orphans, were in danger"; optionally add from Julian's own Letter to the Athenians that his father was among those killed.
- **Evidence:** CCEL page of Socrates 3.1 opened: "had two brothers named Dalmatius and Constantius… The former of these had a son who bore his own name… the soldiery had put the younger brother Dalmatius to death, the lives of his two orphan children were also endangered". Greek reading and Julian's letter: own knowledge, not opened.
- **Confidence:** low

### C · completeness · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/jerome-and-the-vulgate.md
- **Text:** "Jerome's version nonetheless became the common text of the Latin church, and \"vulgate\" means the common edition."
- **Problem:** The entry lets the reader conclude that the Vulgate as transmitted is Jerome's work throughout. It is a composite: Jerome revised only the Gospels (the entry says so), and the Vulgate text of Acts, the Epistles and Revelation is a revision by another hand; Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch and 1–2 Maccabees are unrevised Old Latin; and the Psalter that became standard is his earlier "Gallican" revision made from the Greek, not his translation from the Hebrew. The entry does not mention the Psalters at all, though they are the clearest case of his two methods. (Jerome's own claim in *De viris illustribus* 135, cited in the dating note, is that he "restored the New Testament to the Greek original".)
- **Should be:** Add two sentences: that Jerome twice revised the Psalter from the Greek and then translated it from the Hebrew, and that the Bible later called the Vulgate combines his Gospels and Hebrew translations with his Gallican Psalter, Old Latin deuterocanonical books he did not translate, and a New Testament outside the Gospels revised by someone else.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (standard: Weber–Gryson, *Biblia Sacra Vulgata*, praefatio; Jerome, prefaces to the Psalms). Jerome, *De vir. ill.* 135 (newadvent) for his own claim.
- **Confidence:** high (facts); medium (whether maintainers want it in a 450-word entry)

### C · completeness · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/council-of-constantinople-i.md
- **Text:** "The third gives the bishop of Constantinople \"the prerogative of honour after the Bishop of Rome; because Constantinople is New Rome\" [[cite:canon3]]."
- **Problem:** The entry is tagged `east-and-west` but does not say that no western bishop took part, that Rome did not receive the canons (Leo I told Anatolius in 452 that the canon of "certain bishops" had never been brought to the knowledge of the apostolic see; Gregory I says the Roman church "has not" these canons), or that the council came to be counted as ecumenical only through its use at Chalcedon. Without this the reader takes canon 3 as an agreed ranking.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: "No western bishop was present; Rome did not accept the canon ranking Constantinople second (Leo I, Letter 106), and the council was received as ecumenical in the West only later, through Chalcedon."
- **Evidence:** own knowledge: Leo, Ep. 106.5 (NPNF 2.12); Gregory I, Ep. 7.31 (34). Not opened in this session.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/ambrose-and-theodosius.md
- **Text:** "Because Sozomen and Theodoret connect such a law with the penance, historians correct the year to 390" / "Later writers add a confrontation at the church door"
- **Problem:** The two witnesses closest in time after Ambrose's letter are missing. Rufinus (c. 402, *Church History* 11.18) already reports the public penance and the thirty-day law, a generation before Sozomen and Theodoret; and Paulinus of Milan, Ambrose's own secretary (*Life of Ambrose* 24, c. 422), is the earliest to say that Ambrose refused the emperor entry to the church. The entry therefore dates both the link with the law and the "church door" motif later than the evidence allows.
- **Should be:** Cite Rufinus 11.18 for the penance and the law, and Paulinus, *Vita Ambrosii* 24, as the first account of the exclusion from the church, before Sozomen and Theodoret.
- **Evidence:** NPNF footnote on the Sozomen page opened (7.25) itself refers to "Ruf. H. E. ii. 18"; contents of Rufinus and Paulinus from own knowledge, not opened.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/councils-and-empire/john-chrysostom.md
- **Text:** "Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, then came to the capital with a party of bishops."
- **Problem:** The entry gives no reason for Theophilus's coming, which hides the hinge of the affair. Both sources the entry uses tell that he had driven out the Nitrian monks (the "Tall Brothers", Bishop Dioscorus and his brothers) in a quarrel over Origen, and that they came to Constantinople, where John received them (Socrates 6.7, 6.9; Palladius, *Dialogue* 6–7). Palladius says that Theophilus was then summoned to stand trial before John on their complaint and turned the occasion against him; Socrates 6.15, which the entry follows, has the emperor authorise Theophilus to convene a synod against John. The Origenist quarrel (also Epiphanius's visit, Socrates 6.12–14) is not covered anywhere else in the wiki.
- **Should be:** "Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, had driven from Egypt a group of monks he accused of following Origen; they appealed at Constantinople, where John received them [Socrates 6.7, 6.9]. By Palladius's account Theophilus was summoned to answer their complaint [Palladius 7–8]; he came with a party of bishops and brought charges against John instead."
- **Evidence:** Palladius's *Dialogue* (tertullian.org page opened): "that Theophilus should be summoned by the magistrate to appear, whether willing or unwilling, and take his trial before John"; the expulsion of Dioscorus and the monks is on the same page. Socrates 6.15 opened (emperor "authorized Theophilus to convoke a Synod without delay against John"); Socrates 6.7 and 6.9 from own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high
