# Events: Modern and Global, 1963–2025

Reviewer batch E17. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/events/modern-and-global/john-paul-ii-in-poland.md
- **Text:** "had been arrested on 24 September 1953 and held in isolation until 28 October 1956"
- **Problem:** The two dates reproduce the Dicastery profile cited, but that profile is a day or two off against the documentary record. Wyszyński was arrested late on 25 September 1953 (night of 25/26 September) at the archbishops' house in Warsaw; he was released from Komańcza on 26 October 1956 and returned to Warsaw on 28 October. "Held in isolation until 28 October" also flattens the last year, which he spent under milder internment at Komańcza.
- **Should be:** "arrested on the night of 25 September 1953 and held, in the end at Komańcza, until his release on 26 October 1956; he was back in Warsaw on 28 October" — or keep the Dicastery's dates explicitly attributed and give the other date beside them (authoring guide: "two cited sources that disagree on a date").
- **Evidence:** Opened causesanti.va profile: "Il 24 settembre 1953, fu arrestato e rinchiuso in isolamento. Venne liberato il 28 ottobre 1956" (so the entry matches its source). Against it: Polish Institute of National Remembrance (IPN) biographical exhibition (eng.ipn.gov.pl): arrested 25 September 1953 at the Warsaw Archbishops' House; "on 28 October 1956 the Primate returned to the capital"; dzieje.pl (PAP): arrest in the night of 25/26 September, release 26 October 1956.
- **Confidence:** medium

### B · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/humanae-vitae.md
- **Text:** "but could not treat its conclusions as definitive, because it was not in complete agreement and because some of its criteria departed from the moral doctrine constantly taught by the church"
- **Problem:** The only thing the entry says about the papal commission is Paul VI's own characterisation ("not in complete agreement"). It leaves out the fact that makes the encyclical's reception intelligible: in June 1966 the commission's final report, backed by a large majority of its members (including 9 of the 15 bishops and cardinals voting, and a majority of the theologians), recommended that the teaching on contraception be changed; a small minority (Ford, Zalba, Visser, de Lestapis, with Cardinal Ottaviani) submitted a contrary paper; both texts were leaked and printed in April 1967 (National Catholic Reporter, The Tablet). A reader of the entry would take the commission to have been evenly split or inconclusive.
- **Should be:** Add one attributed sentence, e.g. "The commission's final report of June 1966, adopted by a large majority, had recommended that the church not hold every contraceptive act to be intrinsically wrong; a minority paper defended the existing teaching. Both were published without authorisation in 1967." Cite the published text of the report (The Tablet, 22 April–6 May 1967, or NCR 19 April 1967).
- **Evidence:** HV 6 (cited passage) says only "non omnimoda consensio"; Pontifical Commission on Birth Control (en.wikipedia.org, cross-check): report approved by 9 of 15 prelates and 12 of 19 theologians present; delivered to Paul VI 28 June 1966; own knowledge for the 1967 leak.
- **Confidence:** high (on the facts omitted); the exact tallies vary slightly between accounts, so give no numbers unless taken from a primary record.

### B · plausibility · src/content/events/modern-and-global/medellin-and-liberation-theology.md
- **Text:** "The theologian Gustavo Gutiérrez of Lima, who had been closely involved in the conference, took up the word in an article of 1970" (and summary: "the Catholic bishops … speak of liberation. Gustavo Gutiérrez builds a theology on that word")
- **Problem:** The sequence implies that Gutiérrez took "liberation" from the Medellín documents and built on them afterwards. He had already delivered the lecture "Hacia una teología de la liberación" to the priests' group ONIS at Chimbote, Peru, in July 1968 — a month before the bishops met — and as a peritus he helped draft Medellín texts (notably "Peace" and "Poverty of the Church"). The influence ran at least as much from him to the conference as the other way. This is an order word ("took up", "builds … on that word") that the sources do not support.
- **Should be:** "Gutiérrez, who had lectured 'towards a theology of liberation' at Chimbote in July 1968 and worked at the conference as an adviser, set the idea out in English in an article of 1970 …"; summary: "Gustavo Gutiérrez gives the theology its name and shape". (His book *Teología de la liberación*, Lima 1971, should at least be named in the body; at present only the source record mentions it.)
- **Evidence:** Gutiérrez's own introduction to *A Theology of Liberation* (the name and theme came from the Chimbote talk of July 1968); web search confirms the ONIS meeting at Chimbote, 21–25 July 1968, text printed Montevideo (MIEC) 1969. The wiki's own source record `gutierrez-notes-for-a-theology-of-liberation` describes the article only as "two years after … Medellín". I could not re-open the Theological Studies PDF (archive.org download failed) to see whether its first footnote mentions the earlier versions.
- **Confidence:** medium (high on Chimbote; medium that the wording will be read as a derivation)

### B · dating · src/content/events/modern-and-global/martyrs-of-libya.md
- **Text:** `date: start: 2015, month: 2, day: 15, confidence: firm`; dating note: "both give 15 February 2015 as the day of the deaths"
- **Problem:** 15 February 2015 is the day the video of the killing was published by the "Islamic State" media arm, and the day on which the churches commemorate the men. No document establishes on which day the men were actually killed; it can only be said that they died on or shortly before 15 February (they had been held since late December/early January). The dating note treats the commemoration date as a documented date of death and does not mention the video at all, so `firm` to the day is overstated. The Dicastery's note, moreover, says the video was circulated "poco dopo" (shortly afterwards), which is not what happened — it appeared that same day.
- **Should be:** Keep 15 February as the conventional date but say in the dating note: "15 February 2015 is the day on which the video of the killing was published; the day of the killing itself is not independently documented. Both churches keep that day." Confidence `firm` for the year and month, `traditional`/`estimated` for the day, or add the caveat.
- **Evidence:** Human Rights Watch, "Libya/Egypt: Murder of Egyptians a War Crime", 16 February 2015 (opened): "A video published on February 15, 2015, showed armed men beheading a group of 21 men"; it gives no date for the killing. Dicastery booklet (opened, p. 3): "Il 15 febbraio 2015, ventuno giovani sono stati martirizzati … Il video del loro martirio, diffuso poco dopo".
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/lausanne-congress.md
- **Text:** "Two further congresses built on the covenant."
- **Problem:** Out of date. A Fourth Lausanne Congress met at Seoul–Incheon, South Korea, 22–28 September 2024 (about 5,400 participants on site from some 200 countries) and issued the Seoul Statement, which explicitly builds on the Covenant, the Manila Manifesto and the Cape Town Commitment.

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/christological-declarations.md
- **Text:** summary: "Rome signs common statements of faith in Christ with the Coptic pope and with the patriarch of the Assyrian Church of the East"
- **Problem:** The entry passes over the declaration that went furthest. On 23 June 1984 John Paul II and the Syrian Orthodox patriarch Ignatius Zakka I Iwas signed a common declaration which says that the divisions arose "only because of differences in terminology and culture" and — uniquely — authorises the faithful of each church to receive penance, Eucharist and anointing from the other's priests when their own are unavailable. Also unmentioned: the common declarations with the Armenian catholicoi (Karekin I, 13 December 1996; Aram I, 1997), and, for the Assyrian dialogue, the 2001 Roman guidelines permitting Chaldean and Assyrian faithful to receive the Eucharist in each other's churches in case of need, which qualifies the entry's closing statement that the two "cannot yet celebrate the Eucharist together".
- **Should be:** Add a short paragraph on 1984 (cite the declaration, vatican.va / christianunity.va) and a clause on 2001 ("Guidelines for Admission to the Eucharist between the Chaldean Church and the Assyrian Church of the East", 20 July 2001). Adjust the summary: "with the Coptic, Syrian Orthodox and Assyrian patriarchs".
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; grep of `src/content` for "Zakka|Karekin|Addai" finds nothing relevant (only rabban-sauma.md).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/christological-declarations.md
- **Text:** "It recommended that the churches lift the old anathemas, and submitted this to them for consideration and action"
- **Problem:** The entry stops at the recommendation and does not say what became of it. More than thirty years on, the anathemas have not been lifted by the two families as a whole; several synods welcomed the statements (e.g. Antioch's pastoral agreement with the Syrian Orthodox in 1991, Alexandria–Coptic pastoral agreement on marriages 2001), while others, and the monastic community of Mount Athos (1995), objected. Without this the reader cannot tell whether the 1990 statement had effect.
- **Should be:** One sentence on the non-reception, cited to a church document (e.g. the Antiochian synodal decision of 12 November 1991, or the 1993 Chambésy "Proposals for lifting anathemas" which again remits the matter to the churches).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; not verified online in this pass.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/catechism-of-the-catholic-church.md
- **Text:** "the pope said that it faithfully repeated the doctrinal content of the first"
- **Problem:** The entry ends in 1997 and leaves the impression that the text is fixed. (a) The 1997 editio typica itself altered the wording of some paragraphs, most visibly no. 2267 on the death penalty in the light of *Evangelium vitae* (1995). (b) On 1 August 2018 Pope Francis, by a rescript approving a new text of no. 2267, changed the Catechism to say that the death penalty is "inadmissible". That is the best-known change ever made to the book and is absent from the wiki (grep for "2267|death penalty" finds nothing in this era). The 2005 *Compendium* is also unmentioned.
- **Should be:** Add: "The Latin text revised some paragraphs, among them no. 2267 on the death penalty. In 2018 Francis approved a new wording of that paragraph, which calls the death penalty inadmissible" — cite the rescript and the CDF letter to the bishops of 1 August 2018 (press.vatican.va bulletin 2 August 2018).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (rescript "ex audientia SS.mi" dated 11 May 2018, published 2 August 2018).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/clergy-abuse-crisis.md
- **Text:** summary "From January 2002, reports from Boston…"; body paragraph beginning "The bishops of the United States, through a lay review board, commissioned a national study"
- **Problem:** Several first-rank elements are missing. (1) The US bishops' own response of June 2002, the *Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People* (Dallas) and the Essential Norms approved by Rome that December — the review board that commissioned the John Jay study was itself created by that Charter, so the entry cites its product without the act. (2) The case of Theodore McCarrick (removed from the College of Cardinals July 2018, dismissed from the clerical state February 2019; Secretariat of State report November 2020), which is what turned the crisis toward bishops and led directly to *Vos estis lux mundi*. (3) The entry's starting point of 2002 gives no hint that the crisis was already public before then (Gauthe case, Louisiana, 1985; Mount Cashel, Newfoundland, 1989; Brendan Smyth and the fall of the Irish government, 1994; Cardinal Groër, Vienna, 1995). (4) Among the inquiries, the Irish Murphy Report on the Dublin archdiocese (November 2009), which is what Benedict's letter of March 2010 was answering, and the French CIASE report (October 2021). (5) For "other churches", the Church of England is the obvious second example (IICSA report 2020; the Makin review and the resignation of the Archbishop of Canterbury, November 2024).
- **Should be:** Add the Dallas Charter (usccb.org) before the John Jay sentence; a sentence on McCarrick citing the Holy See's communiqués/report; and one clause noting earlier cases. The others are optional.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; grep of `src/content` for "Dallas|McCarrick|Murphy report|CIASE" returns nothing.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/events/modern-and-global/benedict-resigns-francis-elected.md
- **Text:** "The Holy See's biography of Francis says that Francis met his predecessor periodically until two days before Benedict's death"
- **Problem:** The sentence reports its source correctly (the biography does say "until two days before the latter's death in 2022"), but the source is imprecise: Francis's last visit to Benedict was on Wednesday 28 December 2022, after the general audience at which he asked for prayers, and Benedict died on Saturday 31 December — three days later. The wiki thus passes on a small error.
- **Should be:** "…until a few days before Benedict's death (he last visited him on 28 December 2022)", cited to the Press Office statement of 28 December 2022; or drop "two days".
- **Evidence:** vatican.va biography opened (text as quoted). Press reports of 28 December 2022 (Holy See Press Office director Matteo Bruni: the pope went to the Mater Ecclesiae monastery after the audience); death 31 December 2022, 9:34.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/churches-and-apartheid.md
- **Text:** "the World Alliance of Reformed Churches, meeting at Ottawa in August 1982, suspended the two Dutch Reformed churches … and declared 'that apartheid…is a sin…'"
- **Problem:** The entry presents Ottawa 1982 as the first declaration of this kind. The Lutheran World Federation had already declared at its assembly in Dar es Salaam in June 1977 that the situation in southern Africa constituted a *status confessionis* and that the churches must publicly and unequivocally reject apartheid; in 1984 (Budapest) it suspended two white Lutheran churches. Ottawa followed that precedent. Also absent: the World Council of Churches' Programme to Combat Racism (1969) and its grants, which were the most contested international church action against apartheid, and the SACC's "Message to the People of South Africa" (1968).
- **Should be:** One sentence before Ottawa: "The Lutheran World Federation had declared in 1977 that apartheid created a *status confessionis*" (cite the Dar es Salaam assembly resolution, lutheranworld.org archive).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; grep of `src/content` for "status confessionis|Dar es Salaam|Programme to Combat Racism" returns nothing.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/joint-declaration-on-justification.md
- **Text:** "80 with a yes and five with a no"
- **Problem:** The opposition is under-described, and the figure itself is rounded: the LWF analysis cited also lists four answers "difficult to interpret" (one seeming yes, three seeming no). More important, the entry does not record (a) the public protest of German Protestant university theologians (over 140 signatures in January 1998; about 250 against the Official Common Statement in 1999), the best-known Lutheran dissent, or (b) that Lutheran churches outside the Federation, such as the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, rejected the declaration. As it stands the only reservations reported at length are the Catholic ones.
- **Should be:** Add "four further answers were hard to classify" and one sentence on Lutheran dissent, cited to a document of the dissenters (e.g. the LCMS's "The Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification in Confessional Lutheran Perspective", 1999).
- **Evidence:** archive.wfn.org/1998/06/msg00507.html opened: "80 have answered 'yes.' Five churches have answered 'no.' One answer is difficult to interpret but seems to be a 'yes.' Three answers are difficult to interpret, but seem to be 'no'"; 122 member + 2 associate churches (entry's "124" is right). German professors' protest and LCMS: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/modern-and-global/autocephaly-of-ukraine.md
- **Text:** "On 6 January 2019, at the liturgy in the patriarchal cathedral at the Phanar in Constantinople, Bartholomew handed him the tomos"
- **Problem:** Correct as far as it goes, but the tomos was signed the day before, 5 January 2019, in the same church, and the document bears that act; the entry's front-matter date is the handing-over. Two things a careful reader would also expect: the part played by the Ukrainian state (President Poroshenko's and the parliament's appeal to the Ecumenical Patriarch in April 2018; the president was present at both ceremonies), given that the entry carries the thread `church-and-state`; and that the churches of Alexandria (November 2019) and Cyprus (October 2020) also recognised the new church, after which Moscow ceased commemorating their primates. "Two are given here as examples" (Greece, Serbia) leaves the reader unable to tell how many churches stand on each side.
- **Should be:** "Bartholomew signed the tomos on 5 January 2019 and handed it to Epiphanius at the liturgy the next day"; add a sentence that four churches (Constantinople, Greece, Alexandria, Cyprus) have recognised the Orthodox Church of Ukraine and the others have not.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Ecumenical Patriarchate communiqués of 5 and 6 January 2019); not re-opened.
- **Confidence:** high on the dates, medium on the need

## Completeness of this stretch

The wiki's coverage of 1963–2025 is 20 entries, heavily weighted to Rome and to ecumenical documents. Checked against `event-index.txt` and by grep of `src/content` (patterns given). Missing or thin:

- **The disputes over sexuality and the Anglican/Protestant realignments** — the consecration of Gene Robinson (2003), GAFCON (2008), the United Methodist separation (2019–2024), *Fiducia supplicans* (2023) and the African bishops' refusal. This is the principal church-dividing question of the last quarter-century and the wiki has nothing (grep "Gene Robinson|GAFCON|same-sex|homosexual|Fiducia supplicans": 0 files). Belongs as one entry c. 2003.
- **The shift of Christianity to the global South** — African Initiated Churches after Kimbangu, the growth of Pentecostalism in Latin America, Africa and Korea, Chinese house churches after 1979. Only mentioned in passing in the Cape Town clause of the Lausanne entry (grep "megachurch|Yoido|house church|prosperity": 1 file, not an event). Deserves an entry of its own.
- **Reception of Vatican II in worship** — the Missal of Paul VI (1969/70), the Lefebvre consecrations and excommunications (1988), *Summorum Pontificum* (2007), *Traditionis custodes* (2021). Lefebvre's 1974 declaration is cited inside second-vatican-council.md, but the 1988 break, the only formal schism from Rome in the period, is not told.
- **John Paul II's pontificate after 1979 and Benedict XVI's election (2005)** — Solidarity and the churches in Poland 1980–89 (the entry titled "John Paul II and the Churches under Communism" stops in June 1979), the 1983 Code of Canon Law (grep: 0), Assisi 1986, *Ut unum sint* (1995), the Jubilee "Day of Pardon" (2000), *Dominus Iesus* (2000). The wiki jumps from 1978 to 2013 for the papacy.
- **Francis's pontificate** — only its opening is told. *Laudato si'* (2015), *Amoris laetitia* (2016), the Abu Dhabi document (2019) and the Synod on Synodality (2021–24) are absent as events.
- **The Russian Orthodox Church after 1988** — the millennium of the Baptism of Rus' and the end of Soviet restrictions, the canonisation of the new martyrs (2000), reunion with the Church Abroad (2007), and the church's stance on the war in Ukraine since 2022 (touched only by one clause in autocephaly-of-ukraine.md).
- **Persecution and genocide** — the churches and the Rwandan genocide of 1994 (grep "Rwanda": 0); the destruction of the Christian communities of Iraq and Syria, 2003–2015 (only the Libya entry touches the "Islamic State").
- **Ecumenical agreements outside the Catholic–Lutheran line** — WCC *Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry* (Lima 1982), the Leuenberg Agreement (1973), Porvoo (1992), Catholic–Orthodox Balamand (1993) and Ravenna (2007) statements (grep "Leuenberg|Porvoo": 0; "Balamand|Ravenna" only in unrelated medieval entries).
- **Evangelical politics and doctrine in the United States** — the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978), the Moral Majority (1979) (grep: 0). Could be folded into billy-graham or a new entry.
- **Women's ordination after 1978** — florence-li-tim-oi.md carries the story to Lambeth 1978 and *Ordinatio sacerdotalis* (1994) but not the Church of England's vote (1992) or the first women bishops (1989; Church of England 2014; first woman Archbishop-designate of Canterbury, 2025 — I did not verify the last point in this pass).
- Inside the batch: the 1984 Rome–Syrian Orthodox declaration, the 2018 change to the Catechism, the Dallas Charter and McCarrick, the Fourth Lausanne Congress, and the LWF's 1977 *status confessionis* are each reported as findings above.

## Method note
All 20 entries read in full. The three Bible citations in the batch (Dan 3:16-18, Exod 20:13, John 12:24-25) were checked against the WEB and fit. About 30 pages/documents were opened and read against the text: King's Letter (Stanford PDF, every page locator checked), Walker v. Birmingham, the 1967 Common Declaration, *Ambulate in dilectione*, causesanti.va profiles of Wyszyński and Romero, *Redemptor hominis* 11 and 17, the Victory Square homily, the UN Truth Commission report pp. 127–131, TRC vol. 4 ch. 3 (paras 31, 65, 67, 87–92), the LWF 1998 council action, the Official Common Statement signatures, the Stasi report Bl. 100–101, the Holy See biographies of Francis and Leo XIV, *In unitate fidei*, the İznik address, the 29 November 2025 joint declaration, the WCC conference page, the Dicastery booklet on the Coptic martyrs and the Los Angeles diocese notice, plus six web searches. All 2025 material (Francis's death, Leo XIV's election and first acts, the Wadi El Natrun conference, İznik, the Phanar declaration, Bartholomew's 7 December 2025 address as described in its source record) checked out. Not opened: the Medellín documents, the Lambeth and Canadian texts, the Gutiérrez article (archive.org download failed), the Kairos Document, the Orthodox synod statements of June 2016 and October 2018 and the tomos; these were judged from memory and nothing in the entries' use of them looked wrong.
