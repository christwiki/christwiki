# Events: Judges and United Monarchy, Divided Kingdom

Reviewer batch E02. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · consistency · src/content/events/divided-kingdom/mesha-stele.md
- **Text:** summary "Mesha, king of Moab, throws off the rule of Israel after Ahab's death."; body "The two accounts agree that the house of Omri ruled Moab, that Mesha ended that rule, and that Israel did not win it back. They do not describe the same fighting."
- **Problem:** The entry lists where Kings and the stele agree and differ but leaves out their best-known disagreement, the timing, and the summary states Kings' timing in the wiki's own voice. Kings puts the revolt after Ahab's death (2 Kings 1:1; 3:5). The stele, in the very lines the dating note quotes (7–8), says Israel held Medeba "his [Omri's] days and half the days of his son, forty years", and that Chemosh restored it "in my days" — on the plain reading the turn came halfway through the reign of Omri's son, i.e. while Ahab was alive. The "forty years" also does not fit Kings' figures (Omri 12 years + half of Ahab's 22 = about 23; 1 Kings 16:23, 29). The guide lists "two cited sources that disagree on a date, one silently chosen" as a defect.
- **Should be:** Summary: "Kings says that Mesha, king of Moab, rebelled against Israel after Ahab's death…". Body or dating note: add one sentence, e.g. "The two accounts differ on when: Kings dates the revolt after Ahab's death, while the stele has Israel hold Medeba for Omri's days and half the days of his son, forty years in all, a figure longer than Kings allows for Omri and half of Ahab's reign; some therefore read 'his son' as 'his sons/descendants' and bring the date down to Joram."
- **Evidence:** Compston's translation (the edition the entry cites), lines 7–8, read on archive.org full text: "And 'Omri possessed the land of Mehedeba. And (Israel) dwelt therein, his days and half the days of his son, forty years; and Kemos restored it in my days." WEB 2 Kings 3:5 "when Ahab was dead, the king of Moab rebelled"; 1 Kings 16:23 (Omri twelve years), 16:29 (Ahab twenty-two).
- **Confidence:** medium (the facts are certain; whether it is B or C is a judgment)

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/divided-kingdom/elijah-on-carmel.md
- **Text:** "The Gospels apply that promise to John the Baptist [[bible:Matt 11:13-14; Luke 1:17]]"
- **Problem:** "The Gospels" is stretched over a Gospel that says the opposite: in John, the Baptist is asked "Are you Elijah?" and answers "I am not". Only Matthew and Luke (and Mark 9:11-13 by implication) make the application. The same wording is in `people/elijah.md` ("the Gospels link that promise to John the Baptist").
- **Should be:** "Matthew and Luke apply that promise to John the Baptist [[bible:Matt 11:13-14; Luke 1:17]]; in John's Gospel the Baptist denies that he is Elijah [[bible:John 1:21]]."
- **Evidence:** WEB John 1:21: "They asked him, 'What then? Are you Elijah?' He said, 'I am not.'"
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/judges-and-united-monarchy/david-and-goliath.md
- **Text:** "The Hebrew text makes him six cubits and a span tall … The Greek Septuagint gives his height as four cubits and a span [[cite:lxx-height]], and so does Josephus [[cite:josephus-height]]."
- **Problem:** The contrast is drawn as Hebrew against Greek, but the oldest Hebrew manuscript of the passage, the Dead Sea Scroll 4QSam-a, also reads four cubits and a span. "The Hebrew text" is true only of the Masoretic text; the omission hides the main reason scholars take the shorter figure seriously.
- **Should be:** "The Masoretic Hebrew text makes him six cubits and a span … The Septuagint, Josephus and the oldest Hebrew manuscript, the Samuel scroll from Qumran (4QSam-a), give four cubits and a span." (Cite the scroll: DJD XVII, or the Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library plate.)
- **Evidence:** ESV/NET footnotes at 1 Sam 17:4 ("Hebrew; Septuagint, Dead Sea Scroll and Josephus four"); web search confirmed (crossway.org "How Tall Was Goliath?", en.wikipedia.org "The Samuel Scroll"). Josephus 6.171 and the Brenton LXX were opened and do read four cubits and a span.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/judges-and-united-monarchy/david-takes-jerusalem.md
- **Text:** "Joshua and Judges say that neither Judah nor Benjamin drove the Jebusites out of [[place:jerusalem]], and that they still lived there [[bible:Josh 15:63]] [[bible:Judg 1:21]]."
- **Problem:** The sentence gives one side of what Judges says. The same chapter states that the men of Judah fought against Jerusalem, took it and burned it (Judg 1:8). Citing only 1:21 reports Joshua and Judges as agreeing where Judges itself has two notices that differ.
- **Should be:** Add: "Judges also has a notice that the men of Judah took Jerusalem and set it on fire [[bible:Judg 1:8]]; the same chapter and Joshua say the Jebusites were not driven out and still lived there."
- **Evidence:** WEB Judg 1:8: "The children of Judah fought against Jerusalem, took it, struck it with the edge of the sword, and set the city on fire."
- **Confidence:** high

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/judges-and-united-monarchy/solomons-temple.md
- **Text:** summary "Seven years later the ark is carried into its inner room and the house is dedicated."; `date.end: -959`; body "The work was finished in the eighth month of the eleventh year… At the feast of the seventh month the priests carried the ark…"
- **Problem:** Kings gives no year for the dedication. It dates completion to the eighth month of the eleventh year (1 Kings 6:38) and the dedication to the feast of the seventh month (1 Kings 8:2), so the dedication fell either a month before the stated completion or at least eleven months after it; Kings also places the thirteen years of palace building and the casting of the furnishings between the two (1 Kings 7:1, 51; cf. 9:10). The summary and the end date present "seven years later" as the date of the dedication, and the dating note is silent on the point.
- **Should be:** Summary: "Seven years later the house is finished; at a feast in the seventh month the ark is carried in and the house is dedicated." Add a sentence to the dating note that Kings gives the month but not the year of the dedication, and that the seventh month cannot be that of the eleventh year unless the dedication preceded the completion in the eighth month.
- **Evidence:** WEB 1 Kings 6:38 "In the eleventh year, in the month Bul, which is the eighth month, the house was finished"; 1 Kings 8:2 "at the feast in the month Ethanim, which is the seventh month"; 1 Kings 7:1 "Solomon was building his own house thirteen years".
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/events/divided-kingdom/fall-of-samaria.md
- **Text:** "In the inscription of his palace he says that he besieged and took Samaria, carried off 27,290 of its inhabitants, added fifty chariots to his army … A prism from Nimrud adds that he resettled the city with people from other lands he had conquered and set a governor over it [[cite:prism]]."
- **Problem:** The prism is presented only as adding to the palace inscription, but the cited lines (iv 25–41) give different figures for the same event: 200 chariots taken into the royal force (not 50), and a deportee total that Gadd's edition reads 27,280 (not 27,290). Since the entry makes a point of the numbers ("Kings gives no number for the deported"), the disagreement between Sargon's own inscriptions belongs here.
- **Should be:** "…A prism from Nimrud gives 200 chariots and, as it is read, 27,280 deportees, and adds that he resettled the city…"
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the Nimrud Prism (C. J. Gadd, *Iraq* 16 (1954), pp. 179–182; RINAP 2 no. 74 iv 25–41: "27,280 people … I formed a unit with 200 of their chariots for my royal force"). A web search confirmed the 200-chariot figure for the Nimrud Prism (cojs.org "The Nimrud Prism"; en.wikipedia.org "Sargon II's Prisms"); the 27,280 reading I could not open in the edition (ORACC unreachable), so check that digit before editing.
- **Confidence:** medium (200 chariots: high; 27,280: medium)

### C · scriptural · src/content/events/divided-kingdom/hezekiah-and-sennacherib.md
- **Text:** "Isaiah and Chronicles tell the same story [[bible:Isa 37:33-38]] [[bible:2 Chr 32:20-22]]."
- **Problem:** "The same story" overstates. Neither Isaiah 36–37 nor Chronicles has Hezekiah's submission and tribute (2 Kings 18:14-16 has no parallel in either), which is the very point on which the entry later compares Kings with the Assyrian annals. Chronicles also gives no figure for the dead and says the angel cut off "all the mighty men of valor, the leaders, and captains", and has Sennacherib killed by "those who came out of his own body" without naming them.
- **Should be:** "Isaiah has the same account almost word for word but without the tribute [[bible:Isa 36:1-2; 37:33-38]]; Chronicles tells it more briefly, also without the tribute and without the number of the dead [[bible:2 Chr 32:20-22]]."
- **Evidence:** WEB Isa 36:1-2 passes from the invasion straight to the Rabshakeh's mission; WEB 2 Chr 32:21 as quoted above.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/divided-kingdom/battle-of-carchemish.md
- **Text:** "Daniel puts Nebuchadnezzar's first move against Jerusalem in Jehoiakim's third year [[bible:Dan 1:1]]; the difference is usually explained by a count that leaves out the accession year."
- **Problem:** "Usually explained" presents one side's harmonisation as the common view. The accession-year explanation is the one given by scholars who defend the notice (e.g. D. J. Wiseman; J. G. Baldwin, *Daniel*, 1978). Critical commentators hold instead that Dan 1:1 is not a historical datum but is derived from 2 Kings 24:1 and 2 Chr 36:6-7 (J. J. Collins, *Daniel*, Hermeneia, 1993, on 1:1 — the commentary the wiki's own `daniel-in-babylon` note cites for the critical view). The Babylonian chronicle, as that entry notes, mentions no move against Jerusalem in 605. Also, Jer 46:2 dates the battle and Dan 1:1 a siege of Jerusalem, so the two verses are not dating the same event.
- **Should be:** "…; those who take the notice as historical explain the difference by a count that leaves out the accession year, while others regard the verse as a later inference from Kings and Chronicles (John J. Collins, *Daniel*, 1993). The chronicle records no action against Jerusalem in 605 BC."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the commentaries named; `src/content/events/exile-and-return/daniel-in-babylon.md` dating note ("It does not mention Jerusalem"). I did not open Collins in this session — confirm the page before citing.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

Grepped `src/content/{events,people,places,sources}` for each term named below.

- **Solomon's reign apart from the temple** (wisdom, the Queen of Sheba, the foreign wives and shrines that Kings gives as the cause of the division, 1 Kings 3–4; 10–11). Only in `people/solomon.md`; no event. The division entry starts from Ahijah's oracle without the reason Kings gives for it (1 Kings 11:1-13, 33). Belongs between `solomons-temple` and `kingdom-divides`.
- **Psalms and wisdom literature.** No event or source entry treats the Psalter, Proverbs, Job or Ecclesiastes ("Proverbs" and "Song of Songs" occur only in `bede.md`; "Ecclesiastes" nowhere). For a wiki whose threads include worship and Scripture this is the largest gap in the Old Testament part; an entry on David and the Psalms would sit in the united monarchy with a dating note on the growth of the collection.
- **Omri and the founding of Samaria** (1 Kings 16:23-28). Omri is named in four events only as a patronym ("house of Omri"); the king whom Assyria and Moab both name as Israel's dynast, and the founding of the capital whose fall has an entry, have no entry of their own. Naboth's vineyard (1 Kings 21), to which the Jehu entry alludes, is not told in any event.
- **Athaliah and Joash** (2 Kings 11–12): the near-extinction of David's line, directly relevant to the kingship-and-messiah thread. "Athaliah" occurs in no file.
- **Micah.** No event or person; the name occurs only in `places/bethlehem.md` and `people/miriam.md`. A contemporary of Isaiah whose oracle on Bethlehem Matthew quotes (Mic 5:2; Matt 2:6) and whose prophecy against Zion is cited at Jeremiah's trial (Jer 26:18) — which the `call-of-jeremiah` entry narrates without him.
- **The Assyrian conquest of Galilee and Damascus, 734–732 BC** (2 Kings 15:29; 16:5-9; Tiglath-pileser III's inscriptions). Treated in two sentences inside `call-of-isaiah` and one in `fall-of-samaria`; the first deportation of Israelites has no entry.
- **Manasseh** (2 Kings 21; 2 Chr 33; named as tributary by Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal). Mentioned once, in `josiahs-reform`. Kings makes his reign the reason for Judah's fall (2 Kings 23:26; 24:3), and Chronicles' account of his repentance is a classic Kings/Chronicles difference. "Esarhaddon" occurs in one file. The timeline jumps from 701 to 627 BC.
- **The fall of Nineveh, 612 BC, and the end of Assyria**, with Nahum and Zephaniah. Told only in `places/nineveh.md`; no event, although ABC 3 is already a source and `death-of-josiah` depends on the sequel. Habakkuk and Zephaniah as prophets of Josiah's and Jehoiakim's time are not covered ("Habakkuk" only in the Dead Sea Scrolls entry).
- **Ketef Hinnom silver scrolls** (c. 600 BC, the priestly blessing of Num 6:24-26; the oldest surviving text of a biblical passage). Not in any file; belongs at the end of this era under scripture-and-canon.
- **Inscriptions bearing on Israelite religion**: Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom ("Yahweh and his Asherah", c. 800 BC) are in no file, though the wiki otherwise sets inscriptions beside the text and the Josiah entry speaks of Asherah in the temple. Lower priority: the Deir Alla Balaam text, the Samaria ostraca.
- **Hezekiah's reform and Passover** (2 Kings 18:4; 2 Chr 29–31) get one clause in `hezekiah-and-sennacherib`; acceptable, but the Kings/Chronicles difference in scale could be stated there.
- Covered adequately inside broader entries (no action needed): Samson, Gideon, Deborah (`age-of-the-judges`); Absalom (`david-and-bathsheba`); the Siloam tunnel and Lachish reliefs (`hezekiah-and-sennacherib`); Naaman and Hazael (`elisha-succeeds-elijah`); the Syro-Ephraimite crisis as it concerns Isaiah (`call-of-isaiah`).
