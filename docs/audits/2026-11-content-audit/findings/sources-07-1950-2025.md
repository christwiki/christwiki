# Sources first cited for events c. 1950–2025, and sources no event cites

## Findings

### A · historical · src/content/sources/antioch-holy-synod-statement-6-june-2016.md
- **Text:** "It ends with three unanimous decisions."
- **Problem:** The statement ends with five numbered decisions, not three. The count is wrong (the guide's "a count … taken from a secondary summary").
- **Should be:** "It ends with five unanimous decisions: (1) to ask the Ecumenical Patriarch to seek consensus and, failing that, to postpone the council; (2) that Antioch will not take part until the breach of communion with Jerusalem is resolved; (3) to reaffirm that all autocephalous churches must take part and decide unanimously; (4) to inform the other churches; (5) to ask the faithful to pray."
- **Evidence:** Full English text as reprinted from the patriarchate at https://orthochristian.com/94045.html and https://www.pravmir.com/?p=272323 ("the fathers of the Holy Antiochian Synod unanimously decided the following: 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. …"); the Russian synod's statement of 13 June 2016 (https://mospat.ru/en/news/49405/), which the wiki also holds, quotes the same five resolutions. (The record's own `url` returned HTTP 404 "Page not found" on 17 Nov 2026 — the patriarchate's site appears to have been rebuilt — so I read the text in these reprints.)
- **Confidence:** high

### B · historical · src/content/sources/du-plessis-the-spirit-bade-me-go.md
- **Text:** "written: start: 1963" and edition: "First published 1963."
- **Problem:** 1963 is the date of the scanned (Oakland) printing, not of first publication. Library catalogues list earlier editions of 1960/1961, privately printed by the author; the book is normally cited as 1961 (revised edition Logos, 1970).
- **Should be:** "First published privately by the author in 1961 (catalogues also list a 1960 printing); cited here from the Oakland printing of 1963 scanned by the Internet Archive." `written.start` 1961 (or `circa` 1960–1963).
- **Evidence:** Open Library search API for the title returns publish years 1960, 1961, 1963, 2004 (first_publish_year 1960) and a separate Logos 1970 edition; encyclopedia.com ("Du Plessis, David J(ohannes) 1905-1987") lists it as "privately printed (Oakland, CA), 1961"; archive.org metadata of the linked item gives 1963 for that scan only. I could not open the scan itself (lending only) to read its copyright page.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/sources/paul-vi-athenagoras-joint-communique-1964.md
- **Text:** edition: "from Tomos Agapis, Vatican-Phanar (1958–1970) (Rome and Istanbul, 1971), no. 120."
- **Problem:** 120 is the page, not the document number. In Tomos Agapis the joint communiqué of 6 January 1964 is document no. 50, printed on p. 120 (the Dicastery's page prints only "…1971, 120").
- **Should be:** "…(Rome and Istanbul, 1971), no. 50, p. 120."

The Common Declaration of John Paul II and Bartholomew I of 1 July 2004 on vatican.va cites the same text as "Tomos Agapis, Vatican-Phanar, 1971, n. 50, p. 120" (and the addresses of 5 and 6 January 1964 as nn. 48 and 49).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/jerome-against-helvidius.md
- **Text:** "those called the Lord's brothers were sons of another Mary, the wife of Clopas, whom he takes to be the sister of the Lord's mother (sections 13–17)"
- **Problem:** Jerome does not make this Mary the wife of Clopas. His argument (sections 15–16) is that she is the wife of Alphaeus (so that "James the son of Alphaeus" is the Lord's "brother") and that John calls her "Mary of Clopas" after her father, her kindred "or for some other reason". "Wife of Clopas" is the translators' rendering of John 19:25, not Jerome's identification.
- **Should be:** "…were sons of another Mary, the wife of Alphaeus, whom he identifies with the 'Mary of Clopas' of John 19:25 and takes to be the sister of the Lord's mother (sections 13–17)."
- **Evidence:** https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3007.htm §15: "the Mary who is described as the mother of James the Less was the wife of Alphæus and sister of Mary the Lord's mother, the one who is called by John the Evangelist Mary of Clopas, whether after her father, or kindred, or for some other reason"; §16: "Mary of Clopas, who is the same as the wife of Alphæus".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/bonhoeffer-von-der-dummheit.md
- **Text:** "written: start: 1943, circa: true" … "Wikisource's record gives 1943 as the year of writing" … "the volume of Bonhoeffer's letters and papers from prison"
- **Problem:** The record never says what the piece is. "Von der Dummheit" is one section of the essay "Nach zehn Jahren. Rechenschaft an der Wende zum Jahr 1943", which Bonhoeffer wrote at Christmas 1942 for Bethge, Dohnanyi and Oster, some three months before his arrest (5 April 1943). Dating it from a Wikisource infobox and describing it only as part of the prison volume invites the reader to take it for a prison writing; the date is also knowable to the month.
- **Should be:** "A section of 'Nach zehn Jahren' (After Ten Years), the reckoning Bonhoeffer wrote at Christmas 1942 for his fellow conspirators, before his arrest; Bethge printed the essay at the head of Widerstand und Ergebung (1951)." `written`: December 1942 (display "Christmas 1942").
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (DBW 8, pp. 19–39), confirmed by a web search: publisher's sample for the essay ("'Nach zehn Jahren' entstand zu Weihnachten 1942, kurz vor der Inhaftierung Bonhoeffers… für Hans von Dohnanyi, Hans Oster, die Eltern und Eberhard Bethge"). The Wikisource page itself (opened) gives only "Entstehungsdatum: 1943".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/sources/jerusalem-talmud.md
- **Text:** "They show how the rabbis remembered it, and they are the source of the name Bar Kokhba, \"son of the star\", being linked to a messianic claim."
- **Problem:** Overstated as "the source". The name Bar Kokhba is attested two centuries earlier in Christian writers (Justin, 1 Apology 31, c. 155), and Eusebius (Church History 4.6.2) already says that the name means "star" and that its bearer, relying on it, claimed to be a star come down from heaven — a passage the wiki's own Bar Kokhba entry cites. What the Talmud uniquely supplies is Akiva's application of Numbers 24:17 and the title "King Messiah".
- **Should be:** "…and they are the rabbinic source for Akiva's reading of the name Bar Kokhba, 'son of the star', as a messianic title; Eusebius, a little earlier, also connects the name with a claim to be a star from heaven."
- **Evidence:** Eusebius, HE 4.6.2 (NPNF: "Barcocheba (which signifies a star)… pretended that he was a star that had come down to them out of heaven"); src/content/events/church-under-rome/bar-kokhba-revolt.md line 72 ("Eusebius… calls their leader Barcocheba and says the name means 'star'").
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/sources/paul-vi-address-to-charismatic-renewal-1975.md
- **Text:** "language: French" … "French text published by the Holy See; the Vatican site gives no English version."
- **Problem:** The address was given in three languages. The Holy See's page prints, after the French, a passage the pope spoke in Spanish and one he spoke in English ("Always give great importance to this Sacrament of Baptism…"). So there is an English portion of the address itself on the page, and the language field is incomplete. (It is true that the site's English page is an empty stub with no translation of the French part.)

- **Evidence:** Fetched https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/fr/speeches/1975/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19750519_rinnovamento-carismatico.html (French paragraphs, then "Toda la renovación espiritual…", then "Always give great importance to this Sacrament of Baptism…"); the /en/ version returns a page with no text.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/perier-life-of-pascal.md
- **Text:** (front matter has no `written`) … "It is the family's own account and gives the days of his birth and death."
- **Problem:** The record does not say when the Life was written or printed, so the reader cannot weigh its distance from the events, which the wiki's standard requires. Gilberte Périer wrote it soon after her brother's death (c. 1662–63); it was first printed in 1684 and then prefixed to editions of the Pensées. The text cited is a nineteenth-century reprint.
- **Should be:** add `written: {start: 1662, end: 1663, circa: true, display: "c. 1662–1663; first printed 1684"}` and a sentence to that effect.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (standard in Pascal scholarship, e.g. the Mesnard edition); not verified on the web in this session.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Method note
All 164 records read in full. Bible references in this batch are few; six were run through the local WEB tool (Eph 5:2, John 15:4, John 19:25, John 21:15-17, Num 24:17, Exod 20:13) and none is misused. (The Lund homily record gives John 15:4 as "Abide in me as I abide in you", the wording of the Holy See's English text of the homily, where the WEB has "Remain in me, and I in you"; it is quoted as the homily's theme, so I have not listed it as a finding.) About 75 web pages or PDFs were opened and compared with the records (vatican.va ×25, holycouncil.org, ec-patr.org, mospat.ru, oikoumene.org, lutheranworld.org, lausanne.org, nobelprize.org, newadvent.org, archive.org full texts, the Massachusetts AG, John Jay, UN S/25500 and TRC vol. 4 PDFs, the Stasi Mediathek, germanhistorydocs, sonntagsblatt, the Notre Dame finding aid, the CPC page and others); the 2025 documents (In unitate fidei, İznik address, Joint Declaration of 29 November, Leo XIV's biography, the WCC conference message, Bartholomew's address of 7 December) were all read on the issuing bodies' sites and agree with the records in every detail tested, including paragraph counts, footnote 10 and the 15.50 meeting time.
Not checked against the source: records that rest on web.archive.org captures (the Bulgarian, Georgian, Serbian and Church of Greece synodal texts, Guidepost, the Medellín PDF, Gutiérrez's article, the Lambeth and Episcopal Dictionary pages, Tearfund), because the Internet Archive became unreachable from this environment part-way through; the two kerkargief.co.za PDFs (Cottesloe, Rustenburg; server refused); lending-only or image-only scans (Bennett, du Plessis, Jones, Graham, Padilla); and the Boston Globe article. In particular the counts "six considerations and two resolutions" (Bulgaria), "eleven difficulties" (Georgia) and "seven metropolitans" (Greece) are untested; given the miscount in the Antioch record they are worth a second look.
