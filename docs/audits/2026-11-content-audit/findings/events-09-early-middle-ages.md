# Events: Early Middle Ages

Reviewer batch E09. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/third-council-of-constantinople.md
- **Text:** "The legates reached the capital in November 680."
- **Problem:** This repeats the Liber Pontificalis ("ingredientes die X mensis novembris, indictione VIIII") in the wiki's own voice, but that date is wrong and the cited edition says so on the cited pages: the legates had arrived by 10 September 680, when Constantine IV wrote to Patriarch George that they had come and handed over Agatho's two letters; the council itself opened on 7 November (acts), which the LP also misdates to 22 November. The entry nowhere gives the opening date.
- **Should be:** "The legates were in the capital by September 680 (the emperor's letter to the patriarch George of 10 September announces their arrival; the Liber Pontificalis dates their reception to 10 November), and the council opened on 7 November 680 in the hall called the Trullus."
- **Evidence:** Duchesne, Liber Pontificalis vol. 1, notes 14 and 16 to the Life of Agatho (archive.org item duchesne01, OCR read): "L'arrivée des légats à Constantinople et leur audience de réception à la cour impériale ne peuvent être postérieures au 10 septembre 680 … Les deux premières sessions du concile eurent lieu le 7 et le 10 novembre"; note 16: "Les procès-verbaux indiquent le 7 novembre comme le jour de l'ouverture du concile." en.wikipedia "Third Council of Constantinople": opened 7 November 680.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/third-council-of-constantinople.md
- **Text:** "In the seventh century a further question divided the churches of the empire: whether Christ had one will and one 'operation', or two." / "condemned … Honorius, 'because of what we found written by him to Sergius …'"
- **Problem:** Two things a careful reader would expect are missing. (1) The entry never names the imperial acts that made one will the law of the empire and that the council was undoing: the Ekthesis of Heraclius (638) and the Typos of Constans II (648); nor the Lateran synod of 649 by name (it appears only as "a synod" under Martin, via Theophanes, 170 years later). Neither word occurs anywhere in the wiki. (2) On Honorius, the entry sets Agatho's "has never erred" beside the council's anathema but omits the Roman reception: Leo II's letters confirming the council (682) restate the condemnation in different terms (Honorius "did not illuminate this apostolic church with the teaching of apostolic tradition, but allowed its purity to be stained"), which is the text on which the later Catholic and non-Catholic readings of the case turn.
- **Should be:** Add one sentence naming the Ekthesis (638) and Typos (648) and the Lateran synod of 649, and one sentence giving Leo II's wording when he confirmed the council, each from its document.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; grep of src/content for "Ekthesis|Ecthesis|Typos" returns nothing.
- **Confidence:** high (omission), medium (that it is worth adding)

### C · dating · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/second-council-of-nicaea.md
- **Text:** "he dates its opening to 11 October and the closing session in the palace at Constantinople to November [[cite:th-787]]. The days of the individual sessions are given in the full minutes, which the translation cited here does not reproduce."
- **Problem:** The note gives only Theophanes' days and does not say that they conflict with the council's own acts, the primary record: the first session is dated 24 September 787, the seventh (which adopted the decree) 13 October, and the eighth, in the Magnaura palace at Constantinople, 23 October. A reader is left with 11 October as "the opening" and November for the close.
- **Should be:** Add: "The acts themselves date the first session to 24 September, the seventh, at which the decree was adopted, to 13 October, and the eighth, in the Magnaura palace, to 23 October 787; Theophanes' 11 October and November do not match them."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the acts (Mansi 12–13); en.wikipedia.org "Second Council of Nicaea": "The council opened on 24 September 787 at the Hagia Sophia." The NPNF pages cited in the entry carry no session dates (opened npnf214.xvi.ii, xvi.v, xvi.xii).
- **Confidence:** medium (24 September checked; I did not open Mansi for 13 and 23 October)

### C · dating · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/east-west-schism.md
- **Text:** "Pope Leo IX had died at Rome in April; Hermann of Reichenau gives the sixteenth day before the Kalends of May [[cite:death]]."
- **Problem:** Hermann does read "16. Kalend. Mai obiit" (= 16 April), so the citation is accurate, but the date universally accepted for Leo IX's death, from the Roman and necrological tradition, is 19 April 1054 (XIII Kal. Maii). Giving only Hermann's outlying day, without saying it is three days off the received one, will lead readers to 16 April. (The same wording stands in src/content/sources/hermann-of-reichenau-chronicle.md; the person entry leo-ix gives only the month.)
- **Should be:** "Pope Leo IX had died at Rome on 19 April 1054, the day kept in the Roman and monastic necrologies; Hermann of Reichenau, a contemporary, writes the sixteenth day before the Kalends of May, 16 April."
- **Evidence:** MGH SS 5, p. 133 (OCR of archive.org gri_33125006186593 read): "16. Kalend. Mai obiit, et in basilica sancti Petri … sepultus". F. Neiske, article on the liturgical memoria of Leo IX (uni-muenster.de PDF opened): "Léon IX, le saint pape, qui mourut le 19 avril 1054"; necrology entry under 19 April "Rome depositio beati Leonis pape IX".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/east-west-schism.md
- **Text:** "Three legates followed, led by [[person:humbert-of-silva-candida]]. In 1054 the emperor received them; the patriarch would not meet them."
- **Problem:** The entry gives no reason for the legation. It was sent in answer to letters from the emperor Constantine IX and from Cerularius himself, in the setting of the papal–Byzantine alliance against the Normans of southern Italy after the pope's defeat and capture at Civitate (June 1053); the dispute over azymes had been opened by Leo of Ohrid's letter to John of Trani and by the closing of the Latin churches at Constantinople, which Humbert's bull itself charges against Michael. Without this the emperor's friendliness to the legates and his hostility to the patriarch's handling, both of which the entry reports, are unexplained.
- **Should be:** One or two sentences, from the emperor's and patriarch's letters answered by Leo IX (Will, Acta et scripta, pp. 85–92) and from the bull, on the Norman war, the exchange of letters and the closing of the Latin churches.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; Humbert's Brevis commemoratio as printed in Will, pp. 150–152 (OCR read), names Leo of Ohrid's writing among those refuted; the wiki's own source page hermann-of-reichenau-chronicle mentions the Norman defeat but the event does not.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/conversion-of-bulgaria.md
- **Text:** "Photius protested to the eastern patriarchs … and Bulgaria became part of his quarrel with Rome. By 878 it was Rome that complained."
- **Problem:** The outcome is skipped. Between 867 and 878 the decisive step happened: Rome refused Boris the archbishop he asked for (Formosus of Porto, then the deacon Marinus), Boris sent to Constantinople, and after the last session of the council of 869–870 (early March 870) a meeting of the eastern patriarchs' representatives with the emperor assigned Bulgaria to the see of Constantinople over the Roman legates' protest; Ignatius then consecrated an archbishop and the Latin clergy left. The reader is not told why Greek clergy were in Bulgaria for John VIII to expel, nor that they stayed.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: "In 870, after Rome had twice refused him the archbishop he named, Boris put the question to the council sitting at Constantinople; the representatives of the eastern patriarchs awarded Bulgaria to Constantinople, against the protest of the Roman legates, and Greek bishops replaced the Latin ones" — cited to the Liber Pontificalis, Life of Hadrian II (Duchesne 2, pp. 182–185), or to John VIII's own letters, which presuppose it.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Liber Pontificalis, Hadrian II, chs. 46–64; Anastasius Bibliothecarius' preface to the acts of 869–870). The entry's own citation of John VIII, ep. 71 ("invaders") presupposes it.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/ansgar.md
- **Text:** "On their return Louis founded an archbishopric at [[place:hamburg]], beyond the Elbe, as a base for the northern mission. Ansgar was consecrated to it, and Pope Gregory IV gave him the pallium and named him legate" / summary: "becomes the first archbishop of Hamburg"
- **Problem:** This is Rimbert's account, and the summary states it as plain fact. The foundation of an archbishopric at Hamburg in 831/834 is one of the contested points of Carolingian church history: the imperial diploma and Gregory IV's privilege survive only in interpolated or forged forms, and a serious body of scholarship holds that Ansgar was a missionary bishop with a legatine commission and that the archiepiscopal see of Hamburg(-Bremen) was constructed later, in the 860s–890s, in part by Ansgar and Rimbert themselves. The dating note discusses only the years and gives no hint of this.
- **Should be:** Attribute in the summary ("according to Rimbert, becomes the first archbishop of Hamburg") and add to the dating note that the foundation documents of Hamburg are disputed, naming the scholarship (e.g. E. Knibbs, *Ansgar, Rimbert and the Forged Foundations of Hamburg-Bremen*, 2011).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the literature (Knibbs 2011; earlier R. Drögereit, G. Theuerkauf). Not opened in this task; grep of the wiki for "Knibbs" and for any mention of forgery in ansgar.md or rimbert-life-of-anskar.md returns nothing.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · plausibility · src/content/events/early-middle-ages/synod-of-whitby.md
- **Text:** front matter `date: start: 664, confidence: firm`; dating note "Bede gives the year: the debate took place in AD 664"
- **Problem:** The year has been disputed: on the theory that Bede began his year with the September indiction, F. M. Stenton (*Anglo-Saxon England*) and others following R. L. Poole placed the synod in the autumn of 663; later work (e.g. K. Harrison, *The Framework of Anglo-Saxon History*, 1976) rejected that and 664 is now the usual date. The entry labels the date firm and its note does not mention the alternative. The note also gives Wilfrid's death as 709 without qualification, where 710 is at least as often given.
- **Should be:** Keep 664 but add a sentence on the 663 proposal and why it is no longer generally held; write "Wilfrid's death in 709 or 710".
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; not opened in this task. Bede 3.26 itself, as the note says, gives 664.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Method note

All 25 entries read in full (front matter, dating note, body). All six Bible citations in the batch were checked against the WEB (Matt 24:15; Matt 16:18–19; John 6:38; John 6:9; 1 Cor 14:14–19; 2 Kgs 23) and fit. About 20 external texts were opened and searched, mostly the editions the entries cite: Duchesne's Liber Pontificalis vol. 1 (Agatho), MGH SS 5 (Hermann 1054), Will's Acta et scripta (Leo IX's letter, Humbert's report), the Bonn Theophanes Continuatus (4.6), the Dumbarton Oaks typika of Athanasius and Tzimiskes, Grønlie's Íslendingabók with its note on Ólafía Einarsdóttir, Cross and Sherbowitz-Wetzor's notes 93–94, Robinson's Boniface (p. 91 on Tangl) and Anskar, the Fordham texts of the Xi'an stele and John of Damascus, the 2003 Filioque statement, the Tanner introduction to the council of 869–870, the Usener article record, and Wikipedia for two council dates; weekday and Easter arithmetic for a dozen dates (879/880 sessions, 16 July 1054, 25 December 634, 11 March 843, 26 May 735) was recomputed and agrees with the entries. In every one of these spot checks the entry reported its source correctly; the scholarship named in the dating notes that I could test (Tangl/Robinson, Cross, Ólafía Einarsdóttir, Caspar/Tanner, Usener, Bruel) exists and says what is claimed.
Not checked against the source: the dmgh.de page images (Thietmar, the Kraków and Pressburg annals, the Legenda maior and Hartvic, the letters of Nicholas I and John VIII, the Continuator of Regino), Mansi 17 for the council of 879–880, the Slavonic Lives in their Latin versions, al-Tabari and al-Baladhuri, and the Greek of Theophanes; these were judged from memory only and looked right. One point I could not settle and did not report as a finding: Theophanes' "7 January" 730 for Germanus's resignation carries a weekday in the Greek that fits 17 January, and some reference works give the 17th; worth a look in de Boor, p. 408–409.
