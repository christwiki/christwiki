# Events: Reformation, 1516–1559

Reviewer batch E12. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · historical · src/content/events/reformation/luther-bible.md
- **Text:** "the letter of James was an "epistle of straw", having nothing of the gospel about it; the passage stood only in the edition of 1522"
- **Problem:** The closing section of the 1522 preface (with the "epistle of straw" sentence) was not confined to the September Testament. It was reprinted in Luther's separate New Testament editions for some fifteen years and was dropped from the complete Bibles from 1534 and from the separate New Testaments only after 1537. The entry repeats a loose editorial note of the 1932 Philadelphia edition ("appears only in the edition of 1522" / "is omitted from later editions").
- **Should be:** "the passage was dropped from the complete Bible of 1534 and from editions of the New Testament after 1537" (or simply "Luther later dropped the passage").
- **Evidence:** Opened the cited page (Works of Martin Luther 6, pp. 443–444): its note 1 says "appears only in the edition of 1522", its note 5 "is in the edition of 1522, but is omitted from later editions". Luther's Works (American Edition) 35, p. 358 n. 5 / p. 362, following WA DB 6, says the section "was omitted from all editions of the complete Bible and from editions of the New Testament after 1537"; a web search returned the same statement ("dropped out of editions of Luther's New Testament after 1537"). I could not open LW 35 itself.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/diet-of-worms.md
- **Text:** "B. J. Kidd's collection dates the first bull 15 June 1520 [[cite:exsurge-date]] and the edict 26 May 1521 [[cite:edict]]."
- **Problem:** The entry gives only Kidd's editorial date. The Edict of Worms itself bears the date 8 May 1521; it was signed by Charles V on 26 May, the day after the diet had closed (Kidd's own running head is "The Diet of Worms, 27 Jan.–25 May 1521") and after the elector of Saxony and other estates had left, although the text claims the "unanimous consent" of the electors and estates (Kidd § 26 "unanimi consensu"). A reader comparing the entry with an edition of the edict will find 8 May and no explanation.
- **Should be:** Add to the dating note: "The edict is dated 8 May 1521 in its own text; it was signed and issued on 26 May, after the diet had closed on the 25th."
- **Evidence:** Opened Kidd no. 45 (pp. 87–89): heading "The Edict of Worms, 26 May", dating clause not printed; § 27 speaks of the twenty days of safe-conduct "qui 14 huius mensis Maii terminabuntur", i.e. the text is written as of May before the 14th. The 8 May date and 26 May signature are standard (own knowledge; e.g. Deutsche Reichstagsakten J.R. 2, no. 92).
- **Confidence:** high (for the fact), medium (that it needs adding)

### B · historical · src/content/events/reformation/francis-xavier-in-asia.md
- **Text:** "He spoke Castilian and they "Malabar", so with the help of men who knew both languages he had the catechism translated"
- **Problem:** Xavier's letter does not say that he spoke Castilian. In the Spanish original he says that his own native language is Basque ("vizcaína"). "Castilian" is a slip of Coleridge's English version (made from the Latin tradition), which the entry reproduces as fact. The entry already uses *Monumenta Xaveriana* to correct this same letter's date, so the corrected reading was at hand.
- **Should be:** "They did not understand each other, he writes, their mother tongue being 'Malabar' (Tamil) and his Basque; so with the help of men who knew both languages [i.e. Portuguese/Spanish and Tamil] he had the prayers translated…" — and note in the dating note that Coleridge's "Castilian" is not what the Spanish text has.
- **Evidence:** Opened both texts. Coleridge vol. 1, p. 151: "We could not understand one another, as I spoke Castilian and they Malabar". *Monumenta Xaveriana* vol. 1 (the edition the entry itself cites for this letter), letter of 15 January 1544: "y como ellos no me entendiessen ni yo á ellos, por ser su lengua natural malauar y la mía bizcaina, ayunté los que entre ellos eran más sabios"; the Latin version printed there has "mea vero celtiberica, vulgo vazquenza".
- **Confidence:** high

### A · historical · src/content/events/reformation/marian-persecutions.md
- **Text:** "Mary died on 17 November 1558, and Pole, who had been consecrated archbishop of Canterbury the day after Cranmer's death, within days of her [[cite:machyn-cranmer]] [[cite:machyn-deaths]]."
- **Problem:** Pole died on the same day as the queen, 17 November 1558, in the evening, about twelve hours after her. The entry follows Machyn, who enters the cardinal's death under 19 November ("The xix day of November ded be-twyn v and vj in the morning my lord cardenall Polle"), an entry that is generally regarded as misdated; "within days of her" turns Machyn's slip into the wiki's statement. (The person entry `people/reginald-pole.md`, outside this batch, has the same problem: it offers Machyn's 19th and Foxe's "day after" and never gives the 17th.)
- **Should be:** "Mary died on 17 November 1558; Pole died at Lambeth the same evening (Machyn enters his death under the 19th)."
- **Evidence:** Opened Machyn at british-history.ac.uk (vol42/pp169-184): 17 Nov for Mary, 19 Nov for Pole. Opened Wikipedia "Reginald Pole": he "died in London, during an influenza epidemic, on 17 November 1558, at about 7:00 pm, nearly 12 hours after Queen Mary's death"; this is the date of the ODNB article (T. F. Mayer) as well (own knowledge). The consecration on 22 March 1556 is right.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/elizabethan-settlement.md
- **Text:** "The London diarist Henry Machyn puts the end of the Parliament that passed them on 10 May 1559"
- **Problem:** Correctly reported from Machyn ("The x day of May the parlement was endyd"), but Machyn is two days out: Elizabeth's first Parliament was dissolved on Monday 8 May 1559, when the queen gave the royal assent to the two Acts. The dating note gives only the diarist's date, for the one day on which the statutes actually became law.
- **Should be:** "Parliament was dissolved, and the Acts received the royal assent, on 8 May 1559 (Machyn enters it under 10 May)", cited to the Lords' Journal or D'Ewes.
- **Evidence:** Opened Machyn (vol42/pp184-201) — reads as the entry says. 8 May 1559 for the dissolution: Journals of the House of Lords vol. 1 / History of Parliament Trust (1559 Parliament, 25 Jan.–8 May 1559) — own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/calvin-in-geneva.md
- **Text:** "The translation cited prints the prefatory address with the date "Basle, 1st August 1536" and notes readings of "the edition published at Basle in 1536""
- **Problem:** The only dating evidence the note offers for the first edition is a date that cannot be right for it: the *Institutes* came from the press of Platter and Lasius at Basel in March 1536 (colophon "mense Martio"), and in that first edition the letter to Francis I is dated 23 August ("X. Calendas Septembres"), i.e. 1535. "1 August 1536" is the reading of the later Latin editions that Beveridge translated; it post-dates the book it prefaces by five months. As written, a reader will take 1 August 1536 as the date of the dedication or of publication.
- **Should be:** "The first edition was printed at Basel in March 1536 (colophon); its dedicatory letter to Francis I is dated 23 August [1535]. Later editions, followed by the translation cited, print 'Basle, 1st August 1536'."
- **Evidence:** CCEL page cited ends "Basle, 1st August 1536". Web search: Schaff (New Schaff-Herzog, "Calvin") and Wikipedia "Institutes of the Christian Religion" give March 1536 for publication; reformation21 and EB1911 give 23 August 1535 for the preface of the first edition and note the later "1 August 1536" as discrepant. I did not open a scan of the 1536 edition.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/erasmus-greek-new-testament.md
- **Text:** "The book prints the Greek text and a Latin version side by side in two columns [[cite:john]]." (the citation is to the page with 1 John 5)
- **Problem:** The entry cites the very page of 1 John 5 but uses it only for the layout. It leaves out the things for which this edition matters most in the history of the text: (a) the 1516 text (and the 1519 one) omits the "three heavenly witnesses" of 1 John 5:7–8 (the Comma Johanneum) because Erasmus's Greek manuscripts lacked it; attacked for this by Lee and Stunica, he inserted it in the third edition of 1522 from a recent Greek manuscript (Codex Montfortianus); (b) for Revelation he had one incomplete manuscript and supplied the last six verses (Rev 22:16–21) by translating the Vulgate back into Greek; (c) the "many manuscripts" of the title page were a handful of medieval minuscules at Basel; (d) the Complutensian Polyglot New Testament had already been printed (1514) but was not yet published. Nothing of this is anywhere in the wiki (grep for "Comma Johanneum", "Johannine Comma", "Complutens": 0 files), and the entry's last paragraph ("a New Testament corrected from Greek manuscripts") rests on the title page's own advertisement alone.
- **Should be:** Add two or three sentences, citable from the cited page itself (1 John 5 in the 1516 edition has no Comma), from Erasmus's *Apologia* against Stunica / the 1522 annotations, and from the annotations on Revelation 22; state the title page's "many manuscripts" as its claim (it is so attributed in paragraph 2, but not in the closing paragraph).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Metzger, *The Text of the New Testament*, ch. 3; H. J. de Jonge, "Erasmus and the Comma Johanneum", ETL 56 (1980)); grep of the repository.
- **Confidence:** high (that the facts are right and absent)

### C · historical · src/content/events/reformation/act-of-supremacy.md
- **Text:** summary: "John Fisher and Thomas More, who deny the title, are beheaded in 1535."
- **Problem:** In the wiki's own voice the summary says More denied the king's title. More's whole defence was that he had never denied it but kept silence; he was convicted of denying it on Richard Rich's testimony, which he contradicted, and spoke against the Act only after the verdict (as the body, following Roper, correctly says). The body attributes the charge to the chronicler; the summary asserts it.
- **Should be:** "John Fisher and Thomas More, who will not acknowledge the title, are beheaded in 1535 as traitors."
- **Evidence:** Roper's *Life* (the entry's own source, "the arraignment"): More speaks his mind on the supremacy only "seeing that ye are determined to condemn me"; own knowledge of the indictment and trial reports.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/events/reformation/anabaptists.md
- **Text:** "The *Martyrs Mirror* dates Sattler's execution 21 May 1527 [[cite:sattler]]."
- **Problem:** The note corrects the *Martyrs Mirror* on Manz (1526 → 5 January 1527) but lets its date for Sattler stand without comment. Modern reference works, from the contemporary reports of the trial, give 20 May 1527 (trial 17–18 May).
- **Should be:** Add "modern accounts, from the reports of the trial at Rottenburg, give 20 May".
- **Evidence:** Web search: Deutsche Biographie ("† 20.5.1527"), Wikipedia "Michael Sattler" and the memorial stone at Rottenburg give 20 May; 21 May appears only in *Martyrs Mirror*-derived pages. Not checked against the Flugschrift itself.
- **Confidence:** low-medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/council-of-trent.md
- **Text:** summary: "…define their church's teaching on Scripture and tradition, justification and the sacraments, and decree a reform of the clergy." Body, on the later sessions: "Among its later decrees, one orders every cathedral church to maintain a college… and the last uphold purgatory, the invocation of saints, images and indulgences"
- **Problem:** The summary promises the sacraments, but the body says nothing about them. The whole second period and most of the third are passed over: the seven sacraments and their working *ex opere operato* (Session 7, 3 March 1547), the real presence and transubstantiation (Session 13, 11 October 1551, ch. 4 and canons 1–2), penance and extreme unction (Session 14), communion under one kind (Session 21), the mass as a true propitiatory sacrifice (Session 22, 17 September 1562), holy orders (Session 23), and marriage with the decree *Tametsi* (Session 24). Original sin (Session 5, 17 June 1546) is also missing, though the last paragraph says the Tridentine profession names "original sin and justification". These are the definitions that answer the points the wiki's own Reformation entries raise (Zwingli's article 18, Marburg article 15, Augsburg Confession X and XXII–XXIV, the 1552 Prayer Book, Article XXVIII of the Thirty-Nine Articles), so the entry gives the Catholic answer on Scripture and justification but not on the eucharist. It also does not say that Protestant envoys (Württemberg, electoral Saxony, Strasbourg) did appear under the safe-conduct in January 1552 (Session 15) without result.
- **Should be:** Add a short paragraph: Session 7 (seven sacraments, canon 1; grace conferred *ex opere operato*, canon 8); Session 13 ch. 4 and canon 2 (transubstantiation); Session 22 ch. 2 and canons 1 and 3 (the mass a propitiatory sacrifice); and one clause on Session 5.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the decrees; the session pages are on the site the entry already cites (papalencyclicals.net/councils/trent/seventh-session.htm, thirteenth-session.htm, twenty-second-session.htm).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/our-lady-of-guadalupe.md
- **Text:** "The account as it stands was printed in Mexico City in 1649, in a book whose licences name Luis Laso de la Vega… That is 118 years after the date it gives."
- **Problem:** The entry (and its summary) treats the Nahuatl book of 1649 as the first appearance of the story in print. The first published account of the apparitions is the Spanish book of the priest Miguel Sánchez, *Imagen de la Virgen María, Madre de Dios de Guadalupe* (Mexico, 1648), a year earlier; Laso de la Vega wrote a commendatory letter for it, and the relation of the two books is the centre of the historical debate. The dating note's own phrase "between 1531 and 1648" (Poole) silently depends on Sánchez, whom the entry never names (grep "Sánchez", "Imagen de la Virgen": 0 files). "118 years" should then be 117 for the first printed account.
- **Should be:** Add: "The story first appeared in print in Spanish in Miguel Sánchez's *Imagen de la Virgen María* (Mexico, 1648); the Nahuatl account followed in 1649." Keep "118 years" only for the Nahuatl text.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Poole, *Our Lady of Guadalupe*, 1995, chs. 6–7; Brading, *Mexican Phoenix*, 2001); grep of the repository. The rest of the entry checked out: the Holy See biography (vatican.va, ns_lit_doc_20020731_juan-diego_en.html), which I opened, does say the Nican Mopohua was written in 1556 by Antonio Valeriano and gives 9 and 12 December 1531; 9 December 1531 was a Saturday, as the account says.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/sublimis-deus.md
- **Text:** "…that they are not to be enslaved; and that anything done to the contrary is null." / summary: "Las Casas and the Crown's own laws show that enslavement went on."
- **Problem:** The entry passes from the bull straight to the New Laws and so leaves out what happened to the bull itself. Its executory brief *Pastorale officium* (29 May 1537, to Cardinal Tavera — the document behind the "29 May" the dating note puzzles over) attached automatic excommunication to enslaving Indians; Charles V objected that the documents infringed his rights of patronage, had the copies sent to the Indies recalled, and obtained from Paul III in June 1538 the brief *Non indecens videtur* withdrawing the censures. Whether that also annulled *Sublimis Deus* is disputed. A reader is left to think Rome's declaration simply stood and was ignored by settlers.
- **Should be:** Add one or two sentences on *Pastorale officium* and the revocation of 1538, and say in the dating note that 29 May 1537 is the date of that brief, not a misdating of the bull.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (L. Hanke, "Pope Paul III and the American Indians", HTR 30 (1937)); web search confirms the 1538 letter *Non indecens videtur* removing the censures at Charles V's instance and 29 May 1537 as the date of *Pastorale officium* (Wikipedia "Pastorale officium", "Sublimis Deus"). I could not confirm the exact day (19 June 1538) from a primary text.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/peasants-war.md
- **Text:** "In the middle of May Müntzer was captured with the rebel army at [[place:frankenhausen]]; a confession taken from him was printed…" (and the closing "Luther did not withdraw what he had written.")
- **Problem:** The entry never says how the war ended. The battle of Frankenhausen (15 May 1525) appears only in the dating note, and there only as a date; that the peasant army was destroyed there (several thousand killed, almost no losses on the princes' side), that Müntzer was beheaded at Mühlhausen on 27 May (also only in the dating note), and that the risings in Swabia, Franconia, Alsace and Thuringia were put down by the Swabian League and the princes within weeks, with deaths that modern estimates put in the tens of thousands, is nowhere in the body. "Captured with the rebel army" is also loose: he was found hiding in the town after the rout. An event entry should state the outcome.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence or two: the defeat at Frankenhausen on 15 May, Müntzer's execution on 27 May, the suppression of the rising by the summer of 1525; cite e.g. Landgrave Philip's or Duke George's report of the battle, or Luther's letter to Rühel of 23 May, which the entry already uses.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; entry text. (Checked and found correct: Lotzer as drafter, 27 Feb.–1 March 1525; sermon of 13 July 1524; 16 May 1525 was the Tuesday after Cantate.)
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/events/reformation/luther-bible.md
- **Text:** "The complete Bible, *Biblia, das ist, die gantze Heilige Schrifft Deudsch*, was printed at Wittenberg by Hans Lufft in 1534 [[cite:bible1534]]."
- **Problem:** The entry treats Luther's judgment on New Testament books at length and ends with Trent's ruling "on these questions", but omits the feature of the 1534 Bible that Trent's canon decree actually answered: Luther gathered Judith, Wisdom, Tobit, Sirach, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees and the additions to Esther and Daniel into a separate section between the Testaments headed "Apocrypha: that is, books which are not held equal to the Holy Scriptures and yet are useful and good to read". The closing paragraph quotes Trent only on the Vulgate and interpretation, not on the canon, so the link between the two is lost here (it is made in `council-of-trent.md`, via the English Articles, not via Luther).
- **Should be:** Add one sentence on the Apocrypha section of 1534 with its heading, and in the last paragraph mention that the same session of Trent listed those books as canonical.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (title of the Apocrypha section in the 1534 Bible, WA DB 12); `council-of-trent.md` paragraph 2.
- **Confidence:** high (fact), medium (need)

## Completeness of this stretch

Checked against `event-index.txt` (22 events for 1516–1559) and by grep of `src/content/` for each term in quotation marks below.

- **Luther between the theses and the bull, 1518–1519.** Nothing on the Heidelberg Disputation (April 1518), the hearing before Cardinal Cajetan at Augsburg (October 1518) or the Leipzig Disputation with Johann Eck (July 1519), where Luther was brought to say that councils can err and that Hus was wrongly condemned — the step that explains *Exsurge Domine*. `ninety-five-theses.md` ends with "ordered to Rome"; `diet-of-worms.md` begins in 1520. Grep "Leipzig Disputation", "Johann Eck", "Heidelberg Disputation": 0; "Cajetan": only a source record. Belongs as a paragraph in `diet-of-worms.md` or an entry of its own.
- **Erasmus against Luther on free will (1524–25).** *De libero arbitrio* and *De servo arbitrio*, the break between humanist reform and the Reformation, are absent (grep "servo arbitrio", "Bondage of the Will", "libero arbitrio": 0). The Erasmus entry stops in 1516.
- **Luther's catechisms (1529)** and the reordering of worship and church life in Saxony (visitations, German Mass 1526) — grep "Small Catechism", "Large Catechism": 0. First-rank for how the Reformation was actually taught.
- **The Schmalkaldic League, the Schmalkaldic War, the Augsburg Interim and the Treaty of Passau (1531–1552).** `peace-of-augsburg.md` refers to "the treaty of Passau" without a word on what it was, and nothing explains why the emperor conceded the peace of 1555 (Mühlberg 1547, Interim 1548, the princes' revolt of 1552). Grep "Schmalkald": only the Smalcald Articles in the Book of Concord entries; "Interim": 0. Also absent from that entry: the *Declaratio Ferdinandea* (24 September 1555), Ferdinand's side-assurance to Lutheran nobles and towns in ecclesiastical territories, which with the ecclesiastical reservation was the main bone of contention down to 1618.
- **The Anabaptist kingdom of Münster (1534–35), Menno Simons and the Hutterites.** `anabaptists.md` stops in 1529; `places/munster.md` treats only 1648. Grep "John of Leiden", "Melchior Hoffman", "Menno Simons", "Jakob Hutter": 0 in events/people. Münster shaped every government's view of Anabaptists for a century, and Menno the tradition that survived.
- **The Henrician Reformation after 1535.** No mention anywhere of the dissolution of the monasteries (1536–40), the Pilgrimage of Grace (1536), the Ten and Six Articles, the Great Bible ordered into every parish church (1539), or the fall of Cromwell (grep "issolution", "Pilgrimage of Grace", "Great Bible": 0; "Six Articles": only `people/hugh-latimer.md`). `act-of-supremacy.md` jumps from 1535 to "an Act of 1539". The dissolution is among the largest changes in English religious life in the period. Likewise the Edwardian Forty-two Articles (1553) and the Coverdale/Matthew Bibles appear only in passing in `king-james-bible.md`.
- **Zwingli's death at Kappel (1531) and the Swiss confessional settlement; Bullinger; the Consensus Tigurinus (1549).** Kappel is mentioned only in `people/huldrych-zwingli.md`; the agreement of Zurich and Geneva on the Supper, which made a single "Reformed" confession family, is absent (grep "Consensus Tigurinus": 0). Martin Bucer and Strasbourg appear only as a name in two files.
- **Catholic reform before and beside Trent.** Nothing on the new orders other than the Jesuits (Theatines 1524, Capuchins 1528, Ursulines 1535: grep 0), on the Colloquy of Regensburg of 1541 (Contarini, the agreed article on justification — grep "Regensburg" finds only unrelated files), on the Roman Inquisition (*Licet ab initio*, 1542) or the first Roman Index (1559) under Paul IV (grep "Paul IV", "Carafa": 0; "Index" only for Galileo). The Sack of Rome of 1527 is also absent (the only "sack-of-rome" entry is 410).
- **The Reformation in Scandinavia and the Baltic** (Sweden 1527, Denmark–Norway 1536/37) — no entry; whole national churches changed confession.
- **France before 1562**: the Affair of the Placards (1534), the first national synod at Paris and the Gallican Confession (1559) are absent (grep "Placards", "Gallican Confession": 0); the story begins with St Bartholomew (1572). The Belgic Confession (1561) is absent too.
- **Covered thinly inside existing entries** (see the C findings): Trent's sacramental decrees; the Comma Johanneum and the text-critical character of Erasmus's edition; the outcome of the Peasants' War; Miguel Sánchez (1648) for Guadalupe; the fate of *Sublimis Deus* in 1538. Also Xavier's two years in the Moluccas (Ambon, Ternate, Moro, 1546–47) are skipped: the entry passes from Cape Comorin (1544) to Malacca (December 1547).
