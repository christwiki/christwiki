# Sources first cited for events c. 1300–1558

Reviewer batch S04. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### B · dating · src/content/sources/hutterite-chronicle.md
- **Text:** front matter `written: start: 1542, end: 1665, circa: true`; body: "the chronicle was begun by Kaspar Braitmichel, who carried it to 1542"
- **Problem:** 1542 is the year at which Braitmichel's part of the narrative stops, not the year he began to write. The front matter turns the end of his coverage into the start of composition, so the record understates the gap between the first adult baptism of 1525 and the account of it by some twenty to thirty years. Braitmichel (d. 1573) compiled the book in Moravia in the 1560s, under the eldership of Peter Walpot (elder from 1565).
- **Should be:** `written: start: 1565` (circa) with display such as "Begun c. 1565–1570 by Kaspar Braitmichel (d. 1573), who carried the narrative to 1542; continued by others to 1665", and a sentence in the body that the account of January 1525 was written about forty years after the event.
- **Evidence:** Wolkan's preface in the edition cited (archive.org full text of geschichtbuchder00hutt, opened): "Begonnen wurde das Geschicht-Buch von dem Vorsteher der Gemeinde Kaspar Braitmichel, der es bis zum Jahre 1542 führte, worauf Hanns Kräl und der Schreiber der Gemeinde Hauprecht Zapff sein Werk fortsetzten; von der Hand des letzteren stammt das erhaltene Original", and Zapff marks himself as scribe with the year 1581. So 1542 is the end of Braitmichel's coverage and the surviving codex was written about 1581. For the date of Braitmichel's own work: own knowledge (Mennonite Encyclopedia / GAMEO, "Braitmichel, Kaspar (d. 1573)"; A. J. F. Zieglschmid, Die älteste Chronik der Hutterischen Brüder, 1943); GAMEO refused automated requests, and a web search confirmed only his death in 1573 and that he wrote the chronicle "later in his life".
- **Confidence:** high that 1542 is not the date of writing; medium for "c. 1565–1570"

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/edict-of-worms.md
- **Text:** "Kidd prints extracts and dates the edict 26 May 1521"; front matter `display: "May 1521"`, `language: Latin`; "with, as it says, the consent of the electors, princes and estates assembled there"
- **Problem:** The record gives only the date in Kidd's heading. The edict itself bears the date 8 May 1521; Charles V signed the Latin and German copies on 26 May, the day after the diet was formally closed and after the electors of Saxony and the Palatinate had left, so the "unanimous consent" it claims was that of the estates who remained. Kidd's own extract shows the earlier date (§27: the twenty days of the safe-conduct "which will end on the 14th of this month of May"). The record also gives the language as Latin only: Aleander drafted it in Latin, but it was issued and printed for the empire in German as well.
- **Should be:** "The text is dated 8 May 1521; the emperor signed it on 26 May, after the diet had closed and several princes had left, which is the date Kidd gives." `language: Latin and German`. The same clarification is wanted where [[event:diet-of-worms]] dates the edict from Kidd alone.
- **Evidence:** Kidd no. 45, §27 ("elapsis praefatis viginti diebus qui 14 huius mensis Maii terminabuntur"), read in the archive.org full text. Schaff, History of the Christian Church vol. 7 §56 (ccel.org/ccel/schaff/hcc7.ii.iii.xxviii.html) and other pages found by web search: the edict was "completed and dated May 8 (but not signed till May 26)"; signed after high mass on Trinity Sunday, Latin and German copies.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/charles-v-declaration-on-luther-1521.md
- **Text:** front matter has no `language` field; body does not say in what language the declaration was written
- **Problem:** The original language is missing, and the record is otherwise careful to say that the English is Armstrong's. Charles V wrote the declaration in French, in his own hand, and had it read to the princes in French and then in German.
- **Should be:** `language: French`, with a clause in the body: "written by the emperor in French and read to the estates with a German translation".
- **Evidence:** Kidd no. 43 (opened) gives only Armstrong's English. Web search: Christian History Institute, "The young emperor's confession" (christianhistoryinstitute.org/blog/post/young-emperors-confession): the emperor "showed them the sheet he held in his hand and had it read, first in French and then in a German translation"; the text ends "Made by my hand this 19 April 1521. Signed: Charles." The French original is printed in Deutsche Reichstagsakten, Jüngere Reihe 2, no. 82 (own knowledge).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/sources/university-of-paris-on-the-schism-1393.md
- **Text:** "`display: \"1393, as dated in the source book\"`"; "According to the editors' note, the king of France asked the university in 1393 to find a way out of the schism"; file id and `cite` carry the year 1393
- **Problem:** The record hedges but still files the document under the source book's year. The consultation of the masters (the opinions collected in a chest at the Mathurins, sifted by a commission of fifty-four) took place in January–February 1394, and the university's letter to Charles VI setting out the three ways (cession, compromise, council), drafted by Nicolas de Clamanges, is dated 6 June 1394 and was presented to the king on 30 June 1394. Thatcher and McNeal's "1393" is an old-style or mistaken year.
- **Should be:** `start: 1394`, display "6 June 1394 (the source book dates it 1393)", and a sentence saying so.
- **Evidence:** The Thatcher–McNeal text (no. 168) was opened and does print "1393". Web search: en.wikipedia.org "Nicholas of Clamanges" — the university's letter to Charles VI was "prepared and drafted on June 6 to 8 and presented to the king … on June 30" (1394). Own knowledge for the edition: Denifle–Chatelain, Chartularium Universitatis Parisiensis III, no. 1683; N. Valois, La France et le Grand Schisme, vol. 2, pp. 406–416.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/cochlaeus-commentaria.md
- **Text:** "He wrote more than twenty years later, calls the translators apostates and their book Luther's Testament, and never saw them."
- **Problem:** True of the 1549 text, but it overstates the distance of Cochlaeus's testimony. Arber, on the pages before the extract cited, prints two earlier accounts by Cochlaeus of the same Cologne episode: one in *An expediat laicis legere Novi Testamenti libros lingua vernacula* (colophon Dresden, 10 June 1533), eight years after the event, which Arber calls "his earliest printed reference", and one in his *Scopa* against Richard Morison (Leipzig, March 1538). The record's "more than twenty years" hides the fact that the story was already in print in 1533.
- **Should be:** Add: "He had told the story twice before in print, in 1533 and 1538 (both reprinted by Arber); the fullest version, cited here, is that of 1549."
- **Evidence:** archive.org full text of Arber, The First Printed English New Testament (1871), pp. 18–20: "printed a letter to James V. of Scotland (Colophon dated Dresden, vi. Idus Junij 1533) ... in which occurs his earliest printed reference"; "COCHLÆUS' SECOND ACCOUNT" from the Scopa, "Colophon dated Lipsiae, March 1538".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · dating · src/content/sources/more-dialogue-concerning-heresies.md
- **Text:** front matter `written: start: 1530, display: "Revised edition dated 1530"`; body: "The title page of the text cited is dated 1530 and describes the book as newly revised by its author, by then Chancellor of England."
- **Problem:** The record never says when the work was first published. The Dialogue was first printed by John Rastell in June 1529, when More was still chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster; the revised edition whose title page says "1530" was printed by William Rastell and its colophon is dated May 1531. A reader of the record would date More's reply to Tyndale a year late, and `written.start` gives the title-page year of the second edition, not the date of composition.
- **Should be:** `start: 1529`, display "First printed June 1529; revised edition with title page dated 1530 (colophon 1531)", and one sentence in the body.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (STC 18084 and 18085; Yale Complete Works vol. 6, introduction). A web search confirmed first publication by John Rastell in June 1529 and a second edition by William Rastell in 1531 "newly overseen by the said Sir Thomas More, Chancellor of England"; the "1530" on the second title page was not re-verified online.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/sources/joan-of-arc-trial-of-condemnation.md
- **Text:** "The official record of the trial of Joan of Arc for heresy at Rouen, from 21 February to 30 May 1431" (summary)
- **Problem:** 21 February is the first public interrogation, not the beginning of the trial or of its record. The record opens with the preliminary sessions from 9 January 1431 (the bishop's first meeting, the appointment of the officers, the preparatory inquiry), and it closes after the execution with the posthumous "information" of 7 June. Murray's own chronological table, in the volume the record cites, gives "January 9th, Tuesday. First day of the Trial".
- **Should be:** "…the trial of Joan of Arc for heresy at Rouen, from its opening on 9 January 1431 (the public examinations began on 21 February) to the sentence of 30 May 1431".
- **Evidence:** archive.org full text of T. Douglas Murray, Jeanne d'Arc (1902), chronological table: "January 9th, Tuesday . . First day of the Trial. Preliminary meeting in the Bishop's house. Appointment of officers."; "First Public Examination, in the Chapel-Royal" follows under 21 February.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/ignatius-autobiography.md
- **Text:** front matter `language: Spanish`; body: "passed through a secretary's memory and through translation before it reached this English text"
- **Problem:** The original is not wholly Spanish. Gonçalves da Câmara wrote out the first two stages of the dictation in Spanish at Rome; the last part he dictated at Genoa in December 1555, where, as his own preface says, he had no Spanish secretary, and that part of the original text is in Italian. (The edition used renders the preface "I dictated in Latin the points I had brought with me", which is a slip of O'Conor's version for "in Italian".)
- **Should be:** `language: Spanish, the last part in Italian`, with a clause in the body.
- **Evidence:** archive.org full text of O'Conor's edition, Preface of the Writer: "At Genoa where I went I had no Spanish secretary, so I dictated in Latin the points I had brought with me, and finished the writing at Genoa in December, 1555." Web search (Boston College "Ignatian pilgrimage narratives"; World History Encyclopedia art. 2008): text "mostly in Spanish, save for the final twenty paragraphs, which are in Italian". Standard edition: Fontes Narrativi I (MHSI 66, 1943).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/new-laws-of-the-indies-1542.md
- **Text:** "no new allotment (*encomienda*) may be granted, the existing ones passing to the Crown on the holder's death" … "It does not show how far the commands were obeyed."
- **Problem:** The record presents the clause on the reversion of encomiendas as standing law and says only that obedience is another question. That clause (chapter 30 in the usual numbering) was formally revoked by Charles V at Mechelen on 20 October 1545, after the revolt in Peru and the protests in New Spain; inheritance for a second life was restored. A reader is left thinking the New Laws abolished the hereditary encomienda. The event entry that cites the laws ([[event:sublimis-deus]]) does not mention the revocation either (grep for "1545", "revok").
- **Should be:** Add: "The clause ending the inheritance of encomiendas was revoked by the emperor on 20 October 1545; the ban on enslaving Indians stood."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (L. B. Simpson, The Encomienda in New Spain, ch. 10; L. Hanke, The Spanish Struggle for Justice, pp. 100–102). Web search confirmed the fact and the year ("in 1545 the Crown revoked the inheritance restriction of the New Laws", Wikipedia "New Laws"; encyclopedia.com "New Laws of 1542"); the day and place were not re-verified online.
- **Confidence:** medium (fact and year high)

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/paul-iii-sublimis-deus.md
- **Text:** "the web page that reproduces his translation dates it 29 May. The bull states a principle and attaches no penalty. What followed in the Indies has to be learned from other sources."
- **Problem:** The record treats "29 May" as a bare error and leaves out the two documents without which the bull's standing cannot be weighed. 29 May 1537 is the date of the companion brief *Pastorale officium* to Cardinal Juan de Tavera, which did attach a penalty (excommunication latae sententiae for those who enslaved or despoiled the Indians). At Charles V's protest Paul III annulled that brief, and anything prejudicial to the emperor's rights done on its strength, by the brief *Non indecens videtur* of 19 June 1538; whether that annulment touched *Sublimis Deus* itself is debated. Neither document is named in the record or in [[event:sublimis-deus]].
- **Should be:** Add two sentences: the date 29 May belongs to *Pastorale officium*, the executory brief with the penalty; that brief was revoked by *Non indecens videtur* (19 June 1538) at the emperor's request, and scholars differ on whether the revocation reached the bull.
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org "Sublimis Deus" (opened, raw text): "The ecclesiastical letter Pastorale officium, issued a few days before the encyclical, imposed excommunication on conquerors who enslave the Indigenous people. This punishment was subsequently repealed after petition by the Emperor." Own knowledge for the 1538 brief (L. Hanke, "Pope Paul III and the American Indians", Harvard Theological Review 30, 1937; H. R. Parish and H. E. Weidman, Las Casas en México, 1992).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/inter-caetera-1493.md
- **Text:** "The bull is addressed to Ferdinand and Isabella and dated at St Peter's in Rome on 4 May 1493"
- **Problem:** The record gives the date of the document as a plain fact and treats the bull as a single act. There are two bulls *Inter caetera*: the first, dated 3 May 1493, granted the lands without a line; the one described here, with the line a hundred leagues west of the Azores and Cape Verde, bears the date 4 May but was drawn up and dispatched only in late June 1493 and antedated (with *Eximiae devotionis*, also dated 3 May, expedited in July). For a record that is otherwise strict about dating clauses this should be said, since "4 May" is not when the grant with the line was made.
- **Should be:** Add: "A first bull of the same name, dated 3 May, made the grant without a line; this second one carries the date 4 May but, as the papal registers show, was expedited in June 1493."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (H. Vander Linden, "Alexander VI and the Demarcation of the Maritime and Colonial Domains of Spain and Portugal, 1493–1494", American Historical Review 22, 1916; F. G. Davenport, European Treaties bearing on the History of the United States, 1917, docs. 5–7). Web search confirmed the three documents of 3 May / 3 May / 4 May and one page giving 28 June as the date of issue of the second *Inter caetera*; Vander Linden's article was not opened in full.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/requerimiento.md
- **Text:** "`author: Issued in the name of King Ferdinand and Queen Juana of Castile`" (no drafter named anywhere in the record)
- **Problem:** The record does not say who wrote the text or how its date is known. It was drawn up in 1513 by the royal jurist Juan López de Palacios Rubios, of the Council of Castile, after the junta of Burgos and Valladolid (1512–1513), for the expedition of Pedrarias Dávila; Las Casas and Oviedo both name him. Authorship is one of the first things the task of a source record is to state.
- **Should be:** "Drafted in 1513 by the jurist Juan López de Palacios Rubios for the crown, and first carried by the expedition of Pedrarias Dávila to Darién in 1514."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Las Casas, Historia de las Indias, book 3, ch. 57; L. Hanke, The Spanish Struggle for Justice in the Conquest of America, 1949, ch. 3).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/sources/villani-chronicle.md
- **Text:** "The chronicle of a Florentine who began writing in 1300" (summary); front matter `written: start: 1300, circa: true, display: "Begun in 1300"`
- **Problem:** The summary and front matter state as fact what the body correctly gives as Villani's own claim (book 8, chapter 36). Historians since Louis Green have doubted that the composition began at the jubilee; the surviving text was put together and revised from the 1320s onward, and Villani went on writing until his death in the plague of 1348. `written` has no end year, which hides the fact that the chapters on Anagni (1303) and Clement V (1305) reached their present form decades after the events.
- **Should be:** Summary: "a Florentine who says that he began writing in 1300". Front matter: `start: 1300, end: 1348, circa: true, display: "By his own account begun in 1300; written and revised until 1348"`.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (L. Green, Chronicle into History, 1972, pp. 9–12, 155–164; F. Ragone, Giovanni Villani e i suoi continuatori, 1998). Not re-verified online: the Wikipedia article repeats Villani's own statement.
- **Confidence:** low

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/edict-of-expulsion-1492.md
- **Text:** title "Edict of Expulsion of the Jews from Castile and Aragon"; "The edict is issued in the names of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile … and the name of the secretary Juan de Coloma."
- **Problem:** The text printed by Amador de los Ríos is the Castilian edict. For the crown of Aragon a separate edict of the same day was issued by Ferdinand alone, with a different statement of reasons (it adds the charge of usury and cites the inquisitor general's advice). The record's title and body let the reader suppose a single text for both crowns.
- **Should be:** One sentence: "This is the text issued for Castile; a separate edict of the same date, signed by Ferdinand alone and differently worded, was issued for the crown of Aragon and is not cited here."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (R. Conde y Delgado de Molina, La expulsión de los judíos de la Corona de Aragón: documentos, 1991; H. Beinart, The Expulsion of the Jews from Spain, 2002, ch. 2). Not re-verified online.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/sources/declaration-of-the-cardinals-1378.md
- **Text:** "`author: The cardinals who had elected Urban VI`"; "The manifesto in which the cardinals who had elected Urban VI in April 1378 declared the election void" (summary)
- **Problem:** "The cardinals who had elected" implies the whole electoral college. The declaration issued at Anagni (dated 2 August, published 9 August 1378) came from the "ultramontane" cardinals: the French electors and the Spaniard Pedro de Luna, with Jean de La Grange, who had not been in the conclave — thirteen in all. The three surviving Italian electors (Orsini, Corsini, Brossano) did not put their names to it; they are the "three Italian cardinals" to whom Catherine of Siena later wrote, as the wiki's own record of her letters says. The source book's note also speaks only of "the cardinals of the French party". The event entry is more careful ("cardinals who had elected him").
- **Should be:** author "Thirteen cardinals at Anagni: the French electors of Urban VI and Pedro de Luna"; summary "…in which the French cardinals who had taken part in electing Urban VI, with one Spaniard, declared the election void…"; add that the Italian cardinals did not sign.
- **Evidence:** Thatcher and McNeal no. 167, editors' note (archive.org full text, opened): "the cardinals of the French party became more and more displeased with Urban and soon rebelled"; the text itself says "not all the cardinals were present at the election". Number and names from own knowledge (W. Ullmann, The Origins of the Great Schism, 1948, ch. 4; Catholic Encyclopedia, "Western Schism").
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · dating · src/content/sources/petrarch-liber-sine-nomine.md
- **Text:** front matter `written: start: 1340, end: 1353, circa: true, display: "c. 1340–1353"`
- **Problem:** The record is for the collection, but the range is the sourcebook's bracket for one letter (the years of Petrarch's residence near Avignon). The nineteen letters of the *Liber sine nomine* are usually dated between about 1342 and 1359, the last ones written from Milan after he had left Provence for good, and the preface was added about 1360. The front matter therefore closes the collection six years too early.
- **Should be:** `start: 1342, end: 1359, circa: true`, display "c. 1342–1359 (the letter cited: before 1353)".
- **Evidence:** The Fordham page was opened and does head the extract "Letter to a friend, 1340–1353". Web search: en.wikipedia.org "Liber sine nomine" (nineteen letters) and a 2025 article by G. Cascio cited there, which places the letters "from 1341 to the late 1350s", with the collection assembled at the end of 1359 or by early 1361. Own knowledge: N. P. Zacour, Petrarch's Book Without a Name (Toronto, 1973), introduction.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Method note

All 150 records were read in full. None contains a `[[bible:…]]` citation, so no Scripture checks were needed (grep confirmed zero tokens). About 50 web pages or full texts were opened, mostly the editions the records themselves cite: Kidd's Documents, Thatcher–McNeal, Arber, Raynaldus vol. 25 (the Latin of Inter sollicitudines), Ruelens's facsimile introduction, Rigg's Decameron, Murray's Jeanne d'Arc, O'Conor's Ignatius, Workman–Pope's Hus, Wolkan's Hutterite chronicle, the Göttingen Gutenberg pages, vatican.va, christianunity.va, ccel.org, newadvent.org, legislation.gov.uk, the Pitts and Stuttgart library pages and the English text of the two Palamite tomes. Counts, dating clauses, weekdays (computed) and quotations checked in these all agreed with the records; the batch is unusually accurate as to what its cited editions say, and the findings concern what the records leave out or take over from an edition without correction.
Not checked: Project Gutenberg (down for maintenance), web.archive.org and gameo.org (refused), so the Fita article with Sixtus IV's bulls, the Selfe Villani and the Scudder Catherine were not reopened; shelf-marks of the Basel, Wittenberg and Brussels copies; page and folio locators inside Höfler, Palacký, Quicherat, Bernáldez and Lea. Findings resting mainly on own knowledge (the day and place of the 1545 revocation, Palacios Rubios, the Aragonese edict, Villani, the "1530" title page of More's second edition, the 1538 brief Non indecens videtur) should be confirmed against the works named before editing.
