# Sources first cited for events c. 200–640

Reviewer batch S02. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### A · historical · src/content/sources/ezana-inscription-rie-271.md
- **Text:** "The stone is 1.63 metres high; the record says that it was found at Aksum about 1959."
- **Problem:** The Greek inscription with the Trinitarian opening (RIÉ 271, on the same stone as RIÉ 190) was found at Aksum in 1969 and first published in 1970; "about 1959" is ten years too early. Either the online record was misread or its figure was repeated without a check against the first publication (the authoring guide warns against citing a museum/database record for the circumstances of a find).
- **Should be:** "…it was found at Aksum in 1969 and first published by F. Anfray, A. Caquot and P. Nautin, 'Une nouvelle inscription grecque d'Ézana, roi d'Axoum', Journal des Savants 1970, pp. 260–274."
- **Evidence:** Dictionary of African Christian Biography, s.v. Ezana (https://dacb.org/stories/ethiopia/ezana/): "another inscription discovered only in 1969 shows beyond a doubt that he was a Christian"; the first publication is dated 1970 and titled "Une nouvelle inscription…" (Persée, doi 10.3406/jds.1970.1235; the article's introduction says the new text bears on the Noba campaign and the coming of Christianity). I could not open the Beta maṣāḥǝft record itself (it sits behind an automated-access check), so I cannot say whether the record prints 1959 or the entry misread it; also not checked for the same reason: the 1.63 m height, and the "about two and a half metres" of ezana-inscription-rie-270.md.
- **Confidence:** medium

### B · completeness · src/content/sources/julian-letters.md
- **Text:** "For the religious policy of his reign they are the primary evidence, since they come from the emperor himself. Cited here are … the letter to Arsacius, high priest of Galatia (22), on reforming the pagan priesthood and founding hostels"
- **Problem:** The record vouches for all the cited letters as the emperor's own, but the authenticity of the letter to Arsacius (Wright 22 = Bidez 84) is seriously disputed. It is not transmitted in the manuscript collections of Julian's letters; it survives only as quoted by Sozomen (Church History 5.16), and Peter Van Nuffelen has argued that it is a later Christian forgery. The event entry julian-the-apostate quotes it as Julian's words ("the impious Galilaeans support not only their own poor but ours as well") without any caveat, so the omission here carries through.
- **Should be:** Add: "The letter to Arsacius survives only in Sozomen's Church History (5.16). Its authenticity is disputed: P. Van Nuffelen, 'Deux fausses lettres de Julien l'Apostat (la lettre aux Juifs, Ep. 51 [Wright], et la lettre à Arsacius, Ep. 84 [Bidez])', Vigiliae Christianae 56 (2002), pp. 131–150, argues that it is a forgery; others defend it." And soften "since they come from the emperor himself" to cover the genuine letters only.
- **Evidence:** Bibliographic record of Van Nuffelen's article confirmed (relbib.de/Record/1645896366: Vigiliae Christianae 56/2, 2002, 131–150, title as above). Sole transmission through Sozomen 5.16 and the existence of defences of the letter: own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · historical · src/content/sources/gelasius-letter-to-anastasius.md
- **Text:** "known from its opening words as Duo sunt" (summary; also the title "Letter to the Emperor Anastasius (Duo sunt)")
- **Problem:** "Duo sunt" are not the opening words of the letter. Gelasius's letter 12 opens "Famuli vestrae pietatis, filii mei Faustus magister et Irenaeus…"; "Duo quippe sunt, imperator auguste, quibus principaliter mundus hic regitur" opens its second section, the famous passage, which is all that Robinson's extract translates.
- **Should be:** "known from the opening words of its best-known passage as Duo sunt"; and say in the body that the linked extract is that one passage (section 2) of a much longer letter (Thiel, Epistolae Romanorum pontificum genuinae 1, letter 12).
- **Evidence:** Thiel's text (archive.org/details/epistolaeromanor01cath, OCR): "1. Famuli vestrae pietatis filii mei Faustus magister et Ire[naeus]…" and Thiel's own running head "In Gelasii papae epistolam 12 ('Famuli')". The Fordham page begins directly with "There are two powers, august Emperor".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/sources/passion-of-perpetua-and-felicity.md
- **Text:** "framed by an anonymous editor who witnessed the end" (summary)
- **Problem:** Stated in the wiki's own voice as fact. The editor does not say that he watched the deaths; his preface says only, in words borrowed from 1 John 1, "what we have heard and handled, declare also to you", and the narrative of the arena is told in the third person. That the editor was an eyewitness is an inference of scholars, and the body of the record ("an editor introduces the martyrs and describes their deaths") is more careful than the summary.
- **Should be:** "framed by an anonymous editor who describes their deaths and presents himself as reporting what 'we have heard and handled'."
- **Evidence:** Read the linked translation (newadvent.org/fathers/0324.htm), preface: "And we therefore, what we have heard and handled, declare also to you, brethren and little children"; no explicit claim of presence in the arena narrative (chapter 6).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/gregory-the-great-dialogues.md
- **Text:** "author: Gregory the Great … Gregory wrote the *Dialogues* about 593, three years after he became pope."
- **Problem:** The record does not mention that Gregory's authorship has been challenged in modern scholarship. Francis Clark, The Pseudo-Gregorian Dialogues (2 vols, Leiden: Brill, 1987), argued that the work is a late seventh-century composition using genuine Gregorian fragments. Most scholars (P. Meyvaert, A. de Vogüé, R. Godding) have rejected the thesis, but since rule-of-benedict.md says that "almost everything known about Benedict comes from the second book of the Dialogues", the reader should be told that the attribution has been contested and that the consensus upholds it.
- **Should be:** Add one sentence, e.g. "Gregory's authorship was challenged by Francis Clark (The Pseudo-Gregorian Dialogues, 1987); most scholars have not been persuaded and continue to treat the work as his."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; not re-opened in this session.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/xian-stele.md
- **Text:** "holding: Xi'an Beilin Museum (Forest of Stone Steles), Xi'an, China" / "The stone is now in the Beilin Museum in Xi'an."
- **Problem:** For an object the record gives no find history. The stele was buried and was unearthed near Xi'an (or at Zhouzhi, the accounts differ) in 1623 or 1625; it became known in Europe through the Jesuits (Trigault, Semedo, Kircher), its genuineness was doubted by some European writers into the nineteenth century, and it was moved into the Beilin in 1907. A reader weighing a monument "made in 781" needs to know that it was out of sight for most of the intervening centuries and when it reappeared.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence on the discovery (1623 or 1625, near Xi'an; reported by the Jesuit missionaries; placed in the Forest of Steles in 1907) and that its authenticity, once questioned, is now accepted.
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; the alopen-in-china event entry should be checked for the same omission.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/coptic-synaxarium.md
- **Text:** (front matter has no `written` field) "The narratives were composed long after the persons they describe"
- **Problem:** The record gives no date at all for the compilation, although "how late" is the one thing a reader needs in order to weigh it. The Copto-Arabic Synaxarium is a medieval compilation (thirteenth to fourteenth century; tradition attributes it to Peter Severus al-Jamil, bishop of Malij, and Michael, bishop of Atrib and Malij), and the English text online follows a modern printed recension. The linked 7 Tout entry also dates Dioscorus's death to "the year 451 A.D.", three years before the usual date (454), which illustrates the point.
- **Should be:** Add `written` (c. 1200–1400, circa) and a sentence: "The Arabic Synaxarium was compiled in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries from older lives; the online English text follows a modern edition."
- **Evidence:** copticchurch.net/synaxarium/1_7.html read ("On this day of the year 451 A.D., the blessed father and the great champion of Orthodoxy…"); date and attribution of the compilation from own knowledge (Coptic Encyclopedia, s.v. Synaxarion, Copto-Arabic).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/flavian-appeal-to-leo.md
- **Text:** "language: Latin, from a lost Greek original" … "Its value is that it is immediate"
- **Problem:** The record says nothing about how the text is transmitted. The appeal was unknown until Guerrino Amelli found it, with the appeal of Eusebius of Dorylaeum, in a manuscript of the chapter library of Novara in 1874 (published 1882; re-edited by Mommsen in 1886). A document that surfaced in the nineteenth century in a single Latin manuscript should say so, as the record for the Book of the Himyarites does.
- **Should be:** Add: "It survives in a single Latin manuscript in the chapter library of Novara, where G. Amelli found it in 1874 and published it in 1882."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Amelli, S. Leone Magno e l'Oriente, 1882; Mommsen, Neues Archiv 11, 1886; Lacey's tract is a translation of that text). Not re-opened; the year of discovery should be confirmed in Lacey's preface.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/athanasius-festal-letters.md
- **Text:** "Of the thirty-ninth letter, for Easter 367, the part containing the list of the books of Scripture is preserved in the original Greek by the Byzantine canonist Theodore Balsamon, and in a Syriac translation."
- **Problem:** Omits the Coptic. By far the largest part of Letter 39 survives only in Coptic (ed. L.-Th. Lefort 1955; further leaves R.-G. Coquin 1984), including the context of the list: Athanasius's attack on "apocryphal" books and on teachers who use them. The front matter itself says "surviving mainly in Syriac and Coptic", and the translation linked (NPNF) gives only the Greek fragment, so a reader is left thinking the list is all that remains.
- **Should be:** "…is preserved in Greek in the Byzantine canon-law collections and in Syriac; most of the rest of the letter survives only in Coptic, which the public-domain translation used here does not include."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (D. Brakke, "A New Fragment of Athanasius's Thirty-Ninth Festal Letter", HTR 103 (2010), gives a full translation from Greek, Syriac and Coptic). The Festal Index wording and the death date 7 Pachon were checked at CCEL and are correct.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · consistency · src/content/sources/catholic-oriental-orthodox-commission-report-2016.md
- **Text:** "lists the member churches on the Oriental Orthodox side: … the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church and the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church" / summary: "names the churches that make up the Oriental Orthodox family and send delegates to the dialogue"
- **Problem:** The report does not give a list of "member churches". It names the six churches whose representatives joined the Catholic delegates and then adds: "No representative of the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahdo Church was able to attend." The record folds the absent church into the list of those sending delegates without saying so.
- **Should be:** "names the Oriental Orthodox churches represented at the meeting (Syrian, Armenian of Etchmiadzin and of Cilicia, Coptic, Ethiopian, Malankara) and notes that no representative of the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church could attend."
- **Evidence:** Report text at christianunity.va read: "Joining delegates from the Catholic Church were representatives of the following Oriental Orthodox Churches: the Antiochian Syrian Orthodox Church, the Armenian Apostolic Church (Catholicosate of All Armenians), the Armenian Apostolic Church (Holy See of Cilicia), the Coptic Orthodox Church, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, and the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church. No representative of the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahdo Church was able to attend."
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/zachariah-of-mytilene-chronicle.md
- **Text:** "Zachariah was a native of Gaza who studied law at Alexandria and Beirut"
- **Problem:** Loose. By his own Life of Severus, Zachariah studied grammar and rhetoric at Alexandria (where he met Severus) and law at Beirut; there was no law study at Alexandria. The wiki follows the compressed wording of Hamilton and Brooks's 1899 introduction ("studied law in company with Severus at Alexandria and Berytus").
- **Should be:** "who studied rhetoric at Alexandria and law at Beirut".
- **Evidence:** Hamilton and Brooks's introduction read at tertullian.org/fathers/zachariah00.htm (wording as quoted); the correction is from own knowledge of the Life of Severus.
- **Confidence:** low

### C · historical · src/content/sources/origen-on-first-principles.md
- **Text:** "Where the Greek survives, in book 4, it is printed beside the Latin." (edition)
- **Problem:** Incomplete: the Greek also survives, through the Philocalia, for the long chapter on free will, book 3 chapter 1, and the ANF translation prints the Greek and Latin versions in parallel there as well as in book 4.
- **Should be:** "Where the Greek survives at length, in book 3 chapter 1 and in book 4, both versions are translated side by side."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of ANF vol. 4 (De Principiis 3.1 "translated from the Greek" beside Rufinus's Latin).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/chronicon-paschale.md
- **Text:** "For the reign of [[person:constantine]] the work is therefore a late compilation, three centuries after the events"
- **Problem:** True as far as it goes, but the record never says that for its last decades (c. 600–628) the chronicle is a contemporary Constantinopolitan witness, and its chief value to historians lies there: the Persian capture of Jerusalem in 614, the Avar siege of 626, and the dispatch of Heraclius announcing the fall of Khosrow in 628, which it copies. A reader of the record would take it for a late compilation throughout.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: "For the reigns of Phocas and Heraclius the compiler writes as a contemporary and copies official documents."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Whitby and Whitby, Chronicon Paschale 284–628 AD, 1989, introduction).
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

- **Nestorius, the Bazaar of Heracleides** (grep "Bazaar of Heracl": 0). council-of-ephesus-431.md rightly calls its documents "the file of the winning side" and offers Nestorius's second letter as his best short statement; his own long defence, written in exile about 450 and surviving in Syriac, is the major source from the losing side and is not recorded or mentioned.
- **Hippolytus** (grep: 0) — the Refutation of All Heresies (book 9 on Zephyrinus and Callistus) and the church order known as the Apostolic Tradition (attribution disputed): the main sources for the Roman church and its worship in the early third century; neither the works nor the man appear anywhere. - **The Sayings of the Desert Fathers (Apophthegmata Patrum)** and **John Cassian's Institutes and Conferences** (grep "Apophthegmata|Sayings of the Desert": 0; "Cassian": 2 files, no source record): with the Life of Antony, the Lausiac History and the Pachomian texts already recorded, these are the missing half of the evidence for early monasticism and for its transmission to the Latin West.

- **Aphrahat's Demonstrations and the Acts of the Persian Martyrs** (grep "Aphrahat|Shapur": 0). The great persecution of the Church of the East under Shapur II (from c. 340) is absent as an event and as sources; synodicon-orientale.md begins the wiki's Persian story only in 410.
- **Victor of Vita, History of the Vandal Persecution** (grep "Victor of Vita|Huneric|Geiseric": 0; "Vandals" only in passing in 5 files). The Arian Vandal kingdom in Africa and its persecution of Catholics (429–534) has no event and no source.

The sequel and settlement of the dispute recorded under pelagius/augustine sources is missing; augustine-gift-of-perseverance.md mentions the monks of southern Gaul but nothing says how the controversy ended.
- **Codex Justinianus / Digest** (grep "Codex Justinianus|Code of Justinian|Corpus Juris|Digest": 0; justinian-novels.md alludes to "his Code"). Justinian's codification (529–534), whose first title is "On the Most High Trinity and the Catholic Faith", is at least as important for church–state law as the Theodosian Code, which has a record.
- **Sources for the Persian capture of Jerusalem in 614 and the return of the Cross in 630** (Antiochus Strategos, Sebeos; grep "Strategos|Sebeos|True Cross": 0). There is no event for it; chronicon-paschale.md, which is a contemporary witness for exactly these years, is described only as a late compilation for Constantine (see finding).
- **The Ekthesis of Heraclius (638) and the dossier of Maximus the Confessor** (grep "Ekthesis|Ecthesis": 0; "Maximus the Confessor": 1 file). The opening of the monothelete controversy falls inside this stretch; it should be checked whether the event third-council-of-constantinople covers it.
- **Columbanus (letters, Rule; Jonas's Life)** and **Gildas** (grep: 0 for both). The Irish mission to Gaul and Italy (c. 590–615) and the only sixth-century British Christian writer are absent, though Patrick, Columba, Bede and Adomnán are well covered.
- **Sulpicius Severus, Life of Martin** (grep "Martin of Tours": 1 file). sulpicius-severus-chronicle.md mentions the Life, but Martin and the beginnings of monasticism and rural mission in Gaul have no entry.
- **Archaeological evidence for the third century**: the house church and baptistery of Dura-Europos (c. 240; grep "Dura": 0) and the Megiddo prayer-hall mosaic (grep: 0). The wiki's only third-century "church building" evidence is the disputed phrase in the Chronicle of Edessa; Dura is the standard comparandum and is securely dated.
- **Codex Vaticanus** (grep: 1 event mention, no record) beside the two records for Codex Sinaiticus; and the **Peshitta / Old Latin** versions (grep "Peshitta|Old Latin|Vetus Latina": 0) beside the Vulgate and the Gothic Bible.
- **Athanasius's historical dossiers** (Apology against the Arians, History of the Arians; grep: 0) and the canons of **Serdica (343)**: the documents for the councils of Tyre (335) and Serdica and for the exiles, i.e. for the decades between Nicaea and Rimini, are represented only by On the Councils. Pope **Liberius** (grep: 0) is not mentioned anywhere in the wiki.
- Thin rather than absent: **Manichaeism** has no source of its own (grep "Manichae": 6 files; no Cologne Mani Codex or Augustine's anti-Manichaean works); **Severus of Antioch** appears in one file only, though he is the chief theologian of the party whose history jacob-baradaeus and john-of-ephesus records tell.

## Method note
Read all 131 records in full. The batch contains only two Bible citations (Gen 14:14; Matt 16:18); both verified against the WEB. Opened about 50 web pages or scans to test specific numbers, dates and quotations, among them: the Michigan papyrus translation; Chronicle of Edessa (entry numbers, quoted phrase); Mommsen's Chronica minora I OCR (fasti for 203 and 258, Prosper §§757, 1166, 1240); Optatus appendix (33 names in the Arles letter); NPNF introductions to De decretis and Ad Afros; Festal Index; Martyrs of Palestine 11; Satis Cognitum §15; Palladius (prologue, 32.9); Possidius; Life of Macrina; John of Ephesus 4.61 and Lives ch. 49–50 (PO 18–19 OCR); Photius on Zosimus; Livius Agathangelos; Sulpicius 2.9, 2.27, 2.41; Jerome, Against the Luciferians 18; Origo and Rolfe's introduction; Leo, Letters 104–106; Marcellinus (454, 476, 537, preface); Isidore; Thiel's OCR (Gelasius 12 incipit, Felix 6 subscription); Tang Huiyao ch. 49; Annals of Ulster 457/461/492/493; Eugippius; Wardrops' preface; Usener's article (de Goeje, 634); Butts 2025; the 1994, 2016 and 2017 ecumenical texts; weekday arithmetic for six dated documents. Not checkable: the Beta maṣāḥǝft records for RIÉ 270/271 (automated-access wall; hence medium confidence on the find year and no check of the two stated heights), the DASI records for Ja 1028 and CIH 621 (archived copies did not load), the Warsaw museum page for Faras (script-rendered), and page-level locators in Perry, Chabot, Bruns, Westcott and Moberg, which I took on trust. Overall the batch is accurate: translators, years, series, letter numbers, consular dates and quoted phrases matched the sources in every case tested except those reported.
