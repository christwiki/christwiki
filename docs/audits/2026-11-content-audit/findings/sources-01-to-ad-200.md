# Sources first cited for events to c. AD 200

Reviewer batch S01. Severity: A = factual error, B = misleading, unsupported or inconsistent, C = minor imprecision or omission. See the [audit report](../README.md) for method and limits.

## Findings

### A · historical · src/content/sources/orosius-history-against-the-pagans.md
- **Text:** "He closes it at "the present day", in what he counts as the 1,168th year of Rome (7.43), which the index at Attalus.org equates with AD 417."
- **Problem:** The 1,168th year of the City is not the date at which Orosius closes. It is the date with which chapter 7.43 *opens* ("In the one thousand one hundred and sixty-eighth year of the City, Count Constantius … drove the Goths from Narbo"), i.e. the events of 414/415 (Orosius puts Alaric's sack of Rome, AD 410, in his year 1164, so his 1168 is AD 414). The closing sentence (7.43.19) counts from the Creation instead: "from the Creation to the present day, a period of five thousand six hundred and eighteen years". The Attalus heading "310 to 417 A.D." covers chapters 26–43 as a whole; it does not equate AUC 1168 with 417.
- **Should be:** "The last chapter (7.43) opens in the 1,168th year of the City with events of AD 414–415 and carries the story down to "the present day", which Orosius reckons as 5,618 years from the Creation (7.43.19); the mention of Wallia's peace places the close in 417, the end-date the Attalus index gives for the section." The `written` value (c. 417) can stay.
- **Evidence:** attalus.org/translate/orosius7B.html read: 7.39/40 "…sixty-fourth year of the City that Alaric stormed Rome"; 7.42 opens "In the one thousand one hundred and sixty-fifth year of the City"; 7.43.1 opens "In the one thousand one hundred and sixty-eighth year of the City, Count Constantius, who was occupying the city of Arelate in Gaul, drove the Goths from Narbo"; 7.43.19 "from the Creation to the present day, a period of five thousand six hundred and eighteen years".
- **Confidence:** high

### B · consistency · src/content/sources/damascus-document.md
- **Text:** summary: "It tells of a "teacher of righteousness" raised up 390 years after Nebuchadnezzar …"
- **Problem:** CD 1.5–11 does not put the Teacher 390 years after Nebuchadnezzar. It says that 390 years after God gave Israel into Nebuchadnezzar's hand he caused a "root of planting" to grow, that they groped like blind men for twenty years, and that God *then* raised up the Teacher of Righteousness. The wiki's own event entry (events/second-temple/qumran-community.md, body and dating note) tells it correctly, so the source summary contradicts both the text and the event.
- **Should be:** "It dates the group's beginning 390 years after Nebuchadnezzar and says that twenty years later God raised up a "teacher of righteousness"; it also speaks of a "new covenant in the land of Damascus"." (The body's "Its figure of 390 years" is fine.)
- **Evidence:** CD 1.5–11 (Schechter's translation, p. xxxi of the linked volume; own knowledge of the text); events/second-temple/qumran-community.md lines 82 and 93 ("390 years after Nebuchadnezzar … For twenty years they groped like blind men, until God raised up for them 'a teacher of righteousness'").
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/josephus-jewish-war.md
- **Text:** "The seven books begin with the seizure of Jerusalem by Antiochus Epiphanes in the second century BC and end with the fall of Masada" ; summary "the Jewish revolt against Rome, AD 66–73"
- **Problem:** The work does not end with Masada. After the fall of Masada (7.252–406) Josephus goes on to the Sicarii who fled to Alexandria, the closing of the Jewish temple of Onias at Leontopolis, and the affair of Jonathan the weaver and the governor Catullus at Cyrene (7.407–453), and then his epilogue (7.454–455). In addition the summary's flat "AD 66–73" does not match the wiki's own event `fall-of-masada`, which is dated "AD 73 or 74".
- **Should be:** "… and run down to the fall of Masada and its aftermath in Egypt and Cyrene"; summary "AD 66–73/74".
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of War 7 (Niese sections as above; Whiston 7.10–11 follow 7.8–9 on Masada); events/apostolic-age/fall-of-masada.md front matter (`display: AD 73 or 74`).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/black-obelisk.md
- **Text:** "The second caption says that the king received the payment of *Iaua* of *Bit-Humri*, literally "Jehu son of Omri""
- **Problem:** The wording is back to front. What stands on the stone is *Ia-ú-a mār Ḫu-um-ri-i*, "Iaua son of Ḫumri"; *Bīt-Ḫumri* ("house of Omri") is the Assyrian name of the land found in other texts and is how editors interpret the phrase (RIAo translates "Jehu of the house of Omri"). The entry presents the interpretation as the wording and the wording as the gloss. The wiki's event and person entries (jehu-pays-tribute, jehu) correctly say that the scribes call him "son of Omri".
- **Should be:** "…the payment of *Iaua mār Ḫumrî*, literally "Jehu son of Omri"" — then the existing sentence explaining that the phrase means ruler of the land the Assyrians called the house of Omri (*Bīt-Ḫumri*).
- **Evidence:** RIMA 3 A.0.102.88 (ma-da-tu šá ᵐia-ú-a DUMU ᵐḫu-um-ri-i), own knowledge; Oracc was returning HTTP 503 during this audit so the RIAo page could not be reopened. events/divided-kingdom/jehu-pays-tribute.md line 46.
- **Confidence:** high (for the wording on the stone)

### C · scriptural · src/content/sources/shalmaneser-iii-annals.md
- **Text:** "A statue from Assur says that Hadad-ezer of Damascus died and that Hazael, "son of a nobody", took the throne (i 25–ii 6). That is the Assyrian way of saying he had no claim by birth, and it agrees with the account of his rise in [[bible:2 Kings 8:7-15]]."
- **Problem:** "Agrees" is stretched. Kings names the king whom Hazael succeeds Ben-hadad, not Hadad-ezer, and says Hazael smothered him; the Assyrian text names Hadad-ezer and says only that he died (or "disappeared"). The two agree that Hazael was not the heir and took the throne; they differ on the predecessor's name, and the manner of death is in Kings alone. The person entry `hazael` handles this more carefully ("In Kings, Hazael is an officer of Ben-hadad … An inscription … puts it more bluntly: Hadad-ezer died").
- **Should be:** "…and it fits the account in 2 Kings 8:7–15, in which Hazael is an officer who takes the throne after his master's death; Kings calls that master Ben-hadad and says Hazael killed him, which the Assyrian text does not."
- **Evidence:** 2 Kgs 8:7–15 WEB read ("Benhadad the king of Syria was sick … he took a thick cloth, dipped it in water, and spread it on the king's face, so that he died. Then Hazael reigned in his place"); people/hazael.md line 24.
- **Confidence:** high

### B · plausibility · src/content/sources/justin-dialogue-with-trypho.md
- **Text:** summary: "Its opening chapters are the only source for Justin's own path from Greek philosophy to Christianity."
- **Problem:** A false absolute, and contradicted by the wiki's own record `justin-second-apology`, which says "Two passages bear on Justin's own life. He recalls that while he was still a follower of Plato he saw Christians facing death without fear and concluded that the charges against them could not be true (12)". 2 Apology 12 is a second, independent statement by Justin about what moved him from Platonism to Christianity.
- **Should be:** "Its opening chapters are the fullest account of Justin's own path from Greek philosophy to Christianity; the Second Apology (12) adds that the courage of Christian martyrs moved him."
- **Evidence:** src/content/sources/justin-second-apology.md (body and summary: "Justin says how the courage of Christians first moved him"); 2 Apol. 12, own knowledge.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/cyril-of-jerusalem-catechetical-lectures.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 348, end: 350` / "Five further lectures on baptism, anointing and the Eucharist follow them." / summary "followed by five on the rites of initiation"
- **Problem:** The record gives one author and one date (c. 348–350) for all twenty-four pieces. The five Mystagogical Catecheses are a separate set: their authorship has long been disputed (several manuscripts ascribe them to Cyril's successor John II of Jerusalem, or to both), and those who keep them for Cyril generally date them to his last years, the 370s or 380s, not to c. 350. A reader citing the mystagogical lectures for fourth-century baptismal or eucharistic practice needs to know this.
- **Should be:** Add a sentence: "The five mystagogical lectures are transmitted separately in part of the manuscript tradition; some manuscripts name John, Cyril's successor, as their author, and they are usually dated later than the pre-baptismal lectures, to the 370s or 380s." Widen `written.end` or say that the date applies to the Procatechesis and Lectures 1–18.
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechetical_Lectures opened ("considerable controversy" over authorship; some attribute them to John II; "likely … in the 370s or 380s"); own knowledge (Swaans 1942; Piédagnel, SC 126).
- **Confidence:** high

### C · plausibility · src/content/sources/kollam-copper-plates.md
- **Text:** "The plates give only the regnal year, the fifth of King Sthanu Ravi. Gopinatha Rao, who edited them in 1916, assigned them on the form of the letters to the ninth century or the earlier part of the tenth (p. 62). The chronology of the Syro-Malabar eparchy cited in this wiki gives the year as 849, without naming a source"; `written: 800–925`; `holding: "In 1916 …"`
- **Problem:** A settled point is presented as open. The year 849 is not merely a church's unsourced tradition: it is the date given in the scholarship since the mid-twentieth century, because Sthanu Ravi's accession is fixed to 844 by an astronomical commentary dated in his twenty-fifth year (Sankaranarayana's *Laghubhaskariya-vyakhya*, AD 869/870). The record cites only an edition of 1916 and leaves the reader with a 125-year window. The `holding` line is likewise frozen in 1916.
- **Should be:** "…the fifth year of Sthanu Ravi, which later scholarship places in 849 (his accession being fixed at 844 from an astronomical work dated in his twenty-fifth year; see M. G. S. Narayanan, *Perumals of Kerala*)"; display "AD 849 (fifth year of Sthanu Ravi); the first editor said ninth or early tenth century"; holding "Devalokam Aramana, Kottayam (Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church) and Poolatheen Aramana, Tiruvalla (Mar Thoma Syrian Church)". The author/title/year of the scholarship should be confirmed before it is named.
- **Evidence:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollam_Syrian_copper_plates opened: "849 CE … the fifth regnal year of Chera ruler Sthanu Ravi"; present holders Devalokam Aramana and Poolatheen Aramana. The accession argument is from own knowledge and was not opened.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/catechism-of-the-catholic-church.md
- **Text:** `written: start: 1992` / "published by John Paul II … dated 11 October 1992 … It is a primary source for what the Catholic Church teaches today" (no `language`)
- **Problem:** The record describes only the 1992 publication, but the text that the linked Vatican page serves and that entries cite "paragraph by paragraph" as present teaching is not the 1992 text. The original was issued in French; the Latin typical edition, with corrections to a number of paragraphs, was promulgated on 15 August 1997 (*Laetamur magnopere*, for which the wiki has its own source record), and paragraph 2267 on the death penalty was rewritten in 2018. For a source used as "what the Church teaches today" the revision history matters.
- **Should be:** Add `language: French (1992); Latin typical edition 1997` and a sentence: "The text was revised for the Latin typical edition promulgated on 15 August 1997, and paragraph 2267 was reworded in 2018; the English text online incorporates these changes."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge; the wiki's own src/content/sources/john-paul-ii-laetamur-magnopere.md exists for the 1997 promulgation.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/josephus-antiquities.md
- **Text:** "The wording of the first is disputed. As it stands in the Greek manuscripts it calls Jesus the Christ … Most scholars think that a Christian copyist added to what Josephus wrote or reworded it; some regard the whole notice as an insertion, and a few defend it as it stands."
- **Problem:** The three scholarly positions are stated fairly, but the record gives none of the external evidence on which the debate turns, so the reader cannot weigh them: (1) the passage is first quoted by Eusebius in the early fourth century (*Church History* 1.11; *Demonstration* 3.5); (2) Origen, about 248, who knew the *Antiquities* and cites the James passage, says that Josephus did not accept Jesus as the Christ (*Against Celsus* 1.47; *Commentary on Matthew* 10.17) — the main reason the received wording is doubted; (3) all Greek manuscripts are of the eleventh century or later.
- **Should be:** Add one or two sentences giving these facts, each attributed (Origen and Eusebius are already sources of the wiki: origen-against-celsus, eusebius-church-history).
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Origen, C. Cels. 1.47; Eusebius, HE 1.11.7–8); not re-opened in this audit.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/polycarp-to-the-philippians.md
- **Text:** "Chapter 9 speaks of Ignatius as dead while chapter 13 still asks for news of him, and the editors of the translation note that many have for that reason thought chapter 13 a later addition; others read it as a request for details." / `written: c. AD 110–140`
- **Problem:** The only explanations offered are those of the 1885 editors. The solution that has dominated the discussion since 1936 is missing: P. N. Harrison, *Polycarp's Two Epistles to the Philippians* (1936), argued that chapter 13 (with or without 14) is an earlier covering note sent with the Ignatian letters while Ignatius was still on his way, and chapters 1–12 a later letter (he put it c. 135). That hypothesis is also the reason for the wide range "c. 110–140" in the front matter, which the body never explains.
- **Should be:** Add: "P. N. Harrison (*Polycarp's Two Epistles to the Philippians*, 1936) proposed that chapter 13 is a separate, earlier note written soon after Ignatius passed through, and chapters 1–12 a later letter; others defend the unity of the letter and date the whole soon after Ignatius's death. The range given above covers both views."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge of the scholarship (Harrison 1936); title, author and year should be re-confirmed when the sentence is written.
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/sources/nabonidus-chronicle.md
- **Text:** "The translation linked here does not print the name of the month in which the army entered. It falls after Ululu and before Arahsamna, which makes it Tašrîtu, the seventh month."
- **Problem:** True of the Livius page, but it leaves the impression that the month is an inference. The tablet itself names it: col. iii 12 begins "In the month Tašrîtu, when Cyrus did battle at Opis…" (Grayson, ABC 7 iii 12; so also Oppenheim in ANET, p. 306, and Glassner). The Livius adaptation has dropped the words (its line iii.12 begins "When¹³ Cyrus did battle at Opis").
- **Should be:** "The translation linked here omits the month-name at iii 12; Grayson's edition reads there 'In the month Tašrîtu', the seventh month, and the fourteenth and sixteenth days that follow belong to it."
- **Evidence:** Livius page opened (iii.10–18 as quoted: "Until the end of the month Ululu … When13Cyrus did13 battle at Opis … On the sixteenth day … On the third day of the month Arahsamna"); Grayson/ANET wording from own knowledge, not re-opened.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · consistency · src/content/sources/historia-augusta.md

### C · consistency · src/content/sources/gregory-of-nazianzus-orations.md
- **Text:** `written: c. AD 362–383` / "Forty-five orations of Gregory survive, delivered between his ordination as a presbyter in Cappadocia and his retirement from Constantinople."
- **Problem:** Gregory left Constantinople in the summer of 381. The front matter rightly runs to 383, because the last orations (44 and 45, and on most datings some of 38–41 are 379–381) were given after his return to Cappadocia; the body's limit contradicts it.
- **Should be:** "…delivered between his ordination as a presbyter in Cappadocia (362) and the years just after his retirement from Constantinople, when he was again at Nazianzus (to about 383)."
- **Evidence:** Own knowledge (Or. 44 "On New Sunday" and Or. 45 "Second Oration on Easter" are usually dated to 383); not re-opened.
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · plausibility · src/content/sources/community-rule.md
- **Text:** "It is written in Hebrew, about two and a half metres long"
- **Problem:** The standard descriptions of 1QS give a length of about 1.86 m (five sheets, eleven columns, c. 24 cm high). A figure of 250 cm appears with the Israel Museum's photograph, and may be where the entry's number comes from, but it is stated in the wiki's own voice and is a third longer than the usual figure.
- **Should be:** Either "about 1.9 metres long (eleven columns)" or attribute the number: "the Israel Museum gives its length as 2.5 metres".
- **Evidence:** Web search: encyclopedia.com "Discipline, Manual of" ("about 6 ft. 2 in. (1.86 m.) long … five pieces of parchment"; 11 columns); a Bridgeman listing of the Israel Museum image gives 24 × 250 cm. The museum's own record (imj.org.il, dss.collections.imj.org.il) could not be opened (empty page / HTTP 503).
- **Confidence:** low

### C · completeness · src/content/sources/tel-dan-stele.md
- **Text:** "the largest in July 1993, two smaller joining pieces in June 1994 … Line numbers follow their arrangement of the fragments … Lines 7–9 name two of them … The editors restored the names as Joram son of Ahab and Ahaziah son of Jehoram … That restoration is widely accepted but is not written on the stone."
- **Problem:** The record is careful about the restored names but does not say that the placing of the 1994 pieces (fragment B) beside the 1993 piece (fragment A) is itself a reconstruction. The two B pieces join each other; B does not join A in any obvious way, as the editors themselves conceded in 1995, and the alignment has been challenged (Galil puts B above A; George Athas, *The Tel Dan Inscription: A Reappraisal and a New Interpretation*, 2003, puts it well below). Lines 7–9 as cited — "[…]ram son of […] king of Israel" and "[…]yahu son of […] of the House of David" in consecutive lines — exist only in the editors' arrangement, so the identification with the two kings of 2 Kings 9 depends on it. The reading *bytdwd* on fragment A is not affected.
- **Should be:** Add: "The 1994 pieces do not join the 1993 fragment directly; the editors set them to its left, and the line numbering and the pairing of the two royal names depend on that placing, which most scholars accept and a few dispute."
- **Evidence:** Web search (newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Tel_Dan_Stele; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Dan_stele; review of Athas at voice.dts.edu): Biran and Naveh 1995, "Fragments A and B cannot be joined in an obvious, unequivocal way"; Galil places B above A; Athas "Fragment B must be placed below Fragment A". Secondary summaries only; the IEJ article and Athas's book were not opened.
- **Confidence:** medium

## Method note
All 128 records were read in full. Fourteen Bible passages cited in the batch were read in the WEB (2 Kgs 3; 8:7–15; 16:7–8; 18:14, 17; 19:37; 20:20; 23:29; Isa 37:38; 2 Chr 32:9, 30; Ezek 4:5; Esth 1:1; Exod 12:40; Acts 18:12); all support their sentences except the "agrees with" at 2 Kgs 8 noted above. About 35 web pages were opened to test locators, numbers and quotations (Livius, Attalus, New Advent, CCEL, Lexundria, LacusCurtius, tertullian.org, Fordham, vatican.va, archive.org full text of Petrie, Louvre, SMB Berlin, Harvard, Manchester, the Leon Levy library, Wikipedia for cross-checks) plus ten web searches (e.g. the BASOR 390 article on the Megiddo fragment, BM 91128 = K.1692, the Athas/Tel Dan debate); apart from the findings above everything opened matched the records, including the more unusual details (Spiegelberg's "the people of Israel is laid waste", Encyclical of 1848 §11, Ephrem hymn 42 stanza 2, Tertullian *Marc.* 1.15 and 1.19, Jerome *Vir. ill.* 16 and 53, Schaff's note on Heidelberg Q. 80, VAT 16378, Harvard 2008.2.105).
Not checkable in this session: every Oracc page (RIAo, RINAP — HTTP 503 throughout), web.archive.org copies (blocked for the fetch tool), britishmuseum.org, imj.org.il (empty page), whc.unesco.org (403) and elephantine.smb.museum (500). So the line numbers and figures of the Assyrian records (Kurkh ii 89–102; Kurbail 21–30; Display Inscription 23–25 and its "Rooms I, IV, VII, VIII and X" — older literature lists Rooms IV, VII, VIII and X only; Nimrud Prism iv 25–41 and the alternative reading "47,280"; Summary Inscription 7 rev. 6′–13′) rest on my own recollection of the editions, which agrees with them except for the two points just flagged as worth a manual look; Epiphanius's section numbers and the Elephantine museum records were likewise not re-opened.
