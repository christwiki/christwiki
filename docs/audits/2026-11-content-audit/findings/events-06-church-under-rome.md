## Findings

### B · completeness · src/content/events/church-under-rome/pliny-and-trajan.md
- **Text:** "ordered those who persisted to be led away. The Latin says only that he ordered them to be led off (*duci iussi*); the words "to prison" in the translation linked here are not in it … Tertullian, citing the letter about 197, took Pliny to have condemned Christians to death"
- **Problem:** The entry never says that Pliny had Christians executed; it leaves the outcome open and presents execution as Tertullian's later inference. In the Latin Pliny first threatens *supplicium* ("iterum ac tertio interrogavi supplicium minatus; perseverantes duci iussi"), and *duci* is the ordinary Roman expression for being led off to execution. That is how the passage is universally read today (Radice's Loeb/Penguin: "I order them to be led away for execution"; Sherwin-White's commentary), and Trajan's reply ("puniendi sunt") presupposes it. The essential outcome of the trials is thus missing, and "the Latin says only" suggests an ambiguity that scholars do not find.
- **Should be:** e.g. "He asked the accused three times whether they were Christians, threatening them with punishment (*supplicium*), and ordered those who persisted to be led away, *duci iussi*, the usual Latin expression for leading a condemned person to execution; the older translation linked here renders it 'to prison'. Tertullian, about 197, already read the letter as saying that Pliny had condemned Christians to death."
- **Evidence:** attalus.org/pliny/ep10b.html#96 opened (Firth's translation: "warning them of the penalties it entails … I order them to be taken away to prison"); Latin of 10.96.3 as quoted in the entry's own Perseus link; en.wikipedia.org "Pliny the Younger on Christians" opened for cross-check ("If they do not recant, then he orders them to be executed"; "it is clear that Pliny executed Christians"); own knowledge for the usage of *duci* and *supplicium*.
- **Confidence:** medium (the entry's sentences are each literally true; the defect is what the reader is left believing)

### C · historical · src/content/events/church-under-rome/perpetua-and-felicity.md
- **Text:** "A group of catechumens was arrested together: Revocatus and his fellow-servant Felicity, Saturninus, Secundulus, and Perpetua … At the end all were killed with the sword"
- **Problem:** Two slips in the cast. (1) Secundulus, one of the five named as arrested, did not reach the arena: the editor says he died in prison (ANF ch. 5 heading and 4.3/5.1, Passio 14), so "all were killed with the sword" is not true of the group as listed. (2) Saturus, who gave himself up after the arrest, whose own vision the *Passion* includes, and who is the man mauled by the leopard in the arena scene the entry summarises ("The men faced a leopard, a bear and a boar"), is never mentioned.
- **Should be:** Add "Secundulus died in prison" and name Saturus ("their teacher Saturus, who had given himself up afterwards"); change "all were killed" to "those who survived the beasts were killed".
- **Evidence:** newadvent.org/fathers/0324.htm opened: "Chapter 5. Argument. Secundulus Dies in the Prison"; "Saturus went up first, who had subsequently delivered himself up freely on our account, not having been present at the time that we were taken prisoners"; ch. 6 heading "Saturus Wounded by a Leopard".
- **Confidence:** high

### C · historical · src/content/events/church-under-rome/perpetua-and-felicity.md
- **Text:** "she climbed a narrow golden ladder hung with iron weapons"
- **Problem:** "Golden" is the reading of the 1885 ANF translation (variant *auream*). The critical Latin text (van Beek, Amat, Heffernan) reads *scalam aeream*, a bronze ladder, and the Greek version agrees (χαλκῆν).
- **Should be:** "a narrow bronze ladder (the older translation linked here has 'golden')", in the same way the entry on Polycarp flags "May" in the older translation.
- **Evidence:** New Advent page opened: "I saw a golden ladder of marvellous height"; own knowledge for the critical text of Passio 4.3 (not opened).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · consistency · src/content/events/church-under-rome/perpetua-and-felicity.md
- **Text:** summary: "The account preserves Perpetua's own prison diary and her visions."
- **Problem:** The summary asserts in the wiki's voice what the body and the person entry take care to attribute ("The account that survives says that from this point it gives Perpetua's story as she wrote it herself"; people/perpetua.md: "a prison diary presented as her own"; sources/passion…: "If the claim is true").
- **Should be:** "The account includes a prison diary and visions presented as Perpetua's own."
- **Evidence:** the three wiki files compared.
- **Confidence:** high (that the summary is stronger than the body); the underlying authenticity is widely accepted

### C · consistency · src/content/events/church-under-rome/marcion.md
- **Text:** "Irenaeus says that he flourished under Anicetus, the tenth bishop of Rome, after Cerdo had come under Hyginus, the ninth [[cite:irenaeus-3-4]] … Irenaeus names Eleutherus as the twelfth bishop"
- **Problem:** The numbers are what *Against Heresies* 3.4.3 prints, but they cannot both stand: Pius comes between Hyginus and Anicetus (so the same passage: "came to Rome in the time of Hyginus, flourished under Pius, and remained until Anicetus"), and in Irenaeus's own list at 3.3.3, which the note goes on to use for Eleutherus as twelfth, Hyginus is the eighth. Repeating "ninth … tenth" side by side without comment reads as if Anicetus directly followed Hyginus, against the Polycarp and Muratorian entries ("Eusebius places Pius between Hyginus and Anicetus").
- **Should be:** Drop the ordinals, or add "(in his list at 3.3.3 Hyginus is the eighth; Pius stands between the two)".
- **Evidence:** newadvent.org/fathers/0103304.htm ("Hyginus, who was the ninth bishop … Anicetus, who held the tenth place"), 0103303.htm (list: … Telesphorus, Hyginus, Pius, Anicetus, Soter, Eleutherius twelfth) and 0103127.htm opened.
- **Confidence:** high (on the texts); low importance

### C · dating · src/content/events/church-under-rome/justin-martyr.md
- **Text:** "The record of his trial gives no date. It names the judge as Rusticus, prefect of Rome [[cite:acts]]. Eusebius places Justin's death under … Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus … About 165 is the conventional year."
- **Problem:** The note mentions Rusticus but does not use him, and so rests the date on Eusebius alone. The independent anchor historians use is that Q. Iunius Rusticus (cos. II 162, the Stoic teacher of Marcus Aurelius) was urban prefect from about 162/163 to 167/168; that tenure, not Eusebius, brackets the trial, and "165" is the year given by the seventh-century *Chronicon Paschale*.
- **Should be:** Add one sentence: Rusticus's prefecture (c. 162–168) gives the limits; the conventional 165 comes from the *Chronicon Paschale*. (A primary citation would be needed for the prefecture, e.g. the *Digest* 49.1.1.3 / *Historia Augusta*, Marcus 3.3; if none can be cited, at least name it as the scholarly basis.)
- **Evidence:** own knowledge; newadvent.org/fathers/0133.htm opened for the Acts (Rusticus, six companions, "for the second time", scourged and beheaded all confirmed).
- **Confidence:** medium

### C · completeness · src/content/events/church-under-rome/battle-of-the-milvian-bridge.md
- **Text:** "The Christian sources explain the victory in different ways … The senate's monument is more reserved."
- **Problem:** The entry sets Lactantius, Eusebius (twice), Zosimus and the arch side by side but leaves out the two contemporary pagan speeches that every modern discussion of the vision uses: the panegyric of 310 (*Panegyrici Latini* 6(7).21), which has Constantine see Apollo with Victory offering him laurel wreaths at a temple in Gaul two years before the battle, and the panegyric of 313 (12(9).2–4), delivered within a year of the victory, which credits a divine power that advised Constantine and names no Christian sign. They are primary, earlier than Lactantius, and bear directly on the "sign shown beforehand".
- **Should be:** One or two attributed sentences on the panegyrics of 310 and 313.
- **Evidence:** own knowledge (texts not opened); everything the entry does cite was opened and is accurately reported (Lactantius 44, Life of Constantine 1.28–32, Origo 4.12, Zosimus 2.15–16, calendar 28/29 Oct, prefect list for 312).
- **Confidence:** medium

## Completeness of this stretch

Grepped `src/content` (events, people, places, sources) and the event index for each topic below.

- **Gnosticism and Valentinus as a second-century movement** — no event; "Gnostic" occurs only in `nag-hammadi-library` (1945) and Valentinus only as Irenaeus's opponent. The movement that provoked the rule of faith, the four-Gospel canon and the succession lists has no entry of its own (c. 140–180, Rome/Alexandria).
- **The persecution of Valerian (257–260) and the rescript of Gallienus (260)** — only a clause in `cyprian-on-unity` ("Under the emperor Valerian…"). No entry for the edicts of 257–258 (Sixtus II, Lawrence, Cyprian) or for Gallienus's restitution of church property (Eusebius, HE 7.13), the first legal toleration and the start of forty years of peace before 303. "Gallienus" appears only in `ulfilas`.
- **Paul of Samosata and the synods of Antioch (264–268); Aurelian's ruling (272)** — absent (grepped "Paul of Samosata", "Synod of Antioch", "Aurelian"). First deposition of a major bishop by a council for Christology, the first recorded use and rejection of *homoousios*, and the first appeal to an emperor to settle a church dispute (Eusebius, HE 7.27–30); direct background to Nicaea.
- **Mani and Manichaeism (c. 240–277)** — "Mani" absent; "Manich" only in the Augustine entries. A world religion born in this period, banned by Diocletian in 302, and Augustine's faith for nine years.
- **The Easter (Quartodeciman) controversy under Victor of Rome (c. 190)** — mentioned in passing in `irenaeus-against-heresies` and `martyrdom-of-polycarp`; no entry. The first recorded series of synods across the empire and the first attempt by a Roman bishop to excommunicate other churches (Eusebius, HE 5.23–25); needed background for Nicaea's Easter ruling.
- **Monarchianism, Sabellius, Hippolytus and Callistus at Rome (c. 200–235)** — "Hippolytus", "Monarchian" absent; "Sabelli" in one non-event file; Praxeas only via Tertullian. The Roman side of the Trinitarian debate, the first rival bishop of Rome, the dispute over penance, and the church order ascribed to Hippolytus.
- **Tatian's Diatessaron (c. 170)** — absent. The Gospel text of the Syriac churches for two centuries; belongs beside `christianity-in-edessa` and the canon thread.
- **Archaeology of the pre-Constantinian church** — "Dura", "catacomb", "house church" all absent. The Dura-Europos house church (before 256), the Roman catacombs (Callistus, c. 200) and the Abercius inscription are the main non-literary evidence for this era, in a wiki that elsewhere uses inscriptions and objects.
- **Earliest New Testament manuscripts** — P52, P46, P66, P75 (Rylands, Chester Beatty, Bodmer) not mentioned anywhere; they would anchor the scripture-and-canon thread in the second and third centuries.
- **The Scillitan martyrs (17 July 180)** — absent. The earliest dated document of Latin Christianity and of the church in Africa, twenty years before Tertullian.
- **Clement of Alexandria; the other apologists** — Clement has a person page but no event; Melito, Athenagoras (the second-century apologist), Theophilus, the Epistle to Diognetus, the Shepherd of Hermas (only as an item in the Muratorian list) and Celsus's *True Doctrine* (only as the target of Origen's reply) are untreated. Thin rather than missing.
- **Synod of Elvira (c. 305)** — absent; the earliest council whose disciplinary canons survive.
- Covered elsewhere and therefore not missing: Edict of Milan, Donatist schism and Arles, Pachomius (all in `councils-and-empire`); Hadrian's rescript (in `people/hadrian.md`); Origen's later condemnation (`council-of-constantinople-ii`).
